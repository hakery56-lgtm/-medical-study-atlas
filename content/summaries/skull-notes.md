## 1. Definition, orientation & method of study

- **Skull** = **skeleton of the head**.
- Anatomical position: the **inferior margin of the orbit** and the **superior margin of the external acoustic meatus** must be **horizontal** → this line is the ⭐ **orbitomeatal (Frankfurt) plane**.
- **Skull without the mandible = cranium**, divided into:
  - **A) Calvaria (brain box, upper part):** 1. **skull cap**, 2. **cranial base**.
  - **B) Facial skeleton (lower part).**
- **Methods of study:** (A) individual bones; (B) the skull as a whole → **external views** and **internal views**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Skull = skeleton of the head; cranium = skull without mandible.
- Orbitomeatal (Frankfurt) plane = lower orbital margin + upper margin of the external acoustic meatus horizontal.
- Cranium = calvaria (skull cap + base) + facial skeleton.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the orbitomeatal plane?</summary>

**Answer:** The standard orientation of the skull in which the inferior margin of the orbit and the superior margin of the external acoustic meatus lie in the same horizontal line (also called the Frankfurt plane).

**Why:** Defines how any skull view is described.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How is the cranium divided?</summary>

**Answer:** The skull without the mandible is the cranium. It has an upper calvaria (brain box), made of the skull cap and the cranial base, and a lower facial skeleton.

**Why:** Cranium → calvaria + face.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How can the skull be studied?</summary>

**Answer:** Either as individual bones, or as a whole through its external views and internal views.

**Why:** The lecture's "method of study" slide.
</details>

## 2. Norma verticalis (superior view)

- **Shape:** **oval — wide posteriorly, narrow anteriorly.**
- **Bones:** **frontal, occipital and parietal**.

| Suture | Between |
|---|---|
| **Coronal** | frontal bone (anterior) and the **two parietal** bones (posterior) |
| **Sagittal** | the **two parietal** bones |
| **Lambdoid** | occipital bone and the **two parietal** bones |

| Landmark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Bregma** | meeting of **coronal and sagittal** sutures; **unossified from birth until 1.5 years (18 months)** = **anterior fontanelle** |
| ⭐ **Lambda** | meeting of **sagittal and lambdoid** sutures; **fuses at 3 months** = **posterior fontanelle** |
| **Vertex** | **highest point** on the sagittal suture |
| **Vault** | the arched roof (dome) of the skull |
| **Parietal foramen** | **1.5 inch in front of lambda** |
| **Obelion** | point on the sagittal suture **between the two parietal foramina** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Oval, wide behind, narrow in front; frontal + parietal + occipital bones.
- Coronal (frontal–parietals), sagittal (parietal–parietal), lambdoid (occipital–parietals).
- Bregma = anterior fontanelle (closes ≈ 18 months); lambda = posterior fontanelle (closes ≈ 3 months).
- Vertex = highest point; obelion = between the parietal foramina.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe norma verticalis: shape, bones and sutures.</summary>

**Answer:** The skull seen from above is oval, wide posteriorly and narrow anteriorly. It is formed by the frontal, occipital and parietal bones joined by the coronal suture (frontal and the two parietals), the sagittal suture (between the two parietals) and the lambdoid suture (occipital and the two parietals).

**Why:** Shape + bones + three sutures.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Define bregma and lambda and state their clinical correlates.</summary>

**Answer:** Bregma is the meeting point of the coronal and sagittal sutures; it stays unossified from birth until about 18 months and corresponds to the anterior fontanelle. Lambda is the meeting point of the sagittal and lambdoid sutures; it fuses at about 3 months and corresponds to the posterior fontanelle.

**Why:** Fontanelle ages are directly examinable.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the vertex, the parietal foramen and the obelion?</summary>

**Answer:** The vertex is the highest point on the sagittal suture. The parietal foramen lies 1.5 inch in front of lambda. The obelion is the point on the sagittal suture between the two parietal foramina.

**Why:** Three small landmarks, easy marks.
</details>

## 3. Norma occipitalis (posterior view)

- Looks like a **broad arch, flattened below, convex upward**.
- **Bones:** parietal, occipital and the **mastoid part of the temporal** bone.

| Suture | Between |
|---|---|
| **Lambdoid** | occipital and parietal |
| **Parietomastoid** | parietal and mastoid |
| **Occipitomastoid** | mastoid and occipital |

- ⭐ **External occipital protuberance:** prominent ridge = **junction of head and neck**; its most prominent point = **inion**. **Trapezius** attaches to its **upper** part; **ligamentum nuchae** to its **lower** part.
- **Highest nuchal line:** starts from the upper part of the protuberance; attaches **epicranial aponeurosis** (medially) and **occipitalis** (laterally).
- **Superior nuchal line:** starts from the protuberance and runs laterally, **1 cm below** the highest line medially; **origin of trapezius** laterally; **insertion of sternomastoid and splenius capitis**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Posterior view: parietal, occipital, mastoid bones; lambdoid, parietomastoid, occipitomastoid sutures.
- Inion = most prominent point of the external occipital protuberance (head–neck junction).
- Highest nuchal line → epicranial aponeurosis + occipitalis; superior nuchal line → trapezius (origin), sternomastoid and splenius capitis (insertion).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the sutures seen in the posterior view and the bones they join.</summary>

**Answer:** Lambdoid (occipital–parietal), parietomastoid (parietal–mastoid) and occipitomastoid (mastoid–occipital).

**Why:** Each suture is named after the two bones it joins.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the external occipital protuberance and what attaches to it?</summary>

**Answer:** A prominent ridge representing the junction between the head and neck; its most prominent point is the inion. The upper part gives attachment to trapezius and the lower part to the ligamentum nuchae.

**Why:** Landmark + two attachments.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare the highest and superior nuchal lines.</summary>

**Answer:** The highest nuchal line starts from the upper part of the protuberance and attaches the epicranial aponeurosis medially and occipitalis laterally. The superior nuchal line starts from the protuberance and runs laterally about 1 cm below the highest line medially; it gives origin to trapezius laterally and insertion to sternomastoid and splenius capitis.

**Why:** A two-line comparison question.
</details>

## 4. Norma frontalis (anterior view)

- **Oval, wider above than below.**
- **Bones:** frontal, **two maxillae**, **two nasal**, **zygomatic** bones and **mandible**.

| Landmark | Description |
|---|---|
| **Superciliary arch** | rounded elevation **above the medial part of the orbit**; **covers the frontal sinus**; **more marked in males** |
| **Glabella** | median elevation **connecting the two superciliary arches** |
| ⭐ **Nasion** | median point at the **root of the nose** = meeting of the **frontonasal and internasal sutures** |
| **Frontal eminence** | rounded elevation **above the superciliary arch** |

### The orbit
A **four-sided pyramidal bony cavity** with a **base (orbital opening), an apex and walls**. The base has **four margins**:

| Margin | Formed by |
|---|---|
| **Supra-orbital** | **frontal bone**; at the junction of the lateral ⅔ and medial ⅓ is the **supra-orbital notch (foramen)** |
| **Infra-orbital** | **zygomatic (laterally)** and **maxilla (medially)** |
| **Lateral** | **frontal process of zygomatic** + **zygomatic process of frontal** |
| **Medial** | **frontal bone (above)** and **lacrimal crest of the frontal process of maxilla (below)** |

### Anterior nasal aperture (piriform)
- **Pear-shaped**, **wide below, narrow above**; bounded superiorly by the **lower border of the nasal bone**, inferiorly by the **body of the maxilla**.
- **Nasal bone** articulates with: opposite nasal (**internasal suture**), **frontal process of maxilla** (posteriorly), **frontal bone** (**frontonasal suture**, superiorly), **nasal cartilage** (inferiorly).
- **Rhinion** = lower part of the internasal suture; **anterior nasal spine** = sharp projection from the lower border of the aperture.

### Maxilla
Large bone forming much of the face: **nasal notch** (medially), **anterior nasal spine**, ⭐ **infra-orbital foramen** (below the infra-orbital margin), **incisive fossa**, **canine fossa**; its **frontal process** articulates with the **nasal bone anteriorly** and the **lacrimal bone posteriorly**. Also the zygomatic bone and the mandible.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Superciliary arch covers the frontal sinus (more marked in males); glabella joins the two arches; nasion = root of the nose.
- Orbit = four-sided pyramid; four margins (supra-orbital: frontal; infra-orbital: zygomatic + maxilla; lateral: zygomatic + frontal; medial: frontal + lacrimal crest of maxilla).
- Anterior nasal aperture pear-shaped (wide below); rhinion and anterior nasal spine.
- Maxilla: infra-orbital foramen, incisive fossa, canine fossa, nasal notch.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the superciliary arch, glabella and nasion.</summary>

**Answer:** The superciliary arch is a rounded curved elevation just above the medial part of the orbit; it covers the frontal sinus and is more marked in males. The glabella is the median elevation connecting the two superciliary arches. The nasion is the median point at the root of the nose, where the frontonasal and internasal sutures meet.

**Why:** Three landmarks in one answer.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the four margins of the orbital opening.</summary>

**Answer:** Supra-orbital margin — frontal bone, with the supra-orbital notch at the junction of lateral two-thirds and medial third; infra-orbital margin — zygomatic bone laterally and maxilla medially; lateral margin — frontal process of the zygomatic bone and zygomatic process of the frontal bone; medial margin — frontal bone above and the lacrimal crest of the frontal process of the maxilla below.

**Why:** A four-row table the examiner loves.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the anterior nasal aperture and the articulations of the nasal bone.</summary>

**Answer:** It is pear-shaped, wide below and narrow above, bounded above by the lower border of the nasal bone and below by the body of the maxilla. The nasal bone articulates with its fellow at the internasal suture, posteriorly with the frontal process of the maxilla, above with the frontal bone at the frontonasal suture and below with the nasal cartilage.

**Why:** Shape + boundaries + four articulations.
</details>

## 5. Norma lateralis (lateral view)

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Temporal lines** | start as **one line at the zygomatic process of the frontal bone**, ascend **upward and backward** to the parietal bone, where they **divide into superior and inferior lines**: the **superior fades out**, the **inferior continues downward and forward** and joins the **supramastoid crest** |
| ⭐ **Zygomatic arch** | horizontal bar in front of the ear = **temporal process of zygomatic + zygomatic process of temporal**, united by the **zygomaticotemporal suture**; **lateral surface subcutaneous**; **medial and lower surfaces → origin of masseter**; **upper border → temporal fascia** |
| **External acoustic meatus (EAM)** | just **below the posterior part of the posterior root of the zygoma** |
| ⭐ **Suprameatal triangle (McEwen's)** | **posterosuperior to the EAM**; bounded **above by the supramastoid crest**, **behind by the tympanic plate**; forms the **lateral wall of the mastoid antrum** |
| **Mastoid part of temporal** | behind the EAM; articulates with the **parietal** (parietomastoid suture) and the **squamous occipital** (occipitomastoid suture) |
| ⭐ **Asterion** | meeting of the **lambdoid, parietomastoid and occipitomastoid** sutures = **posterolateral fontanelle** |
| **Mastoid process** | large projection from the lower mastoid bone, **posteroinferior to the EAM**; attaches **sternomastoid, splenius capitis, longissimus capitis** |
| **Styloid process** | thin long projection **anteromedial to the mastoid process**, directed **downward, forward and medially**; base ensheathed by the **tympanic plate**, related to the **facial nerve laterally**; apex hidden by the **ramus of the mandible** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Temporal line splits into superior (fades) and inferior (to the supramastoid crest).
- Zygomatic arch: temporal process of zygomatic + zygomatic process of temporal; masseter origin from its medial/lower surface; temporal fascia on its upper border.
- McEwen's triangle = lateral wall of the mastoid antrum.
- Asterion = lambdoid + parietomastoid + occipitomastoid sutures.
- Mastoid process: sternomastoid, splenius capitis, longissimus capitis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the temporal lines.</summary>

**Answer:** They begin as a single line at the zygomatic process of the frontal bone, ascend upward and backward to the parietal bone and divide into a superior and an inferior line; the superior line fades out and the inferior continues downward and forward to become continuous with the supramastoid crest.

**Why:** A path description with one branching point.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the zygomatic arch and the attachments to it.</summary>

**Answer:** It is a horizontal bar in front of the ear, formed by the temporal process of the zygomatic bone and the zygomatic process of the temporal bone, united at the zygomaticotemporal suture. Its lateral surface is subcutaneous, its medial and lower surfaces give origin to masseter and its upper border gives attachment to the temporal fascia.

**Why:** Formation + three surfaces.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the suprameatal (McEwen's) triangle and why is it important?</summary>

**Answer:** It lies posterosuperior to the external acoustic meatus, bounded above by the supramastoid crest and behind by the tympanic plate. It forms the lateral wall of the mastoid antrum, so it is the surgical landmark for entering the antrum.

**Why:** Boundaries + clinical significance.
</details>

## 6. Temporal, infratemporal & pterygopalatine fossae

### Temporal fossa
A **shallow depression on the side of the skull**.
- **Superiorly:** superior temporal line. **Inferiorly:** zygomatic arch laterally, **greater wing of sphenoid** medially.
- **Anterior wall:** zygomatic, frontal and sphenoid bones; the **temporal fascia** attaches to the superior temporal line above and the zygomatic arch below.
- **Floor:** **temporalis** muscle arises from the whole fossa; ⭐ **pterion** = **H-shaped suture** where **frontal, parietal, sphenoid and temporal** bones meet, **1.5 inch above the zygomatic arch** — clinically important because the **middle meningeal vein and the anterior division of the middle meningeal artery lie beneath it**; **middle temporal vessels** mark the bone just above the EAM.

### Infratemporal fossa
An irregular space **below the middle cranial fossa, behind the maxilla, lateral to the lateral pterygoid plate**.

| Wall | Formed by |
|---|---|
| **Roof** | **greater wing of sphenoid** + a small part of **squamous temporal**; pierced by **foramina ovale and spinosum**; communicates with the temporal fossa through a gap deep to the zygomatic arch |
| **Floor / posterior wall** | **open** |
| **Lateral** | **ramus of the mandible** |
| **Medial** | **lateral pterygoid plate** |
| **Anterior** | **posterior surface of the maxilla + medial surface of the zygomatic bone** |

The anterior and medial walls are separated above by the **pterygomaxillary fissure** → communicates with the **pterygopalatine fossa** and with the **orbit**.

### Pterygopalatine fossa
A **pyramidal space deep to the pterygomaxillary fissure, below the apex of the orbit**.
- **Anterior:** posterior surface of the maxilla. **Posterior:** root of the pterygoid process + greater wing of sphenoid. **Medial:** **perpendicular plate of the palatine bone**. **Lateral:** **open**. **Roof:** greater wing of sphenoid.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Temporal fossa: temporalis origin; pterion (H-shaped; frontal, parietal, sphenoid, temporal) overlies the middle meningeal vessels.
- Infratemporal fossa: roof = greater wing + squamous temporal (foramen ovale, spinosum); lateral = ramus of mandible; medial = lateral pterygoid plate; floor and posterior wall open.
- Pterygomaxillary fissure connects the infratemporal fossa with the pterygopalatine fossa and the orbit.
- Pterygopalatine fossa: medial wall = perpendicular plate of palatine; lateral side open.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the pterion and why is it clinically important?</summary>

**Answer:** The pterion is an H-shaped suture on the lateral skull where the frontal, parietal, sphenoid and temporal bones join, about 1.5 inch above the zygomatic arch. It is important because the middle meningeal vein and the anterior division of the middle meningeal artery lie beneath it, so a fracture here can cause extradural haemorrhage.

**Why:** The most tested landmark of this lecture.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the boundaries of the infratemporal fossa.</summary>

**Answer:** Roof: greater wing of the sphenoid and a small part of the squamous temporal bone (pierced by foramen ovale and spinosum); floor and posterior wall: open; lateral wall: ramus of the mandible; medial wall: lateral pterygoid plate; anterior wall: posterior surface of the maxilla and medial surface of the zygomatic bone. The pterygomaxillary fissure separates the anterior and medial walls above.

**Why:** Six walls, two of them open.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the pterygopalatine fossa.</summary>

**Answer:** A pyramidal space deep to the pterygomaxillary fissure and below the apex of the orbit; its anterior wall is the posterior surface of the maxilla, posterior wall the root of the pterygoid process and greater wing of sphenoid, medial wall the perpendicular plate of the palatine bone, roof the greater wing of the sphenoid, and its lateral side is open.

**Why:** Boundaries only — 4 walls + 1 open.
</details>

## 7. Norma basalis (inferior view)

Two transverse lines divide it into **three parts**: one **behind the third molar tooth**, the other through the **anterior margin of the foramen magnum**.

### A. Anterior part
- **Alveolar arch** — sockets of the upper teeth.
- ⭐ **Hard palate** = **palatine process of the maxilla (anterior ⅔)** + **horizontal plate of the palatine bone (posterior ⅓)**, united by the **cruciform suture** (**intermaxillary, interpalatine, palatomaxillary**).
- **Incisive fossa** (median, anterior) → **right and left incisive foramina**.
- **Greater palatine foramen**: just behind the lateral part of the palatomaxillary suture; **lesser palatine foramina**: behind it, perforating the **pyramidal process of the palatine bone**.
- **Posterior border free**, with the **posterior nasal spine** → attachment of **musculus uvulae**.

### B. Middle part (posterior border of hard palate → anterior margin of foramen magnum)
- **Vomer** in the midline separates the two **posterior nasal apertures**; its **inferior border** meets the hard palate, its **superior border splits into two alae** that articulate with the sphenoid; the ala and the **vaginal process of the medial pterygoid plate** form the **vomerovaginal canal**.
- Behind the vomer: a bony bar = **posterior body of sphenoid + basilar part of occipital**; in front of the foramen magnum is the **pharyngeal tubercle** (**superior constrictor** inserts; **roof of nasopharynx** in front; **longissimus capitis** laterally; **rectus capitis** behind).
- **Pterygoid process** (junction of the **greater wing and body of sphenoid**), just behind the 3rd molar → divides into **medial and lateral pterygoid plates**, **fused anteriorly, separated posteriorly by the pterygoid fossa** (**deep head of medial pterygoid + tensor palati**).

| Plate | Features |
|---|---|
| **Medial pterygoid plate** | directed **backward**; lateral surface ← **tensor palati**; medial surface = **lateral wall of the nasopharynx**; posterior border: **pharyngobasilar fascia**, **scaphoid fossa** (origin of **tensor palati**), **pterygoid hamulus** (origin of **superior constrictor and pterygomandibular raphe**) |
| **Lateral pterygoid plate** | directed **backward and laterally**; lateral surface = **medial wall of infratemporal fossa**, origin of the **lower head of lateral pterygoid**; medial surface = origin of the **deep head of medial pterygoid** |

- ⭐ **Foramen ovale** (oval): **posterolateral to the upper end** of the posterior border of the lateral pterygoid plate; **foramen spinosum** (small, circular) **posterolateral** to it; the **spine of the sphenoid** (further posterolateral) gives attachment to the **sphenomandibular ligament** and **tensor palati**.
- Between the greater wing and the basilar occipital lies the **petrous temporal** (triangular): **apex directed forward and medially, perforated by the carotid canal**, separated from the sphenoid by the **foramen lacerum** (short wide canal, 1 cm).
- **Squamous temporal**: forms the **anterior part of the mandibular fossa** (→ **TMJ** with the head of the mandible) and part of the roof of the infratemporal fossa.

### C. Posterior part
- ⭐ **Foramen magnum**: **large, oval, wider posteriorly**.
- **Occipital condyles** (each side of the anterior part) articulate with the **atlas** (**atlanto-occipital joint**); **hypoglossal canal (anterior condylar canal)** is **anterosuperior** to the condyle; **posterior condylar canal** behind it.
- **External occipital crest** from the posterior margin of the foramen magnum to the **external occipital protuberance** (attaches **ligamentum nuchae**); **inferior nuchal line** arises from the middle of the crest.
- **Between the superior and inferior nuchal lines:** **semispinalis capitis** (medially), **superior oblique** (laterally). **Below the inferior nuchal line:** **rectus capitis posterior minor** (medially), **rectus capitis posterior major** (laterally).
- ⭐ **Jugular foramen** at the **posterior end of the occipitopetrous suture**; **stylomastoid foramen** **behind the styloid process**; **mastoid process** behind it; **mastoid notch** (medial to the process) → origin of the **posterior belly of digastric**; **occipital groove** (medial) → **occipital artery**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Three parts: anterior (alveolar arch, hard palate), middle (vomer, pterygoid processes, foramina ovale, spinosum, lacerum, carotid canal, mandibular fossa), posterior (foramen magnum, condyles, jugular foramen, stylomastoid foramen, mastoid).
- Hard palate: maxilla (anterior ⅔) + palatine (posterior ⅓); cruciform suture.
- Medial plate: hamulus (superior constrictor), scaphoid fossa (tensor palati); lateral plate: lateral pterygoid origin.
- Petrous apex has the carotid canal; foramen lacerum lies between it and the sphenoid.
- Occipital condyles ↔ atlas; hypoglossal (anterior condylar) canal above the condyle.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the hard palate and its sutures and foramina.</summary>

**Answer:** The hard palate is formed by the palatine process of the maxilla (anterior two-thirds) and the horizontal plate of the palatine bone (posterior one-third), united by the cruciform suture (intermaxillary, interpalatine and palatomaxillary sutures). Anteriorly in the midline is the incisive fossa with the incisive foramina; the greater palatine foramen lies behind the lateral part of the palatomaxillary suture and the lesser palatine foramina behind it, piercing the pyramidal process of the palatine bone. The free posterior border has the posterior nasal spine, which gives attachment to musculus uvulae.

**Why:** Formation + sutures + foramina + spine.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the pterygoid process and its plates.</summary>

**Answer:** The pterygoid process lies just behind the third molar and represents the junction of the greater wing and body of the sphenoid. It divides inferiorly into medial and lateral plates, fused anteriorly and separated posteriorly by the pterygoid fossa. The medial plate points backward, with the scaphoid fossa (tensor palati origin), the pterygoid hamulus (superior constrictor, pterygomandibular raphe) and the pharyngobasilar fascia on its posterior border; its medial surface forms the lateral wall of the nasopharynx. The lateral plate points backward and laterally; its lateral surface forms the medial wall of the infratemporal fossa and gives origin to the lower head of lateral pterygoid, while its medial surface gives origin to the deep head of medial pterygoid.

**Why:** Plate-by-plate comparison.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the posterior part of norma basalis.</summary>

**Answer:** It contains the large oval foramen magnum (wider posteriorly) with the occipital condyles on either side that articulate with the atlas; the hypoglossal canal lies anterosuperior to the condyle and the posterior condylar canal behind it. The external occipital crest runs back to the external occipital protuberance; the inferior nuchal line arises from the middle of the crest, with muscles inserted between and below the nuchal lines. Laterally are the jugular foramen (posterior end of the occipitopetrous suture), the stylomastoid foramen behind the styloid process, the mastoid process, the mastoid notch (digastric posterior belly) and the occipital groove (occipital artery).

**Why:** Longest description — learn the order: magnum → condyle → canals → crest → jugular → stylomastoid → mastoid.
</details>

## 8. The interior of the skull — the cranial fossae

### Anterior cranial fossa (ACF)
- **Bounded** anteriorly and on both sides by the **frontal bone**; posteriorly by the **lesser wing of sphenoid, anterior clinoid process and anterior margin of the sulcus chiasmaticus**.
- **Floor:** **cribriform plate of ethmoid** (separates ACF from the **nasal cavity**), articulating with the frontal bone anteriorly (leaving the blind **foramen caecum**) and with the **jugum sphenoidale** posteriorly (leaving the **posterior ethmoidal canal**); the **jugum** separates the ACF from the **sphenoidal sinuses**; laterally the **orbital plate of the frontal bone** (separates the ACF from the **orbit**; leaves the **anterior ethmoidal canal**).
- ⭐ **Crista galli** projects upward → **falx cerebri**; cribriform plate has **15–20 perforations each side** for the **olfactory nerve**.
- Lodges the **frontal lobe**; the **lesser wing** has a free posterior border ending in the **anterior clinoid process** → **free margin of the tentorium cerebelli**.

### Middle cranial fossa (MCF)
- **Deeper than the ACF**; bounded anteriorly by the **lesser wing, anterior clinoid process and sulcus chiasmaticus**, posteriorly by the **petrous bone and dorsum sellae**, laterally by the **greater wing, parietal and squamous temporal**.
- **Floor (median):** **sulcus chiasmaticus** → **optic canal** (laterally lesser wing, medially body of sphenoid, anteriorly and posteriorly the two roots of the lesser wing); ⭐ **sella turcica** = **tuberculum sellae** (diaphragma sellae; lateral end = **middle clinoid process**) + **hypophyseal fossa** (**pituitary gland**) + **dorsum sellae** (superolateral angles = **posterior clinoid processes** → **tentorium cerebelli**).
- **Carotid groove**: from the **foramen lacerum** to the **anterior clinoid process**.
- **Superior orbital fissure**: **lesser wing (above), greater wing (below), body of sphenoid (medially)**.
- **Order of foramina:** **foramen rotundum** (posteroinferior to the medial end of the fissure) → **foramen ovale** (posterolateral to rotundum) → **foramen spinosum** (posterolateral to ovale); **foramen lacerum** posteromedial to ovale.
- Behind the lacerum: **trigeminal impression** (**trigeminal ganglion**) → lateral to it the **groove for the greater petrosal nerve** → **groove for the lesser petrosal nerve** → **arcuate eminence** (made by the **superior semicircular canal**); the **superior petrosal sinus** grooves the superior border of the petrous temporal (**tentorium cerebelli** attaches).

### Posterior cranial fossa (PCF)
- ⭐ **Largest and deepest** of the three.
- **Bounded** by the **superior border of the petrous temporal and dorsum sellae** anteriorly, the **squamous occiput** posteriorly, **mastoid temporal and parietal** laterally.
- Lodges the **cerebellum, pons and medulla**.
- **Floor:** the **clivus** (sloping surface in front of the foramen magnum = **posterior body of sphenoid + basilar occipital**) supports the **pons and medulla**; separated from the petrous by the **petro-occipital fissure** (grooved by the **inferior petrosal sinus**); behind the clivus is the **foramen magnum** (**basi-occiput** anteriorly, **squamous occipital** posteriorly, **condylar part** laterally); behind it the **squamous occipital** with the **internal occipital protuberance** (matches the external protuberance).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- ACF: frontal lobes; cribriform plate (olfactory nerves), crista galli (falx cerebri), foramen caecum, jugum sphenoidale; orbital plate of frontal.
- MCF: optic canal, sella turcica (tuberculum, hypophyseal fossa, dorsum), superior orbital fissure, rotundum → ovale → spinosum, lacerum, trigeminal impression, arcuate eminence.
- PCF: largest and deepest; cerebellum, pons, medulla; clivus, foramen magnum, internal occipital protuberance.
- Tentorium cerebelli attaches to the anterior and posterior clinoid processes and the superior petrosal sinus groove.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the anterior cranial fossa: boundaries, floor and contents.</summary>

**Answer:** It is bounded anteriorly and laterally by the frontal bone and posteriorly by the lesser wing of the sphenoid, the anterior clinoid process and the anterior margin of the sulcus chiasmaticus. Its floor is formed by the cribriform plate of the ethmoid (separating it from the nasal cavity), the jugum sphenoidale (separating it from the sphenoidal sinuses) and the orbital plates of the frontal bone (separating it from the orbits). The crista galli gives attachment to the falx cerebri, and the cribriform plate has 15–20 perforations on each side for the olfactory nerve. It lodges the frontal lobes.

**Why:** Boundaries + floor + features + contents.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the sella turcica and list the foramina of the middle cranial fossa in order.</summary>

**Answer:** The sella turcica is the hollow on the upper surface of the sphenoid body: the tuberculum sellae in front (diaphragma sellae attachment; its lateral end forms the middle clinoid process), the hypophyseal fossa in the middle (pituitary gland) and the dorsum sellae behind (its superolateral angles form the posterior clinoid processes, attaching the tentorium cerebelli). The foramina from front to back are the optic canal, superior orbital fissure, foramen rotundum, foramen ovale and foramen spinosum, with the foramen lacerum posteromedial to the ovale.

**Why:** "Rotundum → ovale → spinosum" runs forward to back and medial to lateral.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the posterior cranial fossa.</summary>

**Answer:** It is the largest and deepest fossa, bounded anteriorly by the superior border of the petrous temporal and dorsum sellae, posteriorly by the squamous occipital, and laterally by the mastoid temporal and parietal bones. It lodges the cerebellum, pons and medulla. The clivus (posterior sphenoid body fused with the basilar occipital) supports the pons and medulla and is separated from the petrous by the petro-occipital fissure, grooved by the inferior petrosal sinus; behind it lie the foramen magnum and the internal occipital protuberance.

**Why:** Boundaries, contents, floor features.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Anterior fontanelle (bregma)** closes at ~**18 months**; **posterior (lambda)** at ~**3 months**.
- **Superciliary arch covers the frontal sinus** — more marked in **males**.
- **Hard palate:** **maxilla = anterior ⅔**, **palatine = posterior ⅓**.
- **Zygomatic arch:** masseter arises from its **medial and lower** surface; temporal fascia from the **upper border**.
- **Pterion** = **H-shaped**; **middle meningeal vessels** underneath.
- **Foramen ovale** is **posterolateral** to the pterygoid plate; **spinosum** is **posterolateral to the ovale**; **lacerum** is **posteromedial** to the ovale.
- **Hypoglossal canal = anterior condylar canal** (anterosuperior to the condyle).
- **Posterior fossa** = largest and deepest; contains the **cerebellum, pons and medulla**.
- **Medial pterygoid plate: hamulus; scaphoid fossa → tensor palati.**

## 🔥 Final review — memorize before the exam

1. **Frankfurt plane** = lower orbital margin + upper margin of EAM horizontal; cranium = skull − mandible.
2. **Verticalis:** coronal, sagittal, lambdoid; **bregma = ant. fontanelle (18 mo)**, **lambda = post. fontanelle (3 mo)**; vertex; obelion.
3. **Occipitalis:** lambdoid, parietomastoid, occipitomastoid; **inion**; nuchal lines.
4. **Frontalis:** superciliary arch, glabella, nasion; orbit's 4 margins; piriform aperture; maxilla.
5. **Lateralis:** temporal lines, zygomatic arch, EAM, **McEwen's triangle**, **asterion**, mastoid and styloid processes.
6. **Pterion** (H-shaped) — **middle meningeal vessels**.
7. **Infratemporal fossa** (roof: foramina ovale + spinosum; lateral: ramus; medial: lateral pterygoid plate); **pterygopalatine fossa** (medial: perpendicular plate of palatine).
8. **Basalis:** anterior (hard palate), middle (vomer, pterygoid plates, ovale/spinosum/lacerum, carotid canal, mandibular fossa), posterior (foramen magnum, condyles, jugular, stylomastoid).
9. **ACF:** cribriform plate, crista galli, foramen caecum; **MCF:** sella turcica, optic canal, SOF, rotundum–ovale–spinosum; **PCF:** clivus, foramen magnum.
