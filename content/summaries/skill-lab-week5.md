## 1. Hand anatomy and joints

- **Objectives:** surface anatomy, history of hand pain, hand examination, **median nerve examination**, differentiating common causes of hand pain.
- ⭐ **Wrist joint components:** **metacarpocarpal, intercarpal, ulnocarpal, radiocarpal.** Movements: **flexion, extension, adduction, abduction, circumduction** (conical movement).
- **Always name the affected digit** (index, middle, ring, little, thumb).
- ⭐ **PIP and DIP = hinge joints (flexion and extension only).** **MCP = flexion, extension and some abduction/adduction, greatest when extended.**

## 2. Examining the hand: look, feel, move

- **Position:** patient **seated facing you, arms and shoulders exposed**; start at the **hand and fingers**, then move proximally.
- ⭐ **Look:** **MCP and PIP swelling, erythema, small muscle wasting.** Ask for a **fist**: look for loss of the **"hill-valley-hill"** aspect.
- ⭐ **Feel:** palpate **above and below interphalangeal joints** (sponginess); **MCP joints**: sponginess and **gentle squeeze for pain**; palpate **flexor tendon sheaths** (swelling or tenderness).
- **Move, active:** fist then full finger extension; **pronation/supination**; **prayer and reverse prayer signs**; **ulnar and radial deviation**; **grip** (squeezing the examiner's index and middle fingers).
- **Move, passive:** each finger's flexion and extension (note loss of range); wrist flexion/extension (**range and end-feel**), radial and ulnar deviation.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Look, feel, move; fist test for hill-valley-hill.
- Active, then passive movements; note end-feel at the wrist.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What do you look for in a hand examination?</summary>

**Answer:** Swelling of MCP and PIP joints, erythema, small muscle wasting and loss of the hill-valley-hill aspect when the patient makes a fist.

**Why:** Section "Look".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How do you palpate the hand?</summary>

**Answer:** Palpate above and below interphalangeal joints for sponginess, test MCP joints for sponginess and pain on gentle squeeze, and palpate the flexor tendon sheaths for swelling or tenderness.

**Why:** Section "Feel".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the active and passive movements.</summary>

**Answer:** Active: fist and finger extension, pronation/supination, prayer and reverse prayer signs, radial/ulnar deviation, grip. Passive: finger flexion/extension and wrist flexion/extension with range, end-feel and deviation.

**Why:** Section "Move".
</details>

## 3. Median nerve examination and special tests

- ⭐ **Sequence:** (1) look for **thenar wasting**; (2) **thumb abduction** (**abductor pollicis brevis**), hand palm up, thumb moved **vertically against resistance**; (3) **opposition** (**opponens pollicis**), thumb to ring finger while you pull apart; (4) **sensation** over the **thumb, index, middle and lateral half of the ring finger** (**split ring finger**), pinprick.
- **Weak distal flexion of thumb and index** = **proximal lesion** (**anterior interosseous branch**).
- ⭐ **Tinel's sign:** **tap the distal wrist crease** with a tendon hammer → **tingling in the median territory.**
- ⭐ **Phalen's test:** **forced wrist flexion up to 60 s.** In carpal tunnel syndrome, crowding or repetitive strain inflames the nerve; **flexion makes the flexor retinaculum (a pulley) press the tendons onto the median nerve, acutely raising carpal tunnel pressure and worsening sensory symptoms.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Abductor pollicis brevis = abduction; opponens pollicis = opposition.
- Tinel = tap; Phalen = flex wrist up to 60 s.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Outline the median nerve examination.</summary>

**Answer:** Look for thenar wasting, test thumb abduction (abductor pollicis brevis) and opposition (opponens pollicis), then test sensation over the thumb, index, middle and lateral half of the ring finger, followed by Tinel's and Phalen's tests.

**Why:** Section "Median nerve".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How are Tinel's sign and Phalen's test performed?</summary>

**Answer:** Tinel: tapping the distal wrist crease with a tendon hammer causes tingling in the median territory. Phalen: forced wrist flexion for up to 60 seconds reproduces symptoms.

**Why:** Section "Median nerve".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Explain the mechanism of Phalen's test.</summary>

**Answer:** In carpal tunnel syndrome, wrist flexion makes the flexor retinaculum pull the flexor tendons onto the already inflamed median nerve, acutely increasing carpal tunnel pressure and worsening neuropathic sensory abnormalities.

**Why:** Section "Median nerve".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **PIP/DIP are hinge joints: only flexion and extension.**
- **Median sensation splits the ring finger.**
- **Weak distal thumb and index flexion = proximal lesion (anterior interosseous).**
- **Tinel = tap at the distal wrist crease; Phalen = forced flexion up to 60 s.**
