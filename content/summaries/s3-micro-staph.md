## 1. Pyogenic cocci and classification

- ⭐ **Pyogenic** = **pus-forming**; **cocci** = **spherical bacteria.** Pyogenic cocci: **Staphylococcus (Gram +), Streptococcus (Gram +), Neisseria (Gram −).**
- **Staphylococci:** **Gram-positive clusters resembling grapes**, **facultative anaerobes, catalase-positive**, on skin and mucous membranes; often **opportunistic.**
- **Species (≥ 40):** **S. aureus** (most pathogenic, **coagulase-positive**), **S. epidermidis**, **S. saprophyticus** (the latter two **coagulase-negative**).
- **Coagulase-negative staphylococci:** normal flora; infect **implanted devices** (prostheses, shunts, catheters) in the **very young, old, immunocompromised**; **≈ 75%** of device infections (S. epidermidis). **S. saprophyticus: UTI in young women.**
- Staphylococci **rapidly develop resistance.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Three pyogenic cocci; staphylococci = Gram + clusters, catalase +.
- S. aureus = coagulase +; S. epidermidis and S. saprophyticus = coagulase −.

</div>

## 2. Morphology, culture and growth

- **Morphology:** spherical, **≈ 1 µm**, **irregular clusters** (single, pairs, tetrads); **non-motile, non-spore-forming.**
- **Culture:** most media, **aerobic or microaerophilic**; **rapid at 37 °C**; **pigments at 20-25 °C.**
- **Growth features:** **catalase +** (vs streptococci −); **resist drying, heat (50 °C for 30 min), 9% NaCl.**
- **Colonies:** **S. aureus** on blood agar **beta-hemolytic**, on nutrient agar **golden-yellow**; **S. epidermidis** **white.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 1 µm clusters, non-motile, non-spore-forming.
- Golden-yellow, beta-hemolytic S. aureus vs white S. epidermidis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the morphology of staphylococci.</summary>

**Answer:** Spherical cells about 1 µm in irregular clusters (also singles, pairs, tetrads), non-motile and non-sporing.

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>

**Answer:** They grow aerobically or microaerophilically, rapidly at 37 °C, form pigment at room temperature, are catalase-positive and resist drying, 50 °C for 30 minutes and 9% NaCl.

**Why:** Slide 8.

## 3. Antigenic structure

1. ⭐ **Protein A** (S. aureus): binds the **Fc of IgG**, **inhibits phagocytosis.**
2. **Teichoic acids:** polymers of **glycerol or ribitol phosphate** linked to peptidoglycan; antigenic.
3. **Polysaccharide capsule:** **inhibits phagocytosis** by PMNs without specific antibodies.
4. **Surface receptors for bacteriophages** → **phage typing.**
5. **Peptidoglycan.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Protein A and the capsule block phagocytosis.
- Phage receptors enable epidemiological typing.

</div>

## 4. Enzymes and toxins

| Factor | Action |
|---|---|
| **Catalase** | **H2O2 → H2O + O2**; **staphylococci +, streptococci −** |
| **Coagulase (free) and clumping factor** | **Fibrinogen → fibrin clot**; clots citrated or oxalated plasma |
| **Hyaluronidase** | **Spreading factor** |
| **Staphylokinase** | **Fibrinolysis** |
| **Lipase, β-lactamase, proteinases** | Tissue damage; **penicillin resistance** |
| **Hemolysins** | **Alpha, beta, gamma, delta** lyse RBCs |
| **Leukocidin / Panton-Valentine** | **Kills WBCs**; **pore-forming** |
| **Exfoliative toxins** | **Desquamation, scalded skin syndrome**; **superantigens** |
| **TSST-1 (= enterotoxin F)** | **Superantigen**: fever, shock, multi-system disease, desquamative rash |
| **Enterotoxins** | **Heat-stable, gut-enzyme resistant**; **food poisoning** (25 µg enterotoxin B → vomiting and diarrhea), **incubation 1-8 h**, via **vomiting center** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Coagulase = fibrinogen → fibrin; exfoliative toxin and TSST-1 are superantigens.
- Enterotoxin = heat-stable, short incubation.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What are the roles of catalase and coagulase?</summary>

**Answer:** Catalase converts hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen and separates staphylococci from streptococci; coagulase converts fibrinogen to a fibrin clot and marks S. aureus.

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the staphylococcal toxins.</summary>

**Answer:** Hemolysins (alpha to delta), leukocidin including Panton-Valentine, exfoliative toxins (scalded skin), TSST-1 (toxic shock) and heat-stable enterotoxins (food poisoning).

**Why:** Slides 13-16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe staphylococcal enterotoxin food poisoning.</summary>

**Answer:** The toxin is heat-stable and gut-enzyme resistant; 25 µg of enterotoxin B causes vomiting and diarrhea after 1-8 hours through stimulation of the vomiting center.

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

## 5. Pathogenesis and clinical findings

- **Normal flora** of skin, respiratory and GI tract; **nasal carriage of S. aureus 20-50%.**
- **Pathogenicity** = **extracellular enzymes + toxins + invasiveness.** **Invasive S. aureus:** **coagulase +, yellow, hemolytic**; **S. epidermidis:** **coagulase −, non-hemolytic.**
- **Pyogenic infections:** **boils, abscesses, carbuncles, furuncles, impetigo**; **post-surgical wound infection; osteomyelitis; pulmonary infection; endocarditis.**
- **Toxin-mediated:** **food poisoning (enterotoxin), toxic shock syndrome, scalded skin syndrome.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pyogenic diseases vs toxin-mediated diseases.
- Nasal carriage 20-50%.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What determines S. aureus pathogenicity?</summary>

**Answer:** The combined effect of its extracellular enzymes and toxins with its invasive properties.

**Why:** Slide 17.

**Answer:** Pyogenic: boils, abscesses, carbuncles, furuncles, impetigo, wound infections, osteomyelitis, pneumonia and endocarditis; toxin-mediated: food poisoning, toxic shock and scalded skin syndromes.

**Why:** Slide 18.

## 6. Laboratory diagnosis

- **Specimen:** surface swab, **pus, blood, tracheal aspirate, CSF.** **Gram smear:** **cannot separate S. aureus from S. epidermidis.**
- **Blood agar:** **S. aureus golden-yellow, beta-hemolytic**; **coagulase-negative: white, non-hemolytic.** **Novobiocin:** **S. epidermidis sensitive, S. saprophyticus resistant.**
- **7.5% NaCl medium:** inhibits other flora, not S. aureus. **Mannitol salt agar** (nutrient agar + **1% mannitol + 7.5% NaCl + phenol red**): **S. aureus ferments mannitol → yellow colonies**; **S. epidermidis white.**
- **Catalase test:** **drop of 3% H2O2 + growth → bubbles = positive.**
- **Coagulase test:** plasma **1:5** + broth culture, **37 °C**, control with sterile broth; **clot in 1-4 h = positive** (pathogenic).
- **Susceptibility:** microdilution or disk diffusion; **most S. aureus penicillin-resistant (β-lactamase).** **MRSA** transmitted person to person in hospitals.
- **Phage typing:** epidemiology (hospital or community). **Serology:** useful only in **osteomyelitis and endocarditis.**
- **Note:** the slide says the catalase test detects "cytochrome oxidase"; this is an error (it detects catalase) and no question is built on it.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Mannitol salt agar: yellow = S. aureus.
- Coagulase clot in 1-4 h at 37 °C.
- Novobiocin separates S. epidermidis from S. saprophyticus.

</div>

**Answer:** Golden-yellow beta-hemolytic colonies on blood agar, yellow colonies on mannitol salt agar (mannitol fermentation), catalase-positive and coagulase-positive (clot within 1-4 hours at 37 °C).

**Why:** Slides 19-25.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How are the coagulase-negative staphylococci distinguished?</summary>

**Answer:** By novobiocin: S. epidermidis is sensitive and S. saprophyticus is resistant.

**Why:** Slide 19.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the role of phage typing and serology?</summary>

**Answer:** Phage typing traces the hospital or community source of infection; serology is useful only in osteomyelitis and endocarditis.

**Why:** Slides 26-27.
</details>

## 7. Treatment, epidemiology and control

- **Gastroenteritis** is **self-limiting.** **Nafcillin or cloxacillin** are drugs of choice (widespread resistance); **MRSA → vancomycin.**
- **Sources:** **shedding human lesions, fomites**, human **respiratory tract and skin**; **hospital contact spread** (many staff and patients carry resistant staphylococci in the nose); **chronic nasal carriage ↑ risk.**
- **Summary:** S. aureus is a major pathogen with many virulence factors; diagnosis by **culture and biochemical tests**; **resistance is a growing concern**; **prevention = hygiene and infection control.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Nafcillin or cloxacillin; vancomycin for MRSA.
- Nasal carriage and hospital contact spread the infection.

</div>
