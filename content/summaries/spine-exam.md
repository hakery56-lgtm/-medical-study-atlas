## 1. Anatomy and surface landmarks

- **33 vertebrae:** **7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral (fused), 3-4 coccygeal.**
- **Curves:** **cervical lordosis, thoracic kyphosis, lumbar lordosis, sacral kyphosis**; **primary = thoracic and sacral**, **secondary = cervical and lumbar.**
- ⭐ **Iliac crest line = L4-L5 (lumbar puncture).**

| Landmark | Level |
|---|---|
| **C7** | Most prominent cervical spinous process |
| **Scapular spine** | **T3** |
| **Inferior angle of scapula** | **T7** |
| **12th rib** | **T12** (thoracolumbar) |
| **Iliac crest line** | **L4-L5** |
| **PSIS** | **S2** |
| **Sacral hiatus** | Lower sacrum: **caudal epidural anaesthesia** |

- **Muscles:** superficial **trapezius, latissimus dorsi, deltoid**; deep **erector spinae**; **triangle of auscultation** (medial to scapula) and **lumbar triangle of Petit** (above iliac crest).

## 2. History: SOCRATES and background

| Letter | Ask |
|---|---|
| **S** Site | Where is the pain? |
| **O** Onset | Sudden or gradual? |
| **C** Character | What does it feel like? |
| **R** Radiation | Does it spread (e.g., to the leg)? |
| **A** Associated | Numbness, weakness, fever? |
| **T** Timing | Constant or intermittent? |
| **E** Exacerbating / relieving | Better or worse with what? |
| **S** Severity | **0-10** |

- **Background:** **past medical, past surgical, drug history and allergies, family history (back problems, arthritis, cancer), social history (occupation, smoking, heavy lifting).**
- **OSCE order:** **introduce → open question + SOCRATES → red flags → close (summarize, thank).** **C.C, SOCRATES, PMH, PSH, drugs, family, social, red flags.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- SOCRATES characterizes pain.
- Never leave without asking about bladder, bowel and saddle numbness.

</div>

## 3. Red flags and differential diagnosis

- ⭐ **Red flags:** **urinary retention, bowel incontinence, saddle numbness, progressive leg weakness, fever, unexplained weight loss, history of cancer, recent trauma** → think **cauda equina syndrome** (**urinary retention + saddle numbness** = **emergency**); also **bilateral sciatica, gait change, sexual dysfunction.**
- **Serious pathology (Table 13.12):** **age < 20 or > 55, recent trauma, non-mechanical pain, fever, bladder/bowel/sexual problems, motor weakness, saddle anaesthesia, bilateral sciatica**; **cancer, glucocorticoid use (osteoporotic collapse)**; **weight loss**; **psychosocial factors (anxiety/depression, litigation) → chronicity.**
- **Differential:** **mechanical** (strain, disc herniation, degenerative disc disease, facet OA, stenosis, spondylolisthesis); **traumatic** (fracture); **inflammatory** (ankylosing spondylitis); **infectious** (osteomyelitis, discitis, epidural abscess); **neoplastic** (primary, metastatic); **referred** (renal stones, pyelonephritis, **AAA**). **Table 13.9:** mechanical/degenerative, **axial spondyloarthritis**, pathological (osteomyelitis, tumour, myeloma), deformity (scoliosis, osteoporotic kyphosis, cervical rib).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cauda equina = urinary retention + saddle numbness (emergency).
- Age under 20 or over 55 is a red flag.

</div>

## 4. Examination: look, feel, move

- **Before:** **wash hands, introduce, confirm name and age, explain and consent; expose neck, chest, back; examine standing; ask about pain**; order **look → feel → move** per region then **special tests**; **compare both sides, inspect from behind, side and front.**
- **Look:** **cervical** (posture, lordosis, torticollis, symmetry, wasting of trapezius and sternocleidomastoid, skin); **thoracic** (kyphosis, scoliosis, **rib hump**, symmetry, **gibbus**, skin, chest shape); **lumbar** (lordosis, scoliosis, pelvic tilt, **spina bifida signs: hairy patch, lipoma, dimple**, wasting, pain posture).
- **Feel:** **cervical:** spinous processes occiput to T1, paraspinals, **supraclavicular fossae (cervical rib, nodes)**, thyroid; **thoracic:** T1-T12, **prominent process = wedge collapse**; **lumbar:** spinous processes, steps, tenderness, **percussion with closed fist.**
- **Move:** **cervical active:** **flexion 80°, extension 50°, lateral flexion 45°, rotation 80°** (arc ~130°); **passive** if restricted (gentle; resistance, limit, arm symptoms = nerve root); **thoracic:** seated rotation with arms crossed; **lumbar:** flexion (touch toes), **extension 10-20°**, lateral flexion (slide hand down leg).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Look, feel, move; compare sides.
- Cervical: 80 / 50 / 45 / 80 degrees.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

## 5. Special tests

| Test | Position | Technique | Positive |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Schober's** | Standing | **Mark PSIS level, 10 cm above and 5 cm below; bend forward; measure** | **Increase < 5 cm** (normal ≥ 5 cm: 15 → 20 cm) = **axial spondyloarthritis** |
| **Straight leg raising** (sciatic **L4-S1**) | **Supine** | **Raise leg, knee extended; dorsiflex foot (Bragard)** | **Radiating sciatic pain** |
| **Femoral nerve stretch** (**L2-L4**) | **Prone** | **Flex knee, extend hip** | **Back or anterior thigh pain** |
| **Flip test** (sciatic root) | **Sitting at couch edge**, hips and knees 90° | **Extend the knee as if testing the ankle reflex** | **Leans or falls backward** |

- **Flip test** is **not definitive for functional symptoms**; interpret with history and full neurological examination.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- SLR = L4-S1; femoral stretch = L2-L4.
- Schober positive when < 5 cm increase.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe Schober's test.</summary>

**Answer:** Mark the midline at PSIS level, 10 cm above and 5 cm below, bend forward and measure; an increase under 5 cm is positive (restricted lumbar flexion, axial spondyloarthritis).

**Why:** Slide 31.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the straight leg raising test.</summary>

**Answer:** Supine, passively raise the extended leg and dorsiflex the foot (Bragard); positive when radiating sciatic pain is reproduced.

**Why:** Slide 32.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the femoral stretch and flip tests.</summary>

**Answer:** Femoral stretch: prone, flex knee and extend hip, positive with back or anterior thigh pain; flip test: sitting, extend knee as for ankle reflex, positive if patient leans or falls backward.

**Why:** Slides 33-34.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Primary curves: thoracic and sacral; secondary: cervical and lumbar.**
- **SLR = L4-S1 (supine); femoral stretch = L2-L4 (prone).**
- **Schober normal ≥ 5 cm increase (10 above + 5 below).**
- **Cauda equina: urinary retention and saddle numbness.**
- **Extension 10-20° (lumbar); cervical flexion 80°, extension 50°.**
