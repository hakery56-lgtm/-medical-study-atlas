## 1. Red flags and osteomyelitis: definition and routes

- ⭐ **Link to the PBL (Mr. Qasim):** back pain is usually **mechanical**, but **infection of the vertebra or disc** must be excluded with **red flags:** **fever, night sweats, weight loss; night/rest pain not relieved by lying down; injecting drug use, recent surgery, bacteraemia; diabetes, steroids, immunosuppression; raw milk/fresh cheese (Brucella), TB contact.**
- **Osteomyelitis** = inflammation of bone caused by an infecting organism, usually a bacterium.

| Classification | Types |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | **Acute** (days-weeks, no dead bone); **Chronic** (months; **sequestrum = dead bone**, **involucrum = new bone**, **sinus tracts**) |
| **Source (Waldvogel)** | **Haematogenous; contiguous** (trauma, surgery); **vascular insufficiency** (diabetic foot) |
| **Site** | **Long bones** (children, metaphysis); **vertebrae** (> 50 y, drug users); **prosthesis-related** |

| Route | Features |
|---|---|
| **Haematogenous** | **One organism**; children: **metaphysis**; adults: **vertebrae** |
| **Contiguous** | Open fracture, surgery, bites; **often > 1 organism** |
| **Vascular insufficiency** | Ulcers reach bone; **mixed aerobes + anaerobes** (diabetic foot) |

- **Pathogenesis (haematogenous):** **1 seeding** (slow turbulent flow in metaphyseal loops) → **2 pus and pressure** (ischaemia) → **3 necrosis** (sequestrum) → **4 repair** (involucrum, sinus tracts). **Chronic persistence:** **dead bone + biofilm** shield bacteria, so the **sequestrum needs surgery.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Infection red flags: fever, night pain, drug use, diabetes, raw milk, TB contact.
- Sequestrum = dead bone; involucrum = new bone.
- Haematogenous: single organism, metaphysis (children) or vertebrae (adults).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the red flags that suggest infection in back pain.</summary>

**Answer:** Fever, night sweats, weight loss; night or rest pain; injecting drug use, recent surgery or bacteraemia; diabetes, steroids, immunosuppression; raw milk or fresh cheese (Brucella) and TB contact.

**Why:** Slide "Link to the PBL case".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the pathogenesis of haematogenous osteomyelitis.</summary>

**Answer:** Seeding in slow, turbulent metaphyseal capillary loops; pus raises pressure and compresses vessels; ischaemic bone dies as a sequestrum; the periosteum lays new bone (involucrum) and sinus tracts drain.

**Why:** Slide "Pathogenesis of haematogenous osteomyelitis".
</details>

**Answer:** Dead bone (sequestrum) and biofilm shield bacteria from antibiotics, so the sequestrum must be removed.

**Why:** Same slide.
</details>

## 2. Causative organisms of osteomyelitis

| Patient group | Most likely organisms |
|---|---|
| **Neonates** | **S. aureus, Group B Streptococcus, E. coli** |
| **Children** | **S. aureus; Kingella kingae (< 4 y); S. pyogenes** |
| **Adults (most)** | **S. aureus incl. MRSA** |
| **Sickle cell disease** | **Salmonella, S. aureus** |
| **Injecting drug users** | **S. aureus, Pseudomonas aeruginosa** |
| **Nail puncture through shoe** | **Pseudomonas aeruginosa** |
| **Diabetic foot, pressure ulcer** | **Mixed: S. aureus, streptococci, Gram-negative rods, anaerobes** |
| **Prosthesis/implant** | **Coagulase-negative staph (S. epidermidis), S. aureus, Cutibacterium acnes** |
| **Chronic vertebral (Iraq)** | **M. tuberculosis (Pott's disease), Brucella** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- The host predicts the organism: Salmonella in sickle cell, Pseudomonas after shoe puncture, Kingella in toddlers.
- S. aureus is the commonest cause in adults.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name the organisms of osteomyelitis in sickle cell disease and after a shoe puncture.</summary>

**Answer:** Salmonella (and S. aureus) in sickle cell disease; Pseudomonas aeruginosa after a nail puncture through a shoe.

**Why:** Slide "Causative organisms depend on the host".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which organisms infect prostheses?</summary>

**Answer:** Coagulase-negative staphylococci (S. epidermidis), S. aureus and Cutibacterium acnes.

**Why:** Same slide.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which organisms cause chronic vertebral infection in Iraq?</summary>

**Answer:** Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Pott's disease) and Brucella species.

**Why:** Same slide.

## 3. S. aureus, tuberculous and brucellar spondylitis

- ⭐ **S. aureus:** **purple cocci in clusters**; **catalase +, coagulase +**; golden, **beta-haemolytic** colonies; **S. epidermidis = coagulase negative**; **MRSA = mecA (PBP2a)**, **cefoxitin disc.**
- **Bone virulence factors:** **adhesins (MSCRAMMs)** bind collagen and fibronectin; **biofilm** protects from antibiotics and phagocytes (prostheses); **intracellular survival in osteoblasts** (small-colony variants → relapse); **toxins: PVL** → severe, multifocal disease.

| | **Pott's disease (TB)** | **Brucella spondylitis** |
|---|---|---|
| **Organism** | **M. tuberculosis** (blood spread from lung) | **B. melitensis or abortus** (raw milk, cheese, sheep, goats, cattle) |
| **Site** | **Thoracic spine**; destroys **disc + two vertebrae** | **Lumbar, often L4-L5**; **sacroiliitis** common |
| **Features** | **Cold abscess** (paraspinal/psoas), **kyphosis (gibbus)** | **Small Gram-negative coccobacilli**; slow growth; lab hazard |
| **Diagnosis** | **Ziehl-Neelsen, GeneXpert MTB/RIF, Löwenstein-Jensen, caseating granuloma** | **Prolonged blood culture, Rose Bengal, serum agglutination** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- S. aureus: catalase +, coagulase +; MRSA = mecA, cefoxitin.
- Pott's: thoracic, cold abscess, gibbus; Brucella: lumbar L4-L5, sacroiliitis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the virulence factors of S. aureus in bone.</summary>

**Answer:** MSCRAMM adhesins, biofilm, intracellular survival (small-colony variants) and toxins such as PVL.

**Why:** Slide "Staphylococcus aureus: the leading cause".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe Pott's disease and its diagnosis.</summary>

**Answer:** TB spread through the blood to the thoracic spine, destroying the disc and two vertebrae with cold abscess and gibbus; diagnosed by ZN stain, GeneXpert, Löwenstein-Jensen culture and caseating granuloma.

**Why:** Slide "Chronic vertebral infection".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe Brucella spondylitis.</summary>

**Answer:** B. melitensis or abortus from raw milk, cheese or livestock; lumbar spine (L4-L5) with sacroiliitis; small Gram-negative coccobacilli diagnosed by prolonged blood culture, Rose Bengal and agglutination.

**Why:** Same slide.
</details>

## 4. Septic arthritis

- ⭐ **Medical emergency:** **cartilage can be destroyed within days.** **Knee** most often (then hip); **one joint** usually in non-gonococcal disease.
- **Routes:** **haematogenous (commonest)**, **direct** (injection, arthroscopy, trauma, bites), **adjacent osteomyelitis** (child's hip). **Risk factors:** **RA/damaged joints, prosthesis, diabetes, old age, immunosuppression, drug use, skin infection.**

| Organism | Group |
|---|---|
| **S. aureus** | **Commonest at all ages** |
| **Streptococci** | **Groups A, B, C, G; S. pneumoniae** |
| **N. gonorrhoeae** | **Sexually active young adults** |
| **Kingella kingae** | **Children < 4 y** |
| **Gram-negative rods** | **Elderly, immunocompromised, drug users (Pseudomonas)** |
| **Coagulase-negative staph** | **Prosthetic joints** |

| | **Non-gonococcal** | **Gonococcal** |
|---|---|---|
| **Patient** | **Older, damaged/prosthetic joint** | **Healthy sexually active young adult** |
| **Joints** | **Usually one large joint (knee)** | **Migratory polyarthralgia → one or few joints** |
| **Features** | **High fever, very hot swollen joint** | **Tenosynovitis, pustular skin lesions** |
| **Synovial culture** | **Usually positive** | **Often negative** |
| **Outcome** | **Damage common if delayed** | **Rapid response, good** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Septic arthritis = emergency; knee most common; S. aureus most common.
- Gonococcal: young healthy, migratory polyarthralgia, tenosynovitis, rash.

</div>

**Answer:** Cartilage can be destroyed within days; infection arrives by blood (commonest), direct inoculation or spread from adjacent osteomyelitis.

**Why:** Slide "Septic arthritis: key facts".

## 5. Laboratory diagnosis and treatment principles

- **Specimens:** **two sets of blood cultures before antibiotics** (positive in ~half of haematogenous cases); **bone biopsy = gold standard** (culture + histology); **joint aspiration** (cell count, Gram stain, culture, crystals); **avoid sinus swabs** (skin colonisers).

| Synovial fluid | **Normal** | **Inflammatory (gout, RA)** | **Septic** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Appearance** | Clear, straw | Cloudy, yellow | **Turbid, purulent** |
| **WBC/µL** | **< 200** | **2,000-50,000** | **> 50,000** |
| **Neutrophils** | < 25% | Often > 50% | **> 90%** |
| **Glucose** | Same as blood | Low or normal | **Low** |
| **Gram/culture** | Negative | Negative (crystals in gout) | **Gram + in ~half; culture often +** |

- **Crystals do not exclude infection; negative Gram stain does not exclude infection.**
- **Workflow:** microscopy (Gram; ZN if TB) → culture (**blood, chocolate** for Neisseria/Kingella, **MacConkey**, anaerobic) → identification (catalase, coagulase, oxidase, MALDI-TOF) → susceptibility (**disc or MIC; cefoxitin for MRSA**). **Supporting:** ESR/CRP (**CRP to follow response**), **PCR** (Kingella; **GeneXpert** TB; **16S rRNA** culture-negative), **Brucella serology.**
- **Treatment:** **cover S. aureus first** (add **MRSA cover, e.g. vancomycin**, and Gram-negative cover by risk); **narrow by culture**; **long courses** (**osteomyelitis ~6 weeks; septic arthritis 2-4 weeks**); **source control** (drain joint, debride dead bone, remove infected implants).
- **Take-home:** S. aureus commonest; host predicts organism; Iraq: TB and Brucella in chronic vertebral infection; cultures **before** antibiotics; **aspirate the joint the same day.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Blood cultures x2 before antibiotics; bone biopsy gold standard; avoid sinus swabs.
- Septic fluid: > 50,000 WBC, > 90% neutrophils, low glucose.
- Cover S. aureus; 6 weeks for osteomyelitis, 2-4 for septic arthritis.

</div>
