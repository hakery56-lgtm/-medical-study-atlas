## 1. The immune system, MHC, cells, antigens, antibodies and cytokines

- ⭐ **Immune system** = cells able to **distinguish self from non-self** and to **neutralize, inactivate or destroy** foreign molecules.
- **MHC class I:** on **all nucleated cells** (peptides from intracellular proteins); made in **rough ER and Golgi**; T cells **ignore** these cells. **MHC class II:** only on **APCs** (dendritic cells, macrophages, B cells).
- **Cells:** lymphocytes, plasma cells, macrophages, APCs, neutrophils, eosinophils, mast cells, basophils; found in **blood, lymph, epithelia and connective tissue**, in **lymphoid nodules**, and in **lymph nodes, spleen, thymus, bone marrow.**
- **Antigen** = molecule recognized by immune cells; they react to **epitopes (antigenic determinants).** **Antibody** = **glycoprotein** binding a specific epitope. **Cytokines** = low-mass **peptides/glycoproteins** regulating immune cells.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- MHC I on all nucleated cells; MHC II on APCs only.
- Epitope = the small domain recognized.
- Cytokines regulate immune cell functions.

</div>

## 2. Mechanisms of antigen inactivation

1. ⭐ **Agglutination:** antibodies form **aggregates** with antigens (may be ingested by phagocytes).
2. **Opsonization by complement.**
3. **Opsonization by antibodies** (both stimulate phagocytosis).
4. **Neutralization:** blocks **adhesion** to cells and inactivates **toxins.**
5. **Cell-mediated cytotoxicity:** antibodies on **worms** activate **macrophages and eosinophils.**
6. **Complement activation:** antibodies bind the first complement protein → **cascade and cell lysis.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Six mechanisms; opsonization boosts phagocytosis.
- Eosinophils and macrophages attack worms coated with antibody.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the mechanisms of antigen inactivation.</summary>

**Answer:** Agglutination, opsonization by complement, opsonization by antibodies, neutralization, cell-mediated cytotoxicity and complement activation.

**Why:** Slides "Mechanisms of antigen inactivation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is neutralization?</summary>

**Answer:** Antibody binding to microorganisms blocks their adhesion to cells and inactivates toxins.

**Answer:** Antibodies adhere to the worm surface and activate macrophages and eosinophils, which release molecules that attack the worm.

## 3. Innate immunity

- ⭐ **Innate (non-specific) response** = **first line**: **skin and mucous membranes; neutrophils, macrophages, mast cells, NK cells; complement.**
- **Features:** **present from birth, non-specific, not enhanced by second exposure, no memory**, poorly effective without adaptive immunity.
- **Epithelial clearance:** rapid regeneration, **blinking, tears, ear wax, nasal hairs, coughing, sneezing, mucociliary escalator, vomiting, digestive enzymes, peristalsis, urine flow.**
- **Timing:** **barriers seconds; epithelial activation and complement minutes; cytokines/chemokines minutes to days; neutrophils hours; monocytes/macrophages and NK cells hours to days.**

| Cell | Role |
|---|---|
| **Mast cell** | Histamine and heparin: vasodilation, inflammation; recruits macrophages and neutrophils; wound healing; allergy |
| **Macrophage** | Phagocytosis; stimulates other immune cells |
| **NK cell** | Kills tumor and virus-infected cells |
| **Dendritic cell** | Presents antigens, triggers adaptive immunity; migrates to lymph nodes |
| **Monocyte** | Becomes macrophage or dendritic cell |
| **Neutrophil** | First responder; phagocytosis; toxins; recruits cells |
| **Basophil** | Defense against parasites; histamine; allergy |
| **Eosinophil** | Toxins against bacteria and parasites; tissue damage |

- **Complement:** **~20 plasma proteins** from the **liver**, key protein **C3**: **opsonization, lysis of microbes, recruitment of granulocytes.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Innate = fast, non-specific, no memory.
- Complement (C3) opsonizes, lyses and recruits.
- Dendritic cells link innate to adaptive immunity.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe innate immunity.</summary>

</details>

## 4. Adaptive immunity and lymphocytes

- ⭐ **Adaptive immunity** depends on **recognition of antigens by B and T cells**: **complex, slower, specific, learned by experience, enhanced by second exposure, has memory, uses cellular and humoral components.**
- **Lymphocytes:** **B, T, NK**; only **B and T** recognize a specific epitope.
- **B cells:** epitope encounter → **plasma cells secrete antibodies** to the same epitope; activation usually needs **T helper cells**; some become long-lived **memory B cells.**
- **T cells:** **65-75%** of blood lymphocytes, carry the **TCR**; recognize **peptide-MHC complexes on other cells** (B cells also recognize soluble antigens and surface antigens).

| T cell | Marker | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Helper** | **CD4+** | Cytokines promote B-cell → plasma cell; activate macrophages; activate cytotoxic T cells |
| **Cytotoxic (killer)** | **CD8+** | Lyse foreign or virus-infected cells (lysis or **apoptosis**) |
| **Regulatory** | **CD4+CD25+** | **Tolerance**, unresponsiveness to self-antigens, suppress excessive responses |

- **Helper T activation:** TCR + CD4 bind antigen on **MHC II**; **IL-2** → proliferation. **CTL activation:** binds abnormal peptide on **MHC I**; **IL-2 from helper T cells** → proliferation.
- **NK cells:** lack B/T markers, **10-15%** of circulating lymphocytes, attack **virus-infected, transplanted and cancer cells without prior stimulation** (innate).
- **APCs:** **dendritic cells, macrophages, B cells**; **Langerhans cells** in the epidermis; present antigen to helper lymphocytes.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- CD4 = helper (MHC II); CD8 = cytotoxic (MHC I); CD4+CD25+ = regulatory.
- B cells make plasma and memory cells.
- NK cells need no prior stimulation.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the characteristics of adaptive immunity.</summary>

**Answer:** Complex, slower and specific, learned by experience, pathogen-specific, enhanced by a second exposure, with memory, using cellular and humoral components, and poorly effective without innate immunity.

**Why:** Slide "Adaptive immunity".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare helper, cytotoxic and regulatory T cells.</summary>

**Answer:** Helper CD4+ cells make cytokines, activate B cells, macrophages and cytotoxic T cells; cytotoxic CD8+ cells kill foreign or virus-infected cells by lysis or apoptosis; regulatory CD4+CD25+ cells maintain tolerance and suppress excessive responses.

**Why:** Slide "Types of T cells".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What happens when a B cell meets its epitope?</summary>

**Answer:** Most differentiate into plasma cells that secrete antibodies against the same epitope, usually with T helper cell help; some remain as memory B cells.

**Why:** Slide "B Lymphocytes".
</details>

## 5. Basic events of the immune response

- **To a microorganism:** **APC presents antigen on MHC II** → **helper T cell** (cytokines) → **B cell activation → plasma cells (antibodies) + memory B cells**; helper T cells also give **memory helper T cells**; **cytokine-activated macrophages destroy** the microorganism.
- **To a virus:** infected cell presents antigen on **MHC I** → **cytotoxic T cell**, helped by helper T cytokines → **effector CTLs kill the infected cell** (lytic factors or apoptosis) and leave **memory cytotoxic T cells**; the humoral arm gives plasma cells, antibodies and memory B cells.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Bacteria: MHC II → helper T → B cells and macrophages.
- Virus: MHC I → cytotoxic T cells.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Outline the response to a microorganism.</summary>

**Answer:** APCs present antigen on MHC II to helper T cells, which release cytokines; B cells become plasma cells and memory B cells; macrophages activated by cytokines destroy the microorganism.

**Why:** Slide 27.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Outline the response to a virus.</summary>

**Answer:** Infected cells present antigen on MHC I; cytotoxic T cells, helped by helper T cell cytokines, kill infected cells by lytic factors or apoptosis and leave memory cytotoxic T cells.

**Why:** Slide 28.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which MHC class is involved in each arm?</summary>

**Answer:** MHC class II activates helper T cells; MHC class I presents antigen to cytotoxic T cells.

**Why:** Slide 24.
</details>

- **CD4+CD25+ = regulatory, not helper.**
