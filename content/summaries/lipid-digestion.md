## 1. Dietary lipids

Lipids include a wide variety of substances:
- **Neutral fat (triacylglycerol / triglycerides)**
- **Fatty acids and their derivatives**
- **Phospholipids**
- **Glycolipids**
- **Sterols**
- **Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E and K)**

⭐ **More than 90%** of dietary fat is **triacylglycerols (TAG)**; the rest = **cholesterol, cholesterol ester, phospholipids and free fatty acids**.

## 2. Digestion in the mouth and stomach

| Enzyme | Source | Optimum pH | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Lingual lipase** | from the mouth (enters the stomach with food) | **2.5–5** | acts on **short-chain triglycerides** (milk, butter); **more significant in newborn infants** |
| ⭐ **Gastric lipase** | stomach | around **5.4**; **acid stable** | **up to 30% of TAG digestion** occurs in the stomach |

- **Salivary enzymes** (amylase) have no effect on triacylglycerols — the mouth contributes only through lingual lipase.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Lingual lipase: optimum pH 2.5–5; short-chain TAG (milk, butter); important in newborns.
- Gastric lipase: acid-stable, optimum pH ≈ 5.4; digests up to 30% of TAG.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the digestion of lipids in the stomach.</summary>

**Answer:** Lingual lipase from the mouth enters the stomach with the food; it has an optimum pH of 2.5–5 and acts on short-chain triglycerides such as those of milk and butter, especially in newborn infants. Gastric lipase is acid-stable with an optimum pH of about 5.4; up to 30% of triglyceride digestion occurs in the stomach.

**Why:** Two lipases + numbers.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is lingual lipase more important in newborns?</summary>

**Answer:** Newborns feed on milk, which is rich in short-chain triglycerides, the substrate of lingual lipase, and their pancreatic lipase activity is still limited (standard physiology).

**Why:** Milk fat + immature pancreas.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What percentage of triglyceride digestion occurs in the stomach?</summary>

**Answer:** Up to 30%.

**Why:** A number question.
</details>

## 3. Emulsification & bile salts

### Emulsification
- Lipids are **dispersed into smaller droplets**; **surface tension is reduced**; **surface area of the droplets is increased**.
- Favoured by: **1. bile salts (detergent action)**, **2. peristalsis (mechanical mixing)**, **3. phospholipids**.

### Bile and bile salts in the intestine
- **Bile salts** lower the surface tension and emulsify fat droplets → **more surface area for enzyme action**.
- **Bile** is **produced in the liver, stored in the gallbladder** and released into the small intestine.
- **Bile (pH 7.7)** entering the duodenum **neutralizes the acid chyme** and provides a **favourable pH for the pancreatic enzymes**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Emulsification = smaller droplets, lower surface tension, bigger surface area; helped by bile salts, peristalsis, phospholipids.
- Bile (liver → gallbladder → duodenum, pH 7.7) emulsifies fat and neutralizes the acid chyme.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is emulsification and what favours it?</summary>

**Answer:** Dispersion of lipids into smaller droplets, with reduced surface tension and increased surface area of the droplets; it is favoured by bile salts (detergent action), peristalsis (mechanical mixing) and phospholipids.

**Why:** Definition + three factors.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the role of bile salts in lipid digestion?</summary>

**Answer:** They lower the surface tension and emulsify the fat droplets, which increases the surface area of the lipid particles for the action of the lipolytic enzymes; they also form mixed micelles for absorption.

**Why:** Emulsifiers + micelle formation.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is the pH of bile important?</summary>

**Answer:** Bile (pH about 7.7) neutralizes the acid chyme entering the duodenum and provides a pH favourable for the action of the pancreatic enzymes.

**Why:** Pancreatic lipase needs a near-neutral pH.
</details>

## 4. The pancreatic lipolytic enzymes

| Enzyme | Substrate | Products |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Pancreatic lipase + colipase** | **triacylglycerol** — hydrolyses fatty acids at **positions 1 and 3** | **2-monoacylglycerol + 2 free fatty acids** → TAG digestion is **partial (incomplete)** |
| ⭐ **Cholesterol esterase** | **cholesterol ester** | **cholesterol + free fatty acid** |
| ⭐ **Phospholipase A₂** | **phospholipid** — hydrolyses the fatty acid at **position 2** | **lysophospholipid + fatty acid** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pancreatic lipase (+ colipase) removes fatty acids at positions 1 and 3 of TAG → 2-monoacylglycerol + 2 fatty acids (partial digestion).
- Cholesterol esterase: cholesterol ester → cholesterol + fatty acid.
- Phospholipase A₂: removes the fatty acid at position 2 → lysophospholipid + fatty acid.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the pancreatic lipolytic enzymes and their products.</summary>

**Answer:** Pancreatic lipase with colipase hydrolyses fatty acids at positions 1 and 3 of triacylglycerol giving 2-monoacylglycerol and two fatty acids; cholesterol esterase hydrolyses cholesterol ester into cholesterol and a free fatty acid; phospholipase A₂ removes the fatty acid at position 2 of a phospholipid leaving a lysophospholipid and a fatty acid.

**Why:** Three enzymes × substrate × products.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is the digestion of triacylglycerol by pancreatic lipase partial?</summary>

**Answer:** Lipase hydrolyses only positions 1 and 3, so TAG is converted to a 2-monoacylglycerol and two fatty acids rather than being completely broken down to glycerol and three fatty acids.

**Why:** "Partial (incomplete)" is in the lecture.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the major products of triacylglycerol digestion?</summary>

**Answer:** 2-monoacylglycerol and free fatty acids (with a little glycerol from complete hydrolysis).

**Why:** The products form the micelles.
</details>

## 5. Absorption of lipids — mixed micelles

- The **primary products of lipid digestion** = **free fatty acids, free cholesterol, 2-monoacylglycerol and lysophospholipid**.
- Together with **bile salts** they form ⭐ **mixed micelles**; the **fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K** are packaged in them too.
- Micelles approach the **brush border membrane**; the lipid components are **absorbed into the mucosal cells by diffusion**.
- Net result: **monoacylglycerol, fatty acids, cholesterol and lysophospholipid** enter the mucosal cell.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Mixed micelle = fatty acids + cholesterol + 2-monoacylglycerol + lysophospholipid + bile salts (+ vitamins A, D, E, K).
- Lipids leave the micelle and enter the mucosal cell by diffusion at the brush border.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What are mixed micelles and how are they formed?</summary>

**Answer:** They are aggregates formed when the products of lipid digestion — free fatty acids, free cholesterol, 2-monoacylglycerol and lysophospholipid — combine with bile salts; the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K are packaged within them.

**Why:** Contents + bile salts.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe how lipids are absorbed from the micelles.</summary>

**Answer:** The mixed micelles reach the brush border membrane of the intestinal mucosal cells, where the lipid components are absorbed into the cells by diffusion, transferring monoacylglycerol, fatty acids, cholesterol and lysophospholipid into the mucosal cell.

**Why:** Diffusion, not a carrier.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why are fat-soluble vitamins malabsorbed in bile duct obstruction?</summary>

**Answer:** Without bile salts mixed micelles cannot form, so the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K (carried in the micelles) are not absorbed.

**Why:** Link to steatorrhoea.
</details>

## 6. Fate of lipids in the mucosal cell & chylomicrons

| Absorbed product | Fate inside the mucosal cell |
|---|---|
| **Fatty acids** | activated to **acyl-CoA** (thiokinase) |
| **2-Monoacylglycerols** | **reconverted to triacylglycerols** |
| **Lysophospholipids** | reconverted to **phospholipids** |
| **Cholesterol** | reconverted to **cholesterol esters** |

- The **resynthesized TAG** combines with **cholesterol, phospholipids and proteins (apoprotein B-48)** to form ⭐ **chylomicrons**.
- Chylomicrons enter the **lymphatic system (lacteals)** → **thoracic duct** → **general circulation**.
- The small intestine is very efficient: **very little fat is excreted in the faeces**.

### Summary of lipid digestion (lecture figure)
1. A little digestion in the **stomach** by **gastric lipase**.
2. **Bile** produced in the **liver**, stored in the **gallbladder**, released into the **small intestine**.
3. **Pancreatic lipase** breaks **triglycerides → fatty acids, monoglycerides (and glycerol)**.
4. Digestion products + bile acids form **micelles** → diffuse into the mucosal cells.
5. Inside the cell fatty acids and monoglycerides are **reassembled into triglycerides** and incorporated into **chylomicrons**, which enter the **lymph vessel**.
6. Very little fat is normally excreted.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Inside the enterocyte: fatty acids → acyl-CoA; 2-MAG → TAG; lysophospholipids → phospholipids; cholesterol → cholesterol esters.
- TAG + cholesterol + phospholipids + apoB-48 = chylomicron → lymph → thoracic duct → circulation.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What happens to the absorbed lipids inside the intestinal mucosal cell?</summary>

**Answer:** Fatty acids are activated to acyl-CoA; 2-monoacylglycerols are reconverted to triacylglycerols; lysophospholipids are reconverted to phospholipids and cholesterol to cholesterol esters. The resynthesized triacylglycerol combines with cholesterol, phospholipids and protein (apoprotein B-48) to form chylomicrons.

**Why:** Four re-synthesis reactions + chylomicron.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the chylomicron and its route.</summary>

**Answer:** A chylomicron is a lipoprotein particle with a core of triacylglycerol and cholesterol ester and a shell of phospholipid, free cholesterol and apoproteins (B-48, E, A, C). It leaves the enterocyte into the lymphatic system (lacteals), passes through the thoracic duct and enters the general circulation.

**Why:** Composition + route.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Summarize lipid digestion and absorption in six steps.</summary>

**Answer:** (1) Gastric lipase digests a little fat in the stomach; (2) bile from the liver (stored in the gallbladder) enters the small intestine; (3) pancreatic lipase breaks triglycerides into fatty acids and monoglycerides; (4) the products and bile acids form micelles that diffuse into the mucosal cells; (5) inside the cells they are reassembled into triglycerides and incorporated into chylomicrons that enter the lymph; (6) little fat appears in the faeces.

**Why:** The lecture's summary figure.
</details>

## 7. Clinical significance — steatorrhoea (and chyluria)

- ⭐ **Steatorrhoea** = **increased fat content of the faeces** (fatty stools).
- Causes (lecture): **obstruction of bile flow (biliary obstruction)**, **diseases of the pancreas** and **tropical sprue**.
- **Chyluria** (an objective of the lecture) = passage of **chyle (lymph with fat) in the urine**, from abnormal communication between the intestinal lymphatics and the urinary tract (standard definition; not detailed in the slides).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Steatorrhoea = excess fat in the faeces; causes: biliary obstruction (no bile salts), pancreatic disease (no lipase), tropical sprue (mucosal malabsorption).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is steatorrhoea and what are its causes?</summary>

**Answer:** Increased fat content of the faeces, caused by obstruction of the flow of bile (biliary obstruction), diseases of the pancreas or tropical sprue.

**Why:** Bile + pancreas + mucosa.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain why pancreatic disease causes steatorrhoea.</summary>

**Answer:** Without pancreatic lipase, colipase, cholesterol esterase and phospholipase A₂, dietary fat cannot be hydrolysed and absorbed, so it is excreted in the faeces.

**Why:** Enzyme deficiency = no absorbable products.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why does biliary obstruction cause steatorrhoea?</summary>

**Answer:** Bile salts are absent from the intestine, so fat is not emulsified and mixed micelles are not formed, preventing lipid digestion and absorption and increasing faecal fat.

**Why:** Emulsification + micelles.
</details>

- **>90%** of dietary fat is **TAG**.
