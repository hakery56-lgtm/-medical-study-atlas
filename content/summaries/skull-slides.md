## 1. The head skeleton (skull)

- The skull = **head skeleton** = ⭐ **22 bones** = **cranium + mandible**.
- **Cranium = calvaria + face.**
- **Calvaria** = **4 unpaired + 2 paired** bones (8 bones): unpaired = frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid, occipital; paired = parietal and temporal.
- **Face** = **6 paired + 2 unpaired** bones (**14 facial bones**).

### The 14 facial bones (lecture figure)
| Bone | Number |
|---|---|
| **Nasal** | 2 |
| **Lacrimal** | 2 |
| **Inferior nasal concha** | 2 |
| **Maxilla** | 2 |
| **Palatine** | 2 |
| **Zygomatic** | 2 |
| **Mandible** | 1 |
| **Vomer** | 1 |

**Check:** 8 calvarial + 14 facial = **22 bones**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Skull = 22 bones = cranium + mandible; cranium = calvaria + face.
- Calvaria = 4 unpaired + 2 paired; face = 6 paired + 2 unpaired.
- Facial bones: nasal, lacrimal, inferior concha, maxilla, palatine, zygomatic (paired); mandible and vomer (single).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is the skull divided and how many bones does it have?</summary>

**Answer:** The skull (head skeleton) has 22 bones and consists of the cranium and the mandible. The cranium is made of the calvaria and the face. The calvaria has 4 unpaired and 2 paired bones; the face has 6 paired and 2 unpaired bones.

**Why:** Numbers are the whole question: 22, 4 + 2, 6 + 2.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the 14 facial bones.</summary>

**Answer:** Paired: nasal (2), lacrimal (2), inferior nasal concha (2), maxilla (2), palatine (2) and zygomatic (2); unpaired: mandible (1) and vomer (1).

**Why:** 6 pairs = 12, plus 2 single = 14.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which bones form the calvaria?</summary>

**Answer:** Four unpaired bones (frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid, occipital) and two paired bones (parietal and temporal).

**Why:** The lecture gives the count (4 + 2); the names are standard anatomy.
</details>

## 2. The views of the skull (norma)

### Superior view — **Norma verticalis**
- The **skull cap (vault)**.
- **Sutures:** A. **sagittal**, B. **coronal**, C. **lambdoid**.
- **Bregma** = meeting of sagittal and coronal sutures.
- **Lambda** = meeting of sagittal and lambdoid sutures.
- Figure: frontal bone, parietal bone, **parietal foramen**, occipital bone.

### Posterior view — **Norma occipitalis**
- **Sutures:** sagittal, lambdoid, **occipitomastoid**.
- **External occipital protuberance** and the **inion** (its tip).
- **Nuchal lines**; **mastoid notch**.

### Anterior view — **Norma frontalis**
- Forehead, **superciliary arch**, **glabella**, **nasion**, **orbit**.
- **Supra-orbital, infra-orbital and mental foramina**.
- **Piriform aperture**, **maxilla**, **mandible**.

### Lateral view — **Norma lateralis**
- **Sutures:** coronal, lambdoid, **sphenoparietal**, **sphenotemporal**.
- ⭐ **Pterion**, **temporal lines**, **zygomatic arch**, **temporal bone**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Verticalis: vault; sagittal, coronal, lambdoid sutures; bregma and lambda.
- Occipitalis: external occipital protuberance, inion, nuchal lines, mastoid notch.
- Frontalis: glabella, nasion, orbit, three foramina (supra-orbital, infra-orbital, mental), piriform aperture.
- Lateralis: pterion, temporal lines, zygomatic arch, sphenoparietal and sphenotemporal sutures.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name the five views of the skull and give the sutures seen from above.</summary>

**Answer:** Norma verticalis (superior), occipitalis (posterior), frontalis (anterior), lateralis (lateral) and basalis (inferior). From above the sutures are the sagittal, coronal and lambdoid, with bregma and lambda as landmarks.

**Why:** Five normae; verticalis = three sutures.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What can be seen on the posterior view of the skull?</summary>

**Answer:** The sagittal, lambdoid and occipitomastoid sutures, the external occipital protuberance, the inion, the nuchal lines and the mastoid notch.

**Why:** A short list the examiner can ask directly.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the anterior and lateral views.</summary>

**Answer:** Anterior: forehead, superciliary arch, glabella, nasion, orbit, supra-orbital, infra-orbital and mental foramina, piriform aperture, maxilla and mandible. Lateral: coronal, lambdoid, sphenoparietal and sphenotemporal sutures, the pterion, temporal lines, zygomatic arch and temporal bone.

**Why:** Both lists straight from the slides.
</details>

## 3. Inferior view — **Norma basalis**

- Extends **from the middle incisor to the superior nuchal line**, and to the **mastoid processes and zygomatic arches**.
- Divided (for description) into **three parts**:

| Part | Extent | Main features |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Anterior** | teeth and hard palate | **teeth, hard palate, incisive fossa, palatine foramina, posterior nasal spine** |
| **B. Middle** | **behind the hard palate to the foramen magnum** | **vomer and sphenoid** (body, greater and lesser wings, pterygoid processes, spine); foramina **spinosum, ovale, lacerum**, **carotid canal**; **basilar part of occipital bone**; **pharyngeal tubercle**; **mandibular fossa** |
| **C. Posterior** | **from the anterior edge of foramen magnum to the superior nuchal lines** | **foramen magnum, condyles** (occipital); **mastoid and styloid processes** (temporal); **jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal, stylomastoid foramen** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Norma basalis = 3 parts: anterior (palate), middle (sphenoid + foramina to foramen magnum), posterior (foramen magnum to nuchal lines).
- Middle part: foramina spinosum, ovale, lacerum, carotid canal, mandibular fossa, pharyngeal tubercle.
- Posterior part: foramen magnum, condyles, mastoid and styloid processes, jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal, stylomastoid foramen.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the three parts of the inferior view of the skull.</summary>

**Answer:** Anterior part: teeth, hard palate, incisive fossa, palatine foramina and posterior nasal spine. Middle part (behind the hard palate to the foramen magnum): vomer and sphenoid bone with its wings, pterygoid processes and spine; foramina spinosum, ovale and lacerum, carotid canal; basilar part of the occipital bone, pharyngeal tubercle and mandibular fossa. Posterior part (anterior edge of foramen magnum to the superior nuchal lines): foramen magnum and condyles, mastoid and styloid processes, jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal and stylomastoid foramen.

**Why:** A 3-row table — memorize one landmark per row first.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which foramina are in the middle part of the inferior view?</summary>

**Answer:** Foramen spinosum, foramen ovale, foramen lacerum and the carotid canal.

**Why:** All behind the hard palate, near the sphenoid and petrous bones.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What lies between the hard palate and the foramen magnum on the base of the skull?</summary>

**Answer:** The middle part: the vomer and sphenoid bones, the foramina spinosum, ovale and lacerum, the carotid canal, the basilar part of the occipital bone, the pharyngeal tubercle and the mandibular fossa.

**Why:** "Behind hard palate to foramen magnum" = middle part.
</details>

## 4. Foramina and what passes through them

### Anterior, lateral and superior views
| Foramen | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Supra-orbital** | supra-orbital nerve and vessels |
| **Infra-orbital** | infra-orbital nerve and vessels |
| **Mental** | mental nerve and vessels |
| **Zygomaticofacial** (lateral view) | zygomaticofacial nerve |
| **Parietal** (superior view) | **emissary veins** |

### Inferior view
| Foramen | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Incisive foramina** | **nasopalatine nerve**; **sphenopalatine vessels** |
| **Greater palatine foramen** | greater palatine nerve and vessels |
| **Lesser palatine foramina** | lesser palatine nerves and vessels |
| **Pterygoid canal** | pterygoid nerve and vessels |
| ⭐ **Foramen ovale** | **mandibular nerve [V3]** |
| ⭐ **Foramen spinosum** | **middle meningeal artery** |
| **Foramen lacerum** | **filled with cartilage** |
| **Carotid canal** | **internal carotid artery** and nerve plexus |
| ⭐ **Foramen magnum** | **spinal cord**; **vertebral arteries** and nerve plexuses; **anterior and posterior spinal arteries**; **roots of the accessory nerve** |
| **Condylar canal** | emissary veins |
| **Hypoglossal canal** | **hypoglossal nerve [XII]** and vessels |
| ⭐ **Jugular foramen** | **internal jugular vein**; **inferior petrosal sinus**; **IX, X, XI cranial nerves** |
| **Stylomastoid foramen** | **facial nerve [VII]** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Foramen ovale → V3; spinosum → middle meningeal artery; lacerum → cartilage only; carotid canal → internal carotid.
- Jugular foramen → IJV, inferior petrosal sinus, IX–X–XI; hypoglossal canal → XII; stylomastoid → VII.
- Foramen magnum → spinal cord, vertebral arteries, spinal arteries, accessory nerve roots.
- Supra-orbital, infra-orbital, mental: each carries a nerve and vessels with its own name.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What passes through the foramen ovale, foramen spinosum and foramen lacerum?</summary>

**Answer:** Foramen ovale — the mandibular nerve (V3); foramen spinosum — the middle meningeal artery; foramen lacerum — it is filled with cartilage (nothing important passes through it as such).

**Why:** Three neighbouring foramina — three different answers.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the structures passing through the jugular foramen and the foramen magnum.</summary>

**Answer:** Jugular foramen: internal jugular vein, inferior petrosal sinus and cranial nerves IX, X and XI. Foramen magnum: spinal cord, vertebral arteries and nerve plexuses, anterior spinal artery, posterior spinal arteries and the roots of the accessory nerve.

**Why:** The two biggest foramina of the base.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which foramen transmits (a) the facial nerve, (b) the hypoglossal nerve, (c) emissary veins from the vault?</summary>

**Answer:** (a) Stylomastoid foramen; (b) hypoglossal canal; (c) parietal foramen (the condylar canal also transmits emissary veins).

**Why:** Foramen name often matches the nerve's name.
</details>

## 5. The cranial cavity and the three cranial fossae

**Cranial cavity** = the space within the calvaria that contains the **brain, meninges, proximal parts of the cranial nerves, blood vessels and cranial venous sinuses**.

| Fossa | Position | Contents / boundaries |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior** | above the **nasal cavity and orbits** | filled by the **frontal lobes**; features: **frontal crest, crista galli, foramen cecum, orbital part of frontal bone, cribriform plate (ethmoid) with its foramina, lesser wing of sphenoid, anterior clinoid process, body of sphenoid** |
| **Middle** | behind the anterior fossa | boundary with the anterior fossa in the midline = **anterior edge of the chiasmatic sulcus** (groove between the optic canals across the sphenoid body); posteriorly formed by the **anterior surface of the petrous and mastoid part of the temporal bone**; features: **chiasmatic sulcus, tuberculum sellae, middle clinoid process, optic canal, foramen rotundum, superior orbital fissure, greater wing of sphenoid, carotid canal opening, groove for middle meningeal artery, hypophyseal fossa, posterior clinoid process, dorsum sellae, foramina lacerum / ovale / spinosum, trigeminal impression, groove and hiatus for the greater and lesser petrosal nerves, arcuate eminence, tegmen tympani** |
| **Posterior** | ⭐ **largest and deepest** | contains the **brainstem (midbrain, pons, medulla) and the cerebellum**; anterior boundaries in the midline = **dorsum sellae and clivus**; posteriorly = **squamous part of the occipital bone**; features: **clivus, jugular tubercle, internal acoustic meatus, jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal, foramen magnum, grooves for inferior petrosal, sigmoid and transverse sinuses, superior border of the petrous temporal, internal occipital crest and protuberance** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cranial cavity = brain + meninges + cranial nerve roots + vessels + venous sinuses.
- Anterior fossa: frontal lobes; crista galli, cribriform plate, lesser wing.
- Middle fossa: bounded by the chiasmatic sulcus in front and the petrous temporal behind; optic canal, rotundum, ovale, spinosum, sella turcica.
- Posterior fossa: largest and deepest; brainstem + cerebellum; clivus, foramen magnum, jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the cranial cavity and name its three fossae.</summary>

**Answer:** The cranial cavity is the space within the calvaria that contains the brain, meninges, proximal parts of the cranial nerves, blood vessels and cranial venous sinuses. It is divided into the anterior, middle and posterior cranial fossae.

**Why:** Definition + the three fossae.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the boundaries and contents of the posterior cranial fossa.</summary>

**Answer:** It is the largest and deepest fossa and contains the brainstem (midbrain, pons, medulla) and the cerebellum. Anteriorly in the midline it is bounded by the dorsum sellae and the clivus; posteriorly by the squamous part of the occipital bone. It contains the foramen magnum, jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal, internal acoustic meatus and the grooves for the sigmoid, transverse and inferior petrosal sinuses.

**Why:** Boundaries + contents + openings.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What separates the anterior and middle cranial fossae in the midline, and what forms the posterior limit of the middle fossa?</summary>

**Answer:** In the midline the anterior edge of the chiasmatic sulcus (the groove between the optic canals across the sphenoid body) separates them. The middle fossa is limited posteriorly by the anterior surface of the petrous and mastoid parts of the temporal bone.

**Why:** Boundaries are a favourite OSCE question.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- Skull = **22 bones** (8 calvarial + 14 facial); the 6 ear ossicles are not counted.
- **Bregma** = sagittal + coronal; **lambda** = sagittal + lambdoid.
- **Foramen ovale → V3** (mandibular); **spinosum → middle meningeal artery**; **lacerum = cartilage**.
- **Jugular foramen** carries **IX, X, XI** (not XII — that is the hypoglossal canal).
- **Posterior fossa** = largest and deepest; **anterior fossa** = frontal lobes.
- Inferior view: three parts — **anterior, middle, posterior**.

## 🔥 Final review — memorize before the exam

1. Skull = **22 bones** = cranium + mandible; calvaria **4 + 2**, face **6 paired + 2 unpaired** (14).
2. Views: **verticalis, occipitalis, frontalis, lateralis, basalis**.
3. Sutures: sagittal, coronal, lambdoid (+ occipitomastoid, sphenoparietal, sphenotemporal).
4. Landmarks: **bregma, lambda, inion, glabella, nasion, pterion**.
5. Anterior-view foramina: **supra-orbital, infra-orbital, mental**; lateral: **zygomaticofacial**; superior: **parietal** (emissary veins).
6. Base foramina: **ovale (V3), spinosum (middle meningeal a.), lacerum (cartilage), carotid canal, magnum, jugular (IX–XI), hypoglossal (XII), stylomastoid (VII)**.
7. Cranial fossae: **anterior (frontal lobes)**, **middle**, **posterior (brainstem + cerebellum, largest/deepest)**.
