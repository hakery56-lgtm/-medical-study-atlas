## 3. Learning outcomes

1. **Explain the pathophysiology of Graves' disease.**
2. **Differentiate the causes of thyrotoxicosis.**
3. **Understand pharmacologic management.**
4. **Apply a diagnostic algorithm.**
5. **Integrate health promotion and safety, including thyroid storm.**

⭐ **Standard teaching (not on the slides)** for each outcome:

| Outcome | Standard teaching |
|---|---|
| Graves' pathophysiology | Autoimmune: **TSH-receptor-stimulating antibodies** cause unregulated hormone production and diffuse goitre; eye disease and pretibial myxoedema are specific. |
| Causes | **Hyperthyroidism**: Graves' (commonest), toxic multinodular goitre, toxic adenoma. **Thyrotoxicosis without hyperthyroidism**: thyroiditis, exogenous hormone. |
| Drugs | **Carbimazole/methimazole or propylthiouracil** block synthesis (warn about agranulocytosis); **propranolol** controls symptoms. |
| Algorithm | **TSH first**, then fT4/T3; then **TSH-receptor antibodies** or radioiodine uptake to find the cause. |
| Safety | **Thyroid storm**: fever, severe tachycardia, delirium; precipitated by infection, surgery or stopping drugs; emergency. |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Five outcomes: pathophysiology, causes, drugs, diagnostic algorithm, health promotion and safety including thyroid storm.
- Graves' disease is autoimmune (standard teaching).
- Thyroid storm is an emergency (standard teaching).

</div>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Low TSH + high fT4 = primary hyperthyroidism**; do not read it as pituitary disease.
- **Weight loss with a big appetite** is the clue to hypermetabolism, not anxiety.
- **Thyrotoxicosis is not always hyperthyroidism** (thyroiditis, exogenous hormone).
- **Thyroid storm is a medical emergency.**
