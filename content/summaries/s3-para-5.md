### Introduction
- ***Balantidium coli*** — **phylum Ciliophora, family Balantididae**; the **only ciliate** and the **largest protozoan parasite of humans**.
- **First described by Malmsten in 1857 in the dysenteric stool of patients.**

### Morphology
| | **Trophozoite** | **Cyst (infective)** |
|---|---|---|
| Size/shape | **60–70 × 40–50 µm**; **narrow anterior end, broad posterior end** | **spherical, 40–60 µm** |
| Movement/wall | **actively motile by short delicate cilia** covering the body | **thick, transparent, double-layered wall** protecting it from unsuitable conditions |
| Structures | **cytostome** (groove leading to the mouth), **cytopharynx** (short funnel-shaped gullet), **cytopyge** (small posterior anal pore); **two nuclei: large kidney-shaped macronucleus + small spherical micronucleus**; **1–2 contractile vacuoles** and **several food vacuoles** (starch granules, debris, bacteria, erythrocytes) | **granular cytoplasm** with **macronucleus and micronucleus** |
| Notes | **feeds on cell debris, bacteria, starch grains** in the large intestine of humans, pigs, monkeys | **found mainly in chronic disease and carriers**, and in the stool of pigs, monkeys or humans |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Balantidiasis: cyst orally, large intestine (colon + caecum), zoonosis; natural host = pig, human = accidental host; reservoir = pig, monkey, rat.
- Ciliophora, Balantididae; only ciliate and largest protozoan parasite of humans; Malmsten 1857.
- Trophozoite 60–70 × 40–50 µm, cilia, cytostome/cytopharynx/cytopyge, macro + micronucleus. Cyst 40–60 µm, double wall, infective.

</div>

## 2. Life cycle (one host)

1. **Humans ingest food, water or fingers contaminated with mature cysts** from the stool of patients, carriers or pigs.
2. The **cyst resists gastric juice** and passes through the stomach undamaged; **ingested trophozoites are killed in the stomach**.
3. **Excystation occurs in the small intestine** — **each cyst liberates ONE trophozoite**, which **does not infect the small-intestinal wall** and is carried to the **large intestine**.
4. In the large-intestinal cavity trophozoites **multiply by transverse binary fission** and by **sexual union (conjugation)** with exchange of nuclear material.
5. Some trophozoites pass in the stool; others **encyst under unsuitable conditions as they descend along the colon** and pass as **cysts**, **viable 1–2 days in soil**.
6. Trophozoites **secrete enzymes that help them invade the colonic mucosa**, reaching the wall tissue but **not penetrating the wall**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Direct (one host); infective cyst ingested; cyst resists gastric juice, trophozoite is killed.
- Excystation in the small intestine: 1 cyst → 1 trophozoite → large intestine.
- Transverse binary fission + conjugation; encystment as it descends the colon; cysts viable 1–2 days.

</div>

## 3. Pathogenesis and clinical features

- **Most cases are asymptomatic.**
- **Disease appears when host resistance is lowered:** **malnourishment, alcoholism, achlorhydria (absence of HCl) and concurrent intestinal parasitic or bacterial infection**.
- **Symptoms** are due to **invasion of the colonic wall by trophozoites** → **muco-ulceration, irregular ulcers with submucosal abscesses**; **no extra-intestinal balantidiasis except the appendix, rarely the liver**.
- **Ulcers may be secondarily infected by bacteria**; in chronic disease **intestinal perforation, peritonitis and even death** may occur.

| | **Chronic form** | **Acute form** |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | **recurrent diarrhea alternating with constipation** | **nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain (painful colon)** |
| Frequency | **3–15 motions/day** | **3–30 bowel movements/day** |
| Stool | **mucus, usually NO blood, NO pus cells, NO fever** | **mucus, blood, occasionally pus cells** |
| Other | — | **dehydration and weight loss** are frequent |
- **Masses of trophozoites** are usually found **beneath the edges of the irregular colonic ulcers**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Mostly asymptomatic; symptoms with malnutrition, alcoholism, achlorhydria or co-infection.
- Trophozoites invade the colonic wall (muco-ulceration, submucosal abscesses); no extra-intestinal disease except appendix/rare liver.
- Chronic: 3–15 motions, mucus, no blood/pus/fever; acute: 3–30 motions, mucus + blood ± pus, dehydration.
- Complications: secondary infection, perforation, peritonitis, death.

</div>

## 4. Laboratory diagnosis, treatment and prophylaxis

### Diagnosis
- ⭐ **Microscopic stool examination (best):** **cysts in formed stool**, **trophozoites in diarrheic stool**.
  - **Trophozoite:** **large size, macronucleus, rapid revolving motility**.
  - **Cyst:** **round shape with large macronucleus and micronucleus**.
- **Culture:** **Locke's egg albumin medium**.
- **Biopsy:** when stool is negative — **biopsy or scrapings from the muco-ulceration, stained with PAS (periodic acid–Schiff)**, showing trophozoites or cysts.

### Treatment
- ⭐ **Metronidazole (Flagyl) 500 mg four times a day for 10 days, with tetracycline.**

### Prophylaxis
- **Avoid contamination of food and water with human or animal (pork) feces.**
- **Treat infected animals and individuals shedding cysts.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Stool microscopy: cysts in formed stool, motile trophozoites in diarrheic stool; culture in Locke's egg albumin medium; PAS-stained biopsy if stool negative.
- Metronidazole 500 mg ×4/day for 10 days + tetracycline.
- Prevention: protect food/water from human and pig feces; treat infected animals and carriers.

</div>
