## 2. Function of the hand: grips and the thumb

- **Functions:** **grasping (gripping)** and **main tactile organ** of the body.
- **Two grips:** **power grip** and **precision grip**.

| | **Power grip** | **Precision grip** |
|---|---|---|
| **Example** | Holding a **hammer or screwdriver** | Holding **forceps** in careful dissection |
| **Action** | Fingers and thumb **flexed and wrapped** around the object | **Thumb opposed** to the other fingers; hand acts as a **pincer** |
| **Muscles** | **Long flexor tendons** (forearm), aided by **wrist extensors** | **Short intrinsic muscles** of the hand |

### The thumb
- ⭐ **Peculiarities:** **2 phalanges + 1 metacarpal**; the **1st metacarpal is at a right angle** to the other metacarpals; **saddle 1st CMC joint** (free mobility) so the thumb is functionally **half of the hand**; **opposition is a human character** (pincer); **a hand without a thumb is a hook, not a pincer**.
- **Sesamoids:** **two**, at the base of the proximal phalanx; they **prevent friction** and **increase the mechanical pull** of the muscles.
- **Nine muscles insert into the thumb:** 4 intrinsic muscles, **FPL, EPL, EPB, APL** and the **1st palmar interosseous**.
- **Bennett's fracture** (written "Bonnet's" on the slide) = fracture of the **base of the 1st metacarpal** → **opposition is lost** (opponens pollicis is the only thenar muscle inserted there).
- **Three nerves from the thumb alone:** **abduction** (APL) = **radial**; **adduction** (adductor pollicis) = **ulnar**; **flexion and opposition** = **median**.
- **Movements:** fingers: **abduction/adduction** in the **coronal plane** about the axial line (digit 3) and **flexion/extension** in the sagittal plane; thumb (rotated 90°): **abduction/adduction in the sagittal plane**, **flexion/extension in the coronal plane**, **opposition/reposition**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Power grip = long flexors + wrist extensors; precision grip = intrinsic muscles with the thumb opposed.
- Thumb: 2 phalanges, saddle CMC joint, 2 sesamoids, 9 inserting muscles.
- Base of 1st metacarpal fracture (Bennett's) → loss of opposition.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the power and precision grips.</summary>

**Answer:** In the power grip the fingers and thumb are flexed around the object (hammer, screwdriver), performed by the long flexor tendons aided by the wrist extensors. In the precision grip the thumb is opposed to the other fingers and the hand acts as a pincer (forceps), performed mainly by the short intrinsic muscles.

**Why:** Slides "Power grip" and "Precision grip".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the anatomical peculiarities of the thumb.</summary>

**Answer:** Two phalanges and one metacarpal; the 1st metacarpal at a right angle to the others; a saddle 1st CMC joint giving free mobility (functionally half of the hand); opposition as a human character making the hand a pincer; a hand without a thumb is a hook, not a pincer.

**Why:** Slide "Anatomical peculiarity of thumb".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How can examination of the thumb alone test three nerves?</summary>

**Answer:** Abduction by abductor pollicis longus tests the radial nerve; adduction by adductor pollicis tests the ulnar nerve; flexion and opposition test the median nerve.

**Why:** Slide "By examination of thumb only, we can test integrity of 3 nerves".
</details>

## 3. Nerves of the hand: quick tests

| Test | Nerve |
|---|---|
| **Abduct the thumb** (failure = palsy) | **Median** |
| **Extend the wrist** | **Radial** |
| **Abduct and adduct the fingers** | **Ulnar** |

| Pin-prick site | Nerve |
|---|---|
| **Dorsal aspect of the 1st web space** | **Radial** |
| **Palmar aspect of the distal phalanx of the little finger** | **Ulnar** |
| **Palmar surface of the distal phalanx of the index finger** | **Median** |

- The **1st palmar interosseous** moves the index finger in the adduction test.
- **Ulnar nerve in the hand:** motor to the **hypothenar group, medial two lumbricals** (the slide writes "lateral two") and **adductor pollicis**; sensory to the **medial 1½ fingers and palm**. **Wasted interossei** = ulnar nerve, **myotome T1**.
- **FPL tendon:** supplied by the **AIN**, which also supplies **pronator quadratus** and the **lateral half of FDP**.
- The nerve injury diagram summarizes the deformities (ape hand, claw hand, hand of benediction, OK sign).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Quick motor tests: abduct the thumb (median), extend the wrist (radial), spread the fingers (ulnar).
- Pin-prick: 1st web space dorsum (radial), little finger tip (ulnar), index finger tip (median).
- Interossei wasting = ulnar nerve, T1.

</div>

## 4. Arteries of the hand and Allen's test

- **No ischemia after radial artery ligation** because of the **anastomosis** between the radial and ulnar arteries: **superficial palmar arch** (superficial to the long flexor tendons; continuation of the **ulnar** artery, completed by a branch from the radial), **deep palmar arch** (deep to the tendons; continuation of the **radial** artery) and the **anterior and posterior carpal arches**.
- **Surface level:** superficial arch level with the **outstretched thumb**; deep arch about **1 cm proximal** to it.
- ⭐ **Allen's test:** tests the **patency of the radial and ulnar arteries**: (1) compress both arteries at the wrist; (2) patient makes a **tight fist**; (3) palm turns **cold and pale**; (4) patient **opens the hand**; (5) blood flows quickly through the **ulnar artery** into the arches; (6) palm turns **warm and pink** → ulnar artery patent; (7) **repeat with the ulnar artery compressed** to test the radial artery.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Radial ligation is tolerated thanks to the palmar arches and carpal arches.
- Superficial arch = ulnar artery; deep arch = radial artery.
- Allen's test: compress both, fist, release one; warm pink palm = that artery patent.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Why do the fingers not become ischemic when the radial artery is ligated?</summary>

**Answer:** Because of excellent anastomosis between the radial and ulnar arteries in the hand: the superficial and deep palmar arches and the anterior and posterior carpal arches.

**Why:** Slide "Why hand and fingers do not suffer from ischemia".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the Allen's test.</summary>

**Answer:** Compress both arteries at the wrist, the patient makes a tight fist (palm becomes cold and pale), then opens the hand; if blood flows quickly through the ulnar artery and the palm turns warm and pink, the ulnar artery is patent. Repeat while compressing the ulnar artery to test the radial artery.

**Why:** Slide "What is the Allen's test?".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare the superficial and deep palmar arches.</summary>

**Answer:** The superficial arch lies superficial to the long flexor tendons and is the continuation of the ulnar artery, completed by a branch from the radial; the deep arch lies deep to the tendons, is the continuation of the radial artery and lies about 1 cm proximal to the superficial arch.

**Why:** Slides "Why hand and fingers do not suffer from ischemia" and "Palmar arterial arches".
</details>

## 5. Hand deformities in RA and comparison with OA

| Deformity | Position | Anatomical basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Swan-neck** | **PIP hyperextension, DIP flexion** | **Volar plate laxity at the PIP**, tight intrinsics and lateral bands |
| **Boutonnière** | **PIP flexion, DIP hyperextension** | **Central slip rupture**, volar migration of the lateral bands |
| **Ulnar deviation** | Fingers deviate toward the ulna at the MCP joints | **Radial collateral ligaments stretched**, intrinsic pull toward the ulna |
| **Z-thumb** | **MCP flexion, IP hyperextension** | Imbalance between the flexor and extensor tendons of the thumb |
| **Tendon rupture** | One or more fingers lose extension | Attrition by inflamed synovium or the ulnar head (**EDM, EPL**) |
| **Mannerfelt lesion** | Thumb cannot flex at the IP joint | **Attrition rupture of FPL** by a bony spur or scaphoid osteophyte in the carpal tunnel |
| **Carpal tunnel syndrome** | Numbness and tingling of the lateral 3½ fingers, thenar wasting | **Median nerve compression** by synovial thickening |

| Feature | **RA** | **OA** |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary pathology** | **Synovial inflammation** | **Cartilage degeneration** |
| **Classic deformities** | Swan-neck, boutonnière, ulnar drift | **Heberden's / Bouchard's nodes** (exostosis) |
| **X-ray** | **Erosions, periarticular osteopenia** | **Osteophytes, asymmetric joint loss** |
| **Tendons** | **Ruptures (EPL, FPL)** | Rare |
| **Surgery** | **Synovectomy, tendon transfers** | **Osteophyte excision, fusion** |

- **Boutonnière mechanism (image):** central slip disruption → triangular ligament attenuation → volar migration of the lateral bands → the lateral bands now **flex the PIP and extend the DIP**.
- Note: one thumb image is labelled "IP flexion, MP hyperextension, CMC flexion", whereas the table defines the Z-thumb as MCP flexion with IP hyperextension.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Swan-neck = PIP extension; boutonnière = PIP flexion (central slip rupture).
- RA = synovial inflammation with erosions and tendon ruptures; OA = cartilage degeneration with osteophytes and nodes.
- Mannerfelt = FPL rupture in the carpal tunnel.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the swan-neck and boutonnière deformities and their anatomical basis.</summary>

**Answer:** Swan-neck: PIP hyperextension with DIP flexion from volar plate laxity at the PIP and tight intrinsics and lateral bands. Boutonnière: PIP flexion with DIP hyperextension from central slip rupture of the extensor expansion and volar migration of the lateral bands.

**Why:** Slides "Hand deformities of RA".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare RA and OA of the hand.</summary>

**Answer:** RA is synovial inflammation (swan-neck, boutonnière, ulnar drift; erosions and periarticular osteopenia; tendon ruptures; synovectomy and tendon transfers). OA is cartilage degeneration (Heberden's and Bouchard's nodes; osteophytes and asymmetric joint loss; rare tendon involvement; osteophyte excision and fusion).

**Why:** Slide "Comparative summary table between RA and OA".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the Mannerfelt lesion?</summary>

**Answer:** An attrition rupture of the FPL tendon by a bony spur or scaphoid osteophyte inside the carpal tunnel; the thumb cannot flex at the IP joint, causing loss of thumb flexion.

**Why:** Slide "Hand deformities of RA".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Power grip = long flexors + wrist extensors; precision grip = intrinsic muscles.**
- **Opponens pollicis is the only thenar muscle inserted into the 1st metacarpal**, so Bennett's fracture loses opposition.
- **Thumb abduction tests the median nerve in the quick test**, whereas APL abduction in the thumb examination is radial.
- **Dorsal = abduct (DAB, 4 muscles); palmar = adduct (PAD, 3 muscles).**
- **Swan-neck = PIP extension; boutonnière = PIP flexion.**
- **Superficial arch = ulnar artery; deep arch = radial artery.**
