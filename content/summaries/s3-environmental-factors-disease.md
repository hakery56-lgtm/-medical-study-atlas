## 1. Environment and disease; climate change

- ⭐ Intrinsic **molecular and cellular mechanisms** of disease are **modified by the external environment**; environmental changes affect many systems and **cross specialties**; they affect individuals, **families, communities and populations**.
- **Factors in the figure:** **money, climate, diet, stress, social circumstances, occupation, poisons and pathogens, hygiene**.
- **Climate change** is arguably the **most important environmental health issue** of the 21st century (earlier: lead in water, air pollution); it grew from the **greenhouse gases theory** (over 100 years old).
- **Cause:** **more CO₂ plus habitat destruction**, mainly human. **Consequences:** warming, **rising sea levels**, altered rainfall (**more droughts and floods**).
- **Health impacts:** **change in the range of vector-borne infections** and **adverse weather** (storms, floods, droughts have claimed **millions of lives** in 20 years). **India and China** are vital to the solution.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Environment modifies intrinsic disease mechanisms and affects whole populations.
- Climate change = CO₂ + habitat destruction; sea level rise, droughts and floods, vector-borne disease range changes.

</div>

## 2. Poverty and affluence

| Poverty | Affluence |
|---|---|
| **Respiratory disease** (air pollution) | **Physical inactivity** |
| **Hazards** at work or in the living environment | **Alcohol and drug consumption** |
| **Poor hygiene**: diarrhoeal disease, intestinal parasites | **Suicide, depression, anxiety, stress** |
| **Malnutrition** | **Sexually transmitted infection** |
| **Cardiovascular disease** | **Obesity** |

- Constant pressure to **perform and consume** causes stress; people **overeat, over-shop, watch television, use sex or drugs as painkillers** → **obesity, addiction, mental illness, alcohol-related and sexually transmitted diseases**.
- ⭐ **Double burden:** poor populations (diarrhea, malnutrition) beside affluent urban populations (diabetes, heart disease). **Uneven distribution of wealth** is a **more important determinant** of health than **absolute GDP**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Poverty: malnutrition, diarrhoea, pollution; affluence: obesity, inactivity, addiction, depression.
- Wealth distribution matters more than GDP.

</div>

## 3. Smoking

- ⭐ **Diseases:** a **substantial majority of lung cancer and COPD**; most smokers die of these or **ischemic heart disease**. Also cancers of the **upper respiratory and GI tracts, pancreas, bladder, kidney**; **peripheral vascular disease, stroke, peptic ulceration**.
- **Maternal smoking** → **fetal growth retardation**. **Passive smoking** harms **cardiovascular and respiratory** health.
- Warnings caused an initial decline in the **1960s**, now **plateaued**; rates **rising in young women and developing countries**. **Nicotine addiction** (dependence and withdrawal) makes quitting hard; influences: **peer pressure, beliefs, advertising**.
- **Cessation (by motivation):** **unmotivated** = record status, advice, encourage attitude change; **light (< 10/day)** = advice + support programme; **heavy (10-15/day)** = + **NRT ≥ 8 weeks**; **heavy (> 15/day)** = + **bupropion** if NRT and support fail.
- **Abstinence ≥ 6 months:** placebo/willpower **~2%**; self-help materials **+1%**; doctor's advice **+2%**; specialist face-to-face **+4-7%**; telephone counselling **+2%**; **NRT + support +5-12%**; **bupropion + intensive support +9%**. **Public health:** taxation, bans, smoke-free policies.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Smoking: lung cancer, COPD, ischemic heart disease, many cancers; passive smoking harms the heart and lungs.
- Cessation by stage: advice, support, NRT, then bupropion; NRT + support works best.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List major diseases caused by smoking.</summary>

**Answer:** Lung cancer, COPD and ischemic heart disease; cancers of the upper respiratory and GI tracts, pancreas, bladder and kidney; peripheral vascular disease, stroke and peptic ulceration; fetal growth retardation from maternal smoking.

**Why:** Slides 19 and 20.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Outline the smoking cessation approach by the smoker's motivation and amount.</summary>

**Answer:** Unmotivated: record status, advice, encourage attitude change. Light motivated smokers: advice plus a support programme. Heavy (10-15/day): add NRT for at least 8 weeks. Heavy (> 15/day): add bupropion if NRT and behavioural support fail.

**Why:** Slide 22.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare the success of cessation methods.</summary>

**Answer:** Placebo or willpower about 2%; adding written self-help 1%, doctor's advice 2%, telephone counselling 2%, specialist behavioural support 4-7%, NRT with support 5-12%, bupropion with intensive support 9%.

**Why:** Slide 23.
</details>

## 4. Radiation exposure

- **Types:** **ionizing** (X-rays, CT, radionuclide scans/therapy) and **non-ionizing** (UV, visible light, laser, infrared, microwave; **UV skin therapy**, **laser for diabetic retinopathy**).

| Radiation | Range in air | Range in tissue | Protection |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alpha particles** | A few cm | No penetration | **Paper** |
| **Beta particles** | A few metres | A few mm | **Aluminium sheet** |
| **X-rays/gamma rays** | Kilometres | Pass through | **Lead** |
| **Neutrons** | Kilometres | Pass through | **Concrete or thick polythene** |

- ⭐ **Deterministic effects:** **severity rises with dose above a threshold**. Sensitive: **bone marrow and GI mucosa** (**lymphocyte depletion** = most sensitive marrow marker; **GI toxicity** → earlier death by diarrhea, vomiting, dehydration, sepsis); **gonads** (sterility); **eye** (cataract); **lung and CNS** (inflammation → **pulmonary fibrosis**, neurological deficit); **skin** burns; **bone necrosis, lymphatic fibrosis** after regional therapy (breast cancer); **thyroid** concentrates iodine.
- **Stochastic effects:** **probability rises with dose, severity independent of dose**; **carcinogenesis**: **leukemia ~2 years**, **solid tumors ~10 years**; risk depends on dose, time to accumulate it and interval since exposure.
- **Management:** hydration, control of sepsis, marrow aplasia (**transplant**); treat burns and trauma normally; **potassium iodide 133 mg/day immediately** for radio-iodine contamination.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Deterministic = threshold, severity rises with dose; stochastic = probability rises, severity fixed (cancer).
- Marrow and GI mucosa most sensitive; KI protects the thyroid from radio-iodine.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Differentiate deterministic from stochastic effects.</summary>

**Answer:** Deterministic effects have a dose threshold and their severity increases with dose; stochastic effects (e.g., carcinogenesis) increase in probability with dose but their severity is independent of dose.

**Why:** Slides 31 and 33.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which tissues are most sensitive to ionizing radiation and what happens?</summary>

**Answer:** Bone marrow (lymphocyte depletion is the most sensitive marker) and GI mucosa (severe diarrhea, vomiting, dehydration and sepsis), plus gonads (sterility), eye (cataract), lung and CNS (fibrosis, neurological deficit) and skin (burns).

**Why:** Slides 31 and 32.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is radio-iodine contamination managed?</summary>

**Answer:** Immediate potassium iodide 133 mg/day, which substantially reduces thyroid uptake of radio-iodine.

**Why:** Slide 34.
</details>

## 5. Temperature extremes and thermoregulation

- ⭐ **Cold:** **cutaneous vasoconstriction and shivering**; movement can **increase heat loss** (convection, ventilation). **Heat:** **sweating** is the main loss route, usually above **32.5 °C** or with exercise.

| Mechanism | Hot environment | Cold environment |
|---|---|---|
| **Basal metabolic rate** | No change | **↓ in hypothermia** |
| **Muscle activity** | ↓ by lethargy | **↑ by shivering** |
| **Conduction / convection / radiation** | **↑ by vasodilatation** | **↓ by vasoconstriction** (conduction ↑↑ in water < 31 °C; convection ↑ by wind) |
| **Evaporation** | **↑↑ by sweating** (↓ by high humidity) | ↑ by hyperventilation |

- **Old age:** slower vasomotor, muscle and sweating responses; comorbidity (atherosclerosis, hypothyroidism, sedatives); hypothermia often complicates pneumonia, stroke, fracture; inadequate heating.
- **Thermometer figure:** **heat stroke (> 40 °C)**: organ failure, shock, confusion, loss of sweating; **heat exhaustion**: profuse sweating, dehydration, tachycardia, fatigue; **mild hypothermia 35-32 °C**: shivering, confusion, ataxia; **severe (< 32 °C)**: depressed consciousness, muscle stiffness, **failed shivering**, bradycardia, hypotension, **J waves**; **< 28 °C**: coma, absent pupil reflexes; cardiac standstill near 20 °C.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cold: vasoconstriction + shivering; heat: sweating.
- Elderly have the highest hypothermia risk; thermoregulation fails with age and disease.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the body's protective mechanisms in cold and hot environments.</summary>

**Answer:** In the cold: cutaneous vasoconstriction and shivering; in the heat: sweating (above 32.5 °C or during exercise), with vasodilatation increasing conduction, convection and radiation.

**Why:** Slides 39 and 40.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why are the elderly vulnerable to temperature extremes?</summary>

**Answer:** Slower vasomotor, muscle and sweating responses, comorbidity and medication (atherosclerosis, hypothyroidism, sedatives), hypothermia complicating other diseases, and inadequate heating from financial pressures.

**Why:** Slide 41.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the clinical features of mild versus severe hypothermia.</summary>

**Answer:** Mild (35-32 °C): shivering, confusion, dehydration, ataxia. Severe (below 32 °C): depressed consciousness, muscle stiffness, failed vasoconstriction and shivering, bradycardia, hypotension, ECG J waves and dysrhythmia; below 28 °C coma and absent pupil reflexes.

**Why:** Slide 43.
</details>

## 6. Hypothermia and cold injury

- **Hypothermia:** slow fall of core temperature when thermoregulation is impaired; **very young** (poor thermoregulation, high surface area/weight) but **elderly highest risk**; causes: **hypothyroidism, alcohol, phenothiazines, glucocorticoid insufficiency, stroke, hepatic failure, hypoglycemia**; cold-water immersion; mountaineers.
- ⭐ **Death is hard to diagnose in the cold:** **irreversible hypothermia** probable with **asystole (no carotid pulse 1 minute), rigid chest and abdomen, core < 9 °C**; otherwise **continue resuscitation until core temperature is normal**.
- **Investigations:** **hemoconcentration, metabolic acidosis, J waves, dysrhythmias (VF)**, **PO₂ falls 7% per °C**, **↑ AST, CK, amylase**; if cause unclear check **thyroid, pituitary, glucose, drugs**.
- **Management:** controlled rewarming + treat hypoxia, fluids/electrolytes, dysrhythmias. **Severe (< 32 °C):** handle **gently, horizontal**; rapid immersion cases in **40 °C bath**; gradual cases rewarm slowly; **1 °C per hour**, insulate, ICU, **oxygen, warm IV fluids**, monitor rhythm and blood gases, correct acidosis; **surface rewarming** can cause **hypotension, higher O₂ demand, acidosis**.
- **Frostbite:** **direct freezing** of fingers, toes, ears, face; risk: **smoking, PVD, dehydration, alcohol**; anaesthetized before freezing; **pale, doughy, insensitive** then **hard**; **warm water rewarming (painful)**, **avoid rubbing and direct heat**, **surgery for dead tissue**.
- **Non-freezing cold injury (trench/immersion foot):** prolonged **cold damp** exposure; cold, ischemic, numb; on rewarming **mottled → hyperemic, swollen, painful**; months to recover; **gradual rewarming** less painful; **amitriptyline** for pain/paraesthesia; risk on re-exposure.
- **Chilblains:** **tender red or purplish** lesions in cold and wet; horse riders, cyclists, swimmers; **commoner in women**; short-lived, not serious.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Severe hypothermia: handle gently, rewarm 1 °C per hour, watch for dysrhythmia; continue resuscitation until warm.
- Frostbite: warm water, no rubbing; trench foot: no freezing; chilblains: benign.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List causes and risk groups of hypothermia.</summary>

**Answer:** The elderly (highest risk) and very young; hypothyroidism, alcohol or phenothiazines, glucocorticoid insufficiency, stroke, hepatic failure, hypoglycemia; cold-water immersion and extreme climates.

**Why:** Slide 42.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Outline management of severe hypothermia.</summary>

**Answer:** Handle gently and keep horizontal; rewarm in a controlled way (40 °C bath for rapid immersion cases, slower otherwise; 1 °C per hour), insulate in a warm room or ICU, give oxygen and warm IV fluids, monitor rhythm and blood gases and correct acidosis; beware hypotension and acidosis with surface rewarming.

**Why:** Slides 46 to 48.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare frostbite, trench foot and chilblains.</summary>

**Answer:** Frostbite is direct tissue freezing (pale, doughy, then hard) treated with warm water and surgery for dead tissue; trench foot is a non-freezing injury from prolonged cold damp exposure (cold, numb, then mottled, hyperemic, painful) with slow recovery; chilblains are short-lived tender red or purplish skin lesions in cold wet conditions, not usually serious.

**Why:** Slides 49 to 52.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Passive smoking raises cardiovascular (and respiratory) risk.**
- **Stochastic ≠ deterministic:** cancer is stochastic; severity independent of dose.
- **Lymphocyte depletion is the most sensitive marker of marrow injury, but GI toxicity can kill earlier.**
- **Laser therapy and UV are NON-ionizing.**
- **Wealth distribution matters more than GDP.**
- **Do not rub or use direct heat on frostbite; handle severe hypothermia gently (dysrhythmia risk).**
- **Do not declare death in a cold patient until rewarmed (unless irreversible criteria).**
- **Note:** the slide on thyroid sensitivity is worded unclearly ("not inherently sensitive but its ability to concentrate iodine"); it is read here as the thyroid concentrating radio-iodine.
