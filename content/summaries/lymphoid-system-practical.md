## 1. Ileum, Peyer's patches, colon and appendix

- **Ileum wall (lumen → outside):** **mucosa, submucosa, muscularis externa** (inner circular + outer longitudinal layers), **serosa** (visceral peritoneum).
- ⭐ **Peyer's patches:** aggregates of **lymphatic nodules** in the ileum, **visible with the naked eye**; the **germinal center** is where **B cells proliferate**; a **major source of antibody production**.
- Labelled in the Peyer's patch section: surface epithelium, **villi with lamina propria, lacteals, intestinal glands, germinal centers of lymphatic nodules**, submucosa, serosa, **muscularis mucosae (disrupted)**, muscularis externa.

| | **Ileum** | **Colon** | **Appendix** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mucosa** | **Villi + intestinal glands** | **Crypts, NO villi** (vertical crypts, columnar epithelium, many **goblet cells**) | **Crypts** |
| **Lymphoid tissue** | **Peyer's patches** (lymphatic nodules with germinal centers) | **Lymphoid follicle** | Large **lymph nodule** |
| **Layers labelled** | Mucosa, submucosa, muscularis externa, serosa | Mucosa, submucosa, **muscularis propria**, subserosa; **muscularis mucosae** | Mucosa, submucosa |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Peyer's patches = ileal lymphatic nodules with B-cell germinal centers; antibody production.
- Ileum has villi; colon has crypts without villi (many goblet cells).
- Appendix: crypts + a large lymph nodule.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What are Peyer's patches and what is their function?</summary>

**Answer:** Aggregates of lymphatic nodules in the ileum, visible with the naked eye; the germinal centers are where B cells proliferate and they are a major source of antibody production.

**Why:** Slides "Peyer's patches".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How do you distinguish the colon from the ileum on a slide?</summary>

**Answer:** The colonic mucosa has vertically oriented crypts lined by columnar epithelium with numerous goblet cells and no villi, whereas the ileum has villi (with lacteals) and Peyer's patches.

**Why:** Slides "Colon (low and high power)" and "Peyer's patches".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the labelled layers of the ileal wall.</summary>

**Answer:** Mucosa, submucosa, muscularis externa (inner circular and outer longitudinal layers) and serosa (visceral peritoneum).

**Why:** Slides "Ileum" and "Peyer's patches".
</details>

## 2. Lymph node

- ⭐ The lymph node **filters both lymph and blood**. **Lymph** enters the **subcapsular sinuses** and exits at the **hilar region**; **blood** both enters and exits.
- **Zones:** **cortex** = **lymphoid follicles (B cells)** with **germinal centers** (darkly stained cortex) and **mantle zone**; **paracortex** = **T cells**; **medulla** = **macrophages and plasma cells**.
- **Labelled structures:** pericapsular adipose tissue, **capsule**, **subcapsular sinus**, cortex, trabecular blood vessels, **connective tissue trabeculae**, **afferent lymphatic vessels with valves**, arteriole and venule, **medulla**, **medullary sinuses**, **hilus**, **efferent lymphatic vessels**, **medullary cords**, **lymphatic nodules** and **germinal centers**.
- **Lymph path:** **subcapsular sinus → trabeculae → medulla → medullary cords → hilum**.
- **Plasma cells:** derived from **B lymphocytes**, **antibody factories** producing thousands of antibodies per second for a few days, then dying.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Filters lymph and blood; lymph enters via the subcapsular sinus and leaves at the hilum.
- Cortex = B cells (follicles, germinal centers); paracortex = T cells; medulla = macrophages and plasma cells.
- Path: subcapsular sinus → trabeculae → medulla → medullary cords → hilum.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the zones of the lymph node and their cells.</summary>

**Answer:** The cortex contains lymphoid follicles (B cells) with germinal centers; the paracortex contains T cells; the medulla contains macrophages and plasma cells.

**Why:** Slide "Lymph node (low power)".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Trace the lymph through the lymph node.</summary>

**Answer:** Lymph enters the subcapsular sinus, percolates through the trabeculae to the medulla, is channeled through the medullary cords and leaves at the hilum.

**Why:** Slides "Lymph node" (path of lymph).
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are plasma cells?</summary>

**Answer:** Antibody factories derived from B lymphocytes; they produce thousands of antibodies per second for a few days and then die.

**Why:** Slide "Plasma cells".
</details>

## 3. Palatine tonsil

- **MALT:** epithelium-covered lymphoid tissue in the **oro- and naso-pharynx** containing **antigen-presenting cells, lymphocytes, plasma cells and lymphoid nodules** that protect the frequently invaded mucosa.
- ⭐ Covered by **non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium**, which forms **infoldings (tonsillar crypts)** that **increase the surface area** in contact with the external environment.
- **Partially encapsulated** by dense connective tissue (**hemicapsule**); lymphoid nodules show **lighter germinal centers**.
- **Labelled:** epithelium, **crypts**, **lymphoid nodules**, germinal centers, trabeculae with blood vessels, **capsule**, **skeletal muscle**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Palatine tonsil = MALT covered by non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium.
- Tonsillar crypts increase the surface area; lymphoid nodules with germinal centers.
- Only partially encapsulated (hemicapsule).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the structure of the palatine tonsil.</summary>

**Answer:** Epithelium-covered lymphoid tissue (MALT) with lymphoid nodules and germinal centers, covered by non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium that forms crypts, with dense connective tissue forming a partial capsule.

**Why:** Slides "Tonsil — palatine tonsil".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the function of the tonsillar crypts?</summary>

**Answer:** They are deep invaginations that increase the surface area in contact with the external environment.

**Why:** Slide "Tonsil — palatine tonsil (medium power)".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which cells does the tonsil contain?</summary>

**Answer:** Antigen-presenting cells, lymphocytes, plasma cells and lymphoid nodules.

**Why:** Slide "Tonsil — palatine tonsil (low power)".
</details>

## 4. Thymus

- **Lobules** separated by **connective tissue trabeculae (septa)**, each with **cortex** and **medulla** (medulla continuous between lobules); **capsule**, blood vessels, **adipose cells**.
- **Infant thymus:** lobules separated by connective tissue septa; the **medulla stains lighter than the cortex**.
- **Adult thymus:** after puberty it **involutes**, with **islands of thymic tissue among adipose tissue**.
- ⭐ **Hassall's (thymic) corpuscles:** **multiple layers of epithelioid cells**, in the **medulla**; the thymus has **no germinal centers**.
- **Cells:** **lymphocytes (thymocytes)** and **thymic epithelial cells**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cortex darker, medulla lighter; Hassall's corpuscles in the medulla.
- Thymus has no germinal centers.
- Adult thymus involutes and is replaced by adipose tissue.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What are Hassall's corpuscles and where are they found?</summary>

**Answer:** Distinguishing structures of the thymus made of multiple layers of epithelioid cells, found in the thymic medulla; the thymus has no germinal centers.

**Why:** Slide "Hassall's corpuscles".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare the infant and the adult thymus.</summary>

**Answer:** The infant thymus has lobules separated by connective tissue septa with a lighter-staining medulla; the adult (post-pubescent) thymus involutes and is characterized by areas of adipose tissue.

**Why:** Slides "Infant thymus" and "Adult thymus".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the labelled structures of the thymus overview.</summary>

**Answer:** Capsule, trabeculae, cortex, medulla, blood vessels, thymic (Hassall's) corpuscles, adipose cells, lobule, connective tissue of the trabecula and the medulla continuous between lobules.

**Why:** Slide "Thymus".
</details>

## 5. Spleen

- **Two components:** **white pulp** (**lymphocytes**; **lymphatic nodules with germinal centers** around a **central artery**) and **red pulp** (**open sinusoids containing blood**).
- ⭐ **Red pulp:** **splenic sinuses** (endothelial-lined vascular spaces) and **splenic cords** (reticular cells, macrophages, lymphocytes, other leukocytes and red blood cells).
- **Labelled:** capsule, central artery, **trabeculae**, trabecular vein and artery, **lymphatic nodule (white pulp)**, **germinal center**, **splenic cords and venous sinuses (red pulp)**, pulp arteries.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- White pulp = lymphocytes around a central artery; red pulp = splenic sinuses and cords.
- Red pulp contains red blood cells; white pulp has germinal centers.
- Trabeculae carry the trabecular artery and vein.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the white pulp and red pulp of the spleen.</summary>

**Answer:** The white pulp contains lymphocytes in lymphatic nodules with germinal centers around a central artery; the red pulp consists of open sinusoids with blood: splenic sinuses and splenic cords.

**Why:** Slides "Spleen (low and medium power)".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the structure of the red pulp?</summary>

**Answer:** Splenic sinuses (endothelial-lined vascular spaces) and splenic cords made of reticular cells, macrophages, lymphocytes, other leukocytes and red blood cells.

**Why:** Slide "Spleen (high power)".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the labelled structures of the spleen overview.</summary>

**Answer:** Capsule, central artery, trabeculae, lymphatic nodule (white pulp), trabecular vein and artery, germinal center, splenic cords and venous sinuses (red pulp) and pulp arteries.

**Why:** Slide "Spleen".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Ileum has villi; colon has crypts and NO villi.**
- **Thymus has NO germinal centers;** it has Hassall's corpuscles in the medulla.
- **In the infant thymus the medulla is lighter than the cortex.**
- **Lymph node: cortex = B cells, paracortex = T cells, medulla = macrophages and plasma cells.**
- **Spleen: white pulp = lymphocytes; red pulp = sinuses and cords with blood.**
- **Palatine tonsil is only partially encapsulated** and covered by non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium.

1. Ileum wall: mucosa, submucosa, muscularis externa (circular + longitudinal), serosa.
