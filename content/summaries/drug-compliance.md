## 1. What compliance is and why it matters

- ⭐ **Drug compliance = adherence**: following the prescribed regimen consistently; **pivotal** for the success of any medication therapy.
- **Good adherence →** the **intended therapeutic benefits**: **symptom relief, disease control, prevention of complications**.
- **Non-adherence →** **disease progression**, **more hospitalizations**, **higher healthcare costs**.

## 2. Challenges to drug compliance

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **1. Patient factors** | **Forgetfulness, fear of side effects, lack of understanding** of the condition or medication |
| **2. Socioeconomic barriers** | **Financial constraints, limited access to healthcare**, difficulty affording drugs or reaching facilities (transportation) |
| **3. Therapy complexity** | **Multiple medications or frequent dosing**, different times of day, interactions and side effects |
| **4. Provider influence** | **Poor communication, inadequate education, lack of patient engagement** in decisions |

- **Effective communication, patient education and shared decision-making** build **trust** and understanding of the **rationale** for medications.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Four challenge groups: patient, socioeconomic, therapy complexity, provider.

## 3. Strategies to enhance compliance

| Strategy | Content |
|---|---|
| **1. Patient education** | Condition, importance of medication, **managing side effects**; **clear language**; empower patients |
| **2. Simplify regimens** | **Once-daily doses, single-pill combinations**, schedules fitting routines |
| **3. Use technology** | **Mobile apps** (reminders, tracking, education); **electronic pill dispensers** |
| **4. Healthcare provider role** | **Open communication, trust, shared decisions, regular follow-up** to monitor adherence and adjust regimens |

- **Implementation:** a **collaborative effort** of providers, **pharmacists** and the healthcare team.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Education, simplification, technology, provider role.
- Once-daily dosing and single-pill combinations improve adherence.

</div>

## 4. Tailoring, intentional non-adherence and technology

- **Elderly patients:** **cognitive decline, polypharmacy, interactions** → **medication organizers, caregiver support, simplified regimens**.
- **Chronic conditions (diabetes, hypertension, asthma):** ongoing adherence → **comprehensive education, support groups, regular monitoring**.
- ⭐ **Most non-adherence is intentional.** Top reasons: **fear, cost, misunderstanding, too many medications, lack of symptoms, worry, depression, mistrust**.
- **Technology:** **mobile applications** (reminders, tracking, education; communicate with providers; access records); **electronic pill dispensers** (dispense at scheduled times, **alert caregivers if a dose is missed**); **wearable devices**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Elderly: organizers, caregiver, simplification. Chronic: education, support groups, monitoring.
- Most non-adherence is intentional: fear, cost, misunderstanding, many drugs, no symptoms, worry, depression, mistrust.

</div>

**Why:** Slide "Tailoring strategies".

<details><summary>Q2. List the top reasons for intentional non-adherence.</summary>

## 5. Impact on healthcare costs and conclusion

| Non-adherence | Cost impact |
|---|---|
| **Increased hospitalizations** | **Higher inpatient care costs** |
| **More emergency room visits** | **Increased emergency department costs** |
| **Longer treatment durations** | **Higher medication and healthcare utilization costs** |

- Non-adherence also causes **more complications**, further raising costs.
- **Conclusion:** improving compliance needs **collaboration** of providers, pharmacists and patients: **education, simpler regimens, technology, open communication** → **better outcomes and lower costs**.
