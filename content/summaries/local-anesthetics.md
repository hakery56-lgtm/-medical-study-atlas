## 1. Mode of action and classification

- ⭐ **Local anesthetics (LA)** = **weak bases** that **abolish sensation** (and **motor activity** at higher concentrations) in a limited area by **blocking nerve conduction.**
- **Action:** **block sodium channels**; **membrane stabilizing** (reversibly decrease the rate of depolarization and repolarization). **Vasodilation** → rapid diffusion away → **short duration**; **epinephrine** is added to **prolong action and minimize systemic toxicity.**
- **Ionization:** at physiologic pH the compounds are **charged**; the **ionized cationic form** binds the **sodium channel receptor** (most active), the **uncharged form** is needed for **cell penetration.** At **infected sites the pH drops** → **onset delayed or prevented.**
- **Duration** is determined by **redistribution, not biotransformation.**
- **Classes:** **ester** and **amide.** All LA are weak bases **except benzocaine (weak acid)**, usable at infected sites.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- LA block sodium channels; epinephrine prolongs action.
- Ionized form acts at the receptor; uncharged form penetrates cells.
- Infection lowers pH → delayed onset; benzocaine is a weak acid.

</div>

## 2. Routes and factors affecting action

- **Routes:** **topical, peripheral nerve blocks, neuraxial (spinal, epidural, caudal).**
- **Peripheral nerve block:** LA injected near a nerve to numb the **arm or leg**; **potent analgesia, less opioid use**, lasts **hours to 1-2 weeks.**
- **Infiltration anesthesia:** **superficial localized** loss of sensation; low concentration injected near nerve endings.
- **Systemic absorption depends on:** **dose, site, local blood flow, vasoconstrictor (epinephrine), drug properties.**
- **More lipid-soluble = more potent and longer duration; extensive protein binding = longer duration; esters** are hydrolysed faster than amides → **shorter duration.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Routes: topical, peripheral block, neuraxial; infiltration for superficial areas.
- Lipid solubility and protein binding prolong action.
- Esters act shorter than amides (hydrolysis).

</div>

## 3. Individual drugs

| Drug | Notes | Duration | Toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lidocaine** | **Most commonly used**; topical, IV, infiltration, spinal, epidural, blocks | **1-2 h (2-4 h with epinephrine)** | **CNS excitation, local neurotoxicity** |
| **Mepivacaine** | Like lidocaine; **not for obstetric anesthesia** | | **Neonatal toxicity** |
| **Prilocaine** | Like lidocaine; metabolized in plasma and kidney | | **Methemoglobinemia** |
| **Bupivacaine** | **Longer procedures**; not topical or IV; ropivacaine and levobupivacaine similar | **3-6 h** | **CNS excitation, cardiovascular collapse** |
| **Chloroprocaine** | **Very short procedures**; not topical or IV | **30-60 min (60-90 min with epinephrine)** | **CNS excitation, local neurotoxicity** |
| **Procaine** | Like chloroprocaine, **not epidural** | | |
| **Tetracaine** | **Primarily spinal** | **2-3 h** | |
| **Cocaine** | Also **sympathomimetic**; **high surface activity + vasoconstriction**; topical or parenteral | **1-2 h** | **CNS excitation, convulsions, arrhythmias, hypertension, stroke** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Lidocaine: 1-2 h (2-4 h with epinephrine).
- Bupivacaine: 3-6 h, cardiovascular collapse.
- Prilocaine: methemoglobinemia; mepivacaine: not in obstetrics.
- Tetracaine: spinal; cocaine: vasoconstriction.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare lidocaine and bupivacaine.</summary>

**Answer:** Lidocaine is the commonest LA for short procedures (1-2 h, 2-4 h with epinephrine; CNS excitation and neurotoxicity); bupivacaine is for longer procedures (3-6 h; not topical or IV; CNS excitation and cardiovascular collapse).

**Why:** Slides "Lidocaine" and "Bupivacaine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which LA is avoided in obstetrics and which causes methemoglobinemia?</summary>

**Answer:** Mepivacaine is avoided in obstetric anesthesia (neonatal toxicity); prilocaine's metabolite may cause methemoglobinemia.

**Why:** Slide "Mepivacaine / Prilocaine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is special about cocaine as a local anesthetic?</summary>

**Answer:** Besides sodium channel blockade it has sympathomimetic effects and vasoconstriction; used topically or parenterally (1-2 h); toxicity includes CNS excitation, convulsions, arrhythmias, hypertension and stroke.

**Why:** Slide "Cocaine".
</details>

## 4. Toxicity and special points

- **Toxicity:** **altered mental status, cardiovascular instability, neural injury, transient neurologic symptoms (TNS)** (transient pain or dysesthesia with **spinal/epidural** LA).
- **Allergy:** fairly common reactions are **psychogenic** (**urticaria, edema, bronchospasm**); **true allergy** is caused by **PABA**, a product of the **ester procaine.**
- **Elderly:** **adjust the dose and reduce epinephrine** (liver and cardiovascular compromise).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Toxicity: CNS, cardiovascular, neural injury, TNS.
- True allergy = PABA from esters.
- Elderly: lower dose and less epinephrine.

</div>
