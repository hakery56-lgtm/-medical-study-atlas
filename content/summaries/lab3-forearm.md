## 1. Review: shoulder, axilla and nerve injuries

### Muscle attachments around the shoulder
| Site | Muscles |
|---|---|
| **Coracoid process** | Pectoralis minor, short head of biceps, coracobrachialis |
| **Greater tuberosity** | Supraspinatus (0–15° abduction), infraspinatus + teres minor (adduction, external rotation) |
| **Lesser tuberosity** | Subscapularis |
| **Bicipital groove (lateral → medial) "MLM"** | **M**ajor (pectoralis major), **L**atissimus dorsi, **M**ajor (teres major) (adduction + internal rotation) |

### Axilla dissection
Identify: pectoralis major and minor, coracobrachialis, **short and long heads of biceps**, serratus anterior, subscapularis, teres major, latissimus dorsi; nerves: **musculocutaneous, median, ulnar, long thoracic**; also the **axillary artery**, **radial nerve** (gives the branch to the long head of triceps), **axillary nerve** (entering the quadrangular space), **thoracodorsal** and **upper/lower subscapular nerves**.

### Sites of nerve injury around the humerus
| Site | Nerve |
|---|---|
| **Surgical neck** | **Axillary** |
| **Spiral groove (shaft)** | **Radial** |
| **Supracondylar region** | **Median (AIN)** |
| **Medial epicondyle** | **Ulnar** |
| **Behind the medial epicondyle, long term** | **Tardy ulnar nerve palsy** (with cubitus valgus) |

### Brachial plexus palsies
- ⭐ **Erb's palsy** = **upper trunk (C5, C6)**: axillary (deltoid) → shoulder **adducted**; musculocutaneous (biceps) → elbow **extended**, forearm **pronated** → **"policeman's tip" hand**.
- ⭐ **Klumpke's palsy** = **lower trunk (C8, T1)**: **intrinsic hand muscles** affected → **hyperextension at MCP, flexion at IP** → **claw hand**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Coracoid: pec minor + short head of biceps + coracobrachialis; bicipital groove: "MLM".
- Surgical neck = axillary; spiral groove = radial; supracondylar = median (AIN); medial epicondyle = ulnar.
- Erb's = C5-C6, policeman's tip; Klumpke's = C8-T1, claw hand.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which muscles are attached to the bicipital groove and what do they do?</summary>

**Answer:** From lateral to medial, pectoralis major, latissimus dorsi and teres major ("MLM"); together they adduct and internally rotate the arm.

**Why:** A mnemonic you must know.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Match each humeral injury site with its nerve.</summary>

**Answer:** Surgical neck — axillary nerve; spiral groove — radial nerve; supracondylar region — median nerve (AIN); medial epicondyle — ulnar nerve; long-term damage behind the medial epicondyle — tardy ulnar nerve palsy with cubitus valgus.

**Why:** Fracture–nerve pairs are repeatedly examined.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare Erb's and Klumpke's palsy.</summary>

**Answer:** Erb's palsy involves the upper trunk (C5, C6): the deltoid and biceps are lost, leaving the shoulder adducted, the elbow extended and the forearm pronated (policeman's tip hand). Klumpke's palsy involves the lower trunk (C8, T1): intrinsic hand muscles are affected, producing MCP hyperextension and IP flexion (claw hand).

**Why:** Trunk, muscles and hand position.
</details>

## 2. Bones and radiographs

### Humerus
Head, **anatomical neck**, **surgical neck**, greater and lesser tuberosities, **bicipital groove** (lateral lip, floor, medial lip), **spiral (radial) groove**, **deltoid tuberosity** (V-shaped), **olecranon fossa**, **coronoid and radial fossae**, **trochlea**, **capitulum**, medial and lateral epicondyles and supracondylar ridges.

### Radius and ulna
- **Ulna:** olecranon, **trochlear notch**, **coronoid process**, ulnar tuberosity, **radial notch**, head, styloid process.
- **Radius:** head, neck, **radial tuberosity**, **pronator tuberosity**, anterior oblique line, ulnar notch, styloid process; the two bones are joined by the **interosseous membrane** (interosseous borders face each other).

### Hand bones
- **Carpals:** hamate, pisiform, triquetrum, lunate (proximal-medial group) and trapezium, trapezoid, scaphoid (navicular), capitate.
- **Metacarpals:** 1–5. **Phalanges:** proximal, middle, distal.

### Radiographs
- **Wrist:** metacarpals 1–5, carpal bones, **ulnar and radial styloid processes**, **TFCC** (triangular fibrocartilage complex) at the distal radioulnar joint, **ECU groove** by the ulnar styloid.
- **Elbow:** common extensor origin, common flexor origin, LCL/annular ligament, biceps tendon, UCL, brachialis; on the lateral view: anterior fat pad, radial neck, coronoid and olecranon fossae, radial tuberosity, coronoid process, capitellum, radial head, trochlea. Pediatric films: the **anterior humeral line** and the **radiocapitellar line**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Know the labelled landmarks of the humerus, radius and ulna.
- The wrist X-ray: carpals, styloids and the TFCC.
- Elbow X-ray: fat pads, radiocapitellar line and anterior humeral line.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the landmarks on the proximal ulna and radius.</summary>

**Answer:** Ulna: olecranon, trochlear notch, coronoid process, ulnar tuberosity and radial notch. Radius: head, neck and radial tuberosity (biceps), with the pronator tuberosity and anterior oblique line on the shaft.

**Why:** Typical labelling question.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Name the carpal bones.</summary>

**Answer:** Proximal row: scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform; distal row: trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate.

**Why:** Needed on the wrist radiograph.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the TFCC and where is it seen?</summary>

**Answer:** The triangular fibrocartilage complex is the articular disc of the distal radioulnar joint, seen between the ulnar styloid and the carpus on the wrist radiograph.

**Why:** Marked with a red asterisk in the lab.
</details>

## 3. The 8 rules of the forearm muscles (20 muscles)

**Flexor compartment (8 muscles).**
- **Rule 1 — four groups by action** (palmaris longus excluded): wrist flexion = **FCU, FCR**; thumb flexion = **FPL**; finger flexion = **FDS, FDP**; pronation = **PT, PQ**.
- **Rule 2 — nerve supply:** all flexors are supplied by the **median nerve** directly or through the **AIN**, **EXCEPT FCU and ½ FDP (ulnar)**. The **AIN** supplies **3 muscles: FPL, lateral ½ FDP, PQ**; the **"OK" sign** tests it.

**Extensor compartment (12 muscles).**
- **Rule 3 — 12 muscles:** **3 carpi** (ECU, ECRL, ECRB); **3 pollicis** (APL, EPL, EPB); **3 digit** (ED, EDM, EI); **3 others** (brachioradialis, supinator, anconeus).
- **Rule 4 — four groups by action** (brachioradialis and anconeus excluded): fingers 2–5 (ED, EDM); thumb + index (APL, EPL, EPB, EI); carpal movers (ECRL, ECRB, ECU); supinator.
- **Rule 5 — thumb and index:** thumb = **EPL, EPB, APL** (3 muscles); index = **extensor indicis** (1 muscle).
- **Rule 6 — index and little fingers:** index = **extensor indicis**; little = **extensor digiti minimi**.
- **Rule 7 — nerve supply:** all extensors are supplied by the **posterior interosseous nerve** (a branch of the **radial nerve**); the radial nerve directly supplies **BEST** (brachioradialis, extensors [ECRL], anconeus, as it lies in the spiral groove).
- **Rule 8 — flexors vs extensors:** ECU/FCU and ECR/FCR share the same insertion; **ECU + FCU adduct**, **ECR + FCR abduct** the wrist; **ED attaches to the proximal phalanx**, while FDS and FDP do not — so **ED extends the MP joints only**.

### Why is adduction at the wrist greater than abduction?
Because the **radial styloid extends more distally** than the ulnar styloid and **restricts abduction**. Adduction = **ECU + FCU**; abduction = **APL, FCR, ECRL, ECRB**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 20 muscles = 8 flexors + 12 extensors.
- 12 extensors = 3 carpi + 3 pollicis + 3 digit + 3 others.
- AIN → FPL, lateral ½ FDP, PQ; OK sign.
- Radial nerve proper supplies brachioradialis, ECRL, anconeus; the PIN supplies the rest.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How can you remember the 12 muscles of the posterior compartment?</summary>

**Answer:** Three carpi (ECU, ECRL, ECRB), three pollicis (APL, EPL, EPB), three digit (ED, EDM, EI) and three others (brachioradialis, supinator, anconeus).

**Why:** The lab's rule 3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give the nerve supply of the flexor and extensor compartments.</summary>

**Answer:** Flexors: the median nerve directly or through the AIN, except FCU and the medial half of FDP, which are supplied by the ulnar nerve. Extensors: the posterior interosseous nerve, a branch of the radial nerve; brachioradialis, ECRL and anconeus come directly from the radial nerve.

**Why:** Rules 2 and 7.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why does extensor digitorum extend only the MP joints?</summary>

**Answer:** Because it attaches to the base of the proximal phalanx (via the extensor expansion) rather than to the distal phalanges, unlike FDS and FDP, so it does not act on the PIP and DIP joints.

**Why:** Rule 8.
</details>

## 4. Cubital fossa and the ulnar nerve at the elbow

- **Cubital fossa dissection:** **1 pronator teres**, **2 brachioradialis**, **3 brachialis**, **4 supinator**, **5 biceps tendon**.
- **Ulnar nerve at the elbow:** runs in the **cubital tunnel** behind the medial epicondyle, under the **flexor carpi ulnaris** arch (surgical exposure at the medial elbow).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cubital fossa: PT (medial), brachioradialis (lateral), brachialis (floor), supinator (deep lateral), biceps tendon.
- Ulnar nerve → cubital tunnel under FCU.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Identify the structures of the cubital fossa dissection.</summary>

**Answer:** 1 pronator teres, 2 brachioradialis, 3 brachialis, 4 supinator and 5 the biceps tendon.

**Why:** Straight from the key.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Where does the ulnar nerve pass at the elbow?</summary>

**Answer:** Behind the medial epicondyle in the cubital tunnel, entering the forearm between the two heads of flexor carpi ulnaris.

**Why:** Explains tardy ulnar palsy and compression.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which muscles form the borders and floor of the cubital fossa?</summary>

**Answer:** Pronator teres medially, brachioradialis laterally, with brachialis and supinator forming the floor, and the biceps tendon in the middle.

**Why:** Applied to venipuncture and nerve injury.
</details>

## 5. Muscles on dissected specimens

Spotter slides ask you to identify one labelled structure each: **palmaris longus, flexor digitorum profundus, pronator quadratus, extensor carpi radialis brevis, anconeus, supinator, extensor pollicis longus, the anatomical snuffbox, intertendinous connections and the extensor expansion.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Intertendinous connections link adjacent ED tendons on the dorsum of the hand.
- The extensor expansion is the hood over the finger dorsum.
- The snuffbox is bounded by APL/EPB and EPL.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which structures link the tendons of extensor digitorum on the back of the hand?</summary>

**Answer:** The intertendinous connections, which connect adjacent ED tendons.

**Why:** A frequent specimen label.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the extensor expansion?</summary>

**Answer:** The aponeurotic hood of the extensor digitorum tendon over the dorsum of the digit, receiving the lumbricals and interossei and helping to extend the finger joints.

**Why:** Specimen + function.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which muscle lies across the anterior distal forearm between the radius and ulna?</summary>

**Answer:** Pronator quadratus.

**Why:** A deep flexor labelled on the dissection.
</details>

## 6. Axial section of the forearm and the front of the wrist

### Axial section — key
| Mark | Structure |
|---|---|
| **a** | Basilic vein |
| **b** | Ulnar nerve and artery |
| **c** | Median nerve |
| **d** | Radial nerve and artery |
| **e** | Posterior interosseous nerve and artery |
| **f** | Interosseous membrane |
| **★** | Anterior interosseous nerve and artery |
| **1–13** | 1 FDP, 2 FPL, 3 FCU, 4 FDS, 5 palmaris longus, 6 FCR, 7 brachioradialis, 8 ECRL, 9 ED, 10 EDM, 11 ECU, 12 EPL, 13 abductor pollicis longus |

### Front of the wrist (lateral → medial)
**Radial artery → FCR tendon → median nerve → palmaris longus tendon → FDS → ulnar artery → ulnar nerve → FCU tendon.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Axial section: a basilic v., b ulnar n./a., c median n., d radial n./a., e PIN/a., f interosseous membrane, ★ AIN.
- Muscles 1–13: FDP, FPL, FCU, FDS, PL, FCR, BR, ECRL, ED, EDM, ECU, EPL, APL.
- Wrist: radial a., FCR, median n., PL, FDS, ulnar a., ulnar n., FCU.

</div>

</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Erb's = C5–C6 (policeman's tip)**; **Klumpke's = C8–T1 (claw hand)**.
- **FCU + ½ FDP are ulnar**; every other flexor is median/AIN.
- **AIN = 3 muscles** (FPL, lateral ½ FDP, PQ); **OK sign** = AIN.
- **Brachioradialis, ECRL, anconeus** come **directly from the radial nerve**; the rest of the extensors come from the **PIN**.
- **ED extends MP joints only** (attaches to the proximal phalanx).
- Wrist **adduction > abduction** because of the **longer radial styloid**.
- The **cubital fossa**: **1 pronator teres, 2 brachioradialis, 3 brachialis, 4 supinator, 5 biceps tendon**.

## 🔥 Final review — memorize before the exam

1. **Bicipital groove "MLM":** pec major, latissimus dorsi, teres major.
2. **Fracture–nerve pairs:** surgical neck (axillary), spiral groove (radial), supracondylar (median/AIN), medial epicondyle (ulnar).
3. **Erb's** (C5-C6, policeman's tip) vs **Klumpke's** (C8-T1, claw hand).
4. **20 forearm muscles:** 8 flexors + 12 extensors (3 carpi, 3 pollicis, 3 digit, 3 others).
5. **Flexor nerves:** median/AIN, except FCU + ½ FDP (ulnar); AIN = FPL, lateral ½ FDP, PQ.
6. **Extensor nerves:** PIN from the radial; BEST muscles directly from the radial.
7. **Rule 8:** ECU/FCU adduct, ECR/FCR abduct; ED = MP joints only.
8. **Cubital fossa:** PT, brachioradialis, brachialis, supinator, biceps tendon.
9. **Axial section** letters (a–f, ★) and muscles 1–13.
10. **Front of the wrist:** radial a., FCR, median n., PL, FDS, ulnar a., ulnar n., FCU.
