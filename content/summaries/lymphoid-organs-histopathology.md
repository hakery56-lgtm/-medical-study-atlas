## 1. Classification and thymus

- ⭐ **Primary (central) lymphoid organs:** **bone marrow, thymus.** **Secondary (peripheral):** **lymph nodes, spleen, MALT** (**tonsils, Peyer's patches, appendix**).
- **Thymus:** **anterior mediastinum, behind the sternum**; thin connective tissue **capsule**; **lobules** divided by septa into **cortex (dark, many lymphocytes)** and **medulla (light, fewer lymphocytes)**; **Hassall's corpuscles** = **rounded, pink, concentric structures in the medulla**; cells: **T-lymphocytes, epithelial reticular cells, macrophages**; function: **T-lymphocyte maturation.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Primary = bone marrow + thymus; secondary = nodes, spleen, MALT.
- Thymus: dark cortex, light medulla, Hassall's corpuscles in the medulla.
- Thymus = T-cell maturation.

</div>

## 2. Lymph node

- **Location:** along **lymphatic vessels** throughout the body.
- **Layers:** **capsule** (dense connective tissue with trabeculae); **cortex** (**lymphoid follicles, primary and secondary**); **paracortex** (**T-cell-rich zone**); **medulla** (**medullary cords and sinuses**); **hilum** (**efferent lymphatic exit**).
- **Function:** **filters lymph and activates the immune response.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cortex = follicles; paracortex = T cells; medulla = cords and sinuses.
- Efferent lymphatic leaves at the hilum.
- Filters lymph.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the microscopic zones of a lymph node.</summary>

**Answer:** Capsule with trabeculae, cortex with lymphoid follicles, T-cell-rich paracortex, medulla with cords and sinuses, and the hilum.

**Why:** Slide "Lymph Node".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which zone is T-cell rich and which holds the follicles?</summary>

**Answer:** The paracortex is T-cell rich; the cortex holds the primary and secondary follicles.

**Why:** Slide "Lymph Node".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the function of a lymph node?</summary>

**Answer:** To filter lymph and activate the immune response.

**Why:** Slide "Lymph Node".
</details>

## 3. Spleen

- **Location:** **left upper abdomen near the stomach.**
- **Features:** **capsule** of dense connective tissue with **smooth muscle**; **white pulp** = **central artery surrounded by PALS**; **red pulp** = **splenic cords and sinusoids.**
- **Function:** **filters blood, removes old RBCs, initiates the immune response.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- White pulp = central artery + PALS; red pulp = cords + sinusoids.
- The spleen filters blood and removes old RBCs.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe white and red pulp.</summary>

**Answer:** White pulp is a central artery surrounded by the periarteriolar lymphoid sheath (PALS); red pulp is made of splenic cords and sinusoids.

**Why:** Slide "Spleen".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the splenic capsule made of?</summary>

**Answer:** Dense connective tissue with smooth muscle.

**Why:** Slide "Spleen".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the functions of the spleen?</summary>

**Answer:** Filters blood, removes old RBCs and initiates the immune response.

**Why:** Slide "Spleen".
</details>

## 4. MALT: palatine tonsil and Peyer's patches

- **Palatine tonsil:** **stratified squamous non-keratinized epithelium**, **crypts** (deep invaginations), **lymphoid follicles with germinal centers**, **partial capsule.**
- **Peyer's patches (ileum):** **aggregated lymphoid nodules in the ileum wall**, covered by **simple columnar epithelium with M cells**; **immune defense at mucosal surfaces.**

| Organ | Location | Key histological features | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Thymus** | Mediastinum | **Cortex and medulla, Hassall's corpuscles** | **T-cell maturation** |
| **Lymph node** | Along lymph vessels | **Cortex, paracortex, medulla** | **Filters lymph** |
| **Spleen** | Left upper abdomen | **White and red pulp** | **Filters blood** |
| **MALT** | Mucosal sites | **Follicles and M cells** | **Mucosal immunity** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Tonsil: non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium, crypts, germinal centers, partial capsule.
- Peyer's patches: ileum, simple columnar epithelium with M cells.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the palatine tonsil histologically.</summary>

**Why:** Slide "MALT".

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Hassall's corpuscles are in the thymic MEDULLA (not the cortex).**
- **Thymic cortex is DARK; medulla is LIGHT.**
- **Paracortex = T cells; cortex = follicles.**
- **Spleen: white pulp (PALS) vs red pulp (cords and sinusoids).**
- **Tonsil epithelium is NON-keratinized stratified squamous; Peyer's patches have SIMPLE COLUMNAR epithelium with M cells.**
