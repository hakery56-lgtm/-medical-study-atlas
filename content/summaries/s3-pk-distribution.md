## 2. Drug distribution and its determinants

- **Drug distribution** = the process by which a drug **reversibly leaves the bloodstream and enters the interstitium (extracellular fluid) and/or the cells.**
- Delivery from plasma to the interstitium depends mainly on:
  1. **Blood flow**
  2. **Capillary permeability**
  3. **Degree of binding to plasma proteins**
  4. **Relative hydrophobicity of the drug**

### A. Blood flow
- Flow is **unequal**: **brain, liver and kidney > skeletal muscle, most viscera, skin and fat.**
- E.g. **thiopental**: high blood flow + high lipid solubility → rapidly enters the CNS → **anesthesia**.

### B. Capillary permeability
- Determined by **capillary structure** and the **chemical nature of the drug**.
- **Liver:** endothelium has **large fenestrations** → drugs move freely between blood and interstitium.
- **Brain (BBB):** **tight junctions**, astrocyte foot processes → drugs must pass **through** endothelial cells or be **actively transported** (e.g. **levodopa**).
  - **Lipid-soluble** drugs readily enter the CNS; **ionized/polar** drugs generally fail.

### D. Hydrophobicity
- **Hydrophobic drugs** readily move across most biologic membranes.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Distribution = reversible movement from blood to interstitium/cells.
- Determinants: blood flow, capillary permeability, plasma protein binding, hydrophobicity.
- Brain/liver/kidney have the highest flow; thiopental enters CNS quickly (flow + lipid solubility).
- BBB: tight junctions; lipid-soluble drugs enter, ionized/polar ones don't; levodopa uses active transport.

</div>

## 3. Binding to plasma proteins and drug displacement

- **Reversible binding** to plasma proteins **sequesters the drug in a non-diffusible form** and slows its exit from the vascular compartment.
- **Albumin** = the **major drug-binding protein**; acts as a **drug reservoir**; **strongest affinity for hydrophobic drugs** (hydrophilic drugs do not bind).
- ⭐ **Bound drug = pharmacologically inactive; only free (unbound) drug acts on tissue targets.**

### Competition and displacement
- Two drugs with **high affinity for albumin** compete for binding sites → **displacement**.
- **Clinical example:** **warfarin** is highly bound (99 %); a **sulfonamide displaces warfarin** → **free warfarin rises rapidly** → ↑ therapeutic effect and **toxicity (bleeding)**.

### Plasma protein binding of some drugs
| Drug | % bound | Drug | % bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warfarin** | **99 %** | **Morphine** | **35 %** |
| **Diazepam** | **98 %** | **Digoxin** | **25 %** |
| **Amitriptyline** | **95 %** | **Amoxycillin** | **18 %** |
| **Trimethoprim** | **70 %** | **Ethosuximide** | **0 %** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Albumin is the main binding protein; bound = inactive reservoir; free = active.
- Displacement: sulfonamide displaces warfarin → ↑ free warfarin → bleeding.
- Binding: warfarin 99 %, diazepam 98 %, amitriptyline 95 %, trimethoprim 70 %, morphine 35 %, digoxin 25 %, amoxycillin 18 %, ethosuximide 0 %.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the role of plasma proteins in drug distribution?</summary>

**Answer:** Albumin reversibly binds drugs (especially hydrophobic ones), sequestering them in a non-diffusible, inactive form that acts as a reservoir; only the free unbound drug can reach the target tissues.

**Why:** Binding slows transfer out of the vascular compartment.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain drug displacement with a clinical example.</summary>

**Answer:** When two drugs with high albumin affinity are given together they compete for binding sites. Warfarin is 99 % bound; a sulfonamide displaces it, quickly raising the free warfarin concentration and causing increased effects and toxicity such as bleeding.

**Why:** Small change in bound fraction = large change in free drug.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which of the listed drugs is not bound to plasma proteins?</summary>

**Answer:** Ethosuximide (0 %); the most highly bound is warfarin (99 %), then diazepam (98 %) and amitriptyline (95 %).

**Why:** Data table.
</details>

## 4. Volume of distribution (Vd)

- **Apparent Vd** relates the **amount of drug in the body** to the **concentration (C₀)** in blood or plasma:

  ⭐ **Vd = amount of drug in the body ÷ C₀**
- Useful for calculating a drug's **loading dose**.

### Water compartments
| Compartment | % body weight | Which drugs distribute here |
|---|---|---|
| **Plasma** | **6 %** | **large-molecular-weight drugs trapped** (cannot cross endothelium), bind extensively to protein — e.g. **heparin** |
| **Extracellular fluid** (plasma + interstitial) | **20 %** | **hydrophilic, low-molecular-weight** drugs — e.g. **aminoglycosides** |
| **Total body water** | **60 %** | **hydrophobic, low-molecular-weight** drugs — e.g. **ethanol** |

### Vd of some drugs (L/70 kg → L/kg)
| Drug | Vd (L/70 kg) | Vd (L/kg) |
|---|---|---|
| **Heparin** | **5** | 0.07 |
| **Aspirin** | **11** | 0.15 |
| **Amoxycillin** | **28** | 0.4 |
| **Atenolol** | **77** | 1.1 |
| **Diazepam** | **140** | 2.0 |
| **Digoxin** | **420** | 6.0 |
| **Nortriptyline** | **1000** | 14.2 |
| **Chloroquine** | **13 000** | 185.7 |

- **Any factor that increases Vd can increase t½** and prolong duration of action.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Vd = amount in body / C₀; used for the loading dose.
- Plasma 6 % (heparin), ECF 20 % (aminoglycosides), total body water 60 % (ethanol).
- Vd: heparin 5 L (smallest) → chloroquine 13 000 L (largest).
- ↑ Vd → ↑ t½ → longer duration.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the apparent volume of distribution and state its use.</summary>

**Answer:** Vd relates the amount of drug in the body to its plasma concentration (Vd = amount of drug in body / C₀); it is used to calculate the loading dose.

**Why:** Lecture definition.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which drugs distribute to the plasma, extracellular fluid and total body water compartments?</summary>

**Answer:** Plasma (6 % of body weight): large drugs bound to protein such as heparin. Extracellular fluid (20 %): hydrophilic low-molecular-weight drugs such as aminoglycosides. Total body water (60 %): hydrophobic low-molecular-weight drugs such as ethanol.

**Why:** Size and hydrophobicity decide the compartment.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the effect of increasing Vd on half-life?</summary>

**Answer:** Any factor that increases Vd can increase the half-life and thus extend the duration of action of some drugs.

**Why:** More of the drug sits outside the plasma, away from elimination.
</details>
