## 1. Spinal meninges and lumbar puncture

- ⭐ **Meninges (inner → outer): pia mater, arachnoid mater, dura mater.**
- **Pia:** **adherent to the cord;** thickenings = **denticulate ligaments (foramen magnum to L1, attach to dura, serrated border), subarachnoid septum, filum terminale (to the coccyx).**
- **Arachnoid / subarachnoid space:** **real space between pia and arachnoid; CSF + trabeculae; ends at S2 (pierced by the filum terminale); contains CSF, spinal vessels, nerve roots.** **Lumbar cistern: L2-S2** (safer site for LP).
- **Dura / epidural space:** **foramen magnum to S2 (sheath around the filum terminale);** **epidural space = real space with loose areolar tissue, semiliquid fat, internal vertebral venous plexus.**
- ⭐ **Lumbar puncture:** **diagnostic/therapeutic CSF withdrawal below the end of the cord;** **iliac crest line passes through the L4 spine;** **preferred space L3-L4 (or L4-L5)** because the subarachnoid space is spacious and holds only the **filum terminale and cauda equina.** **Needle passes: skin, fasciae, supraspinous ligament, interspinous ligament, ligamentum flavum, dura, arachnoid.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pia (innermost), arachnoid, dura (outermost).
- LP: L3-L4 or L4-L5, landmark = iliac crest (L4).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the spinal meninges.</summary>

**Answer:** Pia (adherent, forming denticulate ligaments, subarachnoid septum and filum terminale), arachnoid (with the CSF-filled subarachnoid space ending at S2) and dura (outermost, to S2, with the epidural space containing fat and the venous plexus).

**Why:** Slides "Spinal meninges".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How is a lumbar puncture performed anatomically?</summary>

**Answer:** Below the end of the cord at L3-L4 or L4-L5 using the iliac crest line (L4 spine) as the landmark; the needle crosses skin, fasciae, supraspinous and interspinous ligaments, ligamentum flavum, dura and arachnoid to reach the subarachnoid space.

**Why:** Slides "Lumbar puncture".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the lumbar cistern?</summary>

**Answer:** The expanded subarachnoid space below the conus medullaris from L2 to S2 containing the cauda equina, the safer site for lumbar puncture.

**Why:** Slide "Spinal arachnoid mater".
</details>

## 2. The spinal cord: external features and segments

- **Cord:** **upper two-thirds of the canal; from the lower border of the foramen magnum (continuation of the medulla) to the L1/L2 disc; ~45 cm (about 25 cm shorter than the column); cylindrical.**
- **Grooves:** **5 sulci + 1 fissure: anterior median fissure (deepest), posterior median sulcus, 2 anterolateral sulci (ventral/motor roots), 2 posterolateral sulci (dorsal/sensory roots).**
- ⭐ **31 segments: 8 C, 12 T, 5 L, 5 S, 1 Co.** **Rule 1-2-3:** **cervical +1 (C5 vertebra ↔ C6 segment); upper thoracic +2 (T3 ↔ T5); lower thoracic +3 (T10 ↔ L1); T11 ↔ L2-L5; T12 and L1 ↔ all sacral.**
- **Spinal nerve:** **ventral root (motor + sympathetic) + dorsal root (sensory, DRG = 1st order neurons) → mixed nerve → ventral and dorsal rami.**
- ⭐ **Exits:** **C1-C7 above their vertebra; C8 below C7; T1-L5 below; S1-S4 sacral foramina; S5 and Co via sacral hiatus.**
- **Roots:** become **longer and more oblique downward: C1-C2 horizontal, C3-T12 oblique, L1-Co vertical.** **Below L1 only roots (cauda equina) float in CSF (lumbar cistern).** **Stability: filum terminale and denticulate ligaments.**
- **Cauda equina = L2-Co roots; conus medullaris = S1-Co segments at the lower border of L1.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cord ends at L1/L2; segments are higher than vertebrae (rule 1-2-3).
- C1-C7 exit above; C8 and below exit below (C8 below C7).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the extent and external features of the spinal cord.</summary>

**Answer:** It occupies the upper two-thirds of the canal from the lower border of the foramen magnum to the L1/L2 disc (about 45 cm), is cylindrical and has five sulci and one fissure (anterior median fissure, posterior median sulcus, two anterolateral and two posterolateral sulci).

**Why:** Slides "External features".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain the 1-2-3 rule.</summary>

**Answer:** Cord segments lie higher than vertebrae: cervical add 1, upper thoracic add 2, lower thoracic add 3; T11 is opposite L2-L5 and T12/L1 opposite the sacral segments.

**Why:** Slide "Segments".
</details>

**Answer:** C1-C7 above their vertebra, C8 below C7, T1-L5 below their vertebra, S1-S4 through the sacral foramina and S5 plus coccygeal through the sacral hiatus.

**Why:** Slide "Exit of spinal nerves".

## 3. Internal features, tracts and cord syndromes

- **Transverse section:** **H-shaped gray matter inside white matter; halves separated by the posterior median sulcus and anterior median fissure; joined by gray commissure (central canal) and white commissure; terminal ventricle = lower end of the central canal in the conus.**
- **Gray matter:** **anterior horns = motor; posterior horns = sensory; lateral horn T1-L2 (sympathetic) and S2-S4 (parasympathetic).**
- **White matter:** **ventral, lateral, dorsal columns; short intersegmental tracts (local reflexes) and long tracts.**
- ⭐ **Long tracts:** **ascending: gracile, cuneate, lateral and anterior spinothalamic; descending (pyramidal): lateral and anterior corticospinal.**
- **Sensory:** **extroceptive (pain, temperature, crude touch, pressure); conscious proprioception (tactile localization, discrimination, stereognosis).** **Sensory pathway = 3 neurons (DRG, then posterior horn/medulla, then thalamus); motor = 2 (UMN precentral gyrus, LMN anterior horn).**
- ⭐ **Lateral spinothalamic = pain and temperature; anterior = crude touch and pressure (crossed).** **Dorsal column-medial lemniscus = conscious proprioception, discrimination, vibration; lesion → Romberg's sign.**

| | **Conus medullaris syndrome** | **Cauda equina syndrome** |
|---|---|---|
| **Level / lesion** | **L1-L2 vertebral level (segments S1-Co); central cord lesion** | **Roots L2-Co below the conus; peripheral root lesion (LMN)** |
| **Cause** | **Central disc herniation, tumour, trauma, ischaemia** | **Large central/paracentral disc (L4-L5, L5-S1), tumour, trauma, stenosis** |
| **Onset** | **Sudden, symmetrical** | **Gradual, asymmetrical** |
| **Motor** | **Mild symmetric weakness; UMN and LMN signs may coexist** | **Marked asymmetric LMN weakness, atrophy** |
| **Sensory** | **Bilateral symmetric saddle anaesthesia** | **Patchy dermatomal loss ± saddle** |
| **Reflexes** | **Early ankle jerk loss** | **Areflexia in affected roots (ankle and knee)** |
| **Sphincters** | **Early and severe; impotence common and early** | **Late and variable; impotence later** |
| **Pain** | **Mild/absent, often bilateral** | **Severe radicular, often unilateral** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Conus: early sphincter loss, little pain; cauda equina: severe radicular pain, late sphincter loss.
- Lateral spinothalamic = pain/temperature; anterior = crude touch/pressure.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the internal features of the spinal cord.</summary>

**Answer:** H-shaped gray matter (anterior motor horns, posterior sensory horns, lateral horns T1-L2 and S2-S4) inside white matter columns with ascending and descending tracts, joined by gray and white commissures; the central canal ends as the terminal ventricle in the conus.

**Why:** Slides "Internal features".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare conus medullaris and cauda equina syndromes.</summary>

**Answer:** Conus: central cord lesion at L1-L2 with sudden symmetric mild weakness, saddle anaesthesia, early severe sphincter and sexual dysfunction and little pain; cauda equina: root lesion below L2 with gradual asymmetric marked LMN weakness, patchy sensory loss, areflexia, severe radicular pain and late sphincter involvement.

**Why:** Comparison table.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What does a dorsal column lesion produce?</summary>

**Answer:** Loss of conscious proprioception, discrimination and vibration; the patient cannot tell limb position unless looking, so he staggers with eyes closed (Romberg's sign).

**Why:** Slide "Dorsal column-medial lemniscus pathway".
</details>

## 4. Vertebrae and the intervertebral disc

- **33 vertebrae: 24 isolated (7 C, 12 T, 5 L) + 9 fused (sacrum 5, coccyx 4).** **Basic vertebra = body (anterior) + arch (posterior: 2 laminae, 2 pedicles, 7 processes: 2 superior articular, 2 inferior articular, 2 transverse, 1 spinous).** **Vertebrae increase in size downward.** **Vertebrae = 4/5, discs = 1/5.**
- ⭐ **Intervertebral foramen = inferior notch of the upper vertebra + superior notch of the lower; stenosis → shooting radicular pain along the dermatome** (with numbness, tingling, weakness).
- ⭐ **Disc:** **elastic fibrocartilage cushion; secondary cartilaginous joint; degenerates from ~20 years; 23 discs (6 C, 12 T, 5 L); none between atlas and axis, none between sacral vertebrae.** **Functions: flexibility, shock absorption, even transmission of compressive loads.** **Thickness ~3 mm cervical → ~9 mm lumbar.**

| | **Annulus fibrosus** | **Nucleus pulposus** |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | **Sclerotome mesoderm** | **Notochord** |
| **Structure** | **Concentric lamellae of fibrocartilage; oblique alternating collagen fibers (resist torsion and compression)** | **Central gelatinous core; 70-90% water in youth** |
| **Collagen** | **Type I (outer), II (inner)** | **Type II + proteoglycans (aggrecan)** |
| **Function** | **Tensile strength, contains the nucleus** | **Shock absorption, even hydraulic pressure** |

- **Ageing:** **nucleus loses water and elasticity → degeneration and loss of height.**
- ⭐ **Diurnal variation:** **disc height highest in the early morning and lowest in the evening;** **nucleus absorbs fluid lying down and loses it upright;** **we shorten with age (less reabsorption).**
- **Load:** **normal: nucleus distributes load equally to the annulus; degenerated: unequal; severely degenerated: no cushioning → herniation.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Annulus = sclerotome, type I; nucleus = notochord, type II.
- 23 discs; none at C1-C2 or between sacral vertebrae.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the annulus fibrosus and nucleus pulposus.</summary>

**Answer:** The annulus (sclerotome) has concentric fibrocartilage lamellae with oblique alternating collagen (type I outer, II inner) giving tensile strength; the nucleus (notochord) is a gelatinous core of 70-90% water with type II collagen and aggrecan providing shock absorption.

**Why:** Slides "Annulus fibrosus", "Nucleus pulposus" and summary table.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain the diurnal variation of disc height.</summary>

**Answer:** The proteoglycan-rich nucleus swells with water when unloaded at night and loses fluid when upright under pressure, so height is greatest in the morning and least at the end of the day; with age discs reabsorb less, so we shorten.

**Why:** Slide "Diurnal variation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is load transmitted in normal and degenerated discs?</summary>

**Answer:** Normally the nucleus spreads load equally over the annulus; in degeneration it transmits load unequally; in severe degeneration it loses all cushioning, which can lead to herniation.

**Why:** Slide "Distribution of load in IVD".
</details>

## 5. Disc herniation, sciatica and ligaments

- ⭐ **95% of herniations: lumbosacral L4-L5 or L5-S1,** mostly **posterolateral;** cervical **C6-C7.**
- **Exiting root:** leaves at its own level (**L4 at L4-L5**), lateral near the pedicle, hit by **foraminal stenosis/lateral herniation.** **Traversing root:** goes to the next lower foramen (**L5 at L4-L5**), medial beneath the disc, hit by **paracentral/posterolateral herniation.**
- **Relations:** **lumbar disc: exiting (lateral), traversing (posterolateral), cauda equina (posterior); cervical disc: exiting (posterolateral), cord (posterior).**

| Direction | Affects |
|---|---|
| **Posterolateral** | **Traversing root** |
| **Foraminal (lateral)** | **Exiting root** |
| **Central** | **Cauda equina (rare, dangerous; MRI + surgery within 48 h)** |

- ⭐ **True sciatica:** **posterolateral L5-S1 herniation compressing S1;** pain along the sciatic path (back → buttock → posterior thigh → leg → foot) with numbness, tingling, burning, usually unilateral; **worse sitting, better standing;** usually improves. **L4-L5 → L5; L5-S1 → S1.**

| Ligament | Location | Function / limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior longitudinal** | **Anterior bodies** | **Supports disc front; limits hyperextension (tears in whiplash)** |
| **Posterior longitudinal** | **Posterior bodies (inside canal)** | **Reinforces disc posteriorly; limits hyperflexion; narrower, weak laterally** |
| **Ligamentum flavum** | **Between laminae** | **Strongest, most elastic; posture, returns to upright; closes the canal's posterior wall** |
| **Interspinous / supraspinous** | **Between / on tips of spinous processes** | **Posterior support; limit flexion** |
| **Intertransverse** | **Between transverse processes** | **Lateral stability; limits lateral flexion** |

- ⭐ **Why L4-L5 and L5-S1 prolapse:** **highest weight load + PLL thins caudally → thin unsupported annulus; flexion with load ruptures it → posterolateral herniation → narrows the foramen → compresses L4 or L5 root.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Posterolateral → traversing root; foraminal → exiting root; central → cauda equina.
- L4-L5 disc → L5 root; L5-S1 → S1 (true sciatica).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Differentiate exiting and traversing nerve roots.</summary>

**Answer:** The exiting root leaves at its own level (L4 at L4-L5) laterally and is hit by foraminal or lateral herniation; the traversing root runs inferiorly and medially beneath the disc to exit the next foramen (L5 at L4-L5) and is hit by posterolateral herniation.

**Why:** Slide "Difference between traversing and exiting nerve roots".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why does disc prolapse occur commonly at L4-L5 and L5-S1?</summary>

**Answer:** These levels bear the highest body weight and the posterior longitudinal ligament is thin caudally, so the annulus is poorly supported and ruptures during flexion with load; the nucleus herniates posterolaterally, narrows the foramen and compresses L4 or L5.

**Why:** Slide "Why disc prolapse commonly occurs between L4-L5 or L5-S1".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the ligaments of the vertebral column and their roles.</summary>

**Answer:** ALL (limits hyperextension), PLL (reinforces disc, limits hyperflexion), ligamentum flavum (elastic posture ligament), interspinous and supraspinous (posterior support, flexion) and intertransverse (lateral stability).

**Why:** Slide "Ligaments of the vertebral column".
</details>

## 6. Dermatomes, myotomes and neurological examination

- ⭐ **Dermatome = skin area of a single spinal nerve;** tests the **level of cord injury and progress.** **Trunk: no plexuses, easy to test; limbs: plexuses → marked overlap.** **T3 = nipple, T10 = umbilicus, T12 = suprapubic.** **Autonomous areas (minimal overlap; pre/postaxial borders):** **C5 upper lateral arm; C6 thumb pad; C7 index pad; C8 little finger pad; T1 medial elbow.**
- **Lower limb dermatomes:** **L1 inguinal ligament; L2 lateral thigh; L3 lower medial thigh; L4 medial great toe; L5 second toe; S1 little toe to heel; S3 gluteal; S2 strip between.** **"Stand on S1, sit on S3."**
- ⭐ **Myotomes:** **C5 shoulder abduction; C6 elbow flexion; C7 elbow extension; C8 finger flexion; T1 finger abduction; L2 hip flexion; L3 hip abduction; L4 knee extension; L5 dorsiflexion; S1 plantar flexion.**
- **Examine** motor, power, sensation and reflexes.

| Lumbar disc | Root | Motor | Sensory | Reflex |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L3-L4** | **L4** | **Weak dorsiflexion (tibialis anterior)** | **Medial foot, big toe** | **↓ Patellar** |
| **L4-L5** | **L5** | **Weak big-toe extension** | **Dorsum of foot (2nd-4th toes)** | **None** |
| **L5-S1** | **S1** | **Weak plantar flexion** | **Lateral foot, little toe** | **↓ Ankle** |

| Cervical disc | Root | Sensory | Motor | Reflex |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C3-C4** | **C4** | **Tip of shoulder** | **Diaphragm** | **None** |
| **C4-C5** | **C5** | **Upper lateral arm** | **Deltoid (shoulder abduction), elbow flexion** | **↓ Biceps** |
| **C5-C6** | **C6** | **Thumb pad** | **ECRL/ECRB (wrist extension)** | **↓ Brachioradialis** |
| **C6-C7** | **C7** | **Index pad** | **Triceps (elbow extension), wrist flexion, finger extension** | **↓ Triceps** |
| **C7-T1** | **C8** | **Little finger pad** | **FDS (finger flexion)** | **None** |
| **T1-T2** | **T1** | **Medial elbow** | **Interossei (finger abduction)** | **None** |

- **Cervical herniation:** **most at C6-C7 (also C5-C6); neck pain radiating to the ipsilateral arm, numbness, paresthesia, weakness; worse with coughing, sneezing, neck movement.**
- **Case:** **35-year-old, neck pain to the left arm, C5-C6 posterolateral herniation → C6 root;** **weak wrist extension (biceps and deltoid spared), ↓ brachioradialis reflex, thumb pad sensory loss.** **C5-C6 degenerates because of high mobility and stress; PLL is thin laterally.**
- **Quick questions:** **epidural landmark = iliac crest; Achilles tendon reflex = S1.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Disc level N-(N+1) usually compresses the lower root in the lumbar spine (L4-L5 → L5) and the numerically higher root in the cervical spine (C5-C6 → C6).
- L5 has no reflex; L4 = knee jerk; S1 = ankle jerk; C5 biceps, C6 brachioradialis, C7 triceps.

</div>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Posterolateral = TRAVERSING root; foraminal = EXITING root.**
- **L4-L5 disc → L5 root; C5-C6 disc → C6 root.**
- **L5 root compression: NO reflex change.**
- **Conus: early sphincter loss, little pain; cauda equina: severe pain, late sphincter loss.**
- **Disc height is greatest in the morning.**
- **LP at L3-L4 / L4-L5; iliac crest line = L4.**
