## 1. Autonomic pathways and transmitters

- ⭐ **Autonomic efferent pathway = TWO neurons in series (preganglionic + postganglionic)**; the **somatic** pathway has one. The two main autonomic transmitters are **acetylcholine (ACh)** and **noradrenaline (NA).**

| Pathway | Preganglionic | Postganglionic |
|---|---|---|
| **Somatic** (skeletal muscle) | **ACh (nicotinic)** directly to muscle | — |
| **Sympathetic → blood vessels** | **ACh (nic)** | **NA** |
| **Sympathetic → sweat glands** | **ACh (nic)** | **ACh (muscarinic)** |
| **Sympathetic → adrenal medulla** | **ACh (nic)** | — (secretes into blood) |
| **Parasympathetic** (salivary glands etc.) | **ACh (nic)** | **ACh (muscarinic)** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Autonomic = 2 neurons in series; somatic = 1.
- ACh (nicotinic) at all ganglia and the adrenal medulla; ACh (muscarinic) at parasympathetic targets and sweat glands; NA at most sympathetic targets.

</div>

<details><summary>Q1. How do the autonomic and somatic efferent pathways differ?</summary>

**Answer:** The autonomic pathway has two neurons in series (preganglionic and postganglionic), whereas the somatic pathway has a single motor neuron to skeletal muscle.

## 2. ACh synthesis, release and termination (drug sites)

1. **Synthesis:** **acetyl-CoA + choline → ACh** by **choline acetyltransferase (CAT)**; choline enters through the **choline carrier** (**blocked by hemicholinium**).
2. **Storage:** ACh enters synaptic **vesicles** via the **ACh (vesicular) carrier** (**blocked by vesamicol**).
3. **Release:** **exocytosis** (**blocked by presynaptic toxins such as botulinum toxin**).
4. **Action:** ACh acts on the **postsynaptic nicotinic receptor** (**non-depolarising blockers, e.g. tubocurarine, block it; depolarising blockers, e.g. suxamethonium, activate it persistently**).
5. **Termination:** **acetylcholinesterase (AChE) splits ACh into choline + acetate**; choline is recycled. **Anticholinesterases (e.g. neostigmine) inhibit AChE.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- CAT makes ACh from acetyl-CoA + choline; hemicholinium (choline carrier), vesamicol (vesicular carrier), botulinum (release).
- AChE terminates ACh; neostigmine inhibits AChE; tubocurarine (non-depolarising) and suxamethonium (depolarising) act at the nicotinic receptor.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe ACh synthesis and release.</summary>

**Answer:** Acetyl-CoA and choline (taken up by the choline carrier) form ACh via choline acetyltransferase; ACh is stored in vesicles by the vesicular carrier and released by exocytosis.

**Why:** Diagram "ACh synthesis and release".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Where do hemicholinium, vesamicol and botulinum toxin act?</summary>

**Answer:** Hemicholinium blocks the choline carrier, vesamicol blocks the vesicular ACh carrier, and botulinum toxin (a presynaptic toxin) blocks ACh release.

**Why:** Diagram "ACh synthesis and release".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is ACh action terminated and which drugs modify it?</summary>

**Answer:** AChE hydrolyzes ACh into choline and acetate; anticholinesterases such as neostigmine inhibit AChE, while non-depolarising (tubocurarine) and depolarising (suxamethonium) blockers act on the postsynaptic nicotinic receptor.

**Why:** Diagram "ACh synthesis and release".
</details>

## 3. Nicotinic receptors

- **Two types: Nm (muscular) and Nn (neural).** All nAChRs are **pentameric, ionotropic (channel-linked) receptors = ligand-gated ion channels** (muscle subunits **α, α, β, δ, γ**).
- **Two ACh binding sites; BOTH must be occupied** (two ACh molecules) to open the channel. **Na⁺ enters → rapid depolarization → action potential (EPSP).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Nm (muscle) and Nn (neuronal); pentameric ligand-gated channels.
- Need 2 ACh molecules; Na⁺ influx → rapid depolarization (EPSP).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the nicotinic receptor.</summary>

**Answer:** A pentameric ionotropic receptor (ligand-gated ion channel) subdivided into Nm (muscular) and Nn (neural); the muscle receptor has α, β, δ and γ subunits.

**Why:** Slide "Nicotinic receptors".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How does ACh activate the nicotinic receptor?</summary>

**Answer:** Two ACh molecules must occupy the two binding sites to open the channel; Na⁺ then enters, causing rapid depolarization and an action potential (EPSP).

**Why:** Slide "Nicotinic ACh receptor (muscle subtypes)".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Differentiate Nm from Nn receptors.</summary>

**Answer:** Nm receptors are muscular (neuromuscular junction); Nn receptors are neural (autonomic ganglia).

**Why:** Slide "Nicotinic receptors".
</details>

## 4. Muscarinic receptors (M1–M5)

- **G-protein coupled (metabotropic) receptors, 5 subtypes.**
- **M1, M3, M5 → Gq → phospholipase C → PIP₂ → IP₃ + DAG**; **IP₃ → intracellular Ca²⁺ release (smooth muscle contraction)**; **DAG → protein kinase C (secretion).**
- **M2, M4 → Gi → inhibit adenylate cyclase → ↓ cAMP.**
- **Three well characterized: M1 (neural, excitatory), M2 (cardiac, inhibitory), M3 (glandular/smooth muscle, excitatory).**

| | Main locations | Functional response |
|---|---|---|
| **M1** | **Autonomic ganglia; gastric enterochromaffin-like cells; cerebral cortex** | **CNS excitation; gastric acid secretion** |
| **M2** | **Heart (atria); CNS; presynaptic** | **Cardiac inhibition: −ve chronotropic, −ve inotropic, −ve dromotropic (↓ conduction velocity); neural inhibition; autoinhibition of ACh release** |
| **M3** | **Exocrine glands (salivary, lacrimal, sweat, gastric); smooth muscle (GIT, eye circular and ciliary, airways, bladder); blood vessel endothelium; CNS** | **Salivation, lacrimation, sweating, bronchial and GI secretion; GI, bronchial and urinary smooth-muscle contraction (defecation, vomiting, urination, bronchoconstriction); ocular miosis and near-vision accommodation; vasodilatation** |
| **M4** | **CNS** | **Enhanced locomotion** |
| **M5** | **CNS** | **Not known** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- M1, M3, M5 = Gq (IP₃/DAG); M2, M4 = Gi (↓ cAMP).
- M1 neural/gastric acid; M2 heart (↓ rate, force, conduction); M3 glands + smooth muscle + eye + vasodilatation.
- M4 locomotion; M5 unknown.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Explain the signaling of muscarinic receptors.</summary>

**Answer:** M1, M3 and M5 couple to Gq: phospholipase C splits PIP₂ into IP₃ (releases intracellular Ca²⁺, smooth muscle contraction) and DAG (activates protein kinase C, secretion); M2 and M4 couple to Gi, inhibiting adenylate cyclase and lowering cAMP.

**Why:** Slides "Muscarinic receptors".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the cardiac effects of M2 receptors.</summary>

**Answer:** Cardiac inhibition: negative chronotropic (rate), inotropic (force) and dromotropic (decreased conduction velocity) effects; M2 also causes neural inhibition and autoinhibition of ACh release.

**Why:** Table of mAChRs.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the responses to M3 stimulation.</summary>

**Answer:** Salivation, lacrimation, sweating, bronchial and GI secretion; contraction of GI, bronchial and urinary smooth muscle (defecation, vomiting, urination, bronchoconstriction); miosis and near-vision accommodation; vasodilatation.

**Why:** Table of mAChRs.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Autonomic = 2 neurons; somatic = 1.**
- **Sweat glands: sympathetic but postganglionic ACh on MUSCARINIC receptors.**
- **Adrenal medulla: preganglionic ACh (nicotinic), no postganglionic neuron.**
- **Hemicholinium = choline carrier; vesamicol = vesicular carrier; botulinum = release; AChE ends ACh action.**
- **Nicotinic = ion channel (Na⁺, fast); muscarinic = GPCR.**
- **Gq = M1, M3, M5; Gi = M2, M4.**
- **M2 = heart (inhibitory); M3 = glands, smooth muscle, eye, vasodilatation.**
