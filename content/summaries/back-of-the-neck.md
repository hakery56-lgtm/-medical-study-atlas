## 1. Back of the neck and the suboccipital triangle

- **Thick skin** covers a fibrous superficial fascia containing **3 cutaneous nerves** (medial → lateral): **third occipital, greater occipital, lesser occipital.** The **greater and third occipital** nerves pierce **trapezius.**
- Deep to the deep fascia: **trapezius, splenius capitis, semispinalis capitis, longissimus capitis.** Removing them exposes the **suboccipital triangle.**
- **Splenius capitis** inserts on the **mastoid** (deep to sternomastoid). **Semispinalis capitis** → squamous occiput below the **superior nuchal line** (**greater occipital n.**). **Longissimus capitis** → mastoid (**posterior rami**).

| Suboccipital triangle | Structure |
|---|---|
| **Inferior** | **Obliquus capitis inferior** |
| **Superolateral** | **Obliquus capitis superior** |
| **Superomedial** | **Rectus capitis posterior** (major and minor) |
| **Floor** | **Posterior atlanto-occipital membrane** and **posterior arch of atlas** |
| **Contents** | ⭐ **Suboccipital nerve (dorsal ramus of C1)**, **3rd part of vertebral artery**, **suboccipital venous plexus** |

- **Suboccipital muscles (4):** rectus capitis posterior major and minor, obliquus capitis superior and inferior. **Nerve:** suboccipital nerve. **Action:** extend the head at the **atlanto-occipital** joint, rotate it at the **atlanto-axial** joints.
- **Clinical:** **suboccipital (cisternal) puncture** — needle below the **external occipital protuberance** into the **cisterna magna** to take CSF; an alternative to lumbar puncture.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cutaneous nerves medial to lateral: third, greater, lesser occipital.
- Triangle: oblique inferior, oblique superior, rectus posterior; contents = suboccipital nerve, vertebral artery (3rd part), venous plexus.
- Cisternal puncture reaches the cisterna magna below the external occipital protuberance.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Third occipital, greater occipital, lesser occipital; the greater and third occipital nerves pierce trapezius.

**Why:** Slide 3 (Back of the Neck).
</details>

## 2. Prevertebral region and fascial spaces

- **Prevertebral region:** anterior to the cervical vertebrae; muscles covered by **prevertebral deep fascia**, running from the **skull base to the thorax**; they form the **posterior wall of the retropharyngeal space.**
- **Prevertebral muscles:** **longus colli** (capitis and cervicis), **rectus capitis** (anterior and lateralis).

| Space | Boundaries | Contents | Clinical significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prevertebral** | Prevertebral fascia ↔ vertebral bodies | Prevertebral muscles, vertebral column | Usually contained; serious if it spreads to vertebral structures |
| **Retropharyngeal** | **Buccopharyngeal fascia ↔ alar fascia** | Loose connective tissue, **lymph nodes (children)** | Abscess; spreads laterally, usually **upper neck** |
| **Danger** | **Alar fascia ↔ prevertebral fascia**; skull base to **diaphragm** | Loose areolar tissue | ⭐ Infection tracks to the **posterior mediastinum → mediastinitis** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Three spaces behind the pharynx: retropharyngeal (front), danger (middle), prevertebral (back).
- Danger space = direct route to the posterior mediastinum.
- Retropharyngeal nodes are prominent in children.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the prevertebral muscles and say what they form.</summary>

**Answer:** Longus colli and rectus capitis (anterior and lateralis); they form the posterior wall of the retropharyngeal space.

**Why:** Slide 11 (Prevertebral Region).
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare the retropharyngeal and danger spaces.</summary>

**Answer:** Retropharyngeal: between buccopharyngeal and alar fascia, contains nodes, infection causes abscess in the upper neck. Danger: between alar and prevertebral fascia, extends to the diaphragm, infection reaches the posterior mediastinum.

**Why:** Slide 12 (Anatomical Comparison).
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is the danger space dangerous?</summary>

**Answer:** It communicates from the skull base to the diaphragm, so infection can spread directly into the posterior mediastinum causing life-threatening mediastinitis.

**Why:** Slide 12.
</details>

## 3. Lymphatics of the head and neck

- A horizontal **encircling band** of nodes at the **cranio-cervical junction**; enlarged nodes are **palpable.**
- **Superficial ring (5 groups)** draining **face and scalp:** **occipital, mastoid (posterior auricular), pre-auricular and parotid, submandibular, submental.**
- **Flow:** occipital + mastoid → **superficial cervical** nodes (along the **external jugular vein**, on sternocleidomastoid). Pre-auricular/parotid + submandibular + submental → **deep cervical** nodes.
- **Deep cervical nodes:** chain along the **internal jugular vein**, split into upper and lower groups by the **intermediate tendon of omohyoid.**
- ⭐ **Jugulodigastric node** (upper group, where posterior belly of digastric crosses IJV) ← **tonsils.** **Jugulo-omohyoid node** (below omohyoid tendon) ← **tongue.**
- Outflow: **jugular trunks** → **right lymphatic duct** (right) / **thoracic duct** (left).

| Level | Nodal group | Cancer sites |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Submental and submandibular | Lip, anterior tongue, floor of mouth, gingiva, buccal mucosa |
| **II** | Upper jugulodigastric | Oral cavity, pharynx, larynx |
| **III** | Middle jugular | Nasopharynx, oropharynx, oral cavity, hypopharynx, larynx |
| **IV** | Inferior jugular | Hypopharynx, subglottic larynx, oesophagus |
| **V** | Posterior triangle | (not listed on the slide) |
| **VI** | Anterior compartment | (not listed on the slide) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Occipital/mastoid → superficial cervical; the other three → deep cervical.
- Jugulodigastric = tonsil node; jugulo-omohyoid = tongue node.
- Levels I-VI link nodal groups to cancer sites.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name the five superficial node groups and where each drains.</summary>

**Answer:** Occipital, mastoid, pre-auricular/parotid, submandibular, submental. Occipital and mastoid go to the superficial cervical nodes; the other three go to the deep cervical nodes.

**Why:** Slides 14-15.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the deep cervical nodes and the two named nodes.</summary>

**Answer:** A chain along the internal jugular vein divided by the omohyoid intermediate tendon. The jugulodigastric node (top) drains the tonsils; the jugulo-omohyoid node drains the tongue.

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which nodal level drains hypopharynx, subglottic larynx and oesophagus?</summary>

**Answer:** Level IV, the inferior jugular nodes.

**Why:** Slide 17 (classification of lymphatics of head and neck).
</details>

## 4. Pharynx: parts and tonsils

- ⭐ **Pharynx** = **musculofascial half-cylinder** linking the oral and nasal cavities to the **larynx and oesophagus**; a common pathway for **air and food.** Parts: **nasopharynx, oropharynx, laryngopharynx.**

| Part | Superior | Inferior | Anterior | Posterior | Lining |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nasopharynx** | Skull base | Soft palate | **Choanae** | Nasopharyngeal tonsil | Respiratory ciliated |
| **Oropharynx** | Soft palate | **Superior edge of epiglottis** | Oral cavity | **C2-C3** | Stratified squamous |
| **Laryngopharynx** | Superior edge of epiglottis | **Inferior edge of cricoid** | Larynx | **C3-C6** | Stratified squamous |

- **Nasopharynx contents:** **pharyngeal tonsil (adenoid)**, **Eustachian tube opening**, **fossa of Rosenmuller** (posterior and superior to the **torus tubarius**), mucus glands.
- **Oropharynx contents:** palatine tonsils, lingual tonsil, uvula, soft palate, base of tongue, pharyngeal wall and faucial arches.
- **3 sets of tonsils:** pharyngeal, palatine, lingual. **Palatine tonsils** lie in the tonsillar fossa between the **anterior pillar** (palatoglossal arch, contains palatoglossus, boundary between buccal cavity and oropharynx) and the **posterior pillar** (palatopharyngeal arch, contains palatopharyngeus).
- Note: the slide describes the nasopharynx lining as "respiratory ciliated stratified squamous"; the standard description is ciliated pseudostratified columnar (respiratory) epithelium.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Nasopharynx: choanae in front, adenoid behind, Rosenmuller fossa and Eustachian opening on the sides.
- Oropharynx C2-C3; laryngopharynx C3-C6.
- Anterior pillar = palatoglossal; posterior pillar = palatopharyngeal.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name the parts of the pharynx and give the boundaries of the nasopharynx.</summary>

**Answer:** Nasopharynx, oropharynx, laryngopharynx. Nasopharynx: skull base above, soft palate below, choanae in front, nasopharyngeal tonsil behind.

**Why:** Slides 18-19.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the contents of the nasopharynx and locate the fossa of Rosenmuller.</summary>

**Answer:** Adenoid, Eustachian tube opening, fossa of Rosenmuller, mucus-secreting mucosa. The fossa lies on the lateral wall behind and above the torus tubarius.

**Why:** Slide 20.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the palatine tonsil and its pillars.</summary>

**Answer:** It lies in the tonsillar fossa between the anterior pillar (palatoglossal arch with palatoglossus) and the posterior pillar (palatopharyngeal arch with palatopharyngeus).

**Why:** Slide 24.
</details>

## 5. Pharyngeal muscles, vessels and nerves

- **Constrictors** (superior, middle, inferior): **overlap, open anteriorly, attached posteriorly to the median raphe.** **Longitudinal:** **stylopharyngeus, salpingopharyngeus, palatopharyngeus.**

| Gap | Structures passing |
|---|---|
| **Above superior constrictor** (sinus of Morgagni) | Auditory tube, tensor and levator veli palatini, ascending palatine vessels (and ascending pharyngeal artery) |
| **Between superior and middle** | **Stylopharyngeus, glossopharyngeal nerve (IX)** |
| **Between middle and inferior** | **Internal laryngeal nerve**, superior laryngeal vessels |
| **Below inferior constrictor** | ⭐ **Recurrent laryngeal nerve**, inferior laryngeal vessels |

| Muscle | Origin | Nerve | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stylopharyngeus** | Medial side of styloid process base | **Glossopharyngeal** | Elevates pharynx |
| **Salpingopharyngeus** | Pharyngeal end of pharyngotympanic tube | **Vagus** | Elevates pharynx |
| **Palatopharyngeus** | Upper surface of palatine aponeurosis | **Vagus** | Elevates pharynx; closes oropharyngeal isthmus |

- **Arteries:** superior thyroid, ascending pharyngeal, ascending and descending palatine, branches of lingual, facial and maxillary (**external carotid**). **Veins:** pharyngeal venous plexus → **internal jugular.**
- **Motor:** all by **vagus (X)** except **stylopharyngeus (IX).** **Sensory:** nasopharynx **V2**, oropharynx **IX**, laryngopharynx **X.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Gaps between constrictors: above superior (auditory tube), superior-middle (IX), middle-inferior (internal laryngeal), below inferior (recurrent laryngeal).
- Only stylopharyngeus is supplied by IX; the rest by X.
- Sensory: V2, IX, X from top to bottom.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the structures passing through each gap of the pharyngeal constrictors.</summary>

**Answer:** Above the superior: auditory tube, tensor/levator palatini, ascending palatine vessels. Superior-middle: stylopharyngeus and IX. Middle-inferior: internal laryngeal nerve and superior laryngeal vessels. Below the inferior: recurrent laryngeal nerve and inferior laryngeal vessels.

**Why:** Slides 27-29.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Tabulate the origin, nerve and function of the three longitudinal pharyngeal muscles.</summary>

**Answer:** Stylopharyngeus: styloid process, IX, elevates. Salpingopharyngeus: pharyngotympanic tube, X, elevates. Palatopharyngeus: palatine aponeurosis, X, elevates and closes the oropharyngeal isthmus.

**Why:** Slide 31.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give the motor and sensory innervation of the pharynx.</summary>

**Answer:** Motor: vagus for all muscles except stylopharyngeus (glossopharyngeal). Sensory: nasopharynx V2, oropharynx IX, laryngopharynx X.

**Why:** Slide 33.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Greater and third** occipital nerves pierce trapezius, not the lesser.
- **Third part** of the vertebral artery is in the suboccipital triangle.
- **Danger space** (alar ↔ prevertebral) ≠ **retropharyngeal space** (buccopharyngeal ↔ alar).
- **Jugulodigastric = tonsils; jugulo-omohyoid = tongue.**
- **Stylopharyngeus = IX;** all other pharyngeal muscles = **X.**
- **Recurrent laryngeal** passes **below** the inferior constrictor; **internal laryngeal** between middle and inferior.

1. Cutaneous nerves medial → lateral: third, greater, lesser occipital.
2. Suboccipital triangle: oblique inferior, oblique superior, rectus posterior; floor = posterior atlanto-occipital membrane + posterior arch of atlas.
3. Contents: suboccipital nerve (C1), vertebral artery (3rd part), suboccipital venous plexus.
4. Suboccipital muscles extend at the atlanto-occipital joint, rotate at the atlanto-axial joints.
5. Cisternal puncture: below the external occipital protuberance into the cisterna magna.
6. Danger space → posterior mediastinum → mediastinitis.
7. Five superficial node groups; occipital/mastoid → superficial cervical, rest → deep cervical.
8. Deep cervical chain along the IJV; jugulodigastric (tonsil), jugulo-omohyoid (tongue).
9. Pharynx parts: naso- (choanae), oro- (C2-C3), laryngo- (C3-C6).
10. Rosenmuller fossa is behind the torus tubarius.
11. Gaps: auditory tube / IX / internal laryngeal / recurrent laryngeal.
12. Motor X except stylopharyngeus (IX); sensory V2, IX, X.
