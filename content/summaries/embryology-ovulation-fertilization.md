## 1. The ovarian cycle and hormonal control

- ⭐ **Monthly cycle from puberty**, controlled by the **hypothalamus (GnRH)** → **anterior pituitary** → **gonadotropins: FSH and LH.**
- **15-20 primary follicles** grow per cycle under **FSH**; normally **one** becomes the **Graafian follicle** and releases **one oocyte**; the others become atretic (**corpus atreticum**).
- Early growth of primordial → primary (preantral) follicles is **FSH-independent**; **FSH recruits** primary follicles into **secondary (antral)** follicles.
- **Estrogens** from **theca interna and granulosa cells** cause: **proliferative endometrium; thin cervical mucus; stimulation of the pituitary to secrete LH.**
- **Midcycle LH surge:** **oocytes complete meiosis I and begin meiosis II; follicular rupture and ovulation; progesterone production (luteinization).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- GnRH → FSH and LH → follicle growth and estrogen.
- FSH recruits antral follicles; LH surge triggers ovulation.

</div>

## 2. Ovulation and the corpus luteum

- ⭐ **Ovulation** = rupture of the **Graafian follicle**; the oocyte is a **secondary oocyte** surrounded by the **zona pellucida** and **corona radiata**, with a **polar body.**
- **Clinical:** **middle pain** and a **rise in body temperature.**
- **Corpus luteum:** remaining **granulosa cells + theca interna cells → lutean cells**, secreting **estrogens and progesterone.**
- **No fertilization:** degenerates **9 days** after ovulation → **corpus albicans.**
- **Fertilization:** **hCG** from the **syncytiotrophoblast** prevents degeneration → **corpus luteum of pregnancy.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Corpus luteum lives 9 days without pregnancy.
- hCG maintains it in pregnancy.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the oocyte at ovulation.</summary>

**Answer:** A secondary oocyte surrounded by the zona pellucida and the corona radiata (cumulus oophorus), with a polar body within the zona.

**Why:** Slide "Ovulation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the fate of the corpus luteum?</summary>

**Answer:** Without fertilization it degenerates after 9 days into the corpus albicans; with fertilization hCG from the syncytiotrophoblast preserves it as the corpus luteum of pregnancy.

**Why:** Slide "Corpus Luteum".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the clinical signs of ovulation?</summary>

**Answer:** Slight middle pain in some women and a rise in body temperature.

**Why:** Slide "Clinical correlate".

## 3. Fertilization: preparation of the sperm

- ⭐ **Fertilization** = fusion of male and female gametes in the **ampulla of the uterine tube.**
- Sperm must undergo **capacitation** and the **acrosome reaction.**
- **Capacitation:** conditioning in the female tract (**~7 hours**, mainly the uterine tube): a **glycoprotein coat and seminal plasma proteins** are removed from the membrane over the acrosome so the sperm can **pass through the corona cells** and undergo the acrosome reaction.
- **Acrosome reaction:** after binding the **zona pellucida**, **induced by zona proteins**; releases **acrosin and trypsin-like enzymes** to penetrate the zona.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Capacitation ~7 h removes the glycoprotein coat.
- Acrosome reaction releases acrosin.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is capacitation?</summary>

**Answer:** A conditioning period of about 7 hours in the female genital tract (mainly the uterine tube) in which a glycoprotein coat and seminal plasma proteins are removed from the sperm membrane over the acrosome.

**Why:** Slide "Capacitation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the acrosome reaction?</summary>

**Answer:** After binding to the zona pellucida, zona proteins induce the release of acrosin and trypsin-like enzymes needed to penetrate the zona.

**Why:** Slide "The acrosome reaction".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Where does fertilization occur?</summary>

**Answer:** In the ampullary region of the uterine tube.

**Why:** Slide "Fertilization".
</details>

## 4. Phases of fertilization and the egg's response

- ⭐ **Phase 1:** penetration of the **corona radiata** (of **200-300 million** sperm only **300-500** reach the site; **one** fertilizes).
- **Phase 2:** penetration of the **zona pellucida** (a glycoprotein shell that **facilitates and maintains sperm binding and induces the acrosome reaction**); contact with the oocyte membrane triggers **cortical granule** release.
- **Phase 3:** **fusion of sperm and oocyte membranes** (**integrins** on the oocyte and **disintegrins** on the sperm); **head and tail enter**, the **plasma membrane stays** on the surface.
- **Egg responses:** **cortical and zona reactions** (membrane becomes impenetrable, zona altered), **resumption of meiosis II**, **metabolic activation.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Three phases: corona, zona, membrane fusion.
- Cortical reaction blocks polyspermy.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the phases of fertilization.</summary>

**Answer:** Penetration of the corona radiata, penetration of the zona pellucida and fusion of the oocyte and sperm membranes.

**Why:** Slide "Phases of fertilization".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How does the egg block other sperm?</summary>

**Answer:** Cortical granule enzymes make the oocyte membrane impenetrable and alter the zona pellucida so it no longer binds or admits sperm.

**Why:** Slide 15.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the three responses of the egg?</summary>

**Answer:** Cortical and zona reactions, resumption of the second meiotic division and metabolic activation.

**Why:** Slide 15.
</details>

## 5. Results of fertilization

1. ⭐ **Restoration of the diploid number** (half from each parent): a **new chromosome combination.**
2. **Sex determination:** **X sperm → XX (female)**; **Y sperm → XY (male).**
3. **Initiation of cleavage.** Without fertilization the oocyte **degenerates ~24 h after ovulation.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Diploid number restored; sex fixed at fertilization; cleavage starts.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the main results of fertilization.</summary>

**Answer:** Restoration of the diploid chromosome number, determination of the sex of the new individual and initiation of cleavage.

**Why:** Slide "The main results of fertilization".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What determines the sex?</summary>

**Answer:** The sperm: an X-carrying sperm gives a female (XX) and a Y-carrying sperm a male (XY).

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What happens without fertilization?</summary>

**Answer:** The oocyte usually degenerates about 24 hours after ovulation.

**Why:** Slide 16.

## 6. Infertility, ART and cloning

- ⭐ **Infertility affects 14-15% of couples.**
- **Male:** insufficient sperm or poor motility; ejaculate **2-6 mL**, up to **100 million/mL**; **20 million/mL or 50 million per ejaculate** are usually fertile.
- **Female:** **occluded tubes (most commonly pelvic inflammatory disease), hostile cervical mucus, immunity to sperm, absence of ovulation.**
- **IVF:** oocytes aspirated just before ovulation, placed in culture and sperm added at once.
- **ICSI:** a **single sperm injected into the egg cytoplasm**; used in **oligozoospermia or azoospermia.**
- In both, embryos are monitored to the **eight-cell stage** then **placed in the uterus.**
- **Cloning:** the **ovum nucleus is replaced by a somatic cell nucleus (46 chromosomes)**, then transplanted into the uterus.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Fertile threshold: 20 million sperm/mL.
- IVF = sperm added to the egg; ICSI = one sperm injected.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the causes of infertility.</summary>

**Answer:** Male: too few sperm or poor motility; female: occluded uterine tubes (most commonly pelvic inflammatory disease), hostile cervical mucus, immunity to sperm and absence of ovulation.

**Why:** Slide "Infertility".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare IVF and ICSI.</summary>

**Answer:** In IVF aspirated oocytes are placed in culture and sperm are added; in ICSI a single sperm is injected into the egg cytoplasm (used in oligozoospermia or azoospermia); in both, embryos are monitored to the eight-cell stage and transferred to the uterus.

**Why:** Slide "Assisted reproductive technology".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is cloning?</summary>

**Answer:** Replacing the ovum nucleus with the nucleus of a somatic cell (46 chromosomes) and transplanting it into the uterus.

**Why:** Slide "Cloning".
</details>
