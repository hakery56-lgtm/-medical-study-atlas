## 1. Rheumatoid arthritis: definition and clinical features

- ⭐ **RA:** a **chronic progressive inflammatory autoimmune disorder**; persistent inflammation initially affects **peripheral joints** and produces a **non-suppurative proliferative and inflammatory synovitis**.
- Usually starts as an **insidious symmetrical arthritis** with an **unpredictable, variable course**; disability can be minimized by **early, prompt treatment**.
- Can progress to **destruction of articular cartilage** and sometimes **ankylosis (adhesion)**. **Extra-articular lesions:** **skin, heart, blood vessels, lungs**.
- **Epidemiology:** prevalence about **1%** (USA); **female : male = 3 : 1**; any age, **peak in the 3rd–5th decades**.
- **Articular manifestation:** **symmetric arthritis of the small joints of the hands and feet**; **pain and swelling aggravated by movement**; **morning stiffness ≥ 1 hour**.

**Key points**
- Symmetric small-joint arthritis, F:M 3:1, morning stiffness ≥ 1 h.
- Extra-articular: skin, heart, blood vessels, lungs.

## 2. Pathogenesis of RA

- **Genetic predisposition + environmental factors** drive development, progression and chronicity (about **50%** of risk is **inherited**).
- Damage is mediated by **antibodies against self-antigens** and **cytokine inflammation** mainly from **CD4+ T cells**; macrophages, synovial cells, chondrocytes and fibroblasts are activated, producing **proteolytic enzymes and cytokines** that destroy **cartilage**, while increased **osteoclast activity** destroys **bone**.

| Mediator | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **IFN-γ** | TH1 cells | Activates macrophages and synovial cells |
| **IL-17** | TH17 cells | Recruits neutrophils and monocytes |
| **RANKL** | Activated T cells | Stimulates osteoclasts → bone resorption |
| **TNF and IL-1** | Macrophages | Synovial cells secrete proteases → destroy hyaline cartilage |

- **TNF** is the cytokine **most firmly implicated**; **TNF antagonists** are effective therapy.
- The synovium has **germinal centers with secondary follicles** and abundant **plasma cells**; autoantibodies called **rheumatoid factor** may form **immune complexes** in joints, but are **not present in all** RA patients and can occur in people without RA.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- TH1 → IFN-γ; TH17 → IL-17; T cells → RANKL; macrophages → TNF and IL-1.
- TNF is the key cytokine; TNF antagonists work.
- Rheumatoid factor is neither universal nor exclusive.

</div>

## 3. Morphology of RA

- **Gross:** synovium **edematous, thickened, hyperplastic**, with **delicate bulbous villi**.
- **Histology:** (1) **synovial cell hyperplasia**; (2) **dense infiltrate** of CD4+ T cells, B cells, plasma cells, dendritic cells, macrophages; (3) **angiogenesis**; (4) **neutrophils and fibrin** on joint surfaces; (5) **osteoclastic activity** → **periarticular erosions**.
- **Pannus:** edematous synovium, inflammatory cells, granulation tissue and fibroblasts growing over cartilage → **cartilage erosion**. Joint outcomes: **fibrous and bony ankylosis**.
- **Rheumatoid nodules:** infrequent; **subcutaneous** (forearm, elbows, occiput, lumbosacral area); microscopically a **necrotizing granuloma-like** lesion with **central necrosis rimmed by palisaded histiocytes**.
- **Lungs (rare):** rheumatoid nodules, interstitial lung disease.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pannus erodes cartilage; osteoclasts cause periarticular erosions.
- Nodule = central necrosis + palisaded histiocytes, subcutaneous at pressure and extensor sites.

</div>

## 4. Juvenile idiopathic arthritis and reactive arthritis

| Feature | Adult RA | JIA |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Any age (peak 3rd–5th decades) | **Before age 16**, lasting **≥ 6 weeks** |
| Pattern | Small joints, symmetric | **Oligoarthritis** more common; **large joints** more often |
| Systemic disease | Less | **More frequent** |
| Nodules / RF | Present in some | **Usually absent** |
| ANA | — | **Common** |
| Mechanism | TH1/TH17 cytokines | Unknown, similar; **TH1 and TH17** |
| Outcome | — | Variable; only **~10%** serious disability |

- **Reactive arthritis:** **triad = arthritis + nongonococcal urethritis or cervicitis + conjunctivitis**; **men in 20s–30s**; **> 80% HLA-B27 positive**.
- Probably **autoimmune reaction after infection**: genitourinary (**Chlamydia**) or GI (**Shigella, Salmonella, Yersinia, Campylobacter**).
- Within weeks: **low back pain**; **ankles, knees, feet**, often **asymmetric**; severe chronic disease → **spine disease like ankylosing spondylitis**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- JIA: < 16 years, ≥ 6 weeks, oligoarthritis, ANA+, RF usually absent.
- Reactive arthritis: HLA-B27, triad, after Chlamydia or GI infection.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How does JIA differ from adult RA?</summary>

**Answer:** In JIA oligoarthritis is more common, systemic disease is more frequent, large joints are affected more than small joints, rheumatoid nodules and rheumatoid factor are usually absent, and ANA seropositivity is common.

**Why:** Slide "Juvenile idiopathic arthritis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. State the triad and associations of reactive arthritis.</summary>

**Answer:** Arthritis, nongonococcal urethritis or cervicitis, and conjunctivitis; usually men in their 20s or 30s, over 80% HLA-B27 positive.

**Why:** Slide "Reactive arthritis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What triggers reactive arthritis and how does it present?</summary>

**Why:** Slide "Reactive arthritis".
</details>

## 5. Infectious (suppurative) arthritis

- Joints become infected by **hematogenous dissemination**, **direct inoculation through the skin**, or **contiguous spread** from a soft tissue abscess or osteomyelitis.
- Potentially serious: **rapid, permanent joint destruction**.
- **Suppurative arthritis:** bacteria usually enter **from distant sites by the blood**. **H. influenzae** predominates **< 2 years**; **S. aureus** in **older children and adults**.
- **Classic presentation:** **sudden acutely painful, warm, swollen joint with restricted range of motion**; **fever, leukocytosis, raised ESR**.
- **Drug users:** **axial joints** more often involved.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- H. influenzae < 2 years; S. aureus otherwise.
- Hot, swollen, painful joint + fever + leukocytosis + high ESR.

</div>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **RA = symmetric small joints of the hands and feet; JIA = oligoarthritis of large joints.**
- **RF is neither universal in RA nor specific to it; JIA is usually RF-negative and often ANA-positive.**
- **TNF is the cytokine most firmly implicated; IL-17 recruits neutrophils/monocytes; RANKL drives osteoclasts.**
- **Pannus erodes cartilage; rheumatoid nodule = necrosis with palisaded histiocytes.**
- **Reactive arthritis: HLA-B27, triad, asymmetric lower-limb joints.**
- **H. influenzae < 2 years; S. aureus in older children and adults.**
