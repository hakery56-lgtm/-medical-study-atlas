## 1. Classification and choline esters

- ⭐ **Cholinomimetic (parasympathomimetic, cholinergic agonist)** drugs = effects **similar to ACh**; **anticholinergic (parasympatholytic, cholinergic antagonist)** drugs = effects **opposite to ACh.**
- **Cholinomimetics:** **Direct-acting** (act on ACh receptors: **choline esters, alkaloids**) and **Indirect-acting** (**inhibit AChE: reversible, irreversible**).

### Choline esters
- **Acetylcholine and analogues: methacholine, carbachol, bethanechol.** **ACh is not a systemic drug** (**very short t½ — rapidly hydrolyzed; very wide effects**); it is used **topically to induce miosis during eye surgery.**
- **All choline esters are quaternary amines (positively charged) and cannot cross the BBB.**

| Choline ester | Susceptibility to cholinesterase | Muscarinic action | Nicotinic action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acetylcholine** | **++++** | **+++** | **+++** |
| **Methacholine** | **+** | **++++** | **None** |
| **Carbachol** | **Negligible** | **++** | **+++** |
| **Bethanechol** | **Negligible** | **++** | **None** |

| Drug | Key facts and uses |
|---|---|
| **Bethanechol** | **Muscarinic-selective, not hydrolyzed by cholinesterases; acts mainly on M3, little effect on the heart.** **Uses:** postoperative **GIT and bladder stimulation** (abdominal distention, urinary retention), bladder/GIT hypotonia. **Side effects:** sweating, salivation, lacrimation, bronchial secretion, flushing. **Contraindications: asthma, severe cardiac disease.** |
| **Methacholine** | **Muscarinic-selective but hydrolyzed by cholinesterases → short action.** **Uses:** slowing the heart in **paroxysmal atrial tachycardia**; **diagnosis of asthma (methacholine challenge test).** |
| **Carbachol** | **Poorly hydrolyzed but non-selective; can release adrenaline from the adrenal medulla.** **Topical miotic to lower IOP** (pilocarpine preferred). |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Direct-acting (choline esters, alkaloids) vs indirect-acting (anticholinesterases).
- ACh: short t½, wide effects, topical for miosis in eye surgery; all choline esters are quaternary → no CNS entry.
- Bethanechol: M3, GIT/bladder, contraindicated in asthma/cardiac disease; methacholine: asthma challenge test; carbachol: non-selective, topical miotic.

</div>

## 2. Alkaloids: muscarine and pilocarpine, cevimeline

- **Muscarine:** **toxic; experimental tool only.**
- **Pilocarpine:** from **Pilocarpus** (South America); **tertiary amine (crosses the BBB at therapeutic doses); stable to AChE hydrolysis; less potent than ACh; muscarinic activity; used mainly in ophthalmology.**
  - **Eye:** **rapid miosis** (circular iris muscle) and **ciliary muscle contraction (near-vision accommodation)** → opens the **trabecular meshwork around Schlemm's canal** → ↑ aqueous drainage → **immediate fall in IOP, lasting up to 8 hours.**
  - **Other effects:** one of the **most potent stimulators of secretions (sweat, tears, saliva).**
  - **Uses:** **glaucoma — drug of choice in the emergency lowering of IOP in both narrow-angle (closed) and wide-angle (open) glaucoma (topical eye drops).** **Xerostomia** (oral) after **head and neck irradiation** or in **Sjögren's syndrome.**
  - **Adverse effects:** **CNS disturbances, profuse sweating, salivation, lacrimation.**
- **Cevimeline:** newer cholinergic agonist for **xerostomia.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pilocarpine: tertiary amine, muscarinic, stable to AChE; miosis + accommodation → ↓ IOP (up to 8 h).
- Drug of choice for emergency IOP lowering in both closed- and open-angle glaucoma; oral for xerostomia.
- Side effects: CNS, sweating, salivation, lacrimation; cevimeline = newer xerostomia drug.

</div>

**Answer:** It causes rapid miosis (circular iris muscle contraction) and ciliary muscle contraction (near vision), which opens the trabecular meshwork around Schlemm's canal, increases aqueous humor drainage and lowers IOP immediately for up to 8 hours.

**Why:** Slide "Pilocarpine — pharmacological effects".

## 3. Cholinesterase inhibitors and reversible anticholinesterases

- **Anticholinesterases inhibit AChE → ACh accumulates at muscarinic and nicotinic sites → intensified ACh effects.** Inhibition is **reversible or irreversible.**
- **Two cholinesterases:** **acetylcholinesterase (neural tissue and RBCs)** and **butyrylcholinesterase = pseudocholinesterase (plasma, liver, skin, brain, GIT).** Anticholinesterases inhibit **both**; **measuring RBC or plasma cholinesterase activity** is important in diagnosing **organophosphorous poisoning.**
- **Reversible:** binding is reversible; **duration of binding = duration of action**; after dissociation the enzyme is **intact** and hydrolyzes ACh again.

| Drug | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Physostigmine** | **Natural tertiary amine (Physostigma venenosum), carbamic acid ester**; **carbamylated intermediate** → **muscarinic + nicotinic + CNS effects (crosses BBB)**; **duration 2–4 h.** **Uses:** topical glaucoma (pilocarpine preferred), **postoperative GIT/bladder**, **atropine poisoning (both enter the CNS)** and overdose of central anticholinergics, **Alzheimer dementia** (rivastigmine and tacrine preferred: fewer side effects). **Adverse effects:** **CNS convulsions, bradycardia/↓ cardiac output (muscarinic), skeletal paralysis (nicotinic).** **Not used in myasthenia gravis** (CNS effects). |
| **Neostigmine** | **Synthetic carbamic acid ester; quaternary amine → polar, no CNS entry; shorter action (1–2 h).** **Indications:** **myasthenia gravis (nicotinic)**, **termination of curare and other non-depolarizing blockers after anesthesia (nicotinic)**, **atony of bladder and GIT (muscarinic).** **Adverse effects:** salivation, flushing, ↓ BP, nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhea, bronchospasm; **atropine blocks the muscarinic effects** when the nicotinic effect is wanted. |
| **Pyridostigmine** | **Chronic management of myasthenia gravis; duration 3–6 h** (longer than neostigmine); similar adverse effects. **Used by the US army in Gulf War II as prophylaxis against nerve gases; thought responsible for the Gulf War syndrome** (as stated on the slide). |
| **Edrophonium** | **More rapidly absorbed, very short action (10–20 min).** **Uses:** **diagnosis of myasthenia gravis** (i.v. → rapid ↑ in muscle strength) and **differentiating myasthenic vs cholinergic crisis.** |

### Myasthenia gravis and the two crises
- **Myasthenia gravis:** **autoimmune; antibodies against the NMJ → ↓ nAChRs and ↓ ACh vesicles**; weakness starts in the **face and neck**, later **limbs and intercostal muscles.**
- **Myasthenic crisis** = sudden worsening (severe weakness). **Cholinergic crisis** = **drug overdose**; ACh accumulates at the NMJ → **persistent depolarization → weakness/paralysis.**
- **Edrophonium i.v.: patient gets BETTER → myasthenic crisis; gets WORSE → cholinergic crisis.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Physostigmine: tertiary, CNS effects, 2–4 h; atropine poisoning; not for myasthenia.
- Neostigmine: quaternary, 1–2 h; myasthenia, curare reversal, atony; atropine blocks muscarinic side effects.
- Pyridostigmine 3–6 h (chronic MG); edrophonium 10–20 min (diagnosis, crisis differentiation: better = myasthenic, worse = cholinergic).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Physostigmine is a natural tertiary amine that crosses the BBB (CNS effects, 2–4 h), used in atropine poisoning, glaucoma and postoperative atony, and not in myasthenia gravis; neostigmine is a synthetic quaternary amine that does not enter the CNS (1–2 h), used in myasthenia gravis, reversal of non-depolarizing blockers and atony of bladder/GIT.

**Why:** Slides "Physostigmine" and "Neostigmine".
</details>

**Answer:** An autoimmune disease with antibodies against the NMJ that reduce nAChRs and ACh vesicles, causing weakness starting in the face and neck; treated chronically with pyridostigmine or neostigmine, and diagnosed with edrophonium.

**Why:** Slides "Neostigmine", "Pyridostigmine", "Edrophonium".
</details>

**Answer:** Give edrophonium i.v.: if the patient improves it is a myasthenic crisis; if the patient worsens it is a cholinergic crisis (drug overdose with persistent depolarization at the NMJ).

## 4. Irreversible anticholinesterases (organophosphates)

- **Organophosphorous compounds bind COVALENTLY to AChE → phosphorylated enzyme → long-lasting accumulation of ACh at all sites.** **Mechanism:** the phosphate group binds the **serine-OH at the active site**; the enzyme is **permanently inactivated** (new enzyme must be synthesized).
- **Examples:** **military nerve agents: Tabun (GA), Sarin (GB), Soman (GD) = gases; VX = thick liquid.** **Insecticides: parathion, malathion.** **Drugs:** **ecothiophate (topical glaucoma, never systemic)**, **isoflurophate (glaucoma)**, **malathion (scabies)**, **metrifonate (bilharziasis).**
- **Aging:** the phosphorylated enzyme slowly **loses an alkyl (ethyl) group**; then **reactivators such as pralidoxime can no longer break the bond.**

### Toxicity
| Type | Manifestations |
|---|---|
| **Muscarinic (DUMBLES)** | **Diarrhea, Urination, Miosis, Bronchospasm, Bradycardia, Lacrimation, Emesis, Salivation, Sweating** |
| **Nicotinic** | **Muscle fatigue and weakness, twitching, fasciculation, tremors, paralysis** |
| **CNS** | **Ataxia, confusion, convulsions, depressed respiration and cardiovascular function** |

- **In severe acute exposure death can occur within a few minutes.**

### Treatment
- **Initial:** **maintain respiration; suction bronchial secretions; control convulsions; rapid decontamination (GIT, skin and eyes).**
- **Antidotes: atropine and pralidoxime.** **Atropine (antimuscarinic) reverses the muscarinic manifestations only**, so treatment continues with **pralidoxime (2-PAM)**, the **specific antidote that reactivates the enzyme** and improves **nicotinic and CNS manifestations.** **Give pralidoxime EARLY (within the first 16 hours); after 24 hours aging causes permanent inactivation.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Organophosphates: covalent phosphorylation of serine-OH → irreversible; aging prevents reactivation.
- Toxicity = muscarinic (DUMBLES) + nicotinic + CNS; severe cases kill in minutes.
- Treatment: ABC + decontamination; atropine (muscarinic only) + pralidoxime (early, before aging).

</div>

- **DUMBLES = muscarinic signs.**
