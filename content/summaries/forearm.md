# Anatomy of the Forearm — Exam Night Review

Dr. Ahmed Al-Baghdadi's forearm lecture: **the bones of the forearm and hand, the deep fascia, the anterior (flexor) and posterior (extensor) compartments with their nerves and vessels, the distal radioulnar and wrist joints, and the specific areas (front of the wrist, carpal tunnel, anatomical snuffbox).** After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer).

---

## 1. Bones of the forearm and hand

### Distal humerus
- **Medial epicondyle:** origin of the **superficial flexors** (common flexor origin, CFO); **grooved posteriorly by the ulnar nerve**.
- **Lateral epicondyle:** origin of the **superficial extensors** (common extensor origin, CEO).
- Also: trochlea, capitulum, and the **coronoid, radial and olecranon fossae**.

### Ulna
- **Proximal:** articulates with the trochlea. **Olecranon** (separated from skin by the **olecranon bursa**); **coronoid process** = distal attachment of **brachialis**; **radial notch** = facet for the radial head.
- **Distal:** small **head** (articulates with the **ulnar notch of the radius** = distal radioulnar joint) and **styloid process**.

### Radius
- Lies **laterally**, **pivots around the ulna**. Articulates in **four places**: **elbow** (head with capitulum), **proximal radioulnar**, **wrist**, **distal radioulnar**.
- **Head** (encircled by the annular ligament), **neck** (related to the **deep radial nerve** as it pierces supinator), **tuberosity** (**biceps** tendon).
- **Shaft** is triangular; **pronator teres** inserts in the middle of the lateral surface.
- **Distal end:** styloid process, **ulnar notch**, two facets (**scaphoid and lunate** = wrist joint). **Dorsal tubercle of Lister** = pulley for **EPL**.

### Hand (27 bones)
- **8 carpals** (two rows), **5 metacarpals**, **14 phalanges** (thumb has 2).
- **Proximal row (lateral → medial):** **Scaphoid, Lunate, Triquetrum, Pisiform**.
- **Distal row (lateral → medial):** **Trapezium, Trapezoid, Capitate, Hamate** (palmar **hook**).
- **Scaphoid and lunate** articulate with the radius. The **ulna is separated from the carpus by the articular disc** (triangular ligament).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Medial epicondyle = flexors + ulnar nerve groove; lateral epicondyle = extensors.
- Radius articulates in 4 places; ulna head meets the ulnar notch of the radius.
- Proximal row: Scaphoid, Lunate, Triquetrum, Pisiform. Distal row: Trapezium, Trapezoid, Capitate, Hamate.
- Only scaphoid + lunate touch the radius; the disc separates the ulna from the carpus.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the four articulations of the radius.</summary>

**Answer:** (1) The elbow joint — head of the radius with the capitulum; (2) the proximal radioulnar joint — radial head with the radial notch of the ulna; (3) the wrist joint — distal end of the radius with the carpal bones; (4) the distal radioulnar joint — ulnar notch of the radius with the head of the ulna.

**Why:** A classic list question.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Name the carpal bones in each row from lateral to medial.</summary>

**Answer:** Proximal row: scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform. Distal row: trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate (with its hook).

**Why:** Order matters in the practical exam.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which structures are related to the humeral epicondyles and the radial neck?</summary>

**Answer:** The medial epicondyle gives origin to the superficial flexors and is grooved by the ulnar nerve; the lateral epicondyle gives origin to the superficial extensors; the neck of the radius is related to the deep radial nerve as it pierces the supinator.

**Why:** Links bony landmarks to nerves.
</details>

## 2. Fascia and the retinacula

- The forearm is enclosed in the **antebrachial (deep) fascia**; **intermuscular septa** and the **interosseous membrane** divide it into an **anterior (flexor)** and a **posterior (extensor)** compartment, each with its **own muscles, nerves and blood supply**.
- Near the wrist the deep fascia thickens into the **flexor retinaculum** and **extensor retinaculum**, which **keep the tendons in place** during hand movements.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Two compartments, separated by the interosseous membrane and septa.
- Flexor and extensor retinacula hold the tendons at the wrist.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What divides the forearm into compartments?</summary>

**Answer:** The deep (antebrachial) fascia, with the interosseous membrane and the intermuscular septa, divides the forearm into anterior and posterior compartments, each with its own muscles, nerves and blood supply.

**Why:** Straight from the lecture.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the retinacula and what is their function?</summary>

**Answer:** Localized thickenings of the deep fascia near the wrist — the flexor and extensor retinacula — that retain the tendons in position during hand movements.

**Why:** Prevents bowstringing of tendons.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why does each compartment behave as a separate unit?</summary>

**Answer:** Because each is enclosed by fascia, the interosseous membrane and the septa, and has its own muscles, nerve supply and blood supply.

**Why:** Explains compartment-based patterns of nerve injury.
</details>

## 3. Anterior (flexor) compartment — muscles

### Superficial layer (5) — all from the **common flexor origin**, all supplied by the **median nerve except FCU**
| Muscle | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Pronator teres** (humeral + ulnar heads) | Pronator tuberosity (middle of lateral radius) | **Pronation** (main), flexes elbow |
| **Flexor carpi radialis** | Bases of **2nd & 3rd metacarpals** | Flexion + radial deviation of wrist |
| **Palmaris longus** (may be absent) | **Palmar aponeurosis** | Flexes wrist; tendon used as **graft** |
| **Flexor digitorum superficialis** (2 heads, 3 bones) | **Sides of middle phalanges** of medial 4 fingers | Flexes PIP, MCP, wrist |
| **Flexor carpi ulnaris** (humeral + ulnar heads) | **Pisiform** and base of **5th metacarpal** | Flexion + ulnar deviation (**ulnar nerve**) |

### Deep layer (3) — all supplied by the **anterior interosseous nerve (AIN)**
| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flexor digitorum profundus** | Upper ⅔ of anterior/medial ulna + interosseous membrane | **Bases of terminal phalanges** of medial 4 fingers | Flexes wrist and all joints of the 4 fingers |
| **Flexor pollicis longus** | Upper ⅔ of anterior radius + membrane | Base of **terminal phalanx of thumb** | Flexes thumb |
| **Pronator quadratus** | Lower ¼ of ulna | Lower ¼ of radius | **Pronation** |

- **FDP has a dual supply:** lateral half by **AIN**, **medial half by the ulnar nerve**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Superficial 5: PT, FCR, PL, FDS, FCU (CFO). All median except **FCU** (ulnar).
- Deep 3: FDP, FPL, PQ — AIN (FDP medial half = ulnar).
- FDS inserts on the middle phalanges, FDP on the terminal phalanges.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name the superficial flexors, their common origin and their nerve supply.</summary>

**Answer:** Pronator teres, flexor carpi radialis, palmaris longus, flexor digitorum superficialis and flexor carpi ulnaris. They share the common flexor origin from the medial epicondyle and are supplied by the median nerve except FCU, which is supplied by the ulnar nerve.

**Why:** Origin and nerve supply are repeated in every exam.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare the insertions of FDS and FDP.</summary>

**Answer:** FDS splits into four tendons inserting on the sides of the middle phalanges of the medial four fingers (flexing the PIP joints); FDP passes through the split of FDS and inserts on the bases of the terminal phalanges (flexing the DIP joints as well).

**Why:** Explains tests for isolated FDS/FDP function.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which muscle has a double nerve supply and which flexor tendon is used as a graft?</summary>

**Answer:** Flexor digitorum profundus: the lateral half by the anterior interosseous nerve and the medial half by the ulnar nerve. The palmaris longus tendon (absent in some people) is used as a tendon graft.

**Why:** Both are favorite one-liners.
</details>

## 4. Anterior compartment — nerves and vessels

### Median nerve
- **Cubital fossa:** medial to the brachial artery; **descends between the two heads of pronator teres**.
- **Forearm:** runs **between FDS and FDP**; emerges at the lateral border of FDS, becoming superficial near the wrist (covered by the PL tendon).
- **Hand:** passes **deep to the flexor retinaculum** (carpal tunnel).
- **Branches:** all anterior-compartment muscles **except FCU and ½ FDP**; articular branches; **AIN**; **palmar cutaneous branch** (superficial to the retinaculum; skin of the **lateral ⅔ of the palm**).

### Anterior interosseous nerve (AIN)
- **Pure motor**; arises as the median nerve passes between the heads of pronator teres.
- Descends on the interosseous membrane with the anterior interosseous artery, **between FPL and FDP**.
- Supplies **3 muscles (FPL, lateral ½ FDP, PQ)** and **3 joints (wrist, inferior radioulnar, intercarpal)**.

### Ulnar nerve
- Passes **behind the medial epicondyle**, then **between the two heads of FCU**.
- Forearm: **medial to the ulnar artery**, between **FDS and FDP**; enters the hand **superficial to the flexor retinaculum**.
- Supplies **FCU and ½ FDP**; elbow joint; **palmar cutaneous branch** (medial ⅓ of the palm); **dorsal cutaneous branch** (medial ⅓ of dorsum and medial 1½ fingers, except nail beds).

### Arteries
- **Radial artery:** under brachioradialis; at the wrist **between FCR and brachioradialis tendons** (pulse); winds through the **snuffbox**; enters the palm **between the two heads of the first dorsal interosseous**.
- **Ulnar artery:** joined by the ulnar nerve deep to FCU; passes **lateral to the pisiform, superficial to the retinaculum (Guyon canal)**.
- The two arteries anastomose in the **superficial and deep palmar arches**.
- **Common interosseous artery** (from the ulnar) → **anterior** and **posterior interosseous arteries**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Median nerve: between 2 heads of PT, then between FDS and FDP, under the flexor retinaculum.
- AIN: pure motor, 3 muscles + 3 joints, "OK" sign.
- Ulnar nerve: between 2 heads of FCU, medial to the ulnar artery, superficial to the retinaculum.
- Radial pulse: between FCR and brachioradialis tendons.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the course of the median nerve in the forearm.</summary>

**Answer:** In the cubital fossa it lies medial to the brachial artery and descends between the two heads of pronator teres. In the forearm it runs between FDS and FDP, emerges from the lateral side of FDS near the wrist, becomes superficial (partly covered by the palmaris longus tendon) and enters the hand deep to the flexor retinaculum.

**Why:** Course questions are very common.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the type, course and supply of the anterior interosseous nerve?</summary>

**Answer:** It is a pure motor branch of the median nerve arising between the heads of pronator teres, descending on the interosseous membrane with the anterior interosseous artery between FPL and FDP, and ending on the carpal bones. It supplies FPL, the lateral half of FDP and pronator quadratus, plus the wrist, inferior radioulnar and intercarpal joints.

**Why:** "3 muscles and 3 joints."
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the course and branches of the ulnar nerve in the forearm and wrist.</summary>

**Answer:** It passes behind the medial epicondyle, enters the forearm between the two heads of FCU, runs medial to the ulnar artery between FDP and FDS, and enters the hand superficial to the flexor retinaculum. Branches: muscular (FCU and medial half of FDP), articular (elbow), palmar cutaneous (medial third of the palm) and dorsal cutaneous (medial third of the dorsum and medial 1½ fingers).

**Why:** Often asked with its sensory territory.
</details>

## 5. Posterior (extensor) compartment

### Superficial group (7) — all from the **common extensor origin** except brachioradialis and anconeus
| Muscle | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Brachioradialis** (lateral supracondylar ridge) | Lateral radius above the styloid | Flexes elbow in the **midprone position** |
| **ECRL** | Base of **2nd metacarpal** | Extension + radial deviation (abduction) |
| **ECRB** | Base of **3rd metacarpal** | Same as ECRL |
| **Extensor digitorum** | Extensor expansion of **medial 4 fingers** (proximal phalanges) | Extends **MCP** joints |
| **Extensor digiti minimi** | Extensor expansion of little finger | Extends little finger |
| **ECU** | Base of **5th metacarpal** | Extension + ulnar deviation (adduction) |
| **Anconeus** (lateral epicondyle) | Lateral olecranon | Assists triceps in elbow extension |

### Deep group (5) — all from the posterior radius/ulna/membrane **except supinator**
| Muscle | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Supinator** (supinator fossa + crest of ulna) | Upper ⅓ of radius | **Supination** |
| **Abductor pollicis longus** | Base of **1st metacarpal** | Abducts thumb |
| **Extensor pollicis brevis** | Base of **proximal phalanx** of thumb | Extends proximal phalanx |
| **Extensor pollicis longus** | Base of **terminal phalanx** of thumb | Extends all thumb joints |
| **Extensor indicis** | Extensor expansion of index | Extends index finger |

### Nerve and blood supply
- **Posterior interosseous nerve (PIN):** **pure motor**, continuation of the **deep branch of the radial nerve**; passes between the heads of **supinator (arcade of Frohse)**, winds round the radial neck, ends at the **dorsal wrist capsule**. Supplies **all extensors except brachioradialis, ECRL and anconeus** (these come directly from the radial nerve).
- **Posterior interosseous artery** (from the common interosseous): gives the recurrent interosseous artery and joins the **dorsal carpal arch**.

### Extensor expansion
- The **extensor digitorum tendon** expands like a **hood** over the metacarpal head and proximal phalanx; **lumbricals and interossei insert into it**. It **holds the extensor tendons** and lets extensors, lumbricals and interossei **extend the IP joints**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Superficial 7: all from the lateral epicondyle except brachioradialis and anconeus. Deep 5: all from the posterior radius/ulna/membrane except supinator.
- ECRL → 2nd, ECRB → 3rd, ECU → 5th metacarpal.
- Deep extensors pass to thumb and fingers in a **3 : 1** ratio.
- PIN: pure motor; arcade of Frohse; spares brachioradialis, ECRL, anconeus.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the superficial extensors and give their common nerve and origin.</summary>

**Answer:** Brachioradialis, ECRL, ECRB, extensor digitorum, extensor digiti minimi, ECU and anconeus. Except for brachioradialis and anconeus, all arise from the common extensor origin on the lateral epicondyle, and all supply the hand through tendons; they are supplied by the radial nerve and its posterior interosseous branch.

**Why:** Origin + supply summary.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the posterior interosseous nerve.</summary>

**Answer:** A pure motor continuation of the deep branch of the radial nerve. It arises at the radiohumeral joint line, passes between the heads of the supinator (arcade of Frohse), winds around the radial neck within the muscle, reaches the interosseous membrane and ends at the dorsal wrist capsule. It supplies all the extensor muscles except brachioradialis, ECRL and anconeus.

**Why:** Commonly asked with its exceptions.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the extensor expansion and what is its function?</summary>

**Answer:** The extensor digitorum tendon expands over the metacarpal head and proximal phalanx like a hood, receiving the tendons of the lumbricals and interossei. It holds the extensor tendons in place and allows the extensors, lumbricals and interossei to extend the proximal and distal interphalangeal joints.

**Why:** Explains finger extension.
</details>

## 6. Joints: distal radioulnar and wrist

### Distal radioulnar joint
- **Synovial pivot (uniaxial)**; surfaces: **head of ulna** (medially), **ulnar notch of the radius** (laterally), **triangular articular disc** (inferiorly). **Movement: supination and pronation.**
- **Articular disc:** thick triangular fibrocartilage; **apex** to the **styloid process of the ulna**, **base** to the **lower border of the ulnar notch**; **separates the radioulnar joint from the wrist joint**.

### Wrist joint
- **Synovial biaxial (ellipsoid)** joint. Proximal surface: **radius + articular disc**. Distal surface: **scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum**.
- Capsule thickened as **radial and ulnar collateral ligaments**; **anterior and posterior radiocarpal ligaments** force the **carpus to move with the radius** during pronation/supination.
- **Flexion:** FCR, FCU (main), PL. **Extension:** ECRL, ECRB, ECU (main). **Adduction:** FCU + ECU. **Abduction:** ECRL, ECRB, FCR, APL.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- DRUJ = pivot; wrist = ellipsoid, biaxial.
- Disc separates ulna from the carpus.
- Adduction: FCU + ECU; abduction: ECRL, ECRB, FCR, APL.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the distal radioulnar joint.</summary>

**Answer:** A synovial pivot (uniaxial) joint between the head of the ulna, the ulnar notch of the radius and the triangular articular disc. It allows supination and pronation. Its capsule projects upward as a small pouch between radius and ulna.

**Why:** Type, surfaces, movement.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the articular disc and what does it do?</summary>

**Answer:** A thick triangular fibrocartilage whose apex attaches to the ulnar styloid and base to the lower border of the ulnar notch. Its upper concave surface articulates with the ulnar head; its lower convex surface with the lunate/triquetrum. It separates the inferior radioulnar joint from the wrist joint.

**Why:** Explains why the ulna does not reach the carpus.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which muscles produce each movement at the wrist?</summary>

**Answer:** Flexion: FCR and FCU (main) with PL; extension: ECRL, ECRB and ECU; adduction: FCU and ECU; abduction: ECRL, ECRB, FCR and APL; circumduction combines all four.

**Why:** Pairs of muscles that cancel each other produce deviation.
</details>

## 7. Specific areas at the wrist

### Front of the wrist — from lateral to medial
**Radial artery → FCR tendon → median nerve → palmaris longus tendon → FDS → ulnar artery → ulnar nerve → FCU tendon.**
- **Superficial to the flexor retinaculum** (medial → lateral): ulnar nerve, ulnar artery, palmar cutaneous branch of the ulnar nerve, **palmaris longus tendon**, palmar cutaneous branch of the median nerve.

### Carpal tunnel
- **Fibro-osseous canal**: floor = carpal bones, roof = **flexor retinaculum**.
- **Contents:** **median nerve** (most superficial, right behind the retinaculum), **4 FDS tendons** (ulnar bursa), **4 FDP tendons**, **FPL tendon** (radial bursa), recurrent branch of the deep palmar arch.
- **Carpal tunnel syndrome:** compression of the **median nerve**.
- **Guyon canal (ulnar tunnel):** between the **hook of the hamate and the pisiform**; the **ulnar nerve** may be compressed there (e.g., by a ganglion cyst).

### Anatomical snuffbox
- Depression on the posterolateral wrist, more evident with the thumb extended.
- **Anterolateral:** tendons of **APL and EPB**. **Posteromedial:** tendon of **EPL**.
- **Floor:** **radial styloid, scaphoid, trapezium**. **Contents:** radial artery (and the ECRL/ECRB tendons). **Roof:** skin, superficial fascia (beginning of the **cephalic vein**, branches of the **superficial radial nerve**), deep fascia.
- **Clinical:** pain and swelling here = **scaphoid fracture**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Wrist order lateral → medial: radial a., FCR, median n., PL, FDS, ulnar a., ulnar n., FCU.
- Carpal tunnel: median n. + 4 FDS + 4 FDP + FPL (not the ulnar nerve, not PL).
- Snuffbox: APL + EPB (anterior), EPL (posterior); floor = scaphoid; tenderness = scaphoid fracture.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the structures on the front of the wrist from lateral to medial.</summary>

**Answer:** Radial artery, tendon of flexor carpi radialis, median nerve, tendon of palmaris longus, flexor digitorum superficialis, ulnar artery, ulnar nerve and tendon of flexor carpi ulnaris.

**Why:** A very common spot question.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the carpal tunnel: boundaries, contents and its clinical importance.</summary>

**Answer:** A fibro-osseous canal in front of the wrist, bounded posteriorly by the bony groove of the carpal bones and anteriorly by the flexor retinaculum. It transmits the median nerve (most superficial), the four FDS tendons in the common (ulnar) synovial sheath, the four FDP tendons, the FPL tendon in its own (radial) sheath and the recurrent branch of the deep palmar arch. Compression of the median nerve in it causes carpal tunnel syndrome.

**Why:** Boundaries + contents + clinical.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the boundaries and clinical importance of the anatomical snuffbox.</summary>

**Answer:** A depression on the posterolateral wrist bounded anterolaterally by the tendons of APL and EPB and posteromedially by the tendon of EPL. Floor: radial styloid, scaphoid and trapezium. Roof: skin, superficial fascia with the beginning of the cephalic vein and branches of the superficial radial nerve, and deep fascia. The radial artery crosses it, and tenderness suggests a scaphoid fracture.

**Why:** Boundaries and the clinical link.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **FCU** is the only superficial flexor **not** median; **FDP** is the only deep flexor with a **dual** supply.
- **FDS** → middle phalanges; **FDP** → terminal phalanges.
- **Brachioradialis, ECRL, anconeus** are supplied by the **radial nerve proper**, not the PIN.
- **ECRL → 2nd**, **ECRB → 3rd**, **ECU → 5th** metacarpal; **FCR → 2nd & 3rd**.
- The **ulnar nerve does not go through the carpal tunnel** — it goes through **Guyon canal**, superficial to the retinaculum; so does the **palmaris longus tendon**.
- A **median nerve lesion at/above the elbow** abolishes pronation (PT + PQ); a **radial nerve lesion** that paralyzes the supinator still leaves **biceps** to supinate.
- **Radial artery pulse:** between **FCR and brachioradialis** tendons.
- Snuffbox pain = **scaphoid**, not lunate.

## 🔥 Final review — memorize before the exam

1. **Medial epicondyle = flexors + ulnar groove; lateral epicondyle = extensors.**
2. **Radius articulates in 4 places**; **proximal row S-L-T-P**, **distal row Tm-Td-C-H**; scaphoid + lunate meet the radius.
3. **Anterior compartment:** 5 superficial (PT, FCR, PL, FDS, FCU) + 3 deep (FDP, FPL, PQ); **median except FCU and ½ FDP**.
4. **AIN:** pure motor, 3 muscles (FPL, lateral ½ FDP, PQ) + 3 joints.
5. **Ulnar nerve:** behind medial epicondyle → between heads of FCU → medial to ulnar artery.
6. **Radial artery:** pulse between FCR and brachioradialis; snuffbox; first dorsal interosseous.
7. **Posterior compartment:** 7 superficial + 5 deep; **PIN** (arcade of Frohse) supplies all but BR, ECRL, anconeus.
8. **Extensor expansion:** hood for ED, lumbricals and interossei.
9. **DRUJ = pivot; wrist = ellipsoid biaxial; disc separates them.**
10. **Wrist front (lat → med):** radial a., FCR, median n., PL, FDS, ulnar a., ulnar n., FCU.
11. **Carpal tunnel:** median n. + 4 FDS + 4 FDP + FPL; **Guyon canal:** ulnar n.
12. **Snuffbox:** APL + EPB / EPL; floor scaphoid; pain = scaphoid fracture.
