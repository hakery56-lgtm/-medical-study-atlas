## 1. Cytokines

- ⭐ **Cytokines** = **small proteins secreted by immune cells** that **affect the behavior of other cells**; signalling molecules, key in **innate and acquired immunity.**
- They are **chemical messengers, soluble mediators**, produced by many cell types; they regulate **growth, differentiation, activation, chemotaxis and cytotoxicity.**
- **Sources:** **neutrophils** (IL-1α, IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α); **macrophages** (IL-6, IL-8, IL-12); **NK cells** (IFN-γ, TNF-α); cells whose **TLRs** bind microbes; **activated lymphocytes.**
- **Groups:** interleukins (IL), chemokines, growth factors (GF), colony stimulating factors (CSF), tumour necrosis factors (TNF), interferons (IFN).
- ⭐ **A cytokine is NOT antigen specific, but it is receptor specific.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cytokines are small signalling proteins from immune cells.
- Neutrophils: IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α; macrophages: IL-6, IL-8, IL-12; NK cells: IFN-γ, TNF-α.
- Not antigen specific, but receptor specific.

</div>

## 2. Interferons, interleukins and TNF

- ⭐ **Interferons (IFN):** signalling proteins from **virus-infected monocytes and lymphocytes**; **key antiviral proteins** that **interfere with virus replication** and **warn neighbouring cells** (without them most of us might die of influenza).
- **Mechanism:** infected cell releases IFN → neighbours enter an **antiviral state** → when the virus arrives they inhibit it quickly. Inside the cell: **viral dsRNA** activates a host protein → **inhibition of host protein synthesis** → virus cannot replicate.
- **Why produced:** **inhibit viral replication; activate NK cells and macrophages; regulate** the response to prevent excessive damage.
- **Interleukins:** **IL-1 to IL-37**; **not stored**, quickly synthesized and secreted on infection; mostly from **T lymphocytes and macrophages**; they cause **proliferation and activation of immune cells, more antibody production and inflammation.**
- **TNF:** **killing of cancer, fever, inflammation.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- IFN = antiviral; warns neighbours; blocks viral replication via dsRNA-activated host protein.
- Interleukins: IL-1 to IL-37, not stored, from T cells and macrophages.
- TNF: cancer killing, fever, inflammation.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** The infected cell releases IFN; neighbouring cells enter an antiviral state, so when the virus infects them they are pre-warned and quickly inhibit it.

## 3. Complement

- **Complement** = a large group of **plasma proteins (C1–C9)** that react with one another; they **coat microbes**; essential in innate immunity and **enhance adaptive responses.**
- **Cascade:** smaller fragments diffuse away; larger fragments form complexes on cells.
- ⭐ **Three pathways: classical, alternative, lectin** (all lead to the same effects).
- **Roles:** **inflammation, facilitation of phagocytosis** (complement-coated bacteria are recognized by neutrophil complement receptors), **direct lysis** by the **membrane attack complex** (pores → water and salts enter → bacterium swells and bursts).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Complement: C1–C9, cascade, three pathways.
- Functions: inflammation, opsonization/phagocytosis, lysis by the membrane attack complex.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Classical, alternative and lectin pathways; each is triggered by different components but all lead to the same effects.

**Why:** Slide "Complement activation pathways".

## 4. Coagulation and the summary of innate internal defenses

- **Coagulation** stops bleeding after vessel injury; it involves **platelets, coagulation factors and vitamin K.**
- **Clot formation:** **Stage I** platelets attach to the damaged endothelium; **Stage II** platelets release fibrin and begin to seal; **Stage III** the **fibrin network traps RBCs** and completely seals the hole.
- ⭐ **Innate internal defenses:** **cellular** = neutrophils, monocytes/macrophages, NK cells, TLRs; **extracellular** = cytokines, complement, coagulation.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Coagulation needs platelets, factors and vitamin K; fibrin seals the hole.
- Cellular innate: neutrophils, macrophages, NK, TLRs; extracellular: cytokines, complement, coagulation.

</div>

## 5. Inflammation

- ⭐ **Inflammation** = a complex biological process by which the body responds to **pathogens and irritants**; associated with **tissue swelling**; a **key player in innate immunity.** "All roads lead to inflammation."
- **Vascular changes:** **vasodilatation** (heat, redness) and **increased capillary permeability** (swelling); plus **pain, temporary loss of function, fever.**
- **Mast cells** (like blood basophils, present in tissues) release **histamine** in response to wound/infection/irritant → pathogen removal and adaptive response.
- **Role in innate immunity:** **initiate phagocytosis, limit the spread of infection, stimulate the adaptive response, initiate tissue repair.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Inflammation: vasodilatation + increased permeability → heat, redness, swelling, pain, loss of function, fever.
- Mast cells release histamine.
- Roles: phagocytosis, limiting spread, adaptive stimulus, repair.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

## 6. Acute versus chronic inflammation

| | **Acute / short-term** | **Chronic / long-term** |
|---|---|---|
| **Effect** | **Good**: progressive healing | **Bad**: progressive destruction |
| **Examples** | Cuts, trauma, burns, frostbite, allergic reaction, infection | **Cardiovascular disease, rheumatoid arthritis, autoimmune disease, neurological disease, Alzheimer disease, autism** |

- **Chronic inflammation = tissue damage:** macrophages in injured tissue release toxins (**ROS**) that injure tissue; it is almost always accompanied by **tissue destruction.**
- **Cycle:** chronic inflammation → activation of immune cells → killing of host cells → tissue damage → **reduced tissue function.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Acute = healing; chronic = destruction.
- Macrophage ROS damage tissue in chronic inflammation.
- Cycle ends in reduced tissue function.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Why:** Slide "The good and bad about inflammation".
