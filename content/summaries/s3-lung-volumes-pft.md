## 1. What PFTs are and why they are done

- ⭐ **PFTs** = **noninvasive tests** giving measurable feedback about **lung volumes, capacities, flow rates and gas exchange**; evaluated by a doctor they help **diagnose lung disorders**.
- Ventilation problems are **obstructive** (air has **difficulty flowing out** because of **resistance** → decreased flow) or **restrictive** (lungs or chest muscles **cannot expand adequately**).
- **Why done:** diagnose **asthma, bronchitis, emphysema**; find the **cause of shortness of breath**; assess **workplace chemical exposure**; check lung function **before surgery**; assess **medication effect** and treatment progress.

**Key points**
- PFTs are noninvasive and measure volumes, capacities, flows and gas exchange.
- Obstructive = hard to get air out; restrictive = lungs cannot expand.

## 2. Lung volumes and capacities

| Volume | Definition | Normal |
|---|---|---|
| **Tidal volume (TV)** | Air inspired or expired with each **normal breath** | **~500 ml** |
| **Inspiratory reserve volume (IRV)** | Extra air that can be inspired **over the tidal volume** | **~3000 ml** |
| **Expiratory reserve volume (ERV)** | Air that can still be expired **after a normal tidal expiration** | **~1100 ml** |
| **Residual volume (RV)** | Air **remaining after the most forceful expiration**; **not measurable by spirometer** | **~1200 ml** |

| Capacity | Equation | Definition | Normal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inspiratory capacity (IC)** | **TV + IRV** | Maximal air inhaled after a normal expiration | **~3500 ml** |
| **Functional residual capacity (FRC)** | **ERV + RV** | Gas remaining at the end of a **passive expiration** | **~2300 ml** |
| **Vital capacity (VC)** | **IRV + TV + ERV** | Air **forcibly expired after maximal inspiration** | **~4600 ml** |
| **Total lung capacity (TLC)** | **VC + RV** | Maximum volume the lung can be expanded to | **~5800 ml** |

- ⭐ A **capacity** includes **two or more volumes**. **RV, and the capacities containing it (FRC, TLC), cannot be measured by a spirometer.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Volumes: TV 500, IRV 3000, ERV 1100, RV 1200 ml.
- Capacities: IC 3500, FRC 2300, VC 4600, TLC 5800 ml.
- RV cannot be measured by spirometry.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the four lung volumes with normal values.</summary>

**Answer:** Tidal volume (about 500 ml), inspiratory reserve volume (about 3000 ml), expiratory reserve volume (about 1100 ml) and residual volume (about 1200 ml, not measurable by spirometer).

**Why:** Slides 5 and 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Write the equations of the four capacities.</summary>

**Answer:** IC = TV + IRV; FRC = ERV + RV; VC = IRV + TV + ERV; TLC = VC + RV.

**Why:** Slides 7 and 8.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Define vital capacity and total lung capacity.</summary>

**Answer:** Vital capacity is the total volume that can be forcibly expired after maximal inspiration (about 4600 ml); total lung capacity is vital capacity plus residual volume, the maximum volume to which the lung can be expanded (about 5800 ml).

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>

## 3. PFT measurements and the spirometer

- **Forced vital capacity (FVC)** = air exhaled **forcefully and quickly after maximum inspiration**.
- **FEV1** = volume expired in the **first second** of the FVC test.
- ⭐ **FEV1/FVC (%)** = FEV1 / FVC × 100; a **reduced ratio is specific for obstructive** (not restrictive) disease.
- **PEFR** = **peak expiratory flow rate** (the slide words it as "the maximum volume during forced expiration").
- **Spirometer:** measures air breathed in/out and **how quickly** through a **mouthpiece**; records on a **spirograph**; used to **diagnose lung disease and differentiate obstructive from restrictive**.
- **Spirometer cannot measure RV and dead space**; these are measured by **whole body plethysmography**, **helium dilution** and other methods.
- **Normal values** vary by **age, height, sex and race**; compared with **previous results**; abnormal values → **further tests**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- FVC, FEV1, FEV1/FVC, PEFR are the core measurements.
- Low FEV1/FVC = obstruction.
- RV and dead space need plethysmography or helium dilution.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define FVC, FEV1 and FEV1/FVC.</summary>

**Answer:** FVC is the air exhaled forcefully and quickly after maximum inspiration; FEV1 is the volume expired in the first second of the FVC test; FEV1/FVC is their ratio times 100, reduced specifically in obstructive disease.

**Why:** Slides 10 and 11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What can a spirometer not measure, and how is it measured?</summary>

**Answer:** The expected average for someone of the same age, height, sex and race, and the patient's own previous results.

## 4. Interpretation of spirometry and the practical

- **Step 1:** obstruction or not? **FEV1/FVC < 70% = obstruction.** **Step 2:** severity by **FEV1**.
- **Restriction:** **FEV1 and FVC reduced in proportion** (**normal FEV1/FVC**).
- **Flow-volume loops:** **obstruction** = **concave, scooped**; **restriction** = **decreased VC, normal shape**.

| Pattern | Volume-time curve | Flow-volume loop |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | Plateau reaching FVC | Sharp **PEFR** then smooth descent |
| **Mild obstruction** | **Late plateau, FVC preserved** | **PEFR mildly reduced, coving** |
| **Severe obstruction** | **No plateau, FVC reduced** | **PEFR severely reduced, coving** |
| **Restriction** | **Normal plateau, FVC reduced** | **PEFR normal or mildly reduced, no coving** |

- **Loop comparison:** emphysema loop shifted to higher volumes with low scooped flow; fibrosis loop smaller and shifted to lower volumes.
- **Sample report:** 28-year-old male smoker, **normal spirometry** (FVC 5.68 L = 105%, FEV1 5.12 L = 114%, FEV1/FVC 90.1%).
- **Practical:** objective = measure respiratory volumes and function; materials = **subject and spirometer**; procedure = subject **sitting**, **clean disposable mouthpiece**, breathe in or out of the spirometer.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- FEV1/FVC < 70% = obstruction; proportional fall in FEV1 and FVC = restriction.
- Obstruction: scooped loop with coving; restriction: small loop, normal shape.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do you interpret spirometry in two steps?</summary>

**Answer:** Step 1: a low FEV1/FVC (< 70%) means obstruction; step 2: severity is based on FEV1. In restriction FEV1 and FVC fall in proportion with a normal ratio.

**Why:** Slide 15.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare the flow-volume loop in obstruction and restriction.</summary>

**Answer:** Obstruction shows a concave (scooped) expiratory limb with coving and reduced PEFR; restriction shows decreased vital capacity with a normal shape, no coving and normal or mildly reduced PEFR.

**Why:** Slides 15 and 16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the procedure of the spirometry practical.</summary>

**Answer:** The subject is seated, a clean disposable mouthpiece is used, and the subject is asked to breathe in or out of the spirometer to measure respiratory volumes and function tests.

**Why:** Slide 19.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Residual volume, FRC and TLC cannot be measured by a spirometer** (they contain RV).
- **FRC = ERV + RV; IC = TV + IRV; VC = IRV + TV + ERV; TLC = VC + RV.**
- **A low FEV1/FVC means obstruction; a normal ratio with low FEV1 and FVC means restriction.**
- **Coving (scooped loop) = obstruction; no coving = restriction.**
- **FEV1 is a one-second volume; FVC is the whole forced maneuver.**
- **Note:** the slide defines PEFR as "the maximum volume during forced expiration", although peak expiratory flow is a flow rate (volume per time); the slide wording is kept and no exam question is built on this phrase.
