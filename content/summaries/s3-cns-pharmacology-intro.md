## 2. Synaptic potentials: excitatory and inhibitory pathways

- ⭐ At most CNS synapses **receptors are coupled to ion channels**; open channels let ions flow **down their concentration gradients**, producing **depolarization or hyperpolarization.**
- **Excitatory pathway (EPSP):** transmitters such as **glutamate or acetylcholine** → ↑ **Na⁺ permeability** → **Na⁺ influx** → **weak depolarization (EPSP)** toward threshold; with more stimulated neurons the EPSP passes **threshold** → **action potential.** A nerve impulse reflects **thousands of excitatory transmitter molecules from many fibers.**
- **Inhibitory pathway (IPSP):** **GABA or glycine** → ↑ permeability to **K⁺ and Cl⁻** → **Cl⁻ influx and K⁺ efflux** → **weak hyperpolarization (IPSP)** away from threshold → **less action potential.**
- **Combined effects:** most CNS neurons receive **both EPSP and IPSP**; different transmitters act on the same neuron through their own receptors; the **overall action = summation.**

| | **EPSP** | **IPSP** |
|---|---|---|
| **Transmitters** | Glutamate, acetylcholine | GABA, glycine |
| **Ions** | **Na⁺ influx** | **Cl⁻ influx, K⁺ efflux** |
| **Membrane effect** | **Depolarization** | **Hyperpolarization** |
| **Effect on AP** | Toward threshold → AP | Away from threshold → less AP |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- EPSP = Na⁺ influx, depolarization (glutamate, ACh); IPSP = Cl⁻ influx and K⁺ efflux, hyperpolarization (GABA, glycine).
- Neurons sum EPSPs and IPSPs.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is an EPSP generated and how can it fire an action potential?</summary>

**Answer:** Excitatory transmitters (glutamate, acetylcholine) transiently increase Na⁺ permeability; Na⁺ influx causes a weak depolarization that moves the potential toward threshold; with more stimulated neurons the EPSP passes threshold and an action potential results.

**Why:** Slides 9–11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How is an IPSP generated?</summary>

**Answer:** GABA or glycine increase permeability to K⁺ and Cl⁻; Cl⁻ influx and K⁺ efflux cause weak hyperpolarization that moves the potential away from threshold and diminishes action potential generation.

**Why:** Slides 12–14.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the combined effect of EPSP and IPSP?</summary>

**Answer:** Most CNS neurons receive both; each transmitter binds its own receptor and the overall action is the summation of the individual actions.

**Why:** Slide 15.
</details>

## 3. CNS neurotransmitters

| Class | Transmitter | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Amino acids** | **Glutamate** | **Excitatory** |
| | **GABA and glycine** | **Inhibitory** |
| **Acetylcholine** | Mostly **muscarinic** | **M1 excitatory, M2 inhibitory** |
| **Monoamines** | **Dopamine** | Mostly **inhibitory** |
| | **Norepinephrine** | **α1 and β excitatory; α2 inhibitory** |
| | **5-HT** | **5-HT1 and 5 inhibitory; others excitatory** |
| | **Histamine** | **H1 and H2 excitatory; H3 inhibitory** |
| **Purines** | ATP and adenosine | – |
| **Soluble gases** | **Nitric oxide (NO)** | – |
| **Neuropeptides** | e.g. **opioid peptides** | – |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Glutamate excitatory; GABA and glycine inhibitory; dopamine mostly inhibitory.
- NE: α1/β excitatory, α2 inhibitory; histamine: H1/H2 excitatory, H3 inhibitory.
- Other classes: purines, NO, neuropeptides.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the classes of CNS neurotransmitters.</summary>

**Answer:** Amino acids (glutamate, GABA, glycine), acetylcholine, monoamines (dopamine, norepinephrine, 5-HT, histamine), purines (ATP, adenosine), soluble gases (NO) and neuropeptides (e.g. opioid peptides).

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. State the excitatory or inhibitory role of the monoamines.</summary>

**Answer:** Dopamine mostly inhibitory; norepinephrine α1 and β excitatory, α2 inhibitory; 5-HT1 and 5-HT5 inhibitory, others excitatory; histamine H1 and H2 excitatory, H3 inhibitory.

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the role of acetylcholine in the CNS?</summary>

**Answer:** Mostly muscarinic: M1 receptors are excitatory and M2 receptors are inhibitory.

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **EPSP = Na⁺ influx (depolarization); IPSP = Cl⁻ influx + K⁺ efflux (hyperpolarization).**
- **Glutamate = excitatory; GABA and glycine = inhibitory.**
- **The ANS has only TWO primary transmitters (ACh, NE); the CNS has many.**
- **Dopamine is mostly inhibitory; histamine H3 is inhibitory, H1/H2 excitatory.**
- **Norepinephrine: α2 is the inhibitory receptor; α1 and β excitatory.**
- **Integration occurs at the axon initial segment (high Na⁺ channel density).**
