## 1. Transplant rejection: recognition and hyperacute rejection

- ⭐ **Rejection:** the recipient's immune system recognizes the graft as foreign; **T cells and antibodies** destroy it. Main difference = **HLA alleles**. Same-species grafts = **allografts.** Directed against **HLA**, involving **cell-mediated and humoral** responses.
- **Recognition pathways:** **direct** (graft APCs present to recipient T cells; most important for **CTL-mediated acute rejection**) and **indirect** (host APCs process and present; greater role in **chronic rejection**). Both activate **CD8⁺ T cells (CTLs)** and **CD4⁺ T cells (mainly TH1).**
- **Patterns:** **hyperacute, acute, chronic.**
- **Hyperacute rejection:** **minutes to a few hours** after perfusion; **pre-formed antibodies** bind graft **endothelium** → endothelial injury, **widespread small-vessel thrombosis**, focal ischemic necrosis. Causes of pre-formed antibodies: **previous rejection; multiparous women (anti-HLA against paternal antigens); prior transfusions (platelets and WBCs rich in HLA).** Occurs in **< 0.4%**.
- **Morphology (kidney):** cyanotic, mottled, **anuric**; **fibrinoid necrosis** of arterioles/arteries, occluding thrombi, neutrophils in arterioles, glomeruli and peritubular capillaries; glomerular thrombosis; cortical **infarction**; the kidney must be **removed.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Direct pathway → acute (CTL); indirect pathway → chronic.
- Hyperacute = pre-formed antibodies, minutes to hours, thrombosis; rare (< 0.4%).
- Causes of pre-formed antibodies: previous graft, pregnancies, transfusions.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the direct and indirect pathways of allo-antigen recognition.</summary>

**Answer:** Direct: graft APCs present graft antigens to recipient T cells (important in CTL-mediated acute rejection). Indirect: host APCs pick up, process and present graft antigens (greater role in chronic rejection). Both activate CD8⁺ CTLs and CD4⁺ (mainly TH1) cells.

**Why:** Slides 3–4.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is hyperacute rejection and what causes the pre-formed antibodies?</summary>

**Answer:** Rejection within minutes to hours due to pre-formed host antibodies against graft endothelial antigens, causing endothelial injury and thrombosis; antibodies arise from previous rejection, multiparity (anti-HLA against paternal antigens) and prior blood transfusions.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the morphology of hyperacute rejection in a kidney.</summary>

**Answer:** Cyanotic, mottled, anuric kidney; fibrinoid necrosis and thrombi in arterioles and arteries, neutrophils in vessels and glomeruli, glomerular thrombosis, cortical infarction; the graft is non-functional and removed.

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>

## 2. Acute and chronic rejection

- ⭐ **Acute rejection:** **days or weeks**; may follow **stopping immunosuppression**; **humoral and cell-mediated**; acute cellular rejection = **T cells vs donor HLA (especially class II)**; signs of renal failure.
- **Acute cellular (T cell-mediated):** **tubulo-interstitial (type I)** = interstitial inflammation (CD4⁺, CD8⁺), **tubulitis**, focal tubular injury; **vascular (type II, III)** = vessel inflammation, wall necrosis (type III), swollen endothelium, **endotheliitis (intimal arteritis).** CD8⁺ CTLs kill graft cells; CD4⁺ cytokines cause inflammation.
- **Acute antibody-mediated:** damage to **glomeruli and small vessels**, glomerulitis and peritubular capillaritis, **complement deposition**, focal thrombosis.
- **Chronic rejection:** **months to years**; **slow breakdown of tolerance** (possibly inadequate immunosuppression); **creatinine rises over 4–6 months**; **does not respond** to standard immunosuppression. **Vascular changes** (intimal thickening, occlusion; **graft arteriosclerosis**); **transplant glomerulopathy** (basement membrane duplication), peritubular capillary basement membrane multilayering, **interstitial fibrosis, tubular atrophy**, **sparse** mononuclear infiltrates.

| | **Hyperacute** | **Acute** | **Chronic** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Time** | Minutes–hours | Days–weeks | Months–years |
| **Mechanism** | Pre-formed antibodies | T cells ± antibodies | Slow loss of tolerance; vascular and antibody damage |
| **Key lesion** | Thrombosis, infarction | Tubulitis, endotheliitis | Arteriosclerosis, glomerulopathy, fibrosis |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Acute: days–weeks; tubulitis (type I) and endotheliitis (types II–III); antibody type = complement deposition.
- Chronic: months–years; arteriosclerosis, transplant glomerulopathy, fibrosis; unresponsive to immunosuppression.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe acute cellular rejection.</summary>

**Answer:** Occurs within days or weeks (or after stopping immunosuppression); T cells react to donor class II HLA; tubulo-interstitial pattern (interstitial inflammation, tubulitis) or vascular pattern (vessel inflammation, endotheliitis, sometimes necrosis).

**Why:** Slides 9–11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the features of acute antibody-mediated rejection?</summary>

**Answer:** Damage and inflammation of glomeruli and peritubular capillaries, complement deposition and focal small-vessel thrombosis.

**Why:** Slide 12.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe chronic rejection.</summary>

**Answer:** Slow, progressive rejection over months to years from breakdown of tolerance; creatinine rises over 4–6 months; vascular intimal thickening and occlusion, transplant glomerulopathy, interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy with sparse infiltrates; unresponsive to standard immunosuppression.

**Why:** Slides 14–16.
</details>

## 3. Autoimmune diseases and self-tolerance

- ⭐ **Autoimmune disease** = immune reaction against **self antigens** (antibody or cell-mediated) when **self-tolerance breaks down.**
- **Table 5.7:** **antibody-mediated:** organ-specific (autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, atrophic gastritis/pernicious anaemia, **myasthenia gravis, Graves, Goodpasture**), systemic (**SLE**). **T cell-mediated:** **type I diabetes, multiple sclerosis**; systemic **rheumatoid arthritis, systemic sclerosis, Sjögren.** **Postulated:** IBD, primary biliary cirrhosis, chronic active hepatitis, polyarteritis nodosa, inflammatory myopathies.
- **Self-tolerance** = no responsiveness to own antigens: **central** + **peripheral.**
- **Central tolerance:** deletion of self-reactive T cells (**thymus, negative selection by apoptosis**) and B cells (**bone marrow**); some self antigens are absent centrally, so self-reactive cells **escape.**
- **Peripheral tolerance:** **anergy** (functional inactivation), **regulatory T cells**, **deletion by apoptosis**, **immune-privileged sites** (testis, eye, brain).
- **Breakdown:** excess self-antigen access to APCs, excess non-specific signalling, altered antigen presentation (**viruses, free radicals, radiation**), inflammation/tissue damage releasing **sequestered antigens** (post-traumatic **uveitis, orchitis**); **molecular mimicry** (**rheumatic heart disease**: streptococcal M protein cross-reacts with cardiac glycoproteins); **susceptibility genes**; infections and tissue injury.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Central tolerance: thymus (T) and bone marrow (B), negative selection.
- Peripheral: anergy, Tregs, apoptosis, immune-privileged sites.
- Mimicry: strep M protein and cardiac glycoproteins = rheumatic heart disease.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare central and peripheral tolerance.</summary>

**Answer:** Central tolerance deletes self-reactive T cells in the thymus (negative selection) and B cells in the bone marrow; peripheral tolerance silences escaped cells by anergy, regulatory T cells, apoptosis and immune-privileged sites.

**Why:** Slides 18–20.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How may tolerance be broken?</summary>

**Answer:** Excess access of self antigens to APCs, non-specific signalling, altered presentation (viruses, free radicals, radiation), release of sequestered antigens after damage, molecular mimicry, susceptibility genes and infections.

**Why:** Slides 21–24.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Explain molecular mimicry with an example.</summary>

**Answer:** Structural similarity between self and microbial antigens triggers an immune response; antibodies to streptococcal M protein cross-react with cardiac glycoproteins, causing rheumatic heart disease.

**Why:** Slide 23.
</details>

## 4. Amyloidosis

- ⭐ **Amyloidosis:** **extracellular deposits of fibrillar proteins** causing tissue damage. **H&E:** amorphous eosinophilic hyaline; **Congo red:** pink-red, **green birefringence** under polarized light; **EM:** non-branching **7.5–10 nm fibrils in β-pleated sheet.** **95% fibril protein, 5% glycoproteins**; ≥ 30 proteins can form fibrils (biochemically heterogeneous).
- **Mechanism:** **misfolding** into β-sheet → aggregation; from **normal proteins** produced in excess or **mutant proteins.**

### Chemical types and classification
| Type | Source | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| **AL** | **Ig light chains** (plasma cells; chiefly λ) | **Primary**: myeloma (5–15%), monoclonal B-cell proliferation |
| **AA** | **SAA** (liver) | **Secondary / reactive**: chronic inflammation; familial Mediterranean fever |
| **Aβ** | APP | **Alzheimer** (cerebral) |
| **ATTR** | **Transthyretin** (binds thyroxine and retinol) | **Senile systemic** (heart, 70–80 y), familial neuropathies |
| **Aβ₂m** | **β₂-microglobulin** (MHC I) | **Haemodialysis** (chronic renal failure) |
| Localized | A Cal (calcitonin), AIAPP, AANF | Medullary thyroid carcinoma, islets, atrial |

- **Primary (AL):** clonal plasma cells; **Bence Jones protein** (λ and κ light chains in urine; **λ ~6× more likely to deposit**); most AL patients have no overt neoplasm.
- **Secondary (AA):** TB, bronchiectasis, chronic osteomyelitis; now **rheumatoid arthritis, IBD**; cancers (**renal cell carcinoma, Hodgkin**). **Pathogenesis:** inflammation → macrophages → **IL-1, IL-6** → ↑ SAA; enzyme defect → incomplete degradation → **insoluble AA.**
- **Localized:** nodules in **lung, larynx, skin, bladder, tongue, eye region** (possible AL); endocrine amyloid in **medullary thyroid carcinoma, islet cell tumour, pheochromocytoma** (note: the slide labels this TTR while the classification table lists calcitonin (A Cal) for medullary carcinoma); **senile** amyloid (heart); **familial Mediterranean fever** (autosomal recessive febrile illness, serosal inflammation).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Congo red + green birefringence; β-pleated sheet fibrils; 95% fibrils.
- AL = light chains (primary, myeloma); AA = SAA (secondary, chronic inflammation); Aβ = Alzheimer; ATTR = senile/familial; Aβ₂m = dialysis.
- IL-1 and IL-6 raise SAA.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is amyloid identified?</summary>

**Answer:** On H&E as amorphous eosinophilic hyaline material; Congo red gives pink-red colour with green birefringence under polarized light; EM shows non-branching β-pleated sheet fibrils.

**Why:** Slides 26–29.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare primary and secondary amyloidosis.</summary>

**Answer:** Primary: AL from clonal plasma cells (e.g. myeloma, λ light chains). Secondary: AA from SAA in chronic inflammation (TB, bronchiectasis, RA, IBD) or cancers such as renal cell carcinoma and Hodgkin lymphoma, via IL-1/IL-6 and incomplete SAA breakdown.

**Why:** Slides 34–38.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Name the types of amyloid protein.</summary>

**Answer:** AL (Ig light chain), AA (SAA), Aβ (Alzheimer), transthyretin (senile and familial) and β₂-microglobulin (haemodialysis).

**Why:** Slide 30.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Direct pathway = acute; indirect pathway = chronic rejection.**
- **Hyperacute = pre-formed antibodies (minutes–hours), < 0.4%; chronic does not respond to immunosuppression.**
- **Acute cellular rejection involves class II HLA; endotheliitis and tubulitis.**
- **Central tolerance = thymus (T) and bone marrow (B); anergy = inactivation, NOT death.**
- **Immune-privileged: testis, eye, brain.**
- **AL = primary (λ 6× κ); AA = secondary (SAA; IL-1, IL-6).**
- **Congo red: GREEN birefringence; fibrils β-pleated sheet; 95% protein.**
