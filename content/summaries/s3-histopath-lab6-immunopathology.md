## 1. Normal immune response

| Feature | Innate | Adaptive |
|---|---|---|
| **Onset** | **Immediate** | **Slow (1–2 weeks)** |
| **Specificity** | **Non-specific, first line** | **Specific** |
| **Memory** | **None** | **Long-term** |
| **Potency** | **Limited** | **High** |
| **Major reaction** | **Inflammation** | **Immediate and delayed hypersensitivity** |

- **Innate defenses:** **external** (skin, mucosa; sweat, stomach HCl, tears, saliva; normal flora) and **internal** (**neutrophils, monocytes/macrophages, NK cells; cytokines, complement, coagulation proteins**).
- **Adaptive:** **humoral (B cells, antibodies)** and **cell-mediated (T cells)**.
- **Organs:** bone marrow, thymus, spleen, tonsils, lymph nodes.
- **Lymphadenitis** = inflamed node; **lymphangitis** = **red tender streaks** from an infected wound toward the nearest node (e.g., cellulitis of the hand).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Innate: fast, no memory; adaptive: slow, memory.
- Lymphangitis = red streaks.

</div>

## 2. Type I and Type II hypersensitivity

| Type | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **I — immediate** | **Immediate vascular skin reaction** | **Acute urticaria (itchy raised red swellings; intradermal edema)** |
| **II — antibody-mediated** | **IgG and IgM bind fixed tissue/cell surface antigens → phagocytosis/lysis, inflammation, functional interference** | **Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole reaction; Goodpasture syndrome (alveolar hemorrhage with capillaritis)** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Type II: IgG/IgM against fixed antigens.
- Goodpasture: alveolar hemorrhage.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe Type II hypersensitivity.</summary>

**Answer:** IgG and IgM bind cell or tissue surface antigens and injure by phagocytosis or lysis, inflammation, or functional interference.

**Why:** Slide 15.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give Type II examples.</summary>

**Answer:** A reaction to trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole and Goodpasture syndrome.

**Why:** Slides 16–17.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is seen in urticaria histology?</summary>

**Answer:** Intradermal edema after intradermal testing.

## 3. Type III and Type IV hypersensitivity

- **Type III:** **IgG/IgM + exogenous or endogenous antigens → immune complexes deposit in vascular beds → complement activation → acute inflammation.** **Examples:** **cutaneous necrotizing vasculitis, Arthus reaction (vaccine antigen + pre-existing IgG), SLE malar rash, necrotizing vasculitis (fibrinoid necrosis), bird-antigen airway-centered infiltration.**
- **Type IV:** **T-cell mediated: TH1/TH17 cytokines → inflammation, neutrophil and macrophage activation; CD8⁺ CTLs kill cells.** **Examples:** **drug hypersensitivity syndrome (2–8 weeks after starting a drug), granulomatous inflammation, psoriasis (macrophage TNF, scaly plaques on hands, feet, scalp, elbows, knees).**
- **Distinguishing Type III vs IV in a case:** **vasculitis with fibrinoid necrosis = immune complexes; granulomas or lymphocytic infiltrate = T cells.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Type III = immune complexes + complement.
- Type IV = T cells (TH1/TH17, CTL).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe Type III hypersensitivity.</summary>

**Answer:** Immune complexes of IgG/IgM with antigens deposit in vascular beds, activate complement and cause acute inflammation, e.g., necrotizing vasculitis.

**Why:** Slide 18.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe Type IV hypersensitivity.</summary>

**Answer:** T cells (TH1 and TH17) release cytokines that activate neutrophils and macrophages; CD8⁺ CTLs may kill host cells directly.

**Why:** Slide 21.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give Type IV examples.</summary>

**Answer:** Drug hypersensitivity syndrome, granulomatous inflammation and psoriasis.

**Why:** Slides 22–23.
</details>

## 4. Transplant rejection and GVHD

| Pattern | Type | Histology |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyperacute** | **Type II** | **Kidney at 1 day: diffuse hemorrhage, neutrophils in peritubular capillaries, glomerular thrombi** |
| **Acute** | **Type IV** | **Heart: interstitial, perivascular, endocardial lymphocytes with myocytolysis; kidney: lymphocytes and plasma cells around tubules** |
| **Chronic** | **Mainly type IV** | **Intimal proliferation, vessel obliteration, thickened fibrotic arteries, interstitial fibrosis** |

- **GVHD:** **confluent follicular macular rash (trunk, behind ear, palms) and liver cholestasis with bile in canaliculi.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hyperacute = Type II; acute and chronic = Type IV.
- GVHD: skin rash and cholestasis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name the patterns of rejection and their types.</summary>

**Answer:** Hyperacute (Type II), acute (Type IV), chronic (mainly Type IV) and GVHD.

**Why:** Slides 24–27.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What does hyperacute rejection look like?</summary>

**Answer:** Diffuse cortical hemorrhage with neutrophils in peritubular capillaries and glomerular thrombi at one day.

**Why:** Slide 25.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the features of GVHD.</summary>

**Answer:** Confluent follicular macular rash on the trunk, behind the ear and on the palms, with liver cholestasis.

**Why:** Slide 29.
</details>

## 5. Amyloidosis

- **Definition:** deposition of **abnormal misfolded protein (amyloid)** in tissues → dysfunction.
- **Structure:** **long non-branching fibrils in β-pleated sheet conformation.**
- **H&E:** **extracellular, amorphous, acellular, pink ("cotton candy")**; **slow deposition over years**; e.g., **between myofibers in heart**, skin.
- **Standard teaching:** **Congo red** stain (apple-green birefringence).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- β-pleated sheet fibrils.
- Pink amorphous extracellular on H&E.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define amyloidosis.</summary>

**Answer:** A group of diseases with deposition of abnormal misfolded protein (amyloid) in tissues causing dysfunction.

**Why:** Slide 30.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe amyloid histology.</summary>

**Answer:** Extracellular, amorphous, acellular, pink on H&E, deposited slowly over years.

**Why:** Slide 32.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the structure of amyloid fibrils?</summary>

**Answer:** Long non-branching fibrils with a β-pleated sheet conformation.

**Why:** Slide 30.
</details>
