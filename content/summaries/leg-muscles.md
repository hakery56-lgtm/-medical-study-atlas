## 1. Overview and anterior compartment muscles

- **Deep fascia (fascia cruris)** separates the **superficial tissues** (fat, skin, superficial veins) from the **deep tissues** (**3 muscle compartments** and deep veins).
- ⭐ **13 muscles in 3 groups:** **front (extensors/dorsiflexors) = 4**, **lateral (evertors) = 2**, **back (flexors/plantar flexors) = 7** (superficial 3 + deep 4).

### Anterior compartment (4 muscles, medial → lateral)
| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tibialis anterior (TA)** | Upper ½ lateral surface of **tibia** + interosseous membrane | **Medial cuneiform + base of 1st metatarsal** | Dorsiflexes ankle; **inverts** foot |
| **Extensor hallucis longus (EHL)** | Anterior surface of fibula + membrane | **Base of terminal phalanx of big toe** | Dorsiflexes ankle; extends big toe |
| **Extensor digitorum longus (EDL)** | Anterior surface of fibula + membrane | **4 tendons** into the extensor expansions of the lateral 4 toes | Dorsiflexes ankle; extends lateral 4 toes |
| **Peroneus tertius** | Anterior surface of fibula + membrane | **Base of 5th metatarsal** | Dorsiflexes ankle; **everts** foot |

- **General:** all pass in front of the ankle; **all arise from the fibula/membrane EXCEPT TA (the only one from the tibia)**; **deep peroneal nerve** + **anterior tibial artery**; common action = **dorsiflexion**.
- **Details:** TA helps maintain the **medial longitudinal arch**; EHL is deep to TA/EDL at its origin and its tendon emerges between them lower down; EDL passes deep to the superior extensor retinaculum, divides under the inferior retinaculum into 4 tendons joined by **extensor digitorum brevis** to form **dorsal digital expansions** (3 slips: middle → middle phalanx, 2 collaterals → terminal phalanx).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 13 leg muscles: 4 anterior, 2 lateral, 7 posterior (3 + 4).
- Anterior compartment: TA, EHL, EDL, peroneus tertius; deep peroneal nerve; anterior tibial artery; dorsiflexion.
- TA is the only anterior muscle from the tibia; it inverts and supports the medial arch; peroneus tertius everts.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the muscles of the anterior compartment with their insertions and common action.</summary>

**Answer:** From medial to lateral: tibialis anterior (medial cuneiform and base of 1st metatarsal), extensor hallucis longus (terminal phalanx of the big toe), extensor digitorum longus (4 tendons to the lateral 4 toes) and peroneus tertius (base of the 5th metatarsal). They all dorsiflex the ankle.

**Why:** Slides "Muscles of front leg".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the general features of the anterior compartment?</summary>

**Answer:** The muscles pass in front of the ankle and arise from the anterior surface of the fibula and interosseous membrane except tibialis anterior (from the tibia). They are supplied by the deep peroneal nerve and the anterior tibial artery, and the common action is dorsiflexion of the ankle.

**Why:** Slide "General features".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the dorsal digital expansion.</summary>

**Answer:** Each tendon of extensor digitorum longus is joined by a tendon of extensor digitorum brevis to form a dorsal digital expansion, which divides into 3 slips: the middle slip inserts into the base of the middle phalanx and the 2 collateral slips into the base of the terminal phalanx.

**Why:** Slide "Extensor digitorum longus".
</details>

## 2. Extensor retinacula, anterior tibial artery, deep peroneal nerve

| | **Superior extensor retinaculum** | **Inferior extensor retinaculum** |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | **Thick transverse band** | **Y-shaped band** |
| Site | **Immediately above the ankle joint** | **In front of the ankle joint** |
| Attachments | Lower inch of the anterior border of **tibia** (medial) and **fibula** (lateral) | Stem: **superior surface of calcaneus**; upper band: **anterior margin of medial malleolus**; lower band: deep fascia of the **medial side of the foot** |
| Structures deep to it (medial → lateral) | **TA, EHL, (artery, vein, nerve), EDL, peroneus tertius** | Same order |

### Anterior tibial artery
- **Smaller terminal branch of the popliteal artery**; begins at the **lower border of popliteus**; passes **through an opening in the upper interosseous membrane** into the anterior compartment; descends on the membrane **with the deep peroneal nerve**.
- Behind the superior retinaculum: **EHL tendon on its medial side**; **deep peroneal nerve and EDL tendons on its lateral side**. **In front of the ankle it becomes the dorsalis pedis artery** (palpated on the dorsum of the foot).

### Deep peroneal nerve
- One of the **2 terminal branches of the common peroneal nerve**, beginning on the lateral aspect of the **neck of the fibula inside peroneus longus**; **pierces the anterior fascial septum**; descends deep to EDL; **triple relation to the artery: lateral, then anterior, then lateral**; passes behind the extensor retinacula; ends on the dorsum of the foot as **lateral and medial terminal divisions**.
- **Branches:** muscular; articular to the ankle; **sensory to the 1st web space**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Superior retinaculum = above the ankle; inferior = Y-shaped, in front of the ankle; order deep to them: TA, EHL, vessels/nerve, EDL, PT.
- Anterior tibial artery: from the popliteal, through the upper interosseous membrane, becomes dorsalis pedis.
- Deep peroneal nerve: from the neck of the fibula, triple relation to the artery, supplies the 1st web space.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the extensor retinacula and the structures passing deep to them.</summary>

**Answer:** The superior extensor retinaculum is a thick transverse band just above the ankle (lower inch of the anterior borders of tibia and fibula); the inferior is a Y-shaped band in front of the ankle (stem on the calcaneus, upper band to the medial malleolus, lower band to the deep fascia of the medial foot). Deep to them from medial to lateral: TA, EHL, the vessels and nerve, EDL and peroneus tertius.

**Why:** Slides "Superior/Inferior extensor retinaculum".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the anterior tibial artery.</summary>

**Answer:** The smaller terminal branch of the popliteal artery, arising at the lower border of popliteus, passing through the upper interosseous membrane and descending on it with the deep peroneal nerve; EHL lies medial and the nerve and EDL tendons lateral to it behind the retinaculum; it becomes the dorsalis pedis artery in front of the ankle.

**Why:** Slide "Anterior tibial artery".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the deep peroneal nerve and its branches.</summary>

**Answer:** A terminal branch of the common peroneal nerve beginning at the neck of the fibula inside peroneus longus, piercing the anterior fascial septum and descending deep to EDL with a triple relation to the anterior tibial artery (lateral, anterior, lateral). It gives muscular branches, an articular branch to the ankle and a sensory branch to the 1st web space.

**Why:** Slide "Deep peroneal nerve".
</details>

## 3. Posterior compartment: superficial group

- **General:** the **largest compartment**; **plantarflexes and inverts** the foot; **tibial nerve** + **posterior tibial artery**; **7 muscles** in 2 layers separated by fascia.
- **Superficial group (3):** all pass behind the ankle, insert into the **calcaneus via the tendo calcaneus (Achilles tendon)**, supplied by the **tibial nerve**, common action = **plantar flexion**.

| Muscle | Origin | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Gastrocnemius** | **Lateral head:** lateral surface of the lateral femoral condyle; **medial head:** popliteal surface of femur above the medial condyle | **2 heads** (medial larger); groove between them holds the **short saphenous vein and sural nerve**; crosses **2 joints**; helps **knee flexion** |
| **Soleus** | Posterior head and upper ⅓ of fibula; **tendinous arch** between tibia and fibula; **soleal line** and middle ⅓ of medial tibial border | Crosses the ankle only |
| **Plantaris** | Lower end of the **lateral supracondylar ridge** | **Rudimentary**; short belly, long thin tendon between gastrocnemius and soleus (lateral → medial); into Achilles tendon or calcaneus |

### Achilles tendon
- **Thickest and strongest tendon**; **15 cm** long; receives the fleshy fibres of soleus on its anterior surface; **no tendon sheath**; **2 bursae (pre- and retrocalcaneal)** inflamed in distance runners.
- **Functions:** lets the superficial posterior muscles plantarflex the foot; stabilizes the ankle during gait; can bear **up to 12× body weight** while running.

### Triceps surae (gastrocnemius white/fast + soleus red/slow)
| Function | **Soleus** | **Gastrocnemius** |
|---|---|---|
| Plantar flexion | Very strong but slow; **alone** when the knee is flexed | Together with soleus |
| Knee | **No action** | **Flexes** the knee |
| Propulsion (walk, jump, run) | Less | **More active** |
| Antigravity | **Strong**; pulls the leg backward to maintain standing | Antigravity |
| Venous pump | **More active** — "peripheral venous heart" (rich venous plexus, perforating veins) | Less |

Note: the text on the gastrocnemius slide lists soleus-type origins (tibia soleal line and fibula) and "crosses the ankle only", and one soleus bullet says "plantar flexor of knee" — these are slide slips; the table origins above are the ones to learn.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Superficial group: gastrocnemius, soleus, plantaris → Achilles tendon → calcaneus; tibial nerve; plantar flexion.
- Gastrocnemius: 2 heads, crosses 2 joints, flexes the knee, propulsion; soleus: antigravity, venous pump.
- Achilles tendon: thickest, 15 cm, no sheath, 2 bursae, bears 12× body weight.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the Achilles tendon.</summary>

**Answer:** The thickest and strongest tendon of the body, 15 cm long, receiving the fleshy fibres of soleus on its anterior surface; it has no tendon sheath and is related to the pre- and retrocalcaneal bursae (inflamed in runners). It lets the superficial calf muscles plantarflex the foot, stabilizes the ankle in gait and can bear up to 12 times body weight in running.

**Why:** Slide "Achilles tendon".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare gastrocnemius and soleus.</summary>

**Answer:** Both plantarflex the foot; with the knee flexed, soleus works alone. Gastrocnemius crosses the knee and flexes it, is more active in propulsion (walking, jumping, running); soleus has no knee action, is the strong slow antigravity muscle and the more active venous pump.

**Why:** Slide "Gastrocnemius & Soleus (triceps surae)".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is soleus called the peripheral venous heart?</summary>

**Answer:** It contains a rich venous plexus which drains the superficial veins (saphenous tributaries) through perforating veins and pumps the blood into the deep veins during contraction.

**Why:** Slide "Triceps surae – soleus".
</details>

## 4. Posterior compartment: deep group

- **Deep group (4):** all pass **behind the medial malleolus except popliteus**; **tibial nerve**; plantar flex the ankle and **maintain the longitudinal arches** (except popliteus).

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Popliteus** | Groove on the **lateral surface of the lateral femoral condyle** | Posterior tibial surface **above the soleal line** | **Flexes knee; medially rotates tibia at the start of flexion (unlocking)** |
| **Flexor hallucis longus (FHL)** | Posterior surface of **fibula** | Base of terminal phalanx of **big toe** | Flexes ankle and big toe |
| **Flexor digitorum longus (FDL)** | Back of the **tibia below the soleal line** | **4 tendons** to base of the terminal phalanx of the lateral 4 toes | Flexes ankle and lateral 4 toes |
| **Tibialis posterior (TP)** | Posterior surface of tibia, fibula and interosseous membrane | **Navicular tuberosity and all tarsal bones EXCEPT the talus** | Flexes ankle; **inverts** foot |

- **Popliteus:** floor of the popliteal fossa; **intracapsular but extrasynovial**; unlocks the knee.
- **FHL:** grooves **3 bones** (lower tibia, talus, sustentaculum tali); enters the sole behind the medial malleolus, runs in the **2nd layer deep to FDL**, passes **between the 2 sesamoids**; supports the medial arch.
- **FDL:** behind the medial malleolus, **2nd layer of the sole, superficial to FHL**, divides into 4 tendons.
- **TP:** with TA, the **invertors**; maintains the medial arch.
- **Flexor retinaculum:** below and behind the medial malleolus; attached above to the posterior border of the medial malleolus and below to the **medial tubercle of the calcaneus**; deep to it (medial → lateral): **TP, FDL, vessels and nerve, FHL** (the slide prints "EHL", a slip for FHL).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Deep group: popliteus, FHL, FDL, TP; all behind the medial malleolus except popliteus.
- TP inserts on all tarsal bones except the talus and inverts; FHL grooves 3 bones and lies deep to FDL in the sole.
- Under the flexor retinaculum: TP, FDL, vessels/nerve, FHL.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the deep posterior compartment muscles with origin and action.</summary>

**Answer:** Popliteus (lateral femoral condyle; flexes the knee, unlocks it by medial rotation of the tibia), FHL (fibula; flexes ankle and big toe), FDL (tibia below the soleal line; flexes ankle and lateral 4 toes), tibialis posterior (tibia, fibula, membrane; flexes ankle and inverts the foot).

**Why:** Slide "Deep group".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the insertion and role of tibialis posterior.</summary>

**Answer:** It inserts mainly into the navicular tuberosity and sends slips to all tarsal bones except the talus. With tibialis anterior it inverts the foot and it maintains the medial longitudinal arch.

**Why:** Slide "Tibialis posterior".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the flexor retinaculum and what passes deep to it.</summary>

**Answer:** It lies below and behind the medial malleolus (posterior border of the medial malleolus above; medial tubercle of the calcaneus below). Deep to it from medial to lateral: tibialis posterior, flexor digitorum longus, the posterior tibial vessels and tibial nerve, and flexor hallucis longus.

**Why:** Slide "Flexor retinaculum".
</details>

## 5. Posterior compartment: artery and nerve

### Posterior tibial artery
- **Terminal branch of the popliteal artery**; begins at the **lower border of popliteus**; passes **deep to gastrocnemius and soleus**; in the lower leg covered only by **skin and fascia**; passes behind the medial malleolus deep to the flexor retinaculum; **ends as the medial and lateral plantar arteries**.
- **Branches:** **peroneal artery** (largest), muscular, nutrient artery to the tibia, anastomotic branches around the ankle, medial and lateral plantar arteries. Its **venae comitantes join those of the anterior tibial artery to form the popliteal vein**.
- **Peroneal artery:** arises near the origin of the posterior tibial; descends **behind the fibula within or behind FHL**; muscular branches to the lateral compartment, **nutrient artery to the fibula**, a **perforating branch through the interosseous membrane** to the lower front of the leg; ends in the ankle anastomosis.

### Tibial nerve
- **Larger terminal branch of the sciatic nerve**; descends through the popliteal fossa deep to gastrocnemius and soleus; lies on the posterior surface of tibialis posterior; accompanies the posterior tibial artery (**first medial, then posterior, finally lateral to it**); behind the medial malleolus **between FDL and FHL** deep to the flexor retinaculum, where it divides into the **medial and lateral plantar nerves**.
- **Branches:** muscular (**soleus, FDL, FHL, TP**), cutaneous (**medial calcaneal branch** to the skin of the medial heel), articular (ankle), terminal plantar nerves.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Posterior tibial artery: from the popliteal at the lower border of popliteus; branches include the peroneal artery; ends as plantar arteries.
- Peroneal artery: behind the fibula, supplies the lateral compartment and the fibula, has a perforating branch.
- Tibial nerve: larger sciatic branch; medial → posterior → lateral to the artery; between FDL and FHL; divides into plantar nerves.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the course and branches of the posterior tibial artery.</summary>

**Answer:** A terminal branch of the popliteal artery beginning at the lower border of popliteus, passing deep to gastrocnemius and soleus, covered only by skin and fascia in the lower leg, behind the medial malleolus under the flexor retinaculum, ending as the medial and lateral plantar arteries. Branches: peroneal (largest), muscular, nutrient to the tibia, anastomotic, and the plantar arteries.

**Why:** Slides "Posterior tibial artery".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the peroneal artery.</summary>

**Answer:** A large branch arising close to the origin of the posterior tibial artery, descending behind the fibula within or behind FHL; it gives muscular branches to the lateral compartment, a nutrient artery to the fibula and a perforating branch through the interosseous membrane to the lower front of the leg, and takes part in the ankle anastomosis.

**Why:** Slide "Peroneal artery".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the tibial nerve in the leg.</summary>

**Answer:** The larger terminal branch of the sciatic nerve; it runs deep to gastrocnemius and soleus on tibialis posterior with the posterior tibial artery (medial, then posterior, then lateral to it), passes behind the medial malleolus between FDL and FHL under the flexor retinaculum and divides into medial and lateral plantar nerves. It supplies soleus, FDL, FHL and tibialis posterior, gives the medial calcaneal branch to the heel skin and an articular branch to the ankle.

**Why:** Slides "Tibial nerve" and "Branches".
</details>

## 6. Lateral compartment

- **2 muscles:** **peroneus longus** and **peroneus brevis**; pass **behind the lateral malleolus**; arise from the lateral surface of the fibula; held by the **superior and inferior peroneal retinacula**; **superficial peroneal nerve**; arterial supply from the **peroneal branch of the posterior tibial artery**; action = **eversion** and maintenance of the **transverse arch**.
- The **superficial peroneal nerve** is like the **musculocutaneous nerve of the arm**: it supplies **two muscles** and then becomes a cutaneous nerve.

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion |
|---|---|---|
| **Peroneus longus** (superficial) | **Upper ⅔** of the lateral surface of the fibula | Tendon crosses the sole **lateral → medial (4th layer)** to the **medial cuneiform + base of 1st metatarsal** (like TA, but from the plantar surface) → maintains the **transverse arch** |
| **Peroneus brevis** (deep) | **Lower ⅔** of the lateral surface of the fibula | **Tuberosity of the 5th metatarsal** |

- Two muscles insert at the **base of the 5th metatarsal: peroneus tertius and peroneus brevis**.
- **Peroneal retinacula:** **superior** — lateral malleolus above to the lateral calcaneus below; **inferior** — continuous in front with the inferior extensor retinaculum, behind attached to the lateral calcaneus, some fibres to the calcaneal tubercle forming a **septum between the PL and PB tendons**.

### Compartments at a glance
| Compartment | Nerve | Artery | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anterior** | Deep peroneal | Anterior tibial | Dorsiflexion |
| **Lateral** | Superficial peroneal | Peroneal (from posterior tibial) | Eversion |
| **Posterior** | Tibial | Posterior tibial | Plantar flexion / inversion |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Lateral compartment: peroneus longus (upper ⅔; crosses the sole; transverse arch) and brevis (lower ⅔; 5th metatarsal tuberosity).
- Superficial peroneal nerve supplies two muscles then becomes cutaneous (like the musculocutaneous nerve of the arm).
- Peroneal retinacula hold the tendons behind the lateral malleolus.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the muscles of the lateral compartment.</summary>

**Answer:** Peroneus longus (upper two thirds of the lateral fibula; tendon crosses the sole to the medial cuneiform and base of the 1st metatarsal) and peroneus brevis (lower two thirds; tuberosity of the 5th metatarsal). Both pass behind the lateral malleolus, evert the foot and maintain the transverse arch, supplied by the superficial peroneal nerve.

**Why:** Slides "Lateral compartment of leg".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is the superficial peroneal nerve compared with the musculocutaneous nerve of the arm?</summary>

**Answer:** Because, like the musculocutaneous nerve, it supplies two muscles (peroneus longus and brevis) and then becomes a cutaneous nerve.

**Why:** Slide "General features" of the lateral compartment.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the peroneal retinacula.</summary>

**Answer:** The superior retinaculum runs from the lateral malleolus to the lateral surface of the calcaneus; the inferior is continuous in front with the inferior extensor retinaculum and behind attaches to the calcaneus, with some fibres forming a septum between the peroneus longus and brevis tendons.

**Why:** Slide "Peroneal retinacula".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Tibialis anterior is the ONLY anterior-compartment muscle arising from the tibia; it inverts the foot (peroneus tertius everts).**
- **Anterior compartment = deep peroneal nerve; lateral = superficial peroneal; posterior = tibial.**
- **Gastrocnemius crosses TWO joints and flexes the knee; soleus does not act on the knee.**
- **Soleus = peripheral venous heart; gastrocnemius = propulsion.**
- **Achilles tendon: thickest, 15 cm, NO tendon sheath, 12× body weight.**
- **Popliteus is the only deep posterior muscle NOT passing behind the medial malleolus; it unlocks the knee.**
- **Tibialis posterior inserts into all tarsal bones EXCEPT the talus.**
- **Under the flexor retinaculum: TP, FDL, vessels/nerve, FHL (slide misprints EHL).**
- **Peroneus longus crosses the sole in the 4th layer; two muscles insert at the 5th metatarsal base (peroneus tertius and brevis).**
- **Deep peroneal nerve supplies the 1st web space; its relation to the artery is lateral–anterior–lateral.**
