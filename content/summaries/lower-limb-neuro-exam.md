# Clinical Skills — Lower Limb Neurological Examination — Exam Night Review

Clinical skills guide (Husham Salah Hashem and Ali Samer): **the six steps of the lower limb neurological examination — gait, inspection and palpation of muscles, tone, power, reflexes and sensory examination.** After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer).

---

## 1. Gait

- **Normal gait:** **smooth, symmetrical, energy-efficient**; **stance** (foot on ground), **swing** (foot off ground), **double stance** (both feet on ground). Method: **walk barefoot in a straight line, then with shoes**, observed from **front, behind and side**.
- **Abnormal gaits:**

| Gait | Feature | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Antalgic** | **Shortened stance on the painful side**; spine: slow short steps; hip: leans toward the affected side | **Pain** |
| **Structural** | — | **Limb-length discrepancy, joint fusion, malunion, contractures** |
| **Trendelenburg** | **Pelvis drops on the opposite side** | **Weak hip abductors** (hip OA, congenital dislocation) |
| **High-stepping (steppage)** | **Excessive knee elevation** | **Foot drop**, commonly **common peroneal nerve palsy** |
| **Spastic** | Stiff gait | **Increased tone, UMN lesions** (stroke, cerebral palsy) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Trendelenburg: pelvis drops on the opposite side (weak abductors).
- Steppage: foot drop.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the phases of a normal gait.</summary>

**Answer:** Stance phase (foot on the ground, weight-bearing), swing phase (foot off the ground) and double stance (both feet on the ground).

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe Trendelenburg gait.</summary>

**Answer:** The pelvis drops on the opposite side because of weak hip abductors, as in hip osteoarthritis, abductor weakness or congenital hip dislocation.

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the antalgic and high-stepping gaits.</summary>

**Answer:** Antalgic: shortened stance on the painful side; high-stepping: excessive knee lift from foot drop, commonly common peroneal nerve palsy.

**Why:** Slides 6-7.
</details>

## 2. Inspection and palpation of muscles

- **Prepare:** expose with dignity; **compare both sides**, proximally and distally.
- **Look:** **asymmetry, deformity (flexion deformity, pes cavus), bulk (wasting or hypertrophy), fasciculations, abnormal movements.**
- **Bulk:** **wasting** (LMN lesions); **hypertrophy** (athletes); **pseudohypertrophy** (large but weak: **muscular dystrophy**); **proximal wasting** (muscle diseases); **distal wasting** (**myotonic dystrophy**).
- **Fasciculations** = visible twitches (**LMN**); **benign fasciculations** have no weakness; **myokymia** = fine repetitive contractions.
- **Movements:** **myoclonus** (shock-like jerks); **asterixis** (brief loss of tone; **hepatic encephalopathy**); **physiological tremor** (fine; caffeine, anxiety); **essential tremor** (bilateral, on movement or posture); **parkinsonian tremor** (rest, asymmetric, reduced by movement).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Wasting and fasciculations = LMN signs.
- Pseudohypertrophy = muscular dystrophy.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What do you look for when inspecting muscles?</summary>

**Answer:** Asymmetry, deformity, bulk (wasting or hypertrophy), fasciculations and abnormal movements.

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare the types of tremor.</summary>

**Answer:** Physiological: fine, worse with caffeine or anxiety; essential: bilateral on movement or posture; parkinsonian: resting, asymmetric, reduced by voluntary movement.

**Why:** Slide 14.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Define asterixis and myoclonus.</summary>

**Answer:** Asterixis: brief loss of muscle tone (hepatic encephalopathy); myoclonus: sudden shock-like jerks.

**Why:** Slide 14.
</details>

## 3. Muscle tone

- **Tone** = resistance to **passive movement.** **Lower limb:** **roll the leg, briskly flex the knee**; **normal: heel moves up the bed; increased tone: heel lifts off the bed.**
- **Ankle clonus:** knee and ankle at **90°**, **briskly dorsiflex and partially evert**; **positive = rhythmic beats.** **< 6 beats unsustained (may be physiological)**; **sustained = UMN with spasticity.** **Ankle clonus is routine; knee clonus is uncommon.**
- **Myotonia:** fist then relax; **percuss the thenar eminence**; **delayed relaxation, thumb adduction, dimpling**; **myotonic dystrophy.**
- **Hypotonia:** **LMN lesions, cerebellar disease, early cerebral or spinal shock** (weakness, wasting, hyporeflexia).
- **Hypertonia:** **spasticity** (**clasp-knife: resistance rises with speed, sudden catch**; **UMN**; hyperreflexia, extensor plantar, clonus) vs **rigidity** (**throughout range even if slow**; **Parkinsonism**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Spasticity = velocity-dependent (UMN); rigidity = constant (Parkinsonism).
- Sustained clonus = UMN.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is ankle clonus tested and interpreted?</summary>

**Answer:** Knee and ankle at 90°, brisk dorsiflexion and partial eversion; rhythmic beats are positive; fewer than 6 beats may be physiological, sustained clonus indicates UMN damage.

**Why:** Slides 18-19.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Distinguish spasticity from rigidity.</summary>

**Answer:** Spasticity: resistance increases with rapid movement with a catch, UMN lesions; rigidity: resistance throughout the range even when slow, Parkinsonism.

**Why:** Slide 22.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the causes of hypotonia.</summary>

**Answer:** LMN lesions, cerebellar disease and early cerebral or spinal shock.

**Why:** Slide 21.
</details>

## 4. Muscle power

- **Principles:** varies with **age, occupation, fitness**; **MRC 0-5**; ask about **pain**; **compare sides**; **gravity first, then resistance.**
- **Hip:** **flexion** (raise straight leg against your push), **extension** (press down against your lift). **Knee (bent ~60°):** **flexion** and **extension** against resistance. **Ankle:** **dorsiflexion** (resist above toes), **plantar flexion**; **tarsal:** **eversion/inversion**; **trunk:** **sit up from lying.**

| MRC | Description |
|---|---|
| **0** | No contraction |
| **1** | Flicker, no movement |
| **2** | Movement with gravity eliminated |
| **3** | Movement against gravity |
| **4** | Movement against resistance but weaker than normal |
| **5** | Normal power |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- MRC 3 = against gravity; 4 = against resistance.
- Compare both sides and document each muscle group.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give the MRC scale.</summary>

**Answer:** 0 none, 1 flicker, 2 gravity eliminated, 3 against gravity, 4 against resistance but weak, 5 normal.

**Why:** Slide 31.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How is hip and knee power tested?</summary>

**Answer:** Hip flexion by raising the straight leg against pressure and extension by pressing against the bed; knee bent to 60° for flexion and extension against resistance.

**Why:** Slide 26.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How are ankle and trunk power tested?</summary>

**Answer:** Dorsiflexion and plantar flexion against resistance, eversion/inversion with the ankle stabilized, and sitting up from lying for trunk.

**Why:** Slide 29.
</details>

## 5. Reflexes

- **Preparation:** **supine, relaxed**; anxiety and pain increase reflexes; **identical limb positions**; hold hammer at the **far end** with a relaxed wrist.

| Reflex | Root |
|---|---|
| **Biceps** | **C5-C6** |
| **Supinator** | **C5-C6** |
| **Triceps** | **C7-C8** |
| **Knee (patellar)** | **L3-L4** |
| **Ankle (Achilles)** | **S1-S2** |

- **Knee jerk:** **leg flexed ~60°**, weight supported, **strike patellar tendon**, relaxed patient, legs not touching. **Ankle jerk:** **dorsiflex the ankle first, strike Achilles tendon.**
- **Grading:** **0** absent, **+** decreased, **++** normal, **+++** increased, **+/-** only with reinforcement.
- **Reinforcement (Jendrassik):** **interlock fingers and pull apart** (leg reflexes); **tight fist of the opposite hand** (arm reflexes); **don't call a reflex absent before reinforcement.**
- **Plantar reflex (S1-S2):** stroke **lateral border toward the little toe then across the forefoot**; **normal = great toe flexes down**; **Babinski = great toe extends up** (**UMN**); **fanning alone or whole-foot withdrawal is not a true Babinski.**
- **Interpretation:** **hyperreflexia = UMN** (spasticity, clonus, Hoffmann); **hyporeflexia/absent = LMN, mononeuropathy, radiculopathy**; **absent ankle jerk may suggest S1**; reduced ankle jerks can be normal in the elderly.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Knee L3-L4; ankle S1-S2; biceps C5-C6; triceps C7-C8.
- Babinski (upward great toe) = UMN lesion.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give the roots of the deep tendon reflexes.</summary>

**Answer:** Biceps and supinator C5-C6, triceps C7-C8, knee L3-L4, ankle S1-S2.

**Why:** Slide 34.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is reinforcement and when is it used?</summary>

**Answer:** Jendrassik maneuver (hands interlocked and pulled apart, or opposite fist) used when a reflex seems absent, before concluding it is absent.

**Why:** Slide 39.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the plantar reflex and the Babinski sign.</summary>

**Answer:** Stroke the lateral sole toward the little toe then across the forefoot; normal is great toe flexion, Babinski is extension (often with fanning) indicating a UMN lesion.

**Why:** Slide 40.
</details>

## 6. Sensory examination

- **Principles:** guided by history, motor signs and reflexes; patient **maps the disturbance**; look for **sensory level (spinal cord)**, **glove-and-stocking (peripheral neuropathy)**, **dermatome or nerve territory (root or nerve lesion).**

| Modality | Method |
|---|---|
| **Light touch** | **Eyes closed; cotton wool; irregular gentle dabbing**; distal to proximal, compare sides |
| **Pinprick** | **Fresh neurological pin (not hypodermic needle)**; **demonstrate first**; map from **reduced toward normal** |
| **Temperature** | **Cold metallic object (tuning fork)**; water tubes for detail |
| **Vibration** | **128-Hz fork, shown on sternum first**; **big toe → medial malleolus → patella → anterior iliac spine → lower chest/clavicle**; upper limb **index DIP → MCP → wrist → elbow → shoulder → clavicle** |
| **Joint position** | **Hold sides of the distal phalanx of the big toe**, move up/down, **eyes closed**; if impaired go **proximally (toe → ankle → knee → hip)** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sensory level = cord; glove-and-stocking = neuropathy.
- Vibration uses 128-Hz fork starting distally.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is light touch tested?</summary>

**Answer:** With eyes closed, cotton wool applied as irregular gentle dabs from distal to proximal, comparing both sides.

**Why:** Slide 44.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How is vibration sense tested in the lower limb?</summary>

**Answer:** A 128-Hz tuning fork shown on the sternum then placed on the big toe, moving proximally (medial malleolus, patella, anterior iliac spine, lower chest/clavicle) if not felt.

**Why:** Slide 47.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is joint position sense tested?</summary>

**Answer:** Hold the sides of the distal phalanx of the big toe, move it up or down while the patient's eyes are closed and the patient states the direction; move proximally if impaired.

**Why:** Slide 49.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Steppage = foot drop; Trendelenburg = pelvis drops on the opposite side.**
- **Spasticity (velocity-dependent, UMN) vs rigidity (constant, Parkinsonism).**
- **Knee jerk L3-L4; ankle S1-S2.**
- **Unsustained clonus (< 6 beats) may be physiological.**
- **Never call a reflex absent before reinforcement.**
- **Use a neurological pin, not a hypodermic needle.**
