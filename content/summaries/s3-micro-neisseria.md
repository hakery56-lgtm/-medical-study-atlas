## 1. General features of the Neisseria

- ⭐ **Gram-negative, non-motile, kidney-shaped diplococci**, about **0.8 µm**; two medically important species: **N. gonorrhoeae (gonococci)** and **N. meningitidis (meningococci)**. Other species are normal respiratory flora that rarely or never cause disease.
- **Culture:** enriched media (e.g. **chocolate agar**), 48 h → convex, glistening, elevated, mucoid colonies, **1–5 mm**, non-pigmented, transparent or opaque, **non-hemolytic**.
- **Growth:** best **aerobic**, needs **5% CO₂**; oxidize carbohydrates → **acid, no gas**; **fastidious** (heated blood, hemin, animal proteins); sensitive to **desiccation, moist heat, sunlight, disinfectants**.
- ⭐ **Kovac's oxidase test** (tetramethylparaphenylenediamine hydrochloride; detects **cytochrome oxidase**) → Neisseria turn **dark purple** rapidly.

| Feature | N. gonorrhoeae | N. meningitidis |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar used | **Glucose only** | **Glucose and maltose** |
| Capsule | Not mentioned on the slides | **Encapsulated** |
| Reservoir | Human genital tract | Human nasopharynx (obligate commensal) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Gram-negative kidney-shaped diplococci, non-motile, oxidase-positive.
- Fastidious: chocolate agar, 5% CO₂, 37 °C.
- Maltose utilization separates meningococci from gonococci.

</div>

## 2. Neisseria gonorrhoeae — pathogenesis and virulence

- **Reservoir:** humans (genital tract). **Transmission:** **sexual contact** and **birth** (newborn infected in the birth canal).
- Clinical specimens/selective subculture → small colonies of **piliated** bacteria; non-selective subculture also gives larger colonies of **non-piliated** gonococci.
- **Virulence factors:**

| Factor | Role |
|---|---|
| **Pili** | Attachment to host cells; resist phagocytosis |
| **Por protein** | Prevents phagosome–lysosome fusion (no killing in neutrophils); serum resistance by binding **C3b and C4b** |
| **Opa proteins** | Adhesion to host cell receptors |
| **Rmp (protein III)** | Associates with Por to form surface pores |
| **IgA1 protease** | Inactivates **IgA1**, main mucosal antibody |
| **Lipooligosaccharide (LOS)** | Endotoxin; **lacks long O-antigen side chains**; responsible for toxicity |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Transmitted by sexual contact and at birth.
- Pili and Opa = adhesion; Por = survival inside neutrophils and serum resistance.
- LOS is the endotoxin (no O-antigen).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is N. gonorrhoeae transmitted?</summary>

**Answer:** By sexual contact and at birth, when a newborn passes through an infected birth canal.

**Why:** Slide "Pathogenesis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain how the Por proteins help gonococci survive.</summary>

**Answer:** They prevent phagosome–lysosome fusion, so gonococci are not killed inside neutrophils, and they bind C3b and C4b, conferring resistance to the bactericidal activity of normal human serum.

**Why:** Slide "Important virulence factors".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How does gonococcal LOS differ from LPS?</summary>

**Answer:** It lacks the long O-antigen side chains (hence lipooligosaccharide); its endotoxic effects cause the toxicity of gonococcal infection.

**Why:** Slide "Important virulence factors" (continued).
</details>

## 3. Gonococcal diseases, diagnosis, treatment and prevention

- **Sites:** genitourinary tract, eye, rectum, throat → acute suppuration, then chronic inflammation and fibrosis.
- **Men:** urethritis with **yellow creamy pus** and painful urination (may be asymptomatic).
- **Women:** endocervix → urethra, vagina (**mucopurulent discharge**) → uterine tubes: **salpingitis, fibrosis, obliteration**; ⭐ **infertility in 20%**; chronic cervicitis/proctitis often asymptomatic.
- **Gonococcal bacteremia:** skin lesions of hands, forearms, feet, legs. **Ophthalmia neonatorum:** newborn eye infection from the birth canal; rapid, **blindness if untreated**.
- **Diagnosis:** specimens (urethra, cervix, rectum, conjunctiva, throat, synovial fluid; **blood culture** in systemic illness); **Gram smear: diplococci within pus cells**; culture on **chocolate, Thayer-Martin, modified Thayer-Martin (MTM)** at 37 °C, 5% CO₂, identify at 48 h by Gram stain, oxidase, co-agglutination, immunofluorescence; **NAAT** (rapid, better detection, urine usable).
- **Treatment:** **ceftriaxone 250 mg IM** single dose, or **cefixime 400 mg orally** single dose; for possible chlamydia add **azithromycin 1 g** once or **doxycycline 100 mg twice daily × 7 days**.
- **Prevention:** early diagnosis and treatment, avoid multiple partners, **condoms (partial protection)**, chemoprophylaxis (limited by resistance); newborns: **0.5% erythromycin** or **1% tetracycline** ointment.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Men: purulent urethritis; women: cervicitis → salpingitis → infertility (20%).
- Diplococci inside pus cells on Gram smear; chocolate/Thayer-Martin culture; NAAT on urine.
- Ceftriaxone IM or cefixime PO (+ azithromycin/doxycycline for chlamydia).

</div>

## 4. Neisseria meningitidis — features and pathogenesis

- ⭐ **Gram-negative, encapsulated diplococcus; obligate human commensal; nasopharynx = portal of entry;** utilizes **maltose** (unlike gonococci).
- **13 serogroups** by capsular polysaccharide; disease-associated: **A, B, C, X, Y, W-135**.
- **Transmission:** **respiratory droplets**; **5–10%** of healthy people carry it; after nasopharyngeal colonization it reaches the blood, then the meninges; few carriers become ill.
- **Virulence factors:** **pili and Opa/Opc** (adhesins); **capsule** (antiphagocytic, serum resistance); **IgA protease** (inhibits IgA, promotes penetration); **LOS endotoxin** (fever, septic shock).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Encapsulated, maltose-positive, nasopharynx colonizer (5–10% carriers).
- 13 serogroups; A, B, C, X, Y, W-135 cause disease.
- Capsule = anti-phagocytic; LOS = fever and shock.

</div>

## 5. Meningococcal diseases, diagnosis, treatment and prevention

- **Carriage state:** transient nasopharyngeal flora without disease. If it reaches blood → **bacteremia**.
- ⭐ **Fulminant meningococcemia:** high fever + **hemorrhagic rash**. **Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome:** **DIC + circulatory collapse** (adrenal hemorrhage). **Meningococcal meningitis** = most common complication of meningococcemia.
- **Specimens:** blood (culture); **spinal fluid** (smear, culture, chemistry); **nasopharyngeal swab** (carrier surveys); puncture of **petechiae** (smear, culture). **Gram smear:** neisseriae within polymorphs or extracellular.
- **Culture:** blood and CSF on agar; **modified MTM with antibiotics** for nasopharyngeal cultures; identification by Gram stain, **oxidase**, carbohydrate oxidation, agglutination.
- **Serology:** antibodies to capsular polysaccharide (latex agglutination, hemagglutination, bactericidal activity); **PCR** rapid and sensitive.
- **Treatment:** **penicillin G**; if allergic: **chloramphenicol** or a **third-generation cephalosporin (cefotaxime, ceftriaxone)**.
- **Prevention:** capsular polysaccharide vaccines for **A, C, Y, W-135**; **Menveo** (A, C, Y, W135 conjugated to diphtheria toxoid); **Bexsero** (protein-based) for **group B**; **rifampin or ciprofloxacin** chemoprophylaxis for close contacts.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hemorrhagic rash + DIC + collapse = Waterhouse-Friderichsen.
- CSF Gram smear/culture and blood culture; PCR and latex agglutination help.
- Penicillin G; vaccines A/C/Y/W-135 and B; rifampin or ciprofloxacin for contacts.

</div>

**Answer:** Carriage without disease; bacteremia; fulminant meningococcemia with high fever and hemorrhagic rash; Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome with DIC and circulatory collapse; and meningitis, the most common complication of meningococcemia.

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Gonococci use glucose only; meningococci use glucose AND maltose.**
- **Gonococcal LOS has no long O-antigen**; meningococci have a **capsule** (13 serogroups).
- **Infertility 20%** in women with gonococcal salpingitis.
- **Ceftriaxone 250 mg IM / cefixime 400 mg PO** for gonorrhea; **penicillin G** for meningococci.
- **Group B** is covered by **Bexsero**, not by Menveo (A, C, Y, W135).
