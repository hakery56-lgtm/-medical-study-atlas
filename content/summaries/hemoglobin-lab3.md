## 1. Hemoglobin and methods of estimation

- **Hb:** major constituent of the red cell cytoplasm (**~90% of dry weight**); **heme + globin**; **tetramer** of **two pairs of globin chains**, each with a **heme (ferrous iron + protoporphyrin)**.
- **Methods:** **manual** (**cyanmethemoglobin**, **acid hematin**) and **automated** (**Coulter counter**, **Hb meter**). **Other grouping:** **iron content**, **color development** (**Sahli's/acid hematin, cyanmethemoglobin, oxyhemoglobin, alkaline hematin**), **oxygen-combining capacity**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hb = tetramer; heme has Fe²⁺.
- Cyanmethemoglobin = standard manual method.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the structure of hemoglobin.</summary>

**Answer:** A tetramer of two pairs of globin chains, each bound to a heme, which is ferrous iron in protoporphyrin.

**Why:** Slides 2–3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Classify the methods of Hb estimation.</summary>

**Answer:** Manual (cyanmethemoglobin, acid hematin) and automated (Coulter counter, Hb meter); or by iron content, color development and oxygen-combining capacity.

**Why:** Slides 4–5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Name the color-development methods.</summary>

**Answer:** Sahli's (acid hematin), cyanmethemoglobin, oxyhemoglobin and alkaline hematin.

## 2. Cyanmethemoglobin method

- **Accurate, commonly used, recommended by ICSH.**
- **Principle:** **Hb (Fe²⁺) → [K₃Fe(CN)₆, Drabkin's solution] → methemoglobin (Fe³⁺) → [KCN] → cyanmethemoglobin (HiCN).**
- **Measurement:** **spectrophotometer at 540 nm** vs a **standard HiCN solution**.
- **Procedure:** **20 µL blood + 5 mL Drabkin solution → mix well, leave 5–10 min → read sample and standard at 540 nm.**
- **Calculation:** **Hb (g/dL) = (A sample ÷ A standard) × standard concentration.**
- **Equipment:** Drabkin's reagent, micropipette, spectrophotometer.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 20 µL blood : 5 mL Drabkin; 540 nm; 5–10 minutes.
- Product: HiCN.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Explain the principle.</summary>

**Answer:** Ferricyanide in Drabkin's solution oxidizes Hb (Fe²⁺) to methemoglobin (Fe³⁺), which reacts with cyanide to form stable cyanmethemoglobin.

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the procedure.</summary>

**Answer:** Mix 20 µL blood with 5 mL Drabkin solution, leave 5–10 minutes, read sample and standard at 540 nm and calculate Hb.

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give the calculation.</summary>

**Answer:** Hb = absorbance of sample ÷ absorbance of standard × standard concentration (g/dL).

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

## 3. Normal ranges, causes and red cell morphology

| Group | Normal Hb |
|---|---|
| **Female** | **12–16 g/dL (7.4–9.9 mmol/L); 120–160 g/L** |
| **Male** | **13–18 g/dL (8.1–11.2 mmol/L); 130–180 g/L** |
| **Pregnancy** | **Decreased (dilutional)** |
| **Elderly** | **Slightly decreased** |
| **Newborn** | **Increased** |

- **↓ Hb:** **severe hemorrhage; systemic disease** (leukemia, lymphoma, uremia, cirrhosis, hyperthyroidism, carcinomatosis, SLE); **hemolysis** (incompatible transfusion, drugs/chemicals, bacteremia, artificial heart valves); **anemia.**
- **↑ Hb:** **polycythemia, high altitude, CCF, hemoconcentration (severe burns), COPD.**
- **Erythrocytes:** **normocytic** (uniform, central pallor < ⅓ of cell); **hypochromic** (low Hb: **IDA, thalassemia, sideroblastic anemia**); **"hyperchromic"** shown with **hereditary spherocytosis**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Female 12–16; male 13–18 g/dL.
- Hypochromic: IDA, thalassemia, sideroblastic.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give the normal ranges.</summary>

**Answer:** Females 12–16 g/dL, males 13–18 g/dL; decreased in pregnancy (dilutional) and slightly in the elderly; increased in newborns.

**Why:** Slide 12.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List causes of increased Hb.</summary>

**Answer:** Polycythemia, high altitude, congestive cardiac failure, hemoconcentration such as severe burns, and COPD.

**Why:** Slide 14.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Name causes of hypochromic cells.</summary>

**Answer:** Iron deficiency anemia, thalassemia and sideroblastic anemia.

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

## 4. Sources of error and the case

- **Abnormal results:** **tourniquet > 1 min → hemoconcentration; lipemic samples and leukocytosis → false increases; high altitude and smoking ↑ Hb; sample hemolysis alters results.**
- **Drugs ↑ Hb:** **gentamicin, methyldopa.** **Drugs ↓ Hb:** **antibiotics, antineoplastics, apresoline, aspirin, indomethacin, MAO inhibitors, primaquine, rifampin, sulfonamides.**
- **Case:** 35-year-old woman, **24 weeks pregnant**, fatigue, **craves ice (pagophagia)**, pale conjunctiva, **spooning of nails**, stool occult blood negative; **Hb 7.1 g/dL, Hct 23%, MCV 74 fl (85–95), RDW 17.1% (13–15)** → **iron deficiency anemia** (standard teaching: microcytic anemia with high RDW).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Tourniquet > 1 min raises Hb.
- Case = iron deficiency anemia.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give sources of error in Hb measurement.</summary>

**Answer:** Tourniquet over 1 minute, lipemic samples, leukocytosis, high altitude, smoking, sample hemolysis and certain drugs.

**Why:** Slide 18.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the diagnosis in the case and why?</summary>

**Answer:** Iron deficiency anemia: pregnancy, pagophagia, koilonychia, pallor, Hb 7.1 g/dL, low MCV 74 fl and raised RDW.

**Why:** Slide 19 (explanation: standard teaching).
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is RDW raised?</summary>

**Answer:** Because of anisocytosis, increased variation in red cell size.

**Why:** Standard teaching.
