## 1. Open fracture: priorities and immediate complications

- **Open fracture:** **communicates with the external environment through a wound** (the wound may be distant from the fracture) = **time-critical emergency**: **trauma assessment, hemorrhage control, limb perfusion first.**
- **Wound:** **sterile dressing**; **avoid repeated probing**; **document neurovascular status before and after splinting**; assess **compartment syndrome**; involve **orthopaedics** and follow protocols.
- **Immediate life-threatening complications:** **hemorrhage and hypovolemic shock** (femoral shaft can hide **1–2 litres**), **associated injuries** (chest, abdomen, pelvis, head, other long bones), **vascular injury** (pulses, perfusion, temperature, capillary refill), **compartment syndrome** (severe pain, **pain on passive stretch**, tense compartments), **fat embolism syndrome** (respiratory distress, neurological change, petechial rash).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Resuscitate first; sterile dressing; document neurovascular status.
- Compartment syndrome: pain on passive stretch.

</div>

## 2. Gustilo–Anderson classification and complications

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **I** | **Clean wound < 1 cm, low-energy, minimal soft-tissue damage** |
| **II** | **Wound > 1 cm without extensive soft-tissue damage** |
| **III** | **High-energy, extensive soft-tissue damage/contamination (segmental, farm, crush, vascular injuries)** |
| **IIIA** | **Adequate soft-tissue coverage** |
| **IIIB** | **Periosteal stripping/bone exposure needing coverage** |
| **IIIC** | **Arterial injury requiring repair** |

- **Final grading after operative wound assessment.**
- **Early/systemic complications:** hemorrhage and shock, vascular injury and **acute limb ischemia**, nerve injury, **compartment syndrome**, infection (cellulitis, deep infection, sepsis), **tetanus**, **fat embolism**, **AKI**, **VTE (DVT/PE)**, fixation/coverage problems.
- **Delayed/functional:** **delayed union, nonunion, malunion; osteomyelitis; soft-tissue necrosis, repeat debridement, flaps; stiffness, weakness, limb-length discrepancy, chronic pain; deformity and psychological effects.**
- **Risk:** **severe soft-tissue injury, contamination, devascularization, smoking, delayed treatment.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- IIIC = arterial injury.
- Delayed: nonunion, malunion, osteomyelitis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Outline the Gustilo–Anderson classification.</summary>

**Answer:** I clean wound under 1 cm; II wound over 1 cm without extensive damage; III high-energy extensive damage, subdivided A (adequate coverage), B (bone exposure needing coverage), C (arterial injury needing repair).

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List delayed complications.</summary>

**Answer:** Delayed union, nonunion, malunion, osteomyelitis, soft-tissue necrosis, stiffness, weakness, limb-length discrepancy, chronic pain and deformity.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which factors raise the risk of complications?</summary>

**Answer:** Severe soft-tissue injury, contamination, devascularization, smoking and delayed appropriate treatment.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

## 3. Initial management

- **ATLS: airway, breathing, circulation; control bleeding, treat shock.**
- **IV antibiotics ASAP, ideally within 1 hour** (local protocol, allergy history).
- **Tetanus status:** vaccine and/or **TIG** when indicated.
- **Wound:** sterile **saline-moistened dressing** + occlusive layer; **realign and splint** (e.g., Thomas splint with traction); record **pulses, capillary refill, motor/sensory function** and repeat after interventions.
- **Definitive:** **urgent irrigation and debridement**, **fracture stabilization** (external or internal fixation), **soft-tissue coverage plan**, vascular/plastic surgery as needed; **restore perfusion urgently** if vascular injury; **cultures guided by clinical suspicion**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Antibiotics within 1 hour.
- Debridement, stabilization, coverage.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Outline the initial management.</summary>

**Answer:** ATLS resuscitation, early IV antibiotics within 1 hour, tetanus assessment, moist sterile dressing, realign and splint, record neurovascular status, then urgent debridement and stabilization.

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How is the fracture stabilized?</summary>

**Answer:** By external or internal fixation depending on the injury and the patient's condition.

**Why:** Slide 12.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the Thomas splint?</summary>

**Answer:** A splint devised by H.O. Thomas, initially for tuberculosis of the knee, used for temporary immobilization with traction.

**Why:** Image slides 14–15.
</details>

## 4. Tetanus

- **Why:** **Clostridium tetani spores** enter contaminated wounds; **anaerobic** conditions allow toxin; **tetanospasmin blocks inhibitory neurotransmission** → **rigidity and painful spasms**; open fractures are **dirty/major wounds**.
- **Prevention:** **active (vaccine)** and **passive (TIG)**; **antibiotics do not replace either.**

| Wound / history | Action |
|---|---|
| **Clean minor, series complete, last dose ≥ 10 years** | **Booster** |
| **Dirty/major (incl. open fracture), series complete, last dose ≥ 5 years** | **Booster** |
| **Unknown, unvaccinated or incomplete series** | **Tetanus-containing vaccine for any wound** |
| **Dirty/major + unknown/incomplete vaccination, or severe immunodeficiency/HIV** | **Add TIG** |

- **TIG** = **temporary passive antibodies**, **no lasting immunity** → **complete the vaccine series.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Clean ≥ 10 y; dirty ≥ 5 y.
- TIG adds temporary passive cover.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How does tetanus develop?</summary>

**Answer:** C. tetani spores enter a contaminated wound, produce tetanospasmin under anaerobic conditions, and the toxin blocks inhibitory neurotransmission causing rigidity and spasms.

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give the prophylaxis rules.</summary>

**Answer:** Booster if the last dose was 10 years or more ago for clean wounds and 5 years or more for dirty wounds; vaccine for unknown or incomplete history; TIG added for dirty wounds with unknown or incomplete history or severe immunodeficiency.

**Why:** Slide 18.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Can antibiotics replace the vaccine?</summary>

**Answer:** No; antibiotics do not replace tetanus vaccine or TIG, and neither replaces good wound care.

**Why:** Slides 16 and 28.
</details>

## 5. Septic arthritis

- **Definition:** **infection within a native joint**, usually **bacterial**, rapidly destroying cartilage; **organisms:** **S. aureus, streptococci** (gonococcus and Gram-negatives by age/risk).
- **Risk factors:** **joint disease, prosthesis, immunosuppression, diabetes, skin infection, recent surgery/injection, bacteremia.**
- **Presentation:** **acute severe monoarticular pain, swelling, warmth, tenderness; pain on active AND passive movement**; fever possible; **knee common**; children, elderly and immunocompromised may be subtle. **Differentials:** crystal arthritis, inflammatory arthritis, hemarthrosis, cellulitis, osteomyelitis.
- **Investigations:** **arthrocentesis before antibiotics if stable**; synovial fluid **cell count, Gram stain, culture, crystals** (crystals do not exclude infection); **blood cultures, CBC, CRP/ESR** (normal markers do not exclude early disease); **X-ray; ultrasound** (effusion, guide hip aspiration); **MRI must not delay treatment**.
- **Management:** **emergency orthopaedic assessment and admission; IV empiric antibiotics after cultures when feasible; urgent joint drainage; tailor to culture;** monitor pain, fever, ROM, CRP; **special pathways for children, hip, sepsis, prosthetic joints, immunocompromised.**
- **Pathway:** recognize → bloods/cultures/aspirate → **do not delay antibiotics if septic/unstable** → empiric IV antibiotics → drain/washout → review and look for adjacent osteomyelitis.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hot painful joint = septic until proven otherwise.
- Aspirate, antibiotics, drain.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the presentation of septic arthritis.</summary>

**Answer:** Acute severe monoarticular pain with swelling, warmth and tenderness, pain on both active and passive movement, inability to bear weight and possible fever; the knee is most common.

**Why:** Slide 22.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How is septic arthritis investigated?</summary>

**Answer:** Urgent arthrocentesis before antibiotics if stable (cell count, Gram stain, culture, crystals), blood cultures, CBC, CRP/ESR, X-ray and ultrasound; MRI must not delay treatment.

**Why:** Slide 23.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Outline the management.</summary>

**Answer:** Emergency orthopaedic admission, IV empiric antibiotics after cultures when feasible, urgent joint drainage, then tailor antibiotics to cultures and monitor response.

**Why:** Slides 25 and 27.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Clean wound booster at 10 years; dirty wound booster at 5 years.**
- **TIG does not give lasting immunity; antibiotics do not replace the vaccine.**
- **Normal CRP/ESR does not exclude early septic arthritis; crystals do not exclude infection.**
- **Do not delay antibiotics for imaging in a septic patient.**
- The slides contain typing slips ("sever", "litter", stray "??" marks); the meaning is severe, litre.
