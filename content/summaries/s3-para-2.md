## 1. Classification and important species

### Classification by site of infection
| Group | Subgroup | Genera and species |
|---|---|---|
| **Blood and tissue protozoa** | **Flagellates** | ***Leishmania*: *L. donovani, L. tropica, L. braziliensis*; *Trypanosoma*: *T. brucei, T. cruzi*** |
| **Blood and tissue protozoa** | **Non-flagellates (sporozoa)** | ***Plasmodium*: *P. vivax, P. ovale, P. falciparum, P. malariae*; *Toxoplasma gondii*** |
| **Intestinal and urogenital protozoa** | **Intestinal** | **Amoeba → *Entamoeba*: *E. histolytica, E. gingivalis, E. coli*; Flagellate → *Giardia lamblia*; Ciliate → *Balantidium coli*** |
| **Intestinal and urogenital protozoa** | **Urogenital** | ***Trichomonas*: *T. vaginalis, T. hominis, T. tenax*** |

### Important protozoa (1) — intestinal, urogenital, sporozoa
| Protozoa | Habitat | Infective stage / entry | Disease | Reservoir | Diagnostic stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ ***Entamoeba histolytica*** | **large intestine** | **cyst, orally** | **amoebic dysentery + extra-intestinal** | **humans** | **cyst and trophozoite in stool** |
| ⭐ ***Balantidium coli*** | **large intestine** | **cyst, orally** | **balantidium dysentery** | **animals (pigs)** | **cyst and trophozoite in stool** |
| ⭐ ***Giardia lamblia*** | **small intestine** | **cyst, orally** | **diarrhea, malabsorption, steatorrhea** | **humans** | **cyst and trophozoite in stool** |
| ⭐ ***Trichomonas vaginalis*** | **vagina and urethra** | **trophozoite, STD** | **vaginitis/urethritis** | **humans** | **trophozoite from vagina/urethra** |
| ⭐ ***Plasmodium* spp.** | **liver and RBC** | **sporozoite, skin** | **malaria** | **humans** | **ring stage in peripheral blood** |
| ⭐ ***Toxoplasma gondii*** | **obligate intracellular** | **oocyst/tissue cyst, orally** | **congenital toxoplasmosis** | **animals** | **tachyzoite and tissue cyst from bone marrow** |

### Important protozoa (2) — blood and tissue flagellates
| Protozoa | Habitat | Infective stage | Disease | Reservoir | Diagnostic stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ ***L. donovani*** | **reticuloendothelial system** | **promastigote, skin** | **kala-azar** | animals and humans | **amastigote** |
| ⭐ ***L. tropica*** | **skin** | promastigote, skin | **cutaneous leishmaniasis** | humans and animals | amastigote |
| ⭐ ***L. braziliensis*** (espundia) | **naso-oral mucosa** | promastigote, skin | **mucocutaneous leishmaniasis** | humans and animals | amastigote |
| ⭐ ***T. brucei*** | **blood, lymph node, CNS** | **metacyclic trypomastigote, skin** | **sleeping sickness** | animals and humans | **trypomastigote** |
| ⭐ ***T. cruzi*** | **macrophage, bone marrow, nerve, heart, colon** | metacyclic trypomastigote, skin | **Chagas + congenital disease** | animals and humans | trypomastigote |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Blood/tissue: flagellates (Leishmania, Trypanosoma) and sporozoa (Plasmodium, Toxoplasma). Intestinal: Entamoeba, Giardia, Balantidium. Urogenital: Trichomonas.
- Intestinal protozoa: cyst orally; diagnosis = cyst + trophozoite in stool. Trichomonas: trophozoite by STD. Plasmodium: sporozoite via skin; ring stage in blood.
- Leishmania: promastigote infective, amastigote diagnostic; Trypanosoma: metacyclic trypomastigote infective, trypomastigote diagnostic.

</div>

## 2. Hosts and life cycles

### Types of hosts
| Host | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Definitive (final)** | where the **adult parasite lives and reproduces sexually** | ***Entamoeba histolytica, Giardia, E. vermicularis, H. nana*** (human); ***Plasmodium* in female Anopheles**; ***Toxoplasma* in the cat**; ***Leishmania* in humans (slide)** |
| ⭐ **Intermediate** | parasite lives as **immature/larval phase** with **asexual reproduction** | ***Plasmodium* in humans**; ***Schistosoma* in snails** |
| ⭐ **Reservoir** | **animal that is not affected** and maintains the parasite in nature as a source for humans (usually wild animals); **carrier host** in humans | ***Leishmania* in animals**; ***Trichinella spiralis* in pork**; *E. histolytica* carriers |

### Life cycles
- ⭐ **One host (direct):** **intestinal protozoa** (*E. histolytica, G. lamblia, B. coli*) and helminth *H. nana* — only the human; **trophozoite and cyst stages** (cyst = infective and diagnostic).
- ⭐ **Two hosts (indirect):**
  - ***Plasmodium*:** human = **intermediate host (asexual)**; **female Anopheles = definitive host (sexual)**; infective = **sporozoite**; diagnostic = **ring stage**.
  - ***T. brucei*:** human = **definitive host (as written on the slide), asexual**; **tsetse fly = intermediate host**; infective = **metacyclic trypomastigote**; diagnostic = **trypomastigote**.
  - ***Leishmania*:** human = definitive host (slide); **female sand fly = intermediate host**; infective and diagnostic = **promastigote** (as written on the slide).
  - ***Schistosoma*:** human = **definitive (sexual)**; **snail = intermediate (asexual)**; infective = **cercaria in water**; diagnostic = **eggs in stool/urine**.
- **Two or more intermediate hosts:** ***Paragonimus westermani* and *Diphyllobothrium latum*** — human definitive; **snail = 1st intermediate; fish = 2nd intermediate**; infective = **metacercaria** (*Paragonimus*) and **plerocercoid** in fish (*D. latum*).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Definitive host: adult/sexual stage; intermediate: larval/asexual; reservoir: unaffected animal maintaining the parasite.
- One host: Entamoeba, Giardia, Balantidium, H. nana. Two hosts: Plasmodium (human intermediate, Anopheles definitive), Trypanosoma (tsetse), Leishmania (sand fly), Schistosoma (snail).
- Paragonimus/D. latum: snail then fish as intermediate hosts.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define definitive, intermediate and reservoir hosts with examples.</summary>

**Answer:** Definitive host: where the adult parasite lives and reproduces sexually (E. histolytica, Giardia in humans; Plasmodium in Anopheles; Toxoplasma in the cat). Intermediate host: harbors the immature/larval phase with asexual reproduction (Plasmodium in humans; Schistosoma in snails). Reservoir host: an animal unaffected by the infection that maintains the parasite in nature and is a source for humans (Leishmania in animals; Trichinella in pork).

**Why:** Slide "Types of hosts".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the life cycle of Plasmodium regarding hosts and stages.</summary>

**Answer:** Two hosts: the human is the intermediate host with asexual reproduction (schizogony); the female Anopheles is the definitive host with sexual reproduction (sporogony). The infective stage is the sporozoite and the diagnostic stage is the ring stage in peripheral blood.

**Why:** Indirect life cycle.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the life cycle of Schistosoma and of Paragonimus/D. latum.</summary>

**Answer:** Schistosoma: human definitive host (sexual), snail intermediate host (asexual); infective stage cercaria in water, diagnostic stage eggs in stool or urine. Paragonimus westermani and D. latum need two intermediate hosts (snail then fish); the infective stages are metacercaria (Paragonimus) and plerocercoid larva in fish (D. latum).

**Why:** Helminths with more than one intermediate host.
</details>

## 3. Pathogenicity

- Parasitic infections may be **acute, subacute, chronic, latent or recurrent**, depending on **parasite density, site of infection and tissue-damage mechanisms**; **density** depends on species, **host age/condition**, and parasite competition.

### Mechanisms of damage
1. **Destruction of red blood cells** — *Plasmodium* (malaria).
2. **Destruction of skin tissue** — **hookworm filariform larva** (skin ancylostomiasis), **Schistosoma cercaria**.
3. ⭐ **Lytic necrosis** — parasitic **enzymes** destroy tissue: ***E. histolytica* → hepatic abscess and deep ulcers of the large intestine**; cercaria/filariform larva penetrating skin.
4. **Trauma** — **hookworms (*Ancylostoma duodenale*, *Necator americanus*)** attach to **jejunal mucosa** → villus damage and bleeding.
5. **Nutritional robbing** — helminths causing **anemia** (ancylostomiasis, necatoriasis).
6. **Inflammation** due to **secondary bacterial infection**.
7. **Inflammation and obstruction of lymphatics** — **elephantiasis (*Wuchereria bancrofti*)**; ***S. haematobium*** → **sandy patches / bladder granuloma**.
- **Allergic manifestations:** **anaphylactic shock on rupture of a hydatid cyst** (esp. brain), **eosinophilic pneumonia in *Ascaris* infection**.
- **Neoplasia:** **bile duct carcinoma (liver fluke)**, **urinary bladder cancer (*S. haematobium*)**.
- **Brain/spinal inflammation** from toxins — **sleeping sickness (*T. brucei*)**.
- **Hematuria** — ***S. haematobium*** eggs damage the bladder wall.
- **Physical obstruction** — **adult *Ascaris* in the small intestine**; **cerebral malaria** (*P. falciparum* blocks brain capillaries).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Mechanisms: RBC destruction (malaria), skin destruction, lytic necrosis (E. histolytica), trauma (hookworm), nutritional robbing (anemia), inflammation, lymphatic obstruction (filaria), allergy, neoplasia, obstruction.
- S. haematobium: hematuria, bladder granuloma, bladder cancer. Ascaris: eosinophilic pneumonia, intestinal obstruction. P. falciparum: cerebral malaria.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the mechanisms by which parasites damage the host.</summary>

**Answer:** Destruction of red cells (Plasmodium), destruction of skin tissue (hookworm larva, Schistosoma cercaria), lytic necrosis by enzymes (E. histolytica), trauma (hookworm attachment), nutritional robbing (anemia), inflammation, lymphatic obstruction (elephantiasis), allergic reactions, neoplasia and physical obstruction.

**Why:** Slide "Pathogenicity".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the pathogenic effects of Schistosoma haematobium.</summary>

**Answer:** Eggs damage the bladder wall causing hematuria, sandy patches/bladder granuloma and, in chronic infection, urinary bladder cancer.

**Why:** Example of neoplasia and hematuria.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give examples of allergic and obstructive pathology in parasitic disease.</summary>

**Answer:** Allergic: anaphylactic shock on rupture of a hydatid cyst (especially brain) and eosinophilic pneumonia in Ascaris infection. Obstructive: adult Ascaris blocking the small intestine and cerebral malaria from P. falciparum blocking brain capillaries.

**Why:** Immune and mechanical effects.
</details>

## 4. Treatment, prophylaxis, control and vaccination

### Treatment
- **Specific antiparasitic agents**, under **medical supervision**.
  - **Tapeworm:** **praziquantel** (paralyses the adult worm which passes in stool).
  - **Chagas disease (*T. cruzi*):** **nifurtimox** (stimulates production of H₂O₂ that kills the parasite).
  - **Roundworms:** **mebendazole and albendazole**.
  - ⭐ **Metronidazole (Flagyl):** **intestinal protozoa (giardiasis, balantidiasis) and *Trichomonas vaginalis*** — **damages protozoal DNA**.
  - **Antimalarials (chloroquine)** act on different Plasmodium stages; **treat and prevent** malaria.
  - **Surgical removal** for **hydatid cysts** that can't be cured by drugs.
  - Antiprotozoals given **orally or IM**.

### Prophylaxis
- Prevent **food/water contamination** with infective stages in stool; **detect/treat carriers** and exclude them from **food handling**; **health education**; **proper waste/feces disposal**; **hand washing** and diaper disposal; **STD-like prevention** for vaginal trichomoniasis.

### Control
- **Early diagnosis and treatment** (↓ reservoir/carriers); **insect control** (biological/chemical, insecticidal spraying); **destruction of animal reservoirs**; **personal protection** (thick clothing, bed nets, window mesh); **vaccination**; **sanitary disposal of stool** (not as direct fertilizer); **destroy intermediate/reservoir hosts and vectors**; **filtration and disinfection of drinking water**; **cleaning vegetables**; **treat and isolate cases**.
- Steps: **reduce source of infection by treatment**, **health education**, **build biological barriers**.

### Vaccination
- **No effective human vaccine against parasites** so far — because of **complex life cycles, adaptive responses and antigenic variation**; progress has been made in identifying **protective antigens (malaria)**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Praziquantel (tapeworm), mebendazole/albendazole (roundworm), metronidazole (Giardia, Balantidium, Trichomonas; damages DNA), chloroquine (malaria), nifurtimox (Chagas), surgery (hydatid).
- Prophylaxis: protect food/water, treat carriers, hygiene, waste disposal. Control: early treatment, vector/reservoir control, bed nets, vaccination.
- No effective parasite vaccine yet (complex life cycles, antigenic variation).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Mention the drugs used to treat parasitic infections.</summary>

**Answer:** Praziquantel for tapeworms, mebendazole and albendazole for roundworms, metronidazole for giardiasis, balantidiasis and Trichomonas vaginalis (by damaging protozoal DNA), chloroquine and other antimalarials for malaria, nifurtimox for Chagas disease, and surgical removal for hydatid cysts.

**Why:** Slide "Treatment".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Outline the prophylaxis and control of parasitic diseases.</summary>

**Answer:** Prevent contamination of food and water with stool, treat carriers and exclude them from food handling, health education, proper disposal of waste, hand washing; control by early diagnosis and treatment, killing vectors, destroying animal reservoirs, bed nets and screens, filtering and disinfecting water, cleaning vegetables and vaccination.

**Why:** Break the chain of transmission.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is there no effective human vaccine against parasites?</summary>

**Answer:** Because of their complex life cycles, adaptive responses and antigenic variation; although protective antigens have been identified in malaria and other infections for future vaccines.

**Why:** Lecture's closing slide.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Diagnostic stage:** intestinal protozoa = cyst + trophozoite in stool; Plasmodium = ring stage; Leishmania = amastigote; Trypanosoma = trypomastigote; Trichomonas = trophozoite.

- **Plasmodium: human = intermediate host; Anopheles = definitive host.**

- **Balantidium reservoir = pigs.**
- **L. donovani = RES (kala-azar); L. tropica = skin; L. braziliensis = naso-oral mucosa.**
- **Metronidazole damages DNA; praziquantel paralyses tapeworms.**
