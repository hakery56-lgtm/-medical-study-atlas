## 1. Definition and host–microbe interaction

- ⭐ **Bacterial pathogenicity** = **the ability of bacteria to cause disease in humans and animals**; involves complex mechanisms that harm the host and disrupt normal physiology.
- Objectives: define pathogenicity, list **virulence factors (toxins, adhesins, enzymes, capsules, biofilms)**, identify **opportunistic pathogens (E. coli, S. aureus)**, explain **normal flora vs pathogens**, describe **endotoxins vs exotoxins**, and analyze **biofilm and quorum sensing** in chronic infection and resistance.
- **Host–microbe interaction** determines the outcome: many bacteria are harmless; some become pathogenic depending on **environmental conditions and host immune response**.
- **Normal flora** **protect** the host against harmful microbes; **opportunistic pathogens exploit weakened immunity** to invade tissues (local or systemic infection).
- ⭐ **Determinants of pathogenicity:** **bacterial genetics, virulence factors, environment and host immunity** → decide severity, progression and clinical picture.

## 2. Virulence factors

**Virulence factors** = **toxins, adhesion molecules, enzymes and immune-evasion mechanisms** that help the bacterium **invade, damage and survive** in the host.

| Factor | Details |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Adhesion** | **pili, fimbriae and adhesins** attach to **epithelial/mucosal surfaces** → colonization and start of infection |
| ⭐ **Invasion** | **enzymes and structural mechanisms** breach epithelial barriers → deeper tissues and spread |
| ⭐ **Endotoxins** | trigger **systemic inflammation and fever** |
| ⭐ **Exotoxins** | **directly damage host cells or disrupt cellular pathways** → disease severity |
| ⭐ **Enzymes** | **hyaluronidase, coagulase, proteases** break down tissues and aid spread |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Virulence factors: toxins, adhesins (pili, fimbriae), enzymes (hyaluronidase, coagulase, proteases), immune-evasion mechanisms.
- Endotoxin → systemic inflammation and fever; exotoxin → direct cell damage.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the virulence factors of bacteria.</summary>

**Answer:** Toxins (endotoxins and exotoxins), adhesion molecules (pili, fimbriae, adhesins), enzymes (hyaluronidase, coagulase, proteases), capsules/immune-evasion mechanisms and biofilms.

**Why:** Overview slide.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate endotoxins from exotoxins.</summary>

**Answer:** Endotoxins trigger systemic inflammation and fever; exotoxins directly damage host cells or disrupt cellular pathways and strongly determine disease severity.

**Why:** Different targets and effects.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do bacterial enzymes help pathogenicity?</summary>

**Answer:** Enzymes such as hyaluronidase, coagulase and proteases break down tissues and facilitate spread, enabling rapid establishment of infection.

**Why:** "Spreading factors".
</details>

## 3. Immune evasion, biofilm, quorum sensing and opportunists

- **Immune evasion:** **capsules, antigenic variation, immune suppression**; also **neutralizing enzymes** and **hiding intracellularly** → persistent infection.
- **Biofilm:** protective layers made by bacteria, **shielding them from antibiotics and immunity** → **chronic infections**, hard to eradicate.
- ⭐ **Quorum sensing:** bacteria **communicate by chemical signals**, **coordinating virulence-gene expression and biofilm formation**.
- ⭐ **MAMPs (microbial-associated molecular patterns):** act as **chemoattractants** triggering microbial chemotaxis in response to microbial presence.
- **Opportunistic pathogens:** **Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Candida albicans** (also E. coli, S. aureus) — in **hospitalized or immunocompromised** patients.
- **Host immune response:** **innate** (phagocytosis) and **adaptive** (antibodies, T-cells).
- ⭐ **Pathogenicity islands:** **clusters of genes encoding virulence traits**, **acquired by gene transfer**, letting bacteria adapt rapidly and increase disease potential.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Evasion: capsule, antigenic variation, immune suppression, neutralizing enzymes, intracellular hiding.
- Quorum sensing = chemical communication → virulence genes + biofilm.
- Opportunists: Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Candida albicans.
- Pathogenicity islands = acquired gene clusters for virulence.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do bacteria evade the immune system?</summary>

**Answer:** By capsules, antigenic variation, immune suppression, secreting neutralizing enzymes and hiding intracellularly, preventing recognition and clearance and leading to persistent infection.

**Why:** Slides "Immune evasion mechanisms" and "Bacterial resistance to immunity".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is quorum sensing and what do biofilms do?</summary>

**Why:** Together they explain chronic infections.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are pathogenicity islands?</summary>

**Answer:** Clusters of genes encoding virulence traits that are acquired by gene transfer, enabling bacteria to adapt rapidly and enhance their disease-causing potential.

**Why:** Slide on pathogenicity islands.
</details>

## 4. Case studies, diagnosis and prevention

- ***E. coli* (pathogenic strains):** toxins → **diarrhea, hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS), urinary tract infections**; virulence mechanisms differ among strains → **tailored diagnosis and treatment**.
- ***Staphylococcus aureus*:** **skin infections, pneumonia, sepsis** via **toxins and immune evasion**; **MRSA** (methicillin-resistant) = particularly difficult to treat and control.
- **Diagnosis:** **microscopy, culture, serological tests, molecular techniques**; accurate identification guides therapy and limits spread.
- **Prevention:** **vaccination, good hygiene, antibiotic stewardship**; early detection and proper antimicrobial use reduce infections and resistance.

**Key points**
- E. coli: diarrhea, HUS, UTI. S. aureus: skin infections, pneumonia, sepsis; MRSA.
- Diagnosis: microscopy, culture, serology, molecular. Prevention: vaccination, hygiene, antibiotic stewardship.

</div>

## 5. Patient safety, MCQs and discussion

### Patient safety
- Virulence factors (toxins, enzymes, capsules, biofilms) ↑ severity; **opportunistic pathogens infect hospitalized/immunocompromised patients**; **accurate lab diagnosis** avoids wrong treatment; **poor infection control → hospital-acquired infections and resistance**; **hygiene, vaccination, stewardship**; **safe handling of samples/cultures** (flame the loop before and after use).

### Lecture MCQs (answers)
1. **Why biofilm bacteria are harder to treat:** **biofilms protect bacteria from antibiotics and host immunity** (physical barrier ↓ drug penetration).
2. **ICU Pseudomonas risk:** **frequent exposure to antibiotics and invasive devices** (weak immunity).
3. **Key lab safety practice:** **flame the loop before and after use and avoid contamination**.
4. **Strong exotoxin but killed by antibiotics:** the **exotoxins** pose the greatest risk (damage tissues even if bacteria are eliminated).
5. **Why accurate identification is a patient-safety issue:** misidentification → **inappropriate treatment and spread**.

### Model answers to the discussion questions
| Question | Model answer |
|---|---|
| Same bacterium, different severity | **virulence factors, infectious dose, and strength of the host immune system** |
| Biofilms and treatment | protect from antibiotics/immunity → **higher doses, longer treatment or different drugs** |
| Danger in ICUs | **weak immunity, invasive devices, frequent antibiotic exposure** |
| Toxins vs bacteria | **toxins often more dangerous** — damage tissue even if bacteria are killed |
| Immune evasion → chronic infection | bacteria survive longer, resist clearance → **persistent/recurrent infections** |
| Antibiotic misuse | promotes **resistant bacteria**, harder future infections |
| Poor hand hygiene | transfers virulent bacteria between patients → **hospital-acquired infections** |
| Priority virulence factors | **toxins, biofilms and immune-evasion mechanisms** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- ICU patients: weak immunity + devices + antibiotics → opportunistic infection.
- Exotoxins keep harming after bacteria die; biofilms need higher/longer/different drugs.
- Aseptic technique: flame loop before and after use; don't leave plates open.

</div>
