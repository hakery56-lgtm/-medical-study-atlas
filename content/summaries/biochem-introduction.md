## 1. What is medical biochemistry?

- ⭐ **Biochemistry** = the study of the **chemical processes occurring in living organisms.**
- It **connects** molecular structure, cellular function, metabolism, genetics, disease mechanisms and drug action.
- **Medical biochemistry** explains how changes in **enzymes, metabolites, genes or signaling pathways** produce clinical disease.
- Human metabolism = **thousands of interconnected reactions** for **energy, synthesis, detoxification and cellular communication.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Structure determines function.
- Disease arises from altered enzymes, metabolites, genes or signaling.

</div>

## 3. Enzymes

- ⭐ **Enzymes accelerate reactions without being consumed.**
- They determine **reaction rate, pathway direction, metabolic regulation, response to hormones and drugs.**
- **Activity depends on:** **substrate concentration, temperature and pH, cofactors and vitamins, inhibitors and activators.**
- **Defects** cause **accumulation of toxic substrates or deficiency of essential products.**
- **Examples:** **phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency → phenylketonuria**; **G6PD deficiency → oxidative hemolysis.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Enzymes are not consumed.
- PKU = phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency; G6PD deficiency = oxidative hemolysis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What do enzymes do and what do they determine?</summary>

**Answer:** They accelerate reactions without being consumed and determine reaction rate, pathway direction, metabolic regulation and the response to hormones and drugs.

**Why:** Slide "Enzymes: The Catalysts of Life".
</details>

**Answer:** Substrate concentration, temperature and pH, cofactors and vitamins, and inhibitors and activators.

<details><summary>Q3. Give clinical examples of enzyme defects.</summary>

**Answer:** Phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency causes phenylketonuria and G6PD deficiency causes oxidative hemolysis.

**Why:** Slide 5.

## 4. Metabolism

- ⭐ **Catabolism** = **breakdown** of molecules with **energy production**; **anabolism** = **synthesis** of molecules **using energy.**
- **Central pathways:** **glycolysis, pyruvate oxidation, TCA cycle, oxidative phosphorylation, fatty-acid β-oxidation, amino-acid metabolism.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Catabolism releases energy; anabolism uses it.
- Six central pathways.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare catabolism and anabolism.</summary>

**Answer:** Catabolism breaks down molecules and produces energy; anabolism synthesizes molecules using energy.

**Why:** Slide "Metabolism".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the central metabolic pathways.</summary>

**Answer:** Glycolysis, pyruvate oxidation, the TCA cycle, oxidative phosphorylation, fatty-acid beta-oxidation and amino-acid metabolism.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which pathway starts glucose breakdown?</summary>

**Answer:** Glycolysis (first in the list of central pathways).

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>
