## 1. Definition, components, affect and mood

- ⭐ **Emotion:** a **complex state of feeling** that results in **physical and psychological changes** influencing thought and behavior; **unique to each individual** and **perception-based**.
- **Four components:** (1) **interpretation (appraisal)** of the stimulus in terms of well-being; (2) **subjective feeling** (fear, happiness); (3) **physiological responses** (heart rate, breathing); (4) **observable behavior** (smiling, crying).

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Feel** | Subjective experience, physical or emotional | Pain from a nail; warmth from a hug |
| **Emotion** | Subjective experience + physiological + behavioral response; **short-lived**, triggered by a specific event | Joy after good news; fear at a snake |
| **Affect** | **Observable expression** of emotion (face, voice, body language); normal range, **constricted, blunt or flat**; may or may not be **congruent with mood** | Smiling at a celebration |
| **Mood** | **Pervasive and sustained** emotion coloring perception of the world; describe **intensity, duration, fluctuations** | Irritable and down for days |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Emotion = appraisal + subjective feeling + physiological response + observable behavior.
- Affect = what you see now; mood = sustained background state.

</div>

## 2. Functions and levels of emotion

- **Functions:** (1) **increase, decrease or regulate arousal**; (2) **direct perception and attention**; (3) **influence learning and memory**; (4) **organize and motivate behavior**; (5) **communicate with others**.

| Level | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | First, immediate reactions to a stimulus; **8 basic emotions**; often masked by secondary emotions | **Love, joy, surprise, anger, sadness, fear, disgust, anticipation** |
| **Secondary** | Develop from primary emotions with **cognitive processing**; shaped by social and cultural context; reflective or evaluative | **Guilt, shame, pride, embarrassment, jealousy** |
| **Tertiary** | **Augment** primary and secondary emotions; deeper processing and **self-reflection**; vary with personal history | **Nostalgia, existential angst, a sense of fulfillment** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Five functions: arousal, attention, learning/memory, behavior, communication.
- 8 primary emotions; guilt/shame/pride = secondary; nostalgia = tertiary.

</div>

## 3. Neuroscience of emotion and its components

- **Neuroanatomy:** **limbic system and basal ganglia** are involved in recognition and experience. **Amygdala damage** → impaired recognition/expression of **fear, anger, happiness**. **Basal ganglia damage** → impaired **disgust**. Cortex: **prefrontal, posterior cortex, insula**.
- **Hemispheres:** **right** = high activity in **depression**; damage → emotional indifference and inability to read emotions. **Left** = more active in **smiling and positive emotions**; **low left frontal activity** → depression.
- **Pathways:** emotional stimulus → **thalamus → amygdala (direct)**, or **thalamus → cortex → amygdala (indirect)**.
- **Neuroendocrinology:** **HPA axis** (stress hormones for short-term coping): **hypothalamus** → **pituitary ("master gland")** → **adrenal cortex** releases **cortisol**. The **autonomic nervous system** controls responses to cognitive and environmental stimuli.
- **Neurotransmitters:** **serotonin, norepinephrine, dopamine** (also GABA and the excitatory glutamate).
- **Components:** **subjective experience** (highly subjective even if basic emotions are universal); **physiological response** (**sympathetic** fight-or-flight: sweating, racing heart, rapid breathing); **behavioral response** (expression, tied to **emotional intelligence** and body language).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Amygdala = fear/anger/happiness; basal ganglia = disgust.
- Right hemisphere high = depression; left frontal low = depression.
- HPA: hypothalamus → pituitary → adrenal cortex → cortisol.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the effect of amygdala and basal ganglia damage on emotion?</summary>

**Answer:** Amygdala damage impairs recognition and expression of fear, anger and happiness; basal ganglia damage impairs recognition and expression of disgust.

**Why:** Slide "The neuroscience of emotion".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the HPA system in emotion.</summary>

**Answer:** The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal system controls stress hormones for short-term coping: the hypothalamus regulates hormones via the pituitary (master gland), which signals the endocrine glands; the adrenal cortex releases stress hormones such as cortisol.

**Why:** Slide "Neuroendocrinology of emotions".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which nervous system controls the physiological response to emotion?</summary>

**Answer:** The sympathetic nervous system, which controls fight-or-flight reactions such as sweating palms, racing heartbeat and rapid breathing.

**Why:** Slide "The physiological response".
</details>

## 4. Theories of emotion

| Theory | Sequence | Example statement |
|---|---|---|
| **Common-sense** | Stimulus → **emotion** → bodily arousal (via ANS) | "I'm shaking because I'm afraid." |
| **James-Lange** | Stimulus → **physiological response** → emotion (perception of the response) | "I'm afraid because I'm shaking." |
| **Cannon-Bard** | Thalamus sends information **simultaneously** to the cortex and the sympathetic organs → emotion and arousal **at the same time** | "I'm shaking and feeling afraid at the same time." |
| **Facial feedback** | Facial expressions give feedback to the brain, **intensifying and even causing** the emotion | "The fearful expression on my face enhances my sense of being afraid." |
| **Cognitive arousal (two-factor)** | **Physical arousal + labeling** of the arousal from environmental cues, at the same time | "This snarling dog is dangerous and that makes me feel afraid." |
| **Cognitive-mediational (1991)** | Stimulus → **appraisal** → emotional response → **bodily response** | "The snarling dog is dangerous and therefore I should feel afraid." |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- James-Lange: body first. Common-sense: emotion first. Cannon-Bard: simultaneous, via the thalamus.
- Cognitive theories: appraisal or labeling is the key step.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the James-Lange and Cannon-Bard theories.</summary>

**Answer:** James-Lange: a physiological response occurs first and the emotion arises from perceiving it. Cannon-Bard: emotion and physiological arousal occur at the same time because the thalamus sends sensory input simultaneously to the cortex and the sympathetic organs.

**Why:** Slides on Cannon-Bard and James-Lange theories.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the two factors in the cognitive arousal theory?</summary>

**Answer:** Physical arousal and a labeling of that arousal based on cues from the surrounding environment; they occur at the same time and result in labeling of the emotion.

**Why:** Slide "Cognitive theories of emotion".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the cognitive-mediational theory.</summary>

**Answer:** A stimulus causes an immediate appraisal (for example, a snarling dog not behind a fence is dangerous); the appraisal produces the emotional response, which is then followed by the appropriate bodily response.

**Why:** Slide "Cognitive-mediational theory".
</details>

## 5. Freud and Scherer's component process model

- **Freud:** mental illness comes from **repressed emotions in the unconscious**; **release and acceptance** of denied emotions and memories are vital for mental health. Unreleased emotional energy → **physiological symptoms** called **psychosomatic**; severe cases of repressed trauma → **hysteria**.
- **Scherer's Component Process Model (CPM):** (1) **cognitive appraisal**; (2) **physiological arousal** (fight or flight); (3) **motoric component** (facial gestures, non-verbal communication); (4) **motivational component** (take action or not); (5) **subjective feeling**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Freud: repressed emotion → psychosomatic symptoms; severe → hysteria.
- Scherer: appraisal, arousal, motoric, motivational, subjective feeling.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What was Freud's view of repressed emotions?</summary>

**Answer:** Mental illness comes from repressed emotions in the unconscious mind; release and acceptance of repressed emotions and memories are vital for mental health.

**Why:** Slide "Sigmund Freud".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Define psychosomatic symptoms and hysteria.</summary>

**Answer:** Psychosomatic: physiological manifestations of emotional energy that was not released. Hysteria: the diagnosis for severe psychosomatic cases of repressed memories of trauma.

**Why:** Slide "Sigmund Freud".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the five components of Scherer's model.</summary>

**Answer:** Cognitive appraisal, physiological arousal, motoric component, motivational component and subjective feeling.

## 6. Normal and abnormal emotional reactions; communication skills

- **Normal reactions:** normal responses to events (e.g. **grief** after the death of a loved one; a healthy person's response to a **life-threatening diagnosis**).
- **Abnormal reactions:** understandable in the context of stressful events but with **more prolonged impairment in functioning**.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mourning** | Syndrome after loss: preoccupation with the lost person, weeping, sadness, reliving memories |
| **Guilt** | Self-reproach and need for punishment; **shame** = less internalized, relates more to others |
| **Dysphoric mood** | An unpleasant mood |
| **Anhedonia** | Loss of interest in and withdrawal from pleasurable activities (depression) |
| **Alexithymia** | Inability or difficulty in describing or being aware of emotion or mood |
| **Anxiety** | Fear-like state **without justifiable cause**; has a psychic component (apprehension, anticipation of danger) |
| **Irritability** | Reduced control over temper → outbursts |
| **Intropunitive** | Anger turned inward (depressed patients) |
| **Agitation** | Severe anxiety with **excess purposeless motor activity** |
| **Tension** | Increased, unpleasant motor and psychological activity |

- **Communication skills:** emotions communicate internal states through **facial expression, tone and body language**; **emotional intelligence** helps interpret patients' cues, respond **empathetically** and build **rapport and trust**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Normal reaction = grief; abnormal = prolonged impairment.
- Anhedonia = no pleasure; alexithymia = cannot describe emotion; intropunitive = anger inward.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**
