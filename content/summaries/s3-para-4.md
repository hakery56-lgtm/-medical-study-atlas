## 1. Introduction, summary table and morphology

### Summary table
| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| **Disease** | **giardiasis** |
| **Infective stage** | **mature cyst** from the patient's or carrier's stool |
| **Route of entry** | **orally**; **fecal–oral route** |
| **Habitat** | **small intestine, mainly the duodenum and jejunum** |
| **Host** | **human intestine only** |
| **Diagnostic stage** | **cyst and trophozoite in the stool** of the patient or carrier |
| Epidemiology | **more prevalent in children**; **higher in poor communities** |

### Introduction
- First observed by **Antonie van Leeuwenhoek in 1681 in his own stool**; named in **1915 by Giard (Paris) and Lambl (Prague)**.
- ***Giardia*** = an **anaerobic flagellated parasite, phylum Metamonada**, that colonizes and reproduces in the **mucosa of the small intestine**.
- **Does NOT invade tissue or penetrate the wall**; the **commonest protozoan pathogen worldwide**, epidemic in areas of low sanitation; **adheres to the epithelium by a sucking disc**.

### Morphology
| | **Trophozoite** | **Mature cyst (infective)** |
|---|---|---|
| Shape/size | **heart/racket/pyriform**, **15 × 9 µm**, **rounded anteriorly, pointed posteriorly, dorsally convex, ventrally concave** | **small, ovoid, thick hyaline wall**, **12 × 8 µm** |
| Key structures | **two sucking discs** (attachment), **two round nuclei with nucleoli**, **four pairs of flagella** (anterior, lateral, caudal, posterior/ventral), **two parabasal (median) sausage-shaped bodies**, **one pair of axostyles** | **four small spherical nuclei with central nucleoli at one end**, axostyle and parabasal body; **no free flagella** |
| Notes | motile by free flagella | **relatively resistant to chlorination and UV** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Giardiasis: cyst orally (fecal–oral), duodenum/jejunum, human only, cyst + trophozoite in stool; more common in children and poor communities.
- Leeuwenhoek 1681; Giard + Lambl 1915; flagellate (Metamonada); doesn't invade; sucking disc.
- Trophozoite: pear/racket-shaped, 2 nuclei, 4 pairs of flagella, sucking disc. Cyst: 4 nuclei, no free flagella, resistant to chlorination/UV.

## 2. Life cycle (one host: the human)

1. **Ingestion** of the **mature cyst** in contaminated food/water (patient or carrier stool; **fecal–oral**); cysts **survive several months in cold water**.
2. The cyst **resists gastric juice** and reaches the small intestine; **about half an hour after ingestion, excystation occurs in the duodenum** — **each cyst liberates TWO trophozoites**.
3. Trophozoites **multiply by longitudinal binary fission**, colonize the mucosa of the **duodenum and jejunum**, **feed by pinocytosis**, swim freely or **attach by the ventral sucking discs** — they **do NOT invade tissue**.
4. Under unfavorable conditions **encystment** occurs in the lower small intestine/**colon**, and cysts are passed in stool.
- Trophozoites in stool may survive **several weeks** in soil/water; **up to 200,000 cysts per gram of stool**; trophozoites can colonize the **bile duct and pancreas**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Direct (one host). Cysts survive months in cold water, resist gastric juice.
- Excystation in the duodenum after ~30 min: 1 cyst → 2 trophozoites; longitudinal binary fission; pinocytosis; sucking-disc attachment; no tissue invasion.
- Encystment in the lower small intestine/colon; up to 200,000 cysts/g stool.

</div>

## 3. Pathogenesis, clinical features and complications

- Giardia sits **in the crypts of the duodenal/jejunal mucosa**, **lightly adhered by the sucking disc**; **no tissue invasion**.
- **> 50 % of cases are asymptomatic (carriers)**.
- **Symptomatic:** **diarrhea with mucus but WITHOUT blood** (mucosal ulceration), sometimes diarrhea followed by constipation and gas; **enterocyte damage and loss of the brush border → shortened microvilli and altered barrier function**; colonization of the **gallbladder/bile and pancreatic duct → malabsorption of fat and vitamins**; **acute diarrhea, low-grade fever, nausea, anorexia**.
- **Incubation ≈ 2 weeks (1–3 weeks).**
- **Clinical symptoms:** nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, gas, **very commonly pancreatitis**, **foul-smelling greasy floating stool (steatorrhea)**, cramps, dehydration, right abdominal pain with weakness, weight and appetite loss, **biliary colic and jaundice**.

### Complications
- **Reactive arthritis, irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), recurring diarrhea, steatorrhea** for **> 1 year**; **delayed physical and mental growth**, malnutrition.
- **↓ pancreatic lipase excretion (mainly in children)**; **↓ mucoepithelial secretion** predisposes to giardiasis.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Non-invasive; > 50 % asymptomatic carriers; incubation 1–3 weeks.
- Diarrhea with mucus but NO blood; steatorrhea (foul, greasy, floating); malabsorption of fat and vitamins; brush-border loss.
- Complications: reactive arthritis, IBS, growth delay, malnutrition, ↓ pancreatic lipase.

</div>

## 4. Laboratory diagnosis, treatment and prophylaxis

### Stool examination
- **I. Macroscopic:** **offensive odor, pale, fatty (steatorrhea), floats in water**; first **profuse watery**, then **greasy and foul**, **no blood**.
- **II. Microscopic:** **cyst and trophozoites** in the stool using **normal saline and iodine wet preparation**.
- **III. Entero-test (string test):** recovery from the **upper GIT**; stool may need **concentration (formol-ether)**.

### Other tests
- **Serological:** **antigen/antibody detection by IFT or ELISA**.
- **Molecular:** **DNA probes and PCR** to demonstrate the parasite's genome in stool.

### Treatment
- ⭐ **Metronidazole (Flagyl): 250 mg orally 3 times daily for 5–7 days, or 2 g daily for 3 days, with vitamin B12**; or **tinidazole 2 g single dose**; **pregnant women: paromomycin (oral aminoglycoside)**.

### Prophylaxis
- **Proper disposal of waste water and stools**; **disinfect water (chlorination or boiling)**; **hand washing before eating**; **treat carriers**; **proper diaper disposal**; **chlorination inactivates cysts**; **boiling or special membrane filtration** are required.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Stool: pale, fatty, floating, foul, no blood; saline + iodine wet mount (cyst + trophozoite); entero-test (string); IFT/ELISA; PCR.
- Metronidazole 250 mg TID 5–7 days or 2 g × 3 days (+ vitamin B12); tinidazole 2 g single dose; paromomycin in pregnancy.
- Prevention: sanitation, water chlorination/boiling/filtration, hand washing, treat carriers.

</div>
