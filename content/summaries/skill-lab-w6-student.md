## 1. Tools and history

- **Tools:** **reflex hammer, pinprick, tape measure, thermometer, oximeter, gloves** (kit also: stethoscope, face masks, tongue depressors, pen torch).
- **History (10 steps):** **(1) stand on the right, greet, introduce yourself; (2) ask permission; (3) data: name, age, residence, job, marital status, blood group; (4) chief complaint and duration; (5) SOCRATES; (6) past medical and surgical; (7) drugs (OTC, allergy, steroids); (8) family; (9) social (smoking, alcohol, after permission); (10) thank the patient.**
- **SOCRATES:** **site** (patient points; one or both legs), **onset**, **character** (sharp, mild, tingling, numbness), **radiation**, **associated symptoms** (**local:** weakness, gait change, difficulty in micturition, numbness, tingling, redness, sexual dysfunction; **systemic:** malaise, weight loss, fatigue, fever; **loss of urine or stool control**), **timing**, **exacerbating and relieving factors**, **severity /10.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Ask permission before questions and before alcohol history.
- Associated symptoms are local and systemic.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Greet and introduce, ask permission, personal data, chief complaint, SOCRATES, past medical and surgical, drugs, family, social history, thank the patient.

</details>

## 2. Examination: gait, look, feel, move

- **Preparation (6 steps):** **greet, sanitize hands, stand on the right, introduce yourself, ask permission, expose**; then **general examination (general look and vital signs).**
- **Steps:** **gait → look → feel → move.**
- **Gait:** walk a few steps; note **gait, muscles, balance, pain, limited movement.**
- **Look:** from **behind, front and both sides**: **deformity, rib hump, abnormal curvature, lipoma, hairy patch**; **forward deviation = lordosis; backward = kyphosis; lateral = scoliosis.**
- **Feel:** start at **lumbar**: **four fingers together, vertebra by vertebra**, watch the face; then **paraspinal soft tissues each side**; finally **light fist percussion** (warn first).
- **Move:** **flexion, extension, lateral flexion, rotation** (patient standing).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Gait, look, feel, move.
- Percussion last, after warning the patient.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the preparation for the examination.</summary>

**Answer:** Greet, sanitize hands, stand on the right, introduce yourself, ask permission and expose the patient, then perform a general examination.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the Look and Feel steps.</summary>

**Answer:** Look from all sides for deformity, rib hump, curvature, lipoma and hairy patch; feel the vertebrae with four fingers, the soft tissues, then percuss lightly with a fist.

**Why:** Slides 8-11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Define lordosis, kyphosis and scoliosis as in the guide.</summary>

**Answer:** Forward deviation, backward deviation and lateral deviation of the spine respectively.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

## 3. Specific test for the sciatic nerve

- **Supine, passive:** **raise the leg to less than 60°**, then **dorsiflex the foot**; also **flex hip to 90° and knee**, and **press the popliteal fossa at the middle**; **pain or an electric-like sensation** indicates nerve involvement.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Leg raised below 60° with foot dorsiflexion.
- Popliteal fossa pressure gives an electric-like pain if the nerve is involved.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the sciatic nerve test.</summary>

**Answer:** Patient supine; passively raise the leg below 60° and dorsiflex the foot; with hip and knee flexed to 90° press the popliteal fossa; pain or an electric sensation is positive.

**Why:** Slide 13.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What position is the patient in?</summary>

**Answer:** Supine, with passive movement by the examiner.

**Why:** Slide 13.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What does a positive test indicate?</summary>

**Answer:** Involvement or irritation of the sciatic nerve.

**Why:** Slide 13.
</details>

## 4. Lower limb neurological examination

- **Tone:** **knee: shake quickly right and left; lift slightly and let it fall**; **ankle clonus:** brisk foot flexion/extension then **dorsiflexion held; continuing beats = positive.**
- **Power (10 movements):** **hip flexion, extension, abduction, adduction; knee flexion, extension; ankle dorsiflexion, plantar flexion, inversion, eversion**, each against resistance, **comparing sides.**
- **Reflexes:** **hammer at far end, wrist movement, perpendicular strike**; **knee L3-L4** (patellar tendon), **ankle S1** (dorsiflex then strike Achilles), **plantar S1-S2** (blunt key or hammer end): **normal = toes flex; abnormal = big toe extension with fanning.**
- **Sensation:** **eyes closed; demonstrate on the sternum; light touch (cotton) and pinprick by dermatomes.**
- **End:** thank the patient, wish well, sanitize hands.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Ten power movements; three reflexes.
- Plantar: flexion normal, extension with fanning abnormal.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the power movements tested.</summary>

**Answer:** Hip flexion, extension, abduction and adduction; knee flexion and extension; ankle dorsiflexion, plantar flexion, inversion and eversion.

**Why:** Slides 17-19.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give the reflexes and their roots.</summary>

**Answer:** Knee L3-L4, ankle S1, plantar S1-S2.

**Why:** Slides 20-22.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is sensation tested?</summary>

**Answer:** With the eyes closed, after demonstrating on the sternum, test light touch with cotton and pinprick over each dermatome.

**Why:** Slide 23.
</details>
