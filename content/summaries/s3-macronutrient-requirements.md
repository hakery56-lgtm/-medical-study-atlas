## 1. Carbohydrates: classification, roles, digestion and requirements

- ⭐ **Carbohydrates** = sugars, starches and fiber. **Classification:** monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose); disaccharides (sucrose = glucose + fructose, lactose = glucose + galactose, maltose = glucose + glucose); oligo-/polysaccharides (starch, dextrins, glycogen); dietary fiber (cellulose, hemicellulose, pectins, gums, mucilages).
- **Roles:** primary energy source (**brain and RBCs**), **glycogen storage** in liver and muscle, structural role in glycoproteins and membranes, **spares protein** from use as energy.
- **Digestion:** polysaccharides → oligosaccharides → monosaccharides by **amylases and disaccharidases**; absorbed in the **jejunum** (active transport and facilitated diffusion); **portal vein** to liver where fructose and galactose → glucose.
- **Cellular metabolism:** glycolysis (glucose → pyruvate, **cytoplasm**); aerobic: pyruvate → acetyl-CoA → TCA → oxidative phosphorylation → ATP; anaerobic: pyruvate → **lactate** (limited ATP). Excess → glycogen, then fat (lipogenesis). **RBCs use glucose exclusively.**
- **Sources:** cereals, bread, pasta, rice; legumes (lentils, chickpeas, beans, fava beans); fruits and vegetables; sugars.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Minimum carbohydrate for the brain | ~**130 g/day** |
| AMDR for carbohydrate | **45–65%** of total energy |
| Quality emphasis | complex carbs, whole grains, fiber; limit added sugars |

- **Too little** (very low-carb): ↑ gluconeogenesis from amino acids and **ketone production**. **Too much** (refined sugars): weight gain, dyslipidemia, insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Brain and RBCs depend on glucose; RBCs use only glucose.
- Absorbed in the jejunum → portal vein → liver converts fructose and galactose to glucose.
- 130 g/day minimum; 45–65% of energy; excess refined sugar → metabolic syndrome.

</div>

## 2. Dietary fiber

- ⭐ **Dietary fiber** = non-digestible carbohydrates and **lignin** intrinsic and intact in plants; resist digestion in the small intestine, **partly or completely fermented in the large intestine**. Affects motility, glycemic control, serum cholesterol, microbiota, satiety and weight.

### Classification by solubility
| Type | Characteristics | Examples | Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Soluble** | Dissolves, forms viscous gels; fermentable | Pectins, beta-glucans, guar gum, psyllium, inulin | ↓ cholesterol, slows glucose absorption, satiety, microbiota |
| **Insoluble** | Does not dissolve; adds bulk; less fermentable | Cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin | ↑ stool bulk, regularity, ↓ constipation |

### Classification by chemical composition
| Category | Components | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Cellulose** | linear β-1,4 glucose chains | whole grains, bran, vegetables |
| **Hemicellulose** | xylose, arabinose, glucose | cereals, whole grains |
| **Pectins** | rich in galacturonic acid | apples, citrus, berries |
| **Gums and mucilages** | viscous plant polysaccharides | guar, psyllium, locust bean |
| **Beta-glucans** | β-1,3 and β-1,4 glucose polymers | oats, barley |
| **Inulin and FOS** | fructose chains; prebiotic | chicory, garlic, onions, bananas |
| **Lignin** | non-carbohydrate polyphenol | woody stems, fruit seeds, bran |

### Classification by function
- **Viscous (gel-forming):** slows gastric emptying, reduces glucose spikes, lowers LDL.
- **Fermentable (prebiotic):** produces **SCFAs (acetate, propionate, butyrate)**; gut health, ↓ inflammation, colonocyte nutrition.
- **Bulking:** absorbs water, ↑ fecal mass, prevents constipation.
- **SCFA functions:** nourish colon cells (**butyrate**), lower colonic pH (inhibit pathogens), may improve lipid and glucose metabolism.

### Intake and sources
| Group | Daily requirement |
|---|---|
| Adult men | **30–38 g** |
| Adult women | **21–25 g** |
| Children | **14 g per 1000 kcal** |
| WHO/FAO | **≥ 25 g/day** from natural foods |

- Oats, barley: soluble (β-glucan); apples, oranges: soluble (pectin); beans, lentils: soluble + insoluble; wheat bran, carrots, broccoli: insoluble; psyllium, flaxseed: soluble.

### Importance of fiber
1. **Prevents constipation** (normalizes transit time, ↑ fecal bulk). Transit time = contact time of food particles with the colon wall.
2. **Cardiovascular:** soluble fiber lowers cholesterol by 3 mechanisms: **binding bile acids** (liver uses more cholesterol for new bile acids → ↓ LDL), **SCFA production** (propionate inhibits hepatic cholesterol synthesis), **slowing absorption** (↓ postprandial lipemia). Result: ↓ total and LDL cholesterol, better HDL/LDL ratio, ↓ CVD risk.
3. **Diabetes:** gel delays glucose absorption, lowers meal GI, improves insulin sensitivity.
4. **Colon cancer:** ↓ carcinogen concentration, ↓ transit time.
5. Stimulates chewing, saliva and gastric juice.
6. **Obesity:** satiety, delayed gastric emptying, slower absorption.
7. Prevents **diverticulitis**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Soluble = gel, fermentable, lowers cholesterol and glucose; insoluble = bulk, prevents constipation.
- Fermentation gives acetate, propionate, butyrate; butyrate feeds colonocytes, propionate inhibits cholesterol synthesis.
- Adults: men 30–38 g, women 21–25 g; children 14 g/1000 kcal.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare soluble and insoluble fiber.</summary>

**Answer:** Soluble fiber (pectin, beta-glucan, guar, psyllium, inulin) forms viscous gels and is fermentable; it lowers cholesterol, slows glucose absorption and improves satiety. Insoluble fiber (cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin) does not dissolve, adds stool bulk, is less fermentable and relieves constipation.

</details>

</details>

**Answer:** Prevents constipation, lowers cholesterol, helps diabetics, protects against colon cancer, stimulates chewing, saliva and gastric juice, aids obesity management and prevents diverticulitis.

</details>

## 3. Glycemic index

- ⭐ **GI** = ranks carbohydrate foods by how much they raise blood glucose compared with a **reference food** (pure glucose or white bread); carbohydrate amount in reference and test food must be **equal**.

| Category | GI | Examples | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low** | **≤ 55** | oats, barley, brown rice, lentils, chickpeas, apples, pears, yogurt, milk, nuts, most vegetables | slow steady rise, satiety, better control |
| **Intermediate** | **56–69** | sweet corn, basmati rice, couscous, pineapple, whole-wheat bread, quick oats | moderate rise |
| **High** | **≥ 70** | white bread, white rice, cornflakes, mashed potatoes, cakes, candies, soft drinks, honey | rapid rise in glucose and insulin, then fatigue |

- GI graph: high GI = tall quick peak; low GI = low prolonged curve.
- Note: the GI table slide prints "High GI ≤ 70" and "Low GI ≥ 55", reversed relative to the definition slide and the chart (low ≤ 55, high ≥ 70). Use the latter.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Low ≤ 55; intermediate 56–69; high ≥ 70.
- High GI → spike and fatigue; low GI → steady release and satiety.
- Reference food: glucose or white bread, with equal carbohydrate amounts.

</div>

## 4. FODMAPs and the low-FODMAP diet

- ⭐ **FODMAP** = **F**ermentable **O**ligosaccharides, **D**isaccharides, **M**onosaccharides **A**nd **P**olyols: short-chain carbohydrates **poorly absorbed in the small intestine and rapidly fermented** → gas, bloating, GI symptoms.

| Group | Examples | Foods |
|---|---|---|
| **Oligosaccharides** | fructans, GOS | wheat, onions, garlic, legumes |
| **Disaccharides** | lactose | milk, soft cheeses, yogurt |
| **Monosaccharides** | fructose in excess of glucose | honey, apples, HFCS |
| **Polyols** | sorbitol, mannitol, xylitol, maltitol | stone fruits, sugar-free gum, sweeteners |

- **Used in** IBS and functional GI disorders: ↓ bloating, gas, pain, diarrhea, constipation; identifies triggers; improves quality of life.
- **3 phases:** (1) **Elimination 6–8 weeks**; (2) **Reintroduction** one group at a time; (3) **Personalization** long-term diet avoiding only problem FODMAPs.
- **Chart:** avoid excess fructose, lactose, fructans (garlic, onion, wheat, rye, asparagus, broccoli), galactans (beans, chickpeas, lentils), polyols (apricot, peach, plum, sorbitol). Enjoy banana, blueberry, orange, kiwi; carrot, spinach, tomato, potato, zucchini; rice, oats, quinoa, gluten-free bread; lactose-free milk, hard cheeses.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- FODMAPs = poorly absorbed, rapidly fermented short-chain carbohydrates.
- Used in IBS; phases: elimination (6–8 wk), reintroduction, personalization.
- Four groups: oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides, polyols.

</div>

## 5. Lactose intolerance

- ⭐ **Definition:** inability to digest **lactose** because of **lactase deficiency** in the small intestine.
- **Pathophysiology:** normally lactose → glucose + galactose; in intolerance, undigested lactose reaches the colon → bacterial fermentation → gas and acids → symptoms.
- **Symptoms:** bloating, abdominal pain and cramps, flatulence, diarrhea, nausea; **30–120 min** after dairy.
- **Types:** **primary (genetic)**, most common, gradual loss after childhood; **secondary** (celiac disease, gastroenteritis); **congenital**, rare, absent lactase from birth.
- **Diagnosis:** lactose tolerance test (blood glucose); **hydrogen breath test** (↑ hydrogen); **stool acidity test** (infants).
- **Management:** limit or avoid dairy; lactose-free or low-lactose milk; lactase tablets or drops; calcium- and vitamin D-rich non-dairy foods (fortified soy or almond milk).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Lactase deficiency → undigested lactose fermented in colon.
- Symptoms 30–120 min after dairy; primary type is the most common.
- Tests: tolerance test, hydrogen breath test, stool acidity (infants).

</div>

## 6. Proteins and amino acids

- **Subclasses:** **simple** (amino acids only: albumin, globulin); **conjugated** (non-protein part: hemoglobin, glycoproteins); **derived** (breakdown products: peptides, proteoses).
- **Functions:** muscle mass (prevents **sarcopenia**); growth and repair; immunity (antibodies); fluid balance (**albumin** and oncotic pressure); enzymes, transport proteins, hormones.
- **Quality:** **complete** (all essential amino acids: animal proteins, soy); **incomplete** (lacking one or more: many plant proteins); **complementary** (e.g. rice + beans); affected by digestibility, bioavailability, anti-nutritional factors (**phytates**).
- **Metabolism:** dietary protein + body protein breakdown → **amino acid pool** → protein synthesis, other nitrogen compounds (hormones, neurotransmitters, nucleotides), or catabolism: **nitrogen removal → ammonia → urea (liver) → kidneys**, and **carbon skeletons**.
- **Carbon skeleton pathways:** (1) **ATP** via TCA cycle (fasting, exercise); (2) **gluconeogenesis** in liver (glucogenic amino acids; fasting/starvation, for brain and RBCs); (3) **fat synthesis** when energy intake is high; amino acids forming acetyl-CoA are **ketogenic** (leucine, lysine).
- **Deficiency:** muscle wasting, weakness, poor growth, delayed wound healing, impaired immunity, possible oedema. Protein = **4 kcal/g**.

| Population | Recommended protein intake |
|---|---|
| Healthy adults | **0.83 g/kg/day** (EFSA) |
| Healthy older adults | **1.0–1.2 g/kg/day** (ESPEN) |
| Older adults with acute/chronic illness | **1.2–1.5 g/kg/day** (individualized) |
| Pregnancy (EFSA) | add **1, 9 and 28 g/day** in trimesters 1, 2, 3 |
| Lactation (EFSA) | add **19 g/day** (first 6 months), **13 g/day** after |

- Example: 70 kg healthy adult: 70 × 0.83 = **58 g/day**. Individualize for kidney disease, obesity and clinical condition.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Complete proteins: animal, soy; plant proteins can be complemented.
- Amino acid carbon skeletons → ATP, glucose (fasting) or fat; nitrogen → urea.
- Healthy adult 0.83 g/kg/day; older 1.0–1.2; illness 1.2–1.5.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Complete proteins contain all essential amino acids in proper proportions (animal, soy); incomplete lack one or more (many plant proteins); complementary proteins combine plant sources such as rice and beans to give a full profile.

**Why:** Slide "Protein Quality and Complementation".

**Why:** Slide "Protein Requirements".

## 7. Lipids (fats)

- **Roles:** energy storage (**9 kcal/g**, most concentrated); cell membrane structure (phospholipids, cholesterol); hormone synthesis (steroids, eicosanoids); insulation and organ protection; absorption of **fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K**.
- **Classification:** **simple** (triglycerides); **compound** (phospholipids, glycolipids, lipoproteins); **derived** (fatty acids, sterols, fat-soluble vitamins).
- **Dietary fats:** saturated; MUFA; PUFA (**ω-6** linoleic; **ω-3** ALA, EPA, DHA); **trans** fatty acids. ⭐ **Essential fatty acids** (cannot be synthesized): **linoleic (ω-6)** and **alpha-linolenic (ω-3)**.

| Step | Site | Agents | Products |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oral | Mouth | lingual lipase | minor free FAs |
| Gastric | Stomach | gastric lipase | partial hydrolysis |
| Intestinal | Duodenum | bile salts, pancreatic lipase, colipase | monoglycerides, FAs, cholesterol |
| Absorption | Jejunum | micelle + chylomicron formation | re-esterified lipids in lymph |

- **Requirements:** about **20–35%** of energy; **saturated < 10%**; eliminate trans fats.
- **Too little:** poor fat-soluble vitamin absorption, EFA deficiency (skin). **Too much (saturated, trans):** CVD, obesity, dyslipidemia. **Quality** often matters more than quantity.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Fat = 9 kcal/g; carrier of vitamins A, D, E, K.
- Essential FAs: linoleic (ω-6) and alpha-linolenic (ω-3).
- 20–35% of energy; saturated < 10%; avoid trans fats.

</div>

**Answer:** Energy storage (9 kcal/g), membrane structure, hormone synthesis, thermal insulation and organ protection, and absorption of vitamins A, D, E and K.
