# Anatomy of the Hand — Exam Night Review

## 1. Skeleton and joints of the hand

- ⭐ The hand has **27 bones**: **carpal bones, metacarpals and phalanges**.
- The carpal bones form an **arch**; the **flexor retinaculum** spans between the medial and lateral edges of the arch and forms the **carpal tunnel**.

| Row | Bones |
|---|---|
| **Proximal row** | **Scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform** |
| **Distal row** | **Trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate** |

### Clinical features of the carpal bones
| Bone | Key feature |
|---|---|
| **Capitate** | **Largest** carpal bone and the **first to ossify** |
| **Scaphoid** | Lies in the floor of the **anatomical snuffbox**; **most frequently fractured**, risk of **avascular necrosis** |
| **Lunate** | **Most frequently dislocated** |
| **Pisiform** | Palpable **sesamoid bone in the tendon of FCU**; **last to ossify** |
| **Trapezium** | Forms a **saddle joint** with the 1st metacarpal |
| **Hamate** | **Hook**: groove for the **deep branch of the ulnar nerve** (**handlebar neurapraxia**); hook fractured in **racket sports and golf**; depression between hook and pisiform = **Guyon canal** (ulnar tunnel) |

### Joints
- **Intercarpal:** plane. **Midcarpal:** ellipsoid, between the two rows, movement same as the wrist.
- **CMC of the thumb:** between **trapezium and base of the 1st metacarpal**, **saddle**: flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, **opposition**.
- **CMC of the medial 4 fingers:** plane.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 27 bones; proximal row S-L-T-P, distal row T-T-C-H.
- Scaphoid = most fractured; lunate = most dislocated; capitate = largest and first to ossify; pisiform = last to ossify.
- Hook of hamate: ulnar nerve groove, Guyon canal, fractured in racket sports and golf.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the carpal bones in each row and name the clinical feature of the scaphoid and lunate.</summary>

**Answer:** Proximal row: scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform. Distal row: trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate. The scaphoid is the most frequently fractured carpal bone (floor of the snuffbox, risk of avascular necrosis); the lunate is the most frequently dislocated.

**Why:** Slides "Hand skeleton / Carpal bones".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the clinical features of the hook of the hamate?</summary>

**Answer:** The groove behind it carries the deep branch of the ulnar nerve, which is compressed in handlebar neurapraxia; the hook is frequently fractured in racket sports and golf; and the depression between the hook and the pisiform forms the Guyon canal, where the ulnar nerve may be compressed (e.g. by a ganglion cyst).

**Why:** Slide "Carpal bones — Hamate".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the carpometacarpal joint of the thumb.</summary>

**Answer:** It lies between the trapezium and the base of the 1st metacarpal; it is a saddle joint allowing flexion, extension, abduction, adduction and opposition. The CMC joints of the other four fingers are plane joints.

**Why:** Slide "Joints in the hand".
</details>

## 2. Palmaris brevis and the deep fascia of the palm

- **Palmaris brevis:** origin **flexor retinaculum and palmar aponeurosis**; insertion into the **skin of the medial border of the hand**; **wrinkles the medial palmar skin** and helps prevent ulnar displacement of the hypothenar eminence; supplied by the **superficial branch of the ulnar nerve**.
- The **deep fascia** of the palm is specialized into **three structures**: **flexor and extensor retinacula; palmar aponeurosis; fibrous flexor and extensor sheaths** of the digits.
- ⭐ **Palmar aponeurosis:** **triangular** thickened central part of the deep fascia covering the **long flexor tendons and the superficial palmar arch**; **improves grip** (fixes the skin) and **protects** tendons, nerves and vessels.
- **Dupuytren contracture:** progressive **fibrosis and degeneration** of the palmar aponeurosis; **digits 4 and 5** most affected; **males > females**; etiology unknown.
- **Fibrous flexor sheaths:** thickened deep fascia over the long flexor tendons of each digit, from the **metacarpal head to the base of the distal phalanx**; **hold the tendons in position** during flexion.

| Layer of the hand (palmar → dorsal) | Content |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Skin; superficial fascia (veins, cutaneous nerves, fat) |
| 3 | Palmar aponeurosis |
| 4 | Superficial palmar arch + digital nerves |
| 5 | Flexor tendons + synovial sheaths (FDS, FDP, FPL, lumbricals) |
| 6 | Thenar and hypothenar compartments, adductor pollicis |
| 7 | Deep palmar arch |
| 8–10 | Deep interosseous fascia (palmar interossei); metacarpal bones; dorsal interossei and extensor apparatus |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Palmaris brevis: skin of the medial palm; superficial branch of ulnar nerve.
- Deep fascia → retinacula, palmar aponeurosis, fibrous sheaths.
- Dupuytren = fibrosis of the palmar aponeurosis, digits 4 and 5, males.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the palmar aponeurosis and what are its functions?</summary>

**Answer:** The triangular thickened central part of the deep fascia of the palm that covers the long flexor tendons and the superficial palmar arch. It improves grip by fixing the skin and protects the underlying tendons, nerves and vessels.

**Why:** Slide "Deep fascia of the palm — Palmar aponeurosis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is Dupuytren contracture?</summary>

**Answer:** Progressive fibrosis and degeneration of the palmar fascia and aponeurosis; digits 4 and 5 are most affected, it is commoner in males than females and its etiology is unknown.

**Why:** Slide "Dupuytren Contracture".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give the origin, insertion, action and nerve supply of palmaris brevis.</summary>

**Answer:** Origin: flexor retinaculum and palmar aponeurosis. Insertion: skin along the medial border of the hand. Action: wrinkles the medial palmar skin and helps prevent ulnar displacement of the hypothenar eminence. Nerve: superficial branch of the ulnar nerve.

**Why:** Slides "Palmaris brevis".
</details>

## 3. Synovial sheaths

- **Synovial sheaths of the long flexor tendons** = a **lubricating device** that prevents friction as tendons move within the **osseofibrous tunnels**.
- **Parts:** **ulnar bursa, radial bursa, digital synovial sheaths.**
- **Functions:** allow smooth movement under the **flexor retinaculum** and **fibrous flexor sheaths** with minimum friction; **help absorb shock**.
- **Extensor tendons:** at the posterior wrist there are **six tunnels** (extensor compartments), each lined by a **synovial sheath** and separated by **fibrous septa**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Flexor sheaths = ulnar bursa, radial bursa, digital sheaths.
- Role: lubrication, friction reduction, shock absorption.
- Six extensor compartments at the wrist, each with its own synovial sheath.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What are the functions of the synovial sheaths of the long flexor tendons?</summary>

**Answer:** They act as a lubricating device that prevents friction, allow the tendons to move freely and smoothly beneath the flexor retinaculum and fibrous flexor sheaths, and help absorb shock.

**Why:** Slide "Synovial sheaths of long flexor tendons".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Name the parts of the synovial sheaths of the long flexor tendons.</summary>

**Answer:** The ulnar bursa, the radial bursa and the digital synovial sheaths.

**Why:** Slide "Synovial sheaths of long flexor tendons — Parts".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How are the extensor tendons arranged at the wrist?</summary>

**Answer:** In six tunnels (extensor compartments) at the posterior wrist, each lined by a synovial sheath and separated from the others by fibrous septa.

**Why:** Slide "Synovial sheaths of long extensors tendons".
</details>

## 4. Intrinsic muscles: thenar, hypothenar and adductor pollicis

- ⭐ The hand has **20 intrinsic muscles** in **five groups** arranged into **four layers**: (1) thenar and hypothenar; (2) long flexor tendons with the lumbricals; (3) adductor pollicis; (4) metacarpals with the interossei.

| | **Thenar muscles** | **Hypothenar muscles** |
|---|---|---|
| **Muscles** | Abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis, opponens pollicis | Abductor digiti minimi, flexor digiti minimi, opponens digiti minimi |
| **Common origin** | Lateral flexor retinaculum, **scaphoid, trapezium** | Medial flexor retinaculum, **pisiform, hook of hamate** |
| **Insertion** | Base of **proximal phalanx of thumb** (opponens: **1st metacarpal**) | Base of **proximal phalanx of little finger** (opponens: **5th metacarpal**) |
| **Nerve** | **Recurrent branch of the median nerve** | **Deep branch of the ulnar nerve** |

- **Actions (thumb):** APB abducts; **FPB flexes the MCP joint**; **opponens opposes** the thumb against the other fingers.
- **Adductor pollicis:** most medial thumb muscle; **transverse head** from the **3rd metacarpal**, **oblique head** from the **2nd and 3rd metacarpals**; **adducts** the thumb; **ulnar nerve** (the only thumb muscle not supplied by the median nerve).
- **Hypothenar actions:** abduction and flexion of the little finger; opponens pulls the 5th metacarpal forwards.
- **Rule:** 3 hypothenar muscles + adductor pollicis = **ulnar nerve**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 20 intrinsic muscles, five groups, four layers.
- Thenar = median (recurrent branch); hypothenar and adductor pollicis = ulnar (deep branch).
- Opponens muscles insert into the metacarpals; the others into the proximal phalanx.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the thenar and hypothenar muscles (muscles, origin, insertion, nerve).</summary>

**Answer:** Thenar: abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis, opponens pollicis; origin flexor retinaculum, scaphoid and trapezium; inserted into the base of the proximal phalanx of the thumb (opponens into the 1st metacarpal); recurrent branch of the median nerve. Hypothenar: abductor, flexor and opponens digiti minimi; origin flexor retinaculum, pisiform and hook of the hamate; inserted into the base of the proximal phalanx of the little finger (opponens into the 5th metacarpal); deep branch of the ulnar nerve.

**Why:** Slides "Muscles of thenar eminence" and "Muscles of hypothenar eminence".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe adductor pollicis.</summary>

**Answer:** The most medial thumb muscle with two heads: the transverse head from the 3rd metacarpal and the oblique head from the 2nd and 3rd metacarpals. It adducts the thumb and is supplied by the ulnar nerve, unlike the other thumb muscles which are supplied by the median nerve.

**Why:** Slides "Muscles of thenar eminence" and "Nerve supply — adductor pollicis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the four layers of the intrinsic muscles of the hand.</summary>

**Answer:** (1) Thenar and hypothenar muscles; (2) long flexor tendons with the lumbricals arising from them; (3) adductor pollicis; (4) metacarpal bones with the interossei (dorsal and palmar).

**Why:** Slide "Intrinsic muscles of the hand".
</details>

## 5. Lumbricals, extensor expansion and interossei

- **Lumbricals (4):** arise from the **tendons of FDP** (FDS tendons lie immediately superficial to FDP); inserted into the **dorsal extensor expansion**; **flex the MP joints and extend the PIP and DIP joints**. **Nerve:** **median** → 1st and 2nd; **deep branch of ulnar** → 3rd and 4th.
- **Dorsal extensor expansion:** the extensor digitorum tendon expands over the MP joint like a **hood** and fuses anteriorly with the fibrous flexor sheath; lumbrical and interosseous tendons insert into it; it splits into **three slips**: **central slip → base of the middle phalanx**; **two lateral slips → base of the terminal phalanx**.
- **Functions:** ED and EDM **extend the MCP joint**; **interossei abduct/adduct** the MCP joints; **lumbricals and interossei extend the PIP and DIP joints**.
- ⭐ **Interossei:** **4 dorsal + 3 palmar**, on the metacarpal shafts. **PAD** = palmar **ad**duct; **DAB** = dorsal **ab**duct (relative to the **axial line** through the middle finger). All seven are supplied by the **deep branch of the ulnar nerve**.

| | Dorsal interossei | Palmar interossei |
|---|---|---|
| **Number** | 4 | 3 |
| **Action** | **Abduct** the fingers (DAB) | **Adduct** the fingers (PAD) |
| **Nerve** | Deep branch of ulnar | Deep branch of ulnar |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Lumbricals: from FDP; flex MP, extend IP; 1st–2nd median, 3rd–4th ulnar.
- Extensor expansion: central slip → middle phalanx; lateral slips → terminal phalanx.
- Interossei: PAD and DAB, 4 dorsal and 3 palmar, all deep branch of ulnar.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the lumbricals (origin, insertion, action, nerve supply).</summary>

**Answer:** Four muscles arising from the FDP tendons and inserted into the dorsal extensor expansion; they flex the MP joints and extend the PIP and DIP joints. The median nerve supplies the 1st and 2nd; the deep branch of the ulnar nerve supplies the 3rd and 4th.

**Why:** Slides "Lumbrical muscles" and "Nerve supply of lumbricals".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the dorsal extensor expansion and its slips.</summary>

**Answer:** The extensor digitorum tendon expands like a hood over the MP joint and fuses with the fibrous flexor sheath; lumbrical and interosseous tendons insert into it. It splits into a central slip inserted into the base of the middle phalanx and two lateral slips inserted into the base of the terminal phalanx.

**Why:** Slide "Dorsal extensor expansions".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Differentiate the dorsal from the palmar interossei.</summary>

**Answer:** There are four dorsal interossei, which abduct the fingers (DAB), and three palmar interossei, which adduct the fingers (PAD), relative to the axial line through the middle finger. All seven are supplied by the deep branch of the ulnar nerve.

**Why:** Slides "4th layer — interossei" and "Interosseous muscles of the hand".
</details>

## 6. Fascial compartments and spaces of the hand

| Compartment | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Central** | **FDS and FDP tendons, lumbricals**, superficial palmar arch, median nerve branches; **midpalmar space** (deep to the flexor tendons) |
| **Thenar** | **3 thenar muscles**, thumb; **recurrent branch of median**; thenar space |
| **Hypothenar** | **3 hypothenar muscles**, digit 5; **deep branch of ulnar** |
| **Interosseous** | **Dorsal interossei** (abduct digits 2, 3, 4) and **palmar interossei** (adduct digits 2, 4, 5); deep branch of ulnar |
| **Adductor** | **Adductor pollicis**; deep branch of ulnar |

- **Clinical:** infection in **any** palmar compartment tends to cause **swelling on the dorsum** of the hand; infection in the **central** compartment can spread **through the carpal tunnel** into the **anterior compartment of the forearm**.
- **Parona space:** potential space in the **distal forearm** deep to the flexor tendons, between the fascia of **pronator quadratus** and the **FDP tendon sheath**; continues with the **midpalmar space** through the carpal tunnel.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Five compartments: central, thenar, hypothenar, interosseous, adductor.
- Palmar infection → dorsal swelling; central infection → forearm via the carpal tunnel.
- Parona space = distal forearm, between pronator quadratus fascia and FDP sheath.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the fascial compartments of the palm and the contents of the central compartment.</summary>

**Answer:** Central, thenar, hypothenar, interosseous and adductor compartments. The central compartment contains the FDS and FDP tendons, the lumbricals, the superficial palmar arch and median nerve branches, with the midpalmar space deep to the flexor tendons.

**Why:** Slide "Fascia — compartments of the palm".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the clinical importance of palmar infections?</summary>

**Answer:** Infection in any palmar compartment tends to produce swelling on the dorsum of the hand; infection in the central compartment can spread through the carpal tunnel into the anterior compartment of the forearm.

**Why:** Slide "Fascia — compartments of the palm" (clinical connection).
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the Parona space?</summary>

**Answer:** A potential space in the distal forearm (volar wrist) deep to the flexor tendons, between the fascia of pronator quadratus and the flexor digitorum profundus tendon sheath; it continues into the palm with the midpalmar space through the carpal tunnel.

**Why:** Slide "Potential spaces of hand and Parona space".
</details>

## 7. Nerves of the hand

| Nerve | Motor in the hand | Sensory in the hand |
|---|---|---|
| **Median** | **Recurrent branch (LOAF):** **L**ateral 2 lumbricals, **O**pponens pollicis, **A**bductor pollicis brevis, **F**lexor pollicis brevis | **Palmar** surface of **lateral 3½ fingers**; **dorsal** surface of the **distal phalanx** of the lateral 3½ fingers |
| **Ulnar — deep branch** | **Hypothenar muscles, medial 2 lumbricals, adductor pollicis, ALL interossei** | — |
| **Ulnar — superficial branch** | **Palmaris brevis** | **Medial 1½ fingers** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Median = LOAF (thenar + 2 lateral lumbricals) and lateral 3½ fingers.
- Ulnar deep branch = hypothenars, 2 medial lumbricals, adductor pollicis, all interossei.
- Ulnar superficial branch = palmaris brevis + medial 1½ fingers.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the motor and sensory branches of the median nerve in the palm.</summary>

**Answer:** Motor (recurrent branch, LOAF): thenar muscles (abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis, opponens pollicis) and the first two lumbricals. Sensory: palmar surface of the lateral 3½ fingers and the dorsal surface of the distal phalanx of the lateral 3½ fingers.

**Why:** Slide "Nerves of the hand — median nerve".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the branches of the ulnar nerve in the hand.</summary>

**Answer:** Deep branch (motor): hypothenar muscles, medial two lumbricals, adductor pollicis and all interossei. Superficial branch (mixed): motor to palmaris brevis and sensation to the medial 1½ fingers.

**Why:** Slide "Nerves of the hand — ulnar nerve".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which thumb muscle is not supplied by the median nerve, and by which nerve is it supplied?</summary>

**Answer:** Adductor pollicis, supplied by the deep branch of the ulnar nerve; the other thumb muscles (abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis, opponens pollicis) are supplied by the recurrent branch of the median nerve.

**Why:** Slides "Nerve supply" and "Muscles of thenar eminence".
</details>

## 8. Arteries of the hand

| | **Superficial palmar arch** | **Deep palmar arch** |
|---|---|---|
| **Site** | Deep to the **palmar aponeurosis**, superficial to the flexor tendons | **Deep to the flexor tendons** |
| **Level** | Distal border of the **outstretched thumb** | Immediately distal to the **bases of the metacarpals** (about ½ inch proximal to the superficial arch) |
| **Formation** | Mainly **ulnar artery**, completed by the **superficial palmar branch of the radial** | Mainly **radial artery**, completed by the **deep palmar branch of the ulnar** |
| **Branches** | **Four palmar digital arteries** | **Palmar metacarpal arteries** |

- The deep arch is **concave towards the wrist**; the **deep branch of the ulnar nerve** runs in this concavity.
- **Allen's test** assesses the radial and ulnar arteries and the arches.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Superficial arch = ulnar (+ radial superficial branch); deep arch = radial (+ ulnar deep branch).
- Superficial arch gives 4 palmar digital arteries; deep arch gives palmar metacarpal arteries.
- Deep branch of the ulnar nerve runs in the concavity of the deep arch.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the superficial palmar arch.</summary>

**Answer:** It lies immediately deep to the palmar aponeurosis but superficial to the flexor tendons, at the level of the distal border of the outstretched thumb. It is formed mainly by the ulnar artery and completed by the superficial palmar branch of the radial artery, and gives four palmar digital arteries.

**Why:** Slide "Arteries of the hand — superficial palmar arch".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the deep palmar arch.</summary>

**Answer:** It lies deep to the flexor tendons, immediately distal to the bases of the metacarpal bones (about half an inch proximal to the superficial arch). It is formed mainly by the radial artery and completed by the deep palmar branch of the ulnar artery, and gives the palmar metacarpal arteries.

**Why:** Slide "Arteries of the hand — deep palmar arch".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the relation of the deep palmar arch to the ulnar nerve?</summary>

**Answer:** The arch is concave towards the wrist, and the deep branch of the ulnar nerve runs in this concavity.

**Why:** Slide "Arteries of the hand — deep palmar arch (relations)".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Scaphoid = most fractured; lunate = most dislocated.** Do not swap them.
- **Capitate = largest and first to ossify; pisiform = last to ossify.**
- **Adductor pollicis is supplied by the ulnar nerve**, not the median, although it is a thumb muscle.
- **Opponens muscles insert into the metacarpals**; all other thenar/hypothenar muscles insert into the proximal phalanx.
- **PAD and DAB:** palmar interossei adduct (3), dorsal interossei abduct (4).
- **Lumbricals: median supplies the 1st and 2nd; ulnar supplies the 3rd and 4th.**
- **Superficial arch = ulnar artery; deep arch = radial artery.**
- **Parona space is in the distal forearm**, not in the hand.
