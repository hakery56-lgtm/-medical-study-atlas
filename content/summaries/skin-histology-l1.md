## 1. Skin structure and functions

- ⭐ **Largest organ: 15-20% of body weight, 1.5-2 m2.**
- **Epidermis** = epithelium, **ectodermal origin**; **dermis** = connective tissue, **mesodermal**; **hypodermis (subcutaneous tissue)** beneath.
- **Junction:** irregular, **dermal papillae interdigitate with epidermal ridges.**
- **Functions:** **protective** (barrier; selective permeability allows some lipophilic drugs); **sensory**; **thermoregulatory** (sweat, superficial microvasculature); **metabolic** (**vitamin D3 synthesis**, electrolyte loss in sweat).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Epidermis = ectoderm; dermis = mesoderm.
- Four functions: protective, sensory, thermoregulatory, metabolic.

</div>

## 2. Thick and thin skin

- ⭐ Epidermis = **stratified squamous keratinized epithelium.**
- **Cells:** **keratinocytes** (most), **melanocytes, Langerhans cells, Merkel cells.**
- **Thin skin:** epidermis **75-150 μm.** **Thick skin (palms, soles):** **400-1400 μm.**
- **Total skin thickness:** **back ≈ 4 mm; scalp ≈ 1.5 mm.**
- **Appendages:** hair follicles, sweat glands, sebaceous glands.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Thick vs thin refers to the epidermis only.
- Thick skin: palms and soles.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do thick and thin skin differ?</summary>

**Answer:** Thick skin (palms and soles) has an epidermis of 400-1400 μm; thin skin elsewhere has 75-150 μm.

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the epidermal cell types.</summary>

**Answer:** Keratinocytes (most abundant), melanocytes, Langerhans cells and Merkel cells.

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Name the epidermal appendages.</summary>

**Answer:** Hair follicles, sweat glands and sebaceous glands.

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>

## 3. Layers of the epidermis (thick skin)

| Layer | Features |
|---|---|
| **1. Stratum basale** | **Single layer** of basophilic cuboidal/columnar cells; **hemidesmosomes** to the basal lamina, **desmosomes** laterally; **intense mitosis**; progenitor cells |
| **2. Stratum spinosum** | **Thickest layer**; polyhedral cells, **spinous processes with desmosomes**; synthesizes keratins; **resists friction and pressure** |
| **3. Stratum granulosum** | **3-5 layers** of flattened cells; **keratohyaline granules** (non-membrane-bound, pack keratin) and **lamellated granules** (lipids, exocytosed → **water-proof barrier**) |
| **4. Stratum lucidum** | **Thick skin only**; thin translucent, **eosinophilic, no nuclei or organelles**, packed keratin |
| **5. Stratum corneum** | **15-20 layers** of keratinized squamous cells; **squames shed** at the surface |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Order: basale, spinosum, granulosum, lucidum (thick skin only), corneum.
- Lamellated granules give the water barrier.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the stratum basale.</summary>

**Answer:** A single layer of basophilic cuboidal or columnar cells on the basement membrane, attached by hemidesmosomes and joined by desmosomes, with intense mitotic activity and progenitor cells for all epidermal layers.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What do the granules of the stratum granulosum do?</summary>

**Answer:** Keratohyaline granules pack keratin filaments; lamellated granules release lipids by exocytosis to form the waterproof barrier.

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Differentiate stratum lucidum from stratum corneum.</summary>

**Answer:** The lucidum (thick skin only) is a thin translucent eosinophilic layer with no nuclei; the corneum has 15-20 layers of keratinized cells (squames) shed at the surface.

**Why:** Slides 9-10.
</details>

## 4. Medical applications: epidermal diseases

| Disease | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Psoriasis** | **More proliferating cells, shorter cell cycle** → thick epidermis, rapid renewal |
| **Pemphigus** | **Autoimmune damage to intercellular junctions** of keratinocytes → blisters |
| **Basal cell carcinoma** | Malignant tumor of **stratum basale**, **rarely lethal** |
| **Squamous cell carcinoma** | Malignant tumor of **stratum spinosum**, **rarely lethal** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Basal carcinoma from the basale; squamous from the spinosum.
- Pemphigus = autoimmune attack on desmosomal junctions.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is psoriasis?</summary>

**Answer:** Increased proliferating cells with a shorter cell cycle, giving a thicker epidermis and faster renewal.

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is pemphigus?</summary>

**Answer:** A blistering disorder caused by autoimmune damage to intercellular junctions between keratinocytes.

**Why:** Slide 12.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare the two skin carcinomas.</summary>

**Answer:** Basal cell carcinoma arises from the stratum basale and squamous cell carcinoma from the stratum spinosum; both are rarely lethal.

**Why:** Slides 13-14.
</details>

## 5. Keratinocytes and keratinization

- ⭐ **Most abundant epidermal cells.**
- **Keratinization: 15-30 days**; as cells rise, **keratin filaments increase, nucleus and organelles disappear, cells die and are shed**, replaced from the basal layer.
- **Dandruff:** keratinization **< 15 days** → thick scales.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Keratinization takes 15-30 days.
- Too-rapid turnover (< 15 days) = dandruff.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe keratinization.</summary>

**Answer:** In 15-30 days cells move upward, accumulate keratin filaments, lose nucleus and organelles, die and are shed, replaced from the stratum basale.

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What causes dandruff?</summary>

**Answer:** Keratinization faster than 15 days, so keratinized cells accumulate and desquamate as thick scales.

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which cells are the progenitors of keratinocytes?</summary>

**Answer:** Cells of the stratum basale (with the deepest spinosum).

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

## 6. Melanocytes and pigment disorders

- ⭐ **Melanocytes:** pale rounded cells in the **basal layer and hair follicles**, **hemidesmosomes** to the basal lamina, **neural crest origin.**
- **Long extensions** end in **invaginations of 5-10 keratinocytes** and **transfer melanin → supranuclear cap** that **absorbs and scatters UV**, protecting DNA.
- **Eumelanin** = brown/black; **pheomelanin** = red hair. **Keratinocytes are the melanin depot** (more pigment than melanocytes).
- **Melanocyte density is similar in all people**; where sun protection is needed most, melanin is produced **faster and accumulates more.**
- **Skin color causes:** **melanin, carotene (yellow), blood and vessel number (red).**

| Condition | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Malignant melanoma** | **Invasive melanocyte tumor**, spreads via blood and lymph |
| **Vitiligo** | **Loss of whole melanocytes** → patchy depigmentation |
| **Albinism** | **Hereditary** inability to make melanin: **no tyrosinase activity** or no tyrosine uptake |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Melanocytes from the neural crest; keratinocytes hold the pigment.
- Albinism = melanocytes present but cannot make melanin; vitiligo = melanocytes lost.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do melanocytes protect the skin?</summary>

**Answer:** They transfer melanin granules to 5-10 keratinocytes, forming a supranuclear cap that absorbs and scatters sunlight and protects DNA from UV radiation.

**Why:** Slide 17.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the causes of skin color.</summary>

**Answer:** Brownish-black melanin, yellow carotene of keratinocytes, and the red color of blood with the number of blood vessels.

**Why:** Slide 19.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Differentiate vitiligo from albinism.</summary>

**Answer:** Vitiligo is degeneration and loss of melanocytes causing patchy depigmentation; albinism is a hereditary inability to synthesize melanin (absent tyrosinase activity or tyrosine uptake).

**Why:** Slides 21-22.
</details>

## 7. Langerhans cells and Merkel cells

- ⭐ **Langerhans (dendritic) cells:** **APCs from monocytes**, **2-8%** of epidermal cells, best seen in the **stratum spinosum**; processes form a **dense network**; **no microorganism enters without alerting them** → immune response.
- **Merkel cells:** **modified keratinocytes**, highly sensitive **mechanoreceptors** (fingertips, external genitalia), **desmosomes** to basal keratinocytes, **thick and thin skin**, **dense-core granules**, **synapses with unmyelinated afferent terminal discs**; **light touch → neurotransmitter release.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Langerhans = APC (monocyte origin); Merkel = touch receptor (keratinocyte origin).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe Langerhans cells.</summary>

**Answer:** Antigen-presenting dendritic cells of monocyte origin (2-8% of epidermal cells), most visible in the stratum spinosum, forming a network that triggers an immune response when microorganisms penetrate.

**Why:** Slide 23.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe Merkel cells.</summary>

**Answer:** Modified keratinocytes acting as highly sensitive mechanoreceptors, abundant in the fingertips and external genitalia, with dense-core granules and synapses with unmyelinated afferent terminal discs.

**Why:** Slide 24.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is light touch sensed by Merkel cells?</summary>

**Answer:** Light touch releases neurotransmitters at their synapses with afferent nerve terminal discs, producing sensation.

**Why:** Slide 24.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Stratum lucidum is only in thick skin.**
- **Thickest layer is the spinosum; keratinocytes shed from the corneum.**
- **Basal cell carcinoma = basale; squamous cell carcinoma = spinosum.**
- **Vitiligo = no melanocytes; albinism = melanocytes but no tyrosinase.**
- **Keratinocytes, not melanocytes, hold most melanin.**
- **Merkel cells use desmosomes; melanocytes use hemidesmosomes.**
