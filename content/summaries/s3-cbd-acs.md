## 2. The case

### Presentation
- **Mr. Ali, 55-year-old man**, **severe central chest pain for 1 hour**, started **while climbing stairs**.
- **Crushing, heavy pressure radiating to the left arm and jaw**, with **sweating, nausea, shortness of breath**; **not relieved by rest**.
- **First time** he has had such pain; asks: *"Is this just indigestion… or is my heart in danger?"*

### History
- **Type 2 diabetes mellitus and hypertension — both poorly controlled.**
- **Current smoker (20 pack-years).**
- **Family history:** father had an **MI at age 50**.
- **Lifestyle:** **sedentary, high-fat diet, poor adherence to antihypertensive and antidiabetic drugs, high occupational stress; no alcohol or illicit drugs.**

### Examination (in the ED)
- **HR 105 bpm (regular); BP 150/95 mmHg; RR 24/min; SpO₂ 94 % on room air.**
- **Diaphoretic, anxious-looking; S4 gallop; no murmurs.**

### Investigations
- ⭐ **ECG: ST-segment elevation > 2 mm in leads V2–V4, aVL and lead I** (anterior and lateral leads).
- ⭐ **Troponin I: markedly elevated.**
- ⇒ **Diagnosis: anterolateral STEMI** (ST elevation + raised troponin + typical pain).
- Suggested work-up shown on the board: **ECG, troponin, CXR, CBC, BMP, D-dimer**.

## 3. The tasks (with the key teaching points)

| Task | Key points |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **1. Differential diagnosis of chest pain** | **cardiac:** ACS (STEMI/NSTEMI/unstable angina), aortic dissection, pericarditis/myocarditis; **pulmonary:** pulmonary embolism, pneumothorax, pneumonia/pleuritis; **GI:** GERD, esophageal spasm/rupture, peptic ulcer, cholecystitis; **musculoskeletal:** costochondritis |
| ⭐ **2. Pathophysiology of ACS** | **atherosclerotic plaque rupture (or erosion) → exposure of subendothelial collagen → platelet adhesion/activation/aggregation → coagulation cascade → thrombus** occluding the coronary artery (**complete in STEMI; partial in NSTEMI/UA**) → ischemia → necrosis |
| **3. Mechanism mapping** | link risk factors (HTN, DM, smoking, lipids) → **endothelial injury → LDL oxidation → foam cells → plaque → rupture → thrombosis → ischemia → pain, arrhythmia, troponin release** |
| ⭐ **4. Features suggesting ACS over MSK/non-cardiac pain** | **crushing/pressure retrosternal pain**, **radiation to the jaw/arm**, **exertional onset**, **not reproduced by palpation**, **associated diaphoresis, nausea, dyspnea** |
| ⭐ **5. Why low-dose aspirin (75–325 mg) is antithrombotic** | aspirin **irreversibly inhibits COX-1 → ↓ thromboxane A₂** in platelets (which cannot make new enzyme); at **low doses endothelial COX-2/prostacyclin (PGI₂) is spared**, while **high doses also block PGI₂** and lose the antithrombotic advantage |
| ⭐ **6. Metoprolol vs atenolol** | both **β₁-selective**; **metoprolol is lipid-soluble (lipophilic) and hepatically metabolized**; **atenolol is water-soluble (hydrophilic) and renally excreted** |
| ⭐ **7. Why early reperfusion works** | restores flow **before transmural necrosis is complete** ("time is muscle") → **saves myocardium, ↓ infarct size, preserves LV function, ↓ mortality** |
| ⭐ **8. Biopsychosocial analysis** | **Biological:** age 55, male, HTN, T2DM, family history, dyslipidemia, smoking; **Psychological:** work stress, anxiety, denial/minimization ("indigestion?"); **Social/lifestyle:** smoking, sedentary lifestyle, high-fat diet, poor adherence |

### Reperfusion and treatment (standard teaching)
- **STEMI:** **primary PCI** (preferred; **door-to-balloon ≤ 90 min**) or **fibrinolysis** if PCI isn't timely; **dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin + P2Y₁₂ inhibitor)**, **anticoagulation**, **β-blocker**, **statin**, ACE inhibitor; **secondary prevention:** smoking cessation, BP and glucose control, diet, exercise.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- ACS pathophysiology: plaque rupture/erosion → platelet activation → thrombosis (complete in STEMI).
- ACS-favoring features: crushing pressure, radiation to jaw/arm, exertional, not reproducible by palpation, sweating/nausea/dyspnea.
- Low-dose aspirin: irreversible COX-1 inhibition (↓ TXA₂) sparing endothelial PGI₂.
- Metoprolol (lipophilic, hepatic) vs atenolol (hydrophilic, renal). Early reperfusion = salvage of myocardium ("time is muscle"). Primary PCI door-to-balloon ≤ 90 min.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Explain the pathophysiology of ACS.</summary>

**Answer:** Rupture or erosion of an atherosclerotic plaque exposes subendothelial collagen, causing platelet adhesion, activation and aggregation and activation of the coagulation cascade, forming an occlusive thrombus in the coronary artery (complete in STEMI, partial in NSTEMI and unstable angina) that causes myocardial ischemia and necrosis with troponin release.

**Why:** Task 2.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why does low-dose aspirin work as an antithrombotic?</summary>

**Answer:** Aspirin irreversibly inhibits platelet COX-1, reducing thromboxane A₂ (platelets cannot regenerate the enzyme); at low doses the endothelial COX-2/prostacyclin pathway (an inhibitor of platelet aggregation) is spared, whereas high doses also reduce prostacyclin and lose the antithrombotic selectivity.

**Why:** Task 5 and MCQ 2.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How does metoprolol differ from atenolol and why does early reperfusion work?</summary>

**Answer:** Both are β₁-selective blockers, but metoprolol is lipid-soluble and metabolized in the liver while atenolol is water-soluble and excreted by the kidney. Early reperfusion restores coronary flow before irreversible transmural necrosis, salvaging myocardium, limiting infarct size and mortality ("time is muscle").

**Why:** Tasks 6 and 7.
</details>

## 4. Conceptual MCQs (not graded) — answers

1. **Primary mechanism of ACS:** **B. Atherosclerotic plaque rupture with thrombosis.**
2. **Aspirin reduces platelet aggregation by:** **B. Irreversibly inhibiting COX-1.** (Not β₁ blockade, not ADP-receptor blockade, not thrombin inhibition.)
3. **A red-flag feature in chest pain:** **radiation to the jaw with diaphoresis.** (Pain reproducible with palpation, relieved by antacids or sharp pain worsened by breathing suggest non-cardiac causes.)

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- MCQ answers: plaque rupture + thrombosis; irreversible COX-1 inhibition; jaw radiation + diaphoresis = red flag.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which feature is a red flag for ACS and which suggest a non-cardiac cause?</summary>

**Answer:** Red flag: radiation to the jaw with diaphoresis. Non-cardiac clues: pain reproducible with palpation, relief by antacids, sharp pain worsened by breathing.

**Why:** MCQ 3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the primary mechanism of ACS?</summary>

**Answer:** Atherosclerotic plaque rupture with thrombosis (not vasospasm alone, viral myocarditis or valvular prolapse).

**Why:** MCQ 1.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How does aspirin reduce platelet aggregation?</summary>

**Answer:** By irreversibly inhibiting COX-1, which reduces thromboxane A₂; it does not block β₁ receptors, ADP receptors or thrombin.

**Why:** MCQ 2.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **STEMI = ST elevation (here > 2 mm in V2–V4, aVL, I = anterolateral); NSTEMI/UA = no persistent ST elevation (troponin ↑ in NSTEMI, normal in UA).**
- **Primary mechanism = plaque rupture + thrombosis** (not vasospasm alone).
- **Aspirin = irreversible COX-1 inhibitor; low dose spares endothelial PGI₂.**
- **Metoprolol (lipophilic, hepatic) vs atenolol (hydrophilic, renal).**
- **Red flag = jaw radiation + diaphoresis; palpation-reproducible pain = MSK.**
- **S4 gallop = stiff ischemic ventricle.**
- **Primary PCI door-to-balloon ≤ 90 minutes; fibrinolysis if PCI isn't timely.**
