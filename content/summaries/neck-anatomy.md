# Anatomy of the Neck — Exam Night Review

Anatomy lecture (Dr. Munqith Mazin — Department of Anatomy, Al-Ameed University, College of Medicine): **boundaries and compartments of the neck, the cervical fascia, superficial veins, the triangles of the neck and the muscles of the neck (suprahyoid, infrahyoid, sternocleidomastoid, trapezius, platysma, posterior triangle and prevertebral muscles).** After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer).

---

## 1. Boundaries and compartments

- ⭐ **Neck** = tube giving continuity from **head to trunk.**
- **Anterior:** **lower border of the mandible → upper surface of the manubrium.** **Posterior:** **superior nuchal line → disc between C7 and T1.**

| Compartment | Position | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Visceral** | **Anterior** | Parts of the **digestive and respiratory systems** and **endocrine glands** |
| **Vertebral** | **Posterior** | **Cervical vertebrae, spinal cord, cervical nerves**, vertebral muscles |
| **Two vascular** | **Lateral** | **Major blood vessels and the vagus nerve** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Four compartments: one visceral, one vertebral, two vascular.
- Boundaries: mandible to manubrium; superior nuchal line to C7/T1.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the neck and its boundaries.</summary>

**Answer:** A tube connecting head and trunk; anteriorly from the lower border of the mandible to the upper manubrium; posteriorly from the superior nuchal line to the C7-T1 disc.

**Why:** Slide "The Neck".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the compartments of the neck.</summary>

**Answer:** Visceral (anterior), vertebral (posterior) and two lateral vascular compartments.

**Why:** Slide "Compartments of the Neck".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What do the vascular compartments contain?</summary>

**Answer:** The major blood vessels and the vagus nerve.

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>

## 2. Fascia of the neck

- ⭐ **Superficial fascia** contains the **platysma.**
- **Deep cervical fascia layers:** **investing, pre-vertebral, pre-tracheal, carotid sheaths.**

| Layer | Features |
|---|---|
| **Investing** | **Completely surrounds the neck**; attached **posteriorly to ligamentum nuchae and C7 spine**, **anteriorly surrounds infrahyoid muscles**; superiorly **external occipital protuberance and superior nuchal line**, laterally **mastoid process and zygomatic arch**, inferiorly **scapular spine, acromion, clavicle, manubrium** |
| **Pre-vertebral** | **Cylinder around the vertebral column and muscles** (prevertebral, scalenes, deep back muscles); to the **skull base**; extends as the **axillary sheath** around the **brachial plexus and subclavian artery** |
| **Pre-tracheal** | Around the **trachea, esophagus and thyroid**; **hyoid bone → upper thoracic cavity** |
| **Carotid sheath** | Around the **common and internal carotid arteries, internal jugular vein and vagus nerve** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Four deep layers.
- Axillary sheath comes from the pre-vertebral layer.
- Carotid sheath = carotids + IJV + vagus.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the layers of the deep cervical fascia.</summary>

**Answer:** Investing layer, pre-vertebral layer, pre-tracheal layer and the carotid sheaths.

**Why:** Slide "Fascia of the Neck".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the pre-vertebral layer.</summary>

**Answer:** A cylindrical layer around the vertebral column and its muscles (including the scalenes) that attaches to the skull base and extends inferolaterally around the brachial plexus and subclavian artery as the axillary sheath.

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What does the carotid sheath contain?</summary>

**Answer:** The common carotid artery, internal carotid artery, internal jugular vein and vagus nerve.

**Why:** Slide 9.
</details>

## 3. Superficial veins

- ⭐ **External jugular vein:** formed **behind the angle of the mandible** by the **posterior auricular + retromandibular** veins.
- **Anterior jugular veins:** **paired**, drain the anterior neck, begin **above the hyoid bone** and run down on each side of the midline.
- **Both pierce the investing layer** and enter the **subclavian vein.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- EJV = posterior auricular + retromandibular.
- Both veins pierce the investing fascia to reach the subclavian vein.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is the external jugular vein formed?</summary>

**Answer:** Posterior to the angle of the mandible by the union of the posterior auricular and retromandibular veins.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the anterior jugular veins.</summary>

**Answer:** Paired channels draining the anterior neck that begin above the hyoid and descend on either side of the midline.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Where do both veins drain?</summary>

**Answer:** They pierce the investing layer of cervical fascia and enter the subclavian vein.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

## 4. Triangles of the neck

- ⭐ **Anterior triangle:** **sternocleidomastoid, inferior border of mandible, midline.** **Posterior triangle:** **sternocleidomastoid, trapezius, middle third of clavicle.**

| Anterior triangle | Boundaries | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Submental** | **Hyoid** (inferior), **anterior belly of digastric** (lateral), **midline** | **Submental lymph nodes, anterior jugular veins** |
| **Submandibular** | **Mandible** (superior), **anterior and posterior bellies of digastric** (inferior) | **Submandibular gland, facial vessels, submandibular nodes** |
| **Muscular** | **Hyoid** (superior), **superior belly of omohyoid and anterior border of SCM** (lateral), **midline** | **Infrahyoid muscles, pharynx, larynx, thyroid and parathyroid glands** |
| **Carotid** | **Superior belly of omohyoid** (anteroinferior), **stylohyoid and posterior belly of digastric** (superior), **anterior border of SCM** (posterior) | **Carotid sheath (carotid artery, IJV, vagus)**, **glossopharyngeal and hypoglossal nerves**, **deep cervical nodes** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Anterior triangle = submental, submandibular, carotid, muscular.
- Carotid triangle holds the carotid sheath plus CN IX and XII.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give the boundaries of the anterior and posterior triangles.</summary>

**Answer:** Anterior: sternocleidomastoid, inferior border of the mandible and the midline. Posterior: sternocleidomastoid, trapezius and the middle third of the clavicle.

**Why:** Slide "Triangles of the Neck".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the contents of the carotid triangle?</summary>

**Answer:** The carotid sheath (carotid artery, internal jugular vein, vagus nerve), the glossopharyngeal and hypoglossal nerves and deep cervical lymph nodes.

**Why:** Slide "Carotid Triangle".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the contents of the muscular and submandibular triangles?</summary>

**Answer:** Muscular: infrahyoid muscles, pharynx, larynx, thyroid and parathyroid glands. Submandibular: submandibular gland, facial vessels and submandibular lymph nodes.

**Why:** Slides "Submandibular" and "Muscular Triangle".
</details>

## 5. Suprahyoid and infrahyoid muscles

- ⭐ **Functions of neck muscles:** **head movement, stabilizing the upper body, swallowing, elevating the rib cage in inhalation.**
- **Suprahyoid:** **stylohyoid, digastric, mylohyoid, geniohyoid.** **Infrahyoid:** **omohyoid, sternohyoid, thyrohyoid, sternothyroid.**

| Muscle | Origin → Insertion | Nerve | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stylohyoid** | Styloid process base → hyoid body (lateral) | **Facial** | Pulls hyoid **posterosuperiorly** |
| **Digastric, anterior belly** | **Digastric fossa** → intermediate tendon on hyoid | **Mylohyoid nerve** (inferior alveolar) | **Opens mouth**, raises hyoid |
| **Digastric, posterior belly** | **Mastoid notch** → intermediate tendon | **Facial** | Raises hyoid |
| **Mylohyoid** | **Mylohyoid line** → hyoid body and opposite fibers | **Mylohyoid nerve** | Supports and elevates **floor of the mouth**, hyoid |
| **Geniohyoid** | **Inferior mental spine** → hyoid body | **C1 anterior ramus** | Elevates and pulls hyoid **forward**, or **depresses the mandible** |
| **Sternohyoid** | Sternoclavicular joint and manubrium → hyoid | **C1-C3 (ansa cervicalis)** | **Depresses hyoid** after swallowing |
| **Omohyoid** | Superior scapular border → hyoid | **C1-C3 (ansa cervicalis)** | **Depresses and fixes** hyoid |
| **Thyrohyoid** | Oblique line of thyroid cartilage → hyoid | **C1 via hypoglossal nerve** | **Depresses hyoid**; **raises larynx** if hyoid fixed |
| **Sternothyroid** | Manubrium → oblique line of thyroid cartilage | **C1-C3 (ansa cervicalis)** | **Draws larynx down** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Facial nerve: stylohyoid and posterior digastric; mylohyoid nerve: anterior digastric and mylohyoid.
- Ansa cervicalis C1-C3: sternohyoid, omohyoid, sternothyroid.
- Thyrohyoid: C1 on the hypoglossal nerve.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the suprahyoid muscles with their nerve supply.</summary>

**Answer:** Stylohyoid (facial nerve), digastric (anterior belly mylohyoid nerve; posterior belly facial nerve), mylohyoid (mylohyoid nerve) and geniohyoid (C1 branch).

**Why:** Slides 21-22.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the infrahyoid muscles with their nerve supply.</summary>

**Answer:** Omohyoid, sternohyoid and sternothyroid (anterior rami C1-C3 via the ansa cervicalis) and thyrohyoid (C1 fibers along the hypoglossal nerve).

**Why:** Slides 22-23.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the actions of the thyrohyoid?</summary>

**Answer:** It depresses the hyoid bone, but when the hyoid is fixed it raises the larynx.

**Why:** Slide 23.
</details>

## 6. Sternocleidomastoid, trapezius and platysma

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Nerve | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sternocleidomastoid** | **Manubrium and clavicle** | **Mastoid process** | **Motor: accessory nerve**; proprioception **C2, 3** | **Oblique rotation of head**, neck flexion, **accessory respiratory muscle** |
| **Trapezius** | **Spine of C7, superior nuchal line, external occipital protuberance, ligamentum nuchae** | **Lateral third of the clavicle** | **Motor: accessory nerve**; proprioception **C3, 4** | **Stabilizes and moves the scapula** |
| **Platysma** | **Fascia over upper pectoralis major and deltoid** | **Skin and subcutaneous tissue of the face** | **Facial nerve** | **Depresses lower jaw**, draws down lower lip and mouth angles |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- SCM and trapezius share the accessory nerve; platysma is facial.
- SCM proprioception C2-3; trapezius C3-4.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the sternocleidomastoid.</summary>

**Answer:** Arises from the manubrium and clavicle, inserts on the mastoid process; motor supply by the accessory nerve (proprioception C2, 3); rotates the head obliquely, flexes the neck and can act as an accessory muscle of respiration.

**Why:** Slide 26.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the trapezius.</summary>

**Answer:** Arises from the C7 spine, superior nuchal line, external occipital protuberance and ligamentum nuchae, inserts on the lateral third of the clavicle; accessory nerve (proprioception C3, 4); stabilizes and moves the scapula.

**Why:** Slide 27.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the platysma.</summary>

**Answer:** From fascia over the pectoralis major and deltoid to the skin and subcutaneous tissue of the face; facial nerve; depresses the lower jaw and draws down the lower lip and mouth angles.

**Why:** Slide 28.
</details>

## 7. Muscles of the posterior triangle and prevertebral muscles

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Nerve | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Splenius capitis** | Ligamentum nuchae; C7-T4 spines | Mastoid process, skull below superior nuchal line | **Posterior rami of middle cervical nerves** | Together draw head back; singly rotate head to one side |
| **Levator scapulae** | Transverse processes **C1-C4** | Upper medial scapular border | **C3, C4; dorsal scapular (C4, C5)** | **Elevates scapula** |
| **Posterior scalene** | Posterior tubercles C4-C6 | **Rib II** | **C5-C7** | Elevates **rib II** |
| **Middle scalene** | C2-C7 | **Rib I** | **C3-C8** | Elevates **rib I** |
| **Anterior scalene** | Anterior tubercles C3-C6 | **Scalene tubercle, rib I** | **C4-C7** | Elevates **rib I** |
| **Rectus capitis anterior** | Lateral atlas | Basilar occipital | **C1, C2** | Flexes head at **atlanto-occipital joint** |
| **Rectus capitis lateralis** | Transverse process of atlas | Jugular process of occipital | **C1, C2** | Flexes head **laterally** |
| **Longus capitis** | Transverse processes C3-C6 | Basilar occipital | **C1-C3** | Flexes the head |
| **Longus colli** | Anterior cervical and T1-T2 vertebrae | Anterior tubercles of cervical vertebrae | **C2-C6** | Flexes neck, slight rotation to the **opposite** side |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Posterior scalene → rib II; middle and anterior scalenes → rib I.
- Prevertebral muscles flex the head and neck; supplied by C1-C6.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the muscles of the posterior triangle.</summary>

**Answer:** Splenius capitis, levator scapulae, and the posterior, middle and anterior scalene muscles.

**Why:** Slide 29.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare the three scalene muscles.</summary>

**Answer:** The posterior scalene elevates rib II (anterior rami C5-C7); the middle scalene elevates rib I (C3-C8); the anterior scalene elevates rib I via the scalene tubercle (C4-C7).

**Why:** Slide 30.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the prevertebral muscles and their action.</summary>

**Answer:** Rectus capitis anterior and lateralis, longus capitis and longus colli; they flex the head and neck (the lateralis flexes it to the same side).

**Why:** Slides 31-32.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Facial nerve: stylohyoid and posterior belly of digastric; mylohyoid nerve: anterior belly and mylohyoid.**
- **Thyrohyoid gets C1 via the hypoglossal nerve; the other infrahyoids get C1-C3 via the ansa cervicalis.**
- **SCM proprioception C2, 3; trapezius C3, 4 (both motor = accessory nerve).**
- **Posterior scalene → rib II; middle and anterior → rib I.**
- **Carotid triangle: carotid sheath + CN IX and XII.**
- **Muscular triangle is a triangle of the anterior neck, not the posterior.**
