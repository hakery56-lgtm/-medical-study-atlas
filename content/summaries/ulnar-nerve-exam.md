## 1. Introduction and anatomy

- **Most often affected at the elbow** (**superficial in some individuals**): **external compression behind the medial epicondyle, injury (elbow dislocation/fracture), cubital tunnel syndrome** (entrapment neuropathy at the elbow).
- **Roots C8, T1 (C7 inconstant); medial cord of the brachial plexus.**
- **Motor:** **flexor carpi ulnaris; ulnar half of flexor digitorum profundus; hypothenar muscles; 3rd and 4th lumbricals; interossei; adductor pollicis; deep head of flexor pollicis brevis.**
- **Sensory:** **little finger, ulnar half of ring finger, ulnar border of the hand**; **autonomous zone = tip of little finger**; **cutaneous branches: palmar, dorsal, palmar digital.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Ulnar nerve: C8–T1, medial cord.
- Autonomous zone: tip of the little finger.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Why is the ulnar nerve vulnerable at the elbow?</summary>

**Answer:** It is superficial behind the medial epicondyle, so it is prone to external compression, injury and cubital tunnel entrapment.

**Why:** Slide 2.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List its motor supply.</summary>

**Answer:** Flexor carpi ulnaris, ulnar half of FDP, hypothenar muscles, 3rd and 4th lumbricals, interossei, adductor pollicis and deep head of flexor pollicis brevis.

**Why:** Slide 3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give its sensory and autonomous zones.</summary>

**Answer:** Little finger and ulnar half of ring finger and ulnar border of the hand; autonomous zone is the tip of the little finger.

**Why:** Slide 3.
</details>

## 2. Before you start, Look and Feel

- **Equipment:** neurotip or pin, cotton wool, reflex hammer. **Introduction:** wash hands, greet, introduce, explain, **permission**; sit comfortably with **both arms exposed from shoulder to fingertips**; ask about **pain, tingling, weakness, hand dominance**; **compare sides**; then **Look, Feel, Move**.
- **Look (both arms from front, side, back):** **muscle wasting** (hypothenar eminence, first dorsal interosseous, guttering between metacarpals); **deformity — claw hand** (**MCP hyperextension, IP flexion of ring and little fingers**); **scars, bruising, redness, swelling, callosities** around the elbow.
- **Feel:** **tenderness** along forearm and elbow (watch the face); **mass or swelling** along the nerve in the **groove behind the medial epicondyle**; **temperature** compared.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Claw hand: MCP hyperextended, IP flexed.
- Wasting: hypothenar and first dorsal interosseous.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is claw hand?</summary>

**Answer:** Hyperextension of the MCP joints with flexion of the IP joints of the ring and little fingers.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which muscles waste in ulnar palsy?</summary>

**Answer:** The hypothenar eminence and first dorsal interosseous, with guttering between the metacarpals.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is assessed in Feel?</summary>

**Answer:** Tenderness, masses or swelling in the groove behind the medial epicondyle, and temperature on both sides.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

## 3. Sensation and Move

- **Sensation:** **demonstrate on the forehead with eyes open → eyes closed: light touch (cotton wool) then pinprick; compare sides; start at the tip of the little finger, then the palmar surface of the medial hand.**
- **Move:** **active → passive → against resistance; compare sides.**

| Movement | Muscle |
|---|---|
| **Wrist flexion with ulnar deviation against resistance** | **Flexor carpi ulnaris** |
| **Flexion of ring and little fingers at the distal joint** | **Ulnar half of flexor digitorum profundus** |
| **Finger abduction (spread)** | **Dorsal interossei** |
| **Finger adduction (paper held between fingers while you pull)** | **Palmar interossei** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Dorsal interossei abduct; palmar adduct.
- Paper test = ulnar motor.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the sensory examination.</summary>

**Answer:** Demonstrate on the forehead, then with eyes closed use light touch and pinprick, comparing sides, starting at the tip of the little finger and the medial palm.

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which muscle is tested by finger abduction and adduction?</summary>

**Answer:** Abduction tests the dorsal interossei; adduction (paper test) tests the palmar interossei.

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What does wrist flexion with ulnar deviation test?</summary>

**Answer:** Flexor carpi ulnaris.

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>

## 4. Special tests

| Test | Method | Positive |
|---|---|---|
| **Froment's sign** | **Hold paper between thumb and index finger while you pull** (tests **adductor pollicis**) | **Thumb IP joint flexes to keep the grip** |
| **Tinel's sign at the elbow** | **Slightly flex the elbow and tap gently over the nerve behind the medial epicondyle** | **Tingling or electric-shock sensation → cubital tunnel syndrome** |
| **Elbow flexion test** | **Hold the elbow fully flexed with the wrist extended for 60 seconds** | **Tingling in the little and ring fingers** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Froment = adductor pollicis.
- Tinel = tap behind medial epicondyle.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe Froment's sign.</summary>

**Answer:** The patient holds paper between thumb and index finger while you pull it; flexion of the thumb IP joint to keep the grip is positive and reflects adductor pollicis weakness.

**Why:** Slide 9.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe Tinel's sign at the elbow.</summary>

**Answer:** With the elbow slightly flexed, tap gently over the nerve behind the medial epicondyle; tingling or an electric shock suggests cubital tunnel syndrome.

**Why:** Slide 9.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the elbow flexion test.</summary>

**Answer:** Hold the elbow fully flexed with the wrist extended for 60 seconds; tingling in the little and ring fingers is positive.

**Why:** Slide 9.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Froment's sign = adductor pollicis; thumb IP flexes (substitution by median FPL).**
- **Dorsal interossei ABduct; palmar interossei ADduct.**
- **Tinel's is at the medial epicondyle groove, not the wrist.**
