# Clinical Skills — Basic Life Support (CPR and AED) — Exam Night Review

3rd-stage mastery skill: **recognize cardiac arrest, activate EMS, high-quality compressions, rescue breaths and safe AED use.** After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer).

---

## 1. BLS, CPR and the 7 steps

- **BLS = CPR (compressions + breaths) + AED (shock).** **CPR** = **compressions** (circulate blood to brain and heart), **airway management**, **rescue breathing**. **High-quality compressions started quickly = most important factor.**
- **Adult CPR — 7 steps:** **1 safe scene → 2 tap and talk → 3 call 911 + AED → 4 check breathing → 5 start CPR → 6 30 compressions → 7 2 breaths.**
- **Step 1:** safe scene (an injured rescuer cannot help). **Step 2:** tap and ask "Are you okay?"; no response = **unresponsive**.
- **Step 3:** yell for help, send someone for an **AED**, call **911 on speaker**; no phone → send a second rescuer; **stay on the line**. Early EMS = first step of the **Chain of Survival**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Compressions first and fast.
- 7 steps in order.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define BLS and CPR.</summary>

**Answer:** BLS is a set of life-saving skills made of CPR (compressions and breaths) and an AED; CPR includes chest compressions, airway management and rescue breathing.

**Why:** Slides 4 and 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the 7 steps of adult CPR.</summary>

**Answer:** Safe scene, tap and talk, call 911 and get an AED, check breathing, start CPR, 30 compressions, 2 breaths.

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do you call for help?</summary>

**Answer:** Yell for help, send a bystander for an AED, call 911 on speaker, send a second rescuer if no phone, and stay on the line until the operator allows hanging up.

**Why:** Slide 9.
</details>

## 2. Check breathing and start CPR

- **Barely breathing or only gasping:** **gasping is abnormal and ineffective, common in cardiac arrest → begin CPR immediately.**
- **Breathing normally but unresponsive:** **roll on the side** (airway open, no choking on vomit); monitor and be ready for CPR.
- **Give CPR if unresponsive AND not breathing or only gasping.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Gasping = arrest → CPR.
- Normal breathing → recovery position.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do you interpret gasping?</summary>

**Answer:** It is abnormal and generally ineffective and often occurs in cardiac arrest, so begin CPR immediately.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What do you do if the unresponsive person breathes normally?</summary>

**Answer:** Roll them onto their side, monitor breathing and be ready to start CPR.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. When do you start CPR?</summary>

**Answer:** When the person is unresponsive and not breathing or only gasping.

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

## 3. Compressions

- **Position:** person on **firm flat surface**; open clothing; find the **end of the sternum**; **heel of one hand on the lower half of the breastbone**, other hand on top.
- **Danger zone:** **avoid the xiphoid process** (may break off and cut the **liver**).
- **Rate 100–120/min; depth 5–6 cm (2–2.4 in); full recoil** (hands stay in contact, no bouncing or leaning); **switch when tired**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 100–120/min, 5–6 cm, full recoil.
- Avoid the xiphoid.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the hand position.</summary>

**Answer:** Heel of one hand on the lower half of the breastbone with the other hand on top, on a firm flat surface with the chest exposed.

**Why:** Slide 12.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why avoid the xiphoid process?</summary>

**Answer:** It may break off and cut the liver, causing internal bleeding.

**Why:** Slide 13.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give the compression targets.</summary>

**Answer:** 100–120 per minute, 5–6 cm deep, with full recoil and no leaning.

**Why:** Slide 14.
</details>

## 4. Rescue breaths

- **Why:** maintain **oxygen**; compressions carry it to brain and organs; **chest rise = correct breath**. **Mask preferred**; mouth-to-mouth possible.
- **Airway:** **head-tilt/chin-lift** (hand on forehead, fingers on bony chin, tilt back and lift).
- **With mask:** pointed end over **bridge of nose**; open airway; **good seal**; breath **over 1 second**; second breath.
- **Mouth-to-mouth:** hold airway, **pinch nose**, seal mouth, blow **1 second**, repeat.
- **Chest not rising:** reposition (head-tilt/chin-lift) and retry; **two breaths ≤ 10 seconds**; if no rise, resume compressions.
- **Cycle: 30 compressions : 2 breaths** until **AED arrives, advanced help takes over, or the person responds**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 30:2; breaths ≤ 10 seconds.
- Head-tilt/chin-lift.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe breaths with a mask.</summary>

**Answer:** Place the mask over mouth and nose (point over the nose bridge), open the airway, ensure a seal, give a breath over 1 second watching the chest rise, then a second breath.

**Why:** Slide 17.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What if the chest does not rise?</summary>

**Answer:** Reposition the airway and try again; two breaths should not exceed 10 seconds, then resume compressions.

**Why:** Slide 19.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. When is CPR stopped?</summary>

**Answer:** When the AED arrives, advanced help takes over, or the person begins to respond.

**Why:** Slide 20.
</details>

## 5. AED

- **AED:** portable computer that **analyzes rhythm and decides if a shock is needed**; use when a person **collapses, is unresponsive and only gasping/barely breathing**; no AED → **CPR and send someone**.
- **Steps 1–4:** **power on, expose chest, apply pads, connect pads.** **Steps 5–8:** **clear the person, analyze rhythm, follow prompts (shock advised / no shock / check connection), resume CPR.**
- **Safety:** **medication patch → remove before pads; bulge under skin (pacemaker) → do not place pads over it; AED malfunction → continue CPR.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Clear everyone before analysis.
- Pacemaker: move the pads.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give the AED steps.</summary>

**Answer:** Power on, expose the chest, apply and connect pads, clear the person, let it analyze, follow prompts, then resume CPR.

**Why:** Slides 24–25.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the AED safety points?</summary>

**Answer:** Remove medication patches, avoid placing pads over a pacemaker, and continue CPR if the AED malfunctions.

**Why:** Slide 26.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Answer the case: adult collapses, unresponsive and gasping.</summary>

**Answer:** Safe scene, tap and ask, yell for help and send for an AED, check breathing (gasping so start CPR), 30 compressions at 100–120/min and 5–6 cm then 2 breaths, then use the AED when it arrives.

**Why:** Slides 28–29.
</details>

---

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **30:2, 100–120/min, 5–6 cm.**
- **Gasping is NOT normal breathing.**
- **Two breaths take at most 10 seconds.**
- **Clear everyone before the AED analyzes; avoid pads over a pacemaker.**
