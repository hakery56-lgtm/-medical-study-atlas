## 1. What is a computer network?

**Definition:** a system of **two or more electronic devices** connected together to **exchange information, share files, and access common resources** (such as printers and internet connections).
- The devices can be computers, smartphones, tablets, printers, or any digital equipment capable of data communication.
- Example: sending a file from a laptop to another computer over **Wi-Fi** is a simple network in action.

### Key characteristics (benefits) of networking
1. **Connectivity** — devices communicate and exchange data efficiently.
2. **Resource sharing** — many users share hardware resources and services.
3. **Scalability** — the network grows easily by adding new devices.
4. **Reliability** — consistent, stable data transfer.

## 2. Types of computer networks

Networks are classified by **geographical coverage, ownership and purpose**. The four major types, smallest to largest:

| Type | Coverage | Examples / use |
|---|---|---|
| **PAN** — Personal Area Network | **smallest**; one person's devices over a **short distance** | **Bluetooth, USB** (laptop, phone, printer) |
| **LAN** — Local Area Network | a **limited area**: a school, office or organization | **Wi-Fi at home or university**; shares files, apps, printers, internet |
| **MAN** — Metropolitan Area Network | a **city** or large area, **connecting several LANs** | universities, government institutions, city-wide systems |
| **WAN** — Wide Area Network | **largest**; across **countries or continents** | ⭐ **the Internet** is the best-known WAN |

Other types shown in the figure: **CAN** (campus area network), **VPN** (virtual private network), wireless networks.

> 💡 **Memory aid:** **P**-**L**-**M**-**W** from small to large: **P**ersonal → **L**ocal → **M**etropolitan → **W**ide.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- PAN (smallest, personal, Bluetooth/USB) < LAN (building/campus, Wi-Fi) < MAN (city, joins LANs) < WAN (countries; the Internet).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare LAN and WAN.</summary>

**Answer:** A LAN connects devices within a limited area such as a school, office or organization (e.g. home or university Wi-Fi) and lets users share files, applications, printers and internet access. A WAN is the largest type, connecting devices and LANs across countries or continents; the Internet is the best-known example.

**Why:** Coverage + example for each.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is a PAN? Give examples.</summary>

**Answer:** The smallest type of network, intended for individual use, connecting devices such as a laptop, smartphone and printer over a short distance — for example Bluetooth and USB.

**Why:** Definition + two examples.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is a MAN and where is it used?</summary>

**Answer:** A Metropolitan Area Network spans a city or large geographical area by connecting several LANs together; it suits universities, government institutions and city-wide communication systems.

**Why:** "Connects several LANs" is the key phrase.
</details>

## 4. Network security — threats

**Network security:** the collection of **technologies, procedures and best practices** used to **protect networks from unauthorized access, misuse, malfunction or destruction**. Its goals are **confidentiality, integrity and availability**.

### Common threats
| Threat | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Malware** | software designed to **damage or control** computer systems |
| **Phishing** | **tricking users into revealing confidential data** |
| **Denial of Service (DoS)** | **overloading servers with traffic** |
| **Unauthorized access** | **gaining entry without permission** |
| **Social engineering** | **manipulating people** to reveal private information |

> ⚠️ **Exam trap:** **Phishing** tricks users (usually via fake messages/links) into giving data; **social engineering** is the broader idea of **manipulating people**; **DoS** attacks **availability** by flooding servers.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Network security = technologies, procedures and practices protecting against unauthorized access, misuse, malfunction, destruction.
- Goals: confidentiality, integrity, availability.
- Threats: malware, phishing, DoS, unauthorized access, social engineering.

</div>

## 5. Security measures & best practices

### Basic security measures
- **Strong passwords**
- **Two-factor authentication (2FA)**
- **Firewalls**
- **Antivirus & anti-malware**
- **Encryption**
- **Regular software updates**
- (Figure also: intrusion detection systems, data loss prevention, security policies, secure communications.)

### Best practices for users
1. **Don't share** personal or login information.
2. **Avoid unknown links** and suspicious attachments.
3. **Check for `https://`** in website addresses.
4. **Log out** from public computers.
5. **Back up** important data regularly.

### Lecture summary
- Networks enable communication and data exchange; they vary in size (PAN, LAN, MAN, WAN).
- Components include routers, switches, NICs and firewalls.
- Security ensures **confidentiality, integrity and availability**; **awareness and protection** are key.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Measures: strong passwords, 2FA, firewalls, antivirus, encryption, updates.
- User practices: keep credentials private, avoid unknown links, check https://, log out on public PCs, back up data.

</div>
