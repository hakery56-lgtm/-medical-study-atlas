# Bone Fracture: Classification, Clinical Picture & First Aid — Exam Night Review

A short, very practical lecture: **how fractures are classified, how a fracture presents, and what first aid you give** (plus the emergency signs you must never miss). After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer).

---

## 1. Classification of fractures

Fractures are classified in **three ways**:

### A. According to the skin
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Closed (simple)** | the overlying **skin is intact** |
| **Open (compound)** | there is a **wound in the skin** communicating with the fracture — bone may be exposed |

⭐ An open fracture is more serious: the wound is a route for **infection** and there may be **bleeding** from the wound.

### B. According to the fracture line
| Line | Description |
|---|---|
| **Transverse** | straight across the bone, at right angles to its long axis |
| **Oblique** | runs at an angle across the bone |
| **Spiral** | twists around the shaft (usually a twisting injury) |
| **Comminuted** | the bone is broken into **more than two fragments** |

### C. According to displacement
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Undisplaced** | the fragments remain in their normal alignment |
| **Displaced** | the fragments have moved out of alignment |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Three classifications: **skin** (closed/open), **fracture line** (transverse, oblique, spiral, comminuted), **displacement** (undisplaced/displaced).
- Closed = skin intact; open (compound) = skin wound over the fracture ± exposed bone.
- Comminuted = more than two fragments; spiral = twisting line around the shaft.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the three ways fractures are classified, with the types under each.</summary>

**Answer:** (1) According to skin: closed (simple) and open (compound). (2) According to the fracture line: transverse, oblique, spiral and comminuted. (3) According to displacement: undisplaced and displaced.

**Why:** This is the exact framework of the lecture — memorize it as 2 + 4 + 2.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate between a closed and an open fracture.</summary>

**Answer:** In a closed (simple) fracture the overlying skin remains intact. In an open (compound) fracture there is a break in the skin near the site of the bone injury, and the bone may be exposed; it carries a risk of infection and bleeding and needs the wound to be covered with a clean/sterile dressing.

**Why:** The skin decides the name — and changes the first aid.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the four types of fracture line.</summary>

**Answer:** Transverse — straight across the bone; oblique — at an angle across the bone; spiral — twisting around the shaft; comminuted — the bone is broken into more than two fragments.

**Why:** Each name describes the shape of the line seen on X-ray.
</details>

## 2. Clinical picture

How does a patient with a fracture present?

1. **Pain** — usually **severe** and **worsened by movement**.
2. **Swelling and tenderness**.
3. **Bruising or discoloration**.
4. **Deformity**.
5. **Loss of function** / inability to use the limb.
6. **Abnormal mobility** and possible **crepitus** — ⚠️ **do not test these deliberately** (it causes pain and more damage).
7. **Open fracture:** a **wound** and possibly **exposed bone**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pain (severe, worse on movement), swelling, tenderness, bruising, deformity, loss of function.
- Abnormal mobility and crepitus may be present — never test them on purpose.
- Open fracture adds a wound ± exposed bone.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the clinical features of a fracture.</summary>

**Answer:** Pain (usually severe and worsened by movement), swelling and tenderness, bruising or discoloration, deformity, loss of function / inability to use the limb, abnormal mobility and possible crepitus; in an open fracture there is a wound and possibly exposed bone.

**Why:** Seven features — the exam usually asks for at least five.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why should abnormal mobility and crepitus not be tested deliberately?</summary>

**Answer:** Moving the fragments to feel crepitus or abnormal mobility causes severe pain and can increase the damage to the surrounding soft tissues, vessels and nerves, or convert a closed fracture into an open one. They should only be noted if observed.

**Why:** "Do not test deliberately" is written in the lecture — a favourite exam point.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What extra finding distinguishes an open fracture clinically?</summary>

**Answer:** A wound in the skin near the fracture site, through which the bone may be exposed.

**Why:** Open = the fracture communicates with the outside.
</details>

## 3. First aid

### Main first-aid steps
1. **Ensure safety** and **call emergency medical services**.
2. **Immobilize / splint in the position found**.
3. **Support the joints above and below** the suspected fracture.
4. ⚠️ **Do NOT straighten or realign** the bone.
5. **Open fracture:** cover the wound with a **clean / sterile dressing**.
6. ⚠️ **Do NOT push exposed bone back inside**.

### Additional first aid
- Apply a **cold pack wrapped in cloth** near the injury.
- **Do not apply ice directly to the skin**.
- Keep the person **warm and reassured**.
- **Avoid food or drink** if **surgery / anaesthesia** may be required.

The lecture figures show splinting: **whole-body immobilization**, a **forearm splint** and a **lower-limb splint**; and **bleeding control**: pressure with the hands, applying a dressing and pressing, and a **tourniquet** (wrap, wind, secure, **note the time**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Safety + call EMS → splint in the position found → support the joints above and below.
- Never straighten the bone; never push exposed bone back; cover open wounds with a sterile dressing.
- Cold pack wrapped in cloth (not ice on skin); keep warm and reassured; nothing by mouth if surgery is likely.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the first aid of a suspected fracture.</summary>

**Answer:** Ensure safety and call emergency medical services; immobilize/splint the limb in the position found, supporting the joints above and below the fracture; do not straighten or realign the bone; for an open fracture cover the wound with a clean/sterile dressing and do not push the exposed bone back inside. In addition apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth (not ice directly on the skin), keep the person warm and reassured, and avoid food or drink if surgery or anaesthesia may be needed.

**Why:** Main steps + additional steps = full marks.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is the limb splinted including the joints above and below the fracture?</summary>

**Answer:** Movement of the joints above and below moves the fracture fragments; immobilizing both joints keeps the fracture still, reducing pain, bleeding and further injury to nearby vessels and nerves.

**Why:** "Protect: keep the fracture still" is a key point of the lecture.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why should a patient with a fracture avoid food and drink?</summary>

**Answer:** Because surgery under anaesthesia may be required; a full stomach increases the risk of vomiting and aspiration during anaesthesia.

**Why:** A small practical point that is easy to ask.
</details>

## 4. Emergency signs

These need **immediate emergency medical care**:
- **Severe or uncontrolled bleeding**.
- **Difficulty breathing** or **loss of consciousness**.
- **Suspected spinal or pelvic injury**.
- **Absent pulse distal to the injury**.
- **Cold, pale or blue limb** (vascular compromise).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Red flags: uncontrolled bleeding, breathing difficulty / unconsciousness, spinal or pelvic injury.
- Vascular red flags: absent distal pulse; cold, pale or blue limb.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the emergency signs in a patient with a fracture.</summary>

**Answer:** Severe or uncontrolled bleeding; difficulty breathing or loss of consciousness; suspected spinal or pelvic injury; absent pulse distal to the injury; a cold, pale or blue limb. These require immediate emergency medical care.

**Why:** Five red flags straight from the lecture.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What does an absent distal pulse with a cold, pale limb indicate?</summary>

**Answer:** Injury or compression of the main artery by the fracture (vascular compromise) — the limb is not being perfused, so it is an emergency.

**Why:** The pulse and colour check tell you about the circulation beyond the fracture.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why should circulation and sensation be checked before and after splinting?</summary>

**Answer:** To detect vascular or nerve injury caused by the fracture, and to make sure the splint itself is not too tight and compressing the vessels or nerves.

**Why:** It is one of the lecture's key points.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Closed = simple, open = compound** — don't swap them.
- **Comminuted** means **more than two fragments**, not "a small crack".
- Splint **in the position found** — **never straighten** the bone.
- **Cold pack wrapped in cloth** — ice **never directly** on the skin.
- Open fracture: **cover** the wound — **never push the bone back**.
- Crepitus and abnormal mobility are features — but **not tested deliberately**.

## 🔥 Final review — memorize before the exam

1. Classification: **skin** (closed/simple, open/compound), **line** (transverse, oblique, spiral, comminuted), **displacement** (undisplaced/displaced).
2. Clinical picture: **pain** (severe, worse on movement), **swelling, tenderness, bruising, deformity, loss of function**, abnormal mobility/crepitus (don't test).
3. First aid: **safety + EMS → splint in position found → joints above and below**.
4. **Don't straighten; don't push bone back; sterile dressing for open wounds**.
5. **Cold pack in cloth**, keep **warm and reassured**, **nothing by mouth**.
6. Emergency: **bleeding, breathing/consciousness, spine/pelvis, absent distal pulse, cold/pale/blue limb**.
7. Key points: **Recognize → Protect → Don't manipulate → Control bleeding → Check circulation & sensation → Seek assessment**.
