# Shoulder Impingement Syndrome (SAPS) — مراجعة ليلة الامتحان

هلا بيك 👋 هاي الملزمة clinical أكثر: تعريف، anatomy، فحص سريري، imaging، وعلاج. أهم شي فيها الـ **special tests** والفروقات بين الأمراض اللي تشبه بعض. بعد كل موضوع **Key points** و3 أسئلة (اضغط على السؤال حتى يطلع الجواب).

---

## 1. التعريف وتحديث المصطلح

- **Shoulder impingement syndrome** = **clinical syndrome** يتميز بـ **shoulder pain**، خصوصاً مع **رفع اليد (elevation)**، وعادةً يشمل الـ **rotator-cuff tendons** و/أو الـ **subacromial bursa**.
- ⭐ هسه المصادر الحديثة تفضّل اسم **subacromial pain syndrome (SAPS)** أو **rotator-cuff-related shoulder pain**، لأن الألم **multifactorial** وما نكدر ننسبه بس لـ **mechanical compression**.
- ونتيجة هذا التغيير، العلاج تحوّل نحو **exercise-based rehabilitation** بدل **decompression surgery**.

**الأنسجة الأكثر إصابة:**
- **Supraspinatus tendon** (⭐ الأكثر)
- **Infraspinatus tendon**
- **Subacromial bursa**
- **Long head of biceps tendon**
- باقي مكونات الـ rotator cuff

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Impingement = shoulder pain especially on elevation, involving rotator-cuff tendons and/or subacromial bursa.
- Preferred modern terms: subacromial pain syndrome (SAPS) / rotator-cuff-related shoulder pain — pain is multifactorial, not just compression.
- Management shifted to exercise-based rehab rather than decompression surgery.

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. Define shoulder impingement syndrome and explain why the term SAPS is preferred.</summary>

**Answer:** A clinical syndrome of shoulder pain, particularly with elevation of the arm, commonly involving the rotator-cuff tendons and/or subacromial bursa. Subacromial pain syndrome is preferred because the pain mechanism is multifactorial and cannot reliably be attributed to mechanical compression alone.

**ليش؟** التعريف + سبب تغيير الاسم = سؤال مؤكد تقريباً.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the structures commonly involved in subacromial pain syndrome.</summary>

**Answer:** Supraspinatus tendon, infraspinatus tendon, subacromial bursa, long head of biceps tendon and other components of the rotator cuff.

**ليش؟** الـ supraspinatus أول وحدة لأنها الأكثر.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How has the management emphasis of impingement changed?</summary>

**Answer:** It has shifted toward exercise-based rehabilitation rather than subacromial decompression surgery, because symptoms are not simply explained by mechanical impingement.

**ليش؟** يربط تغيير المفهوم بتغيير العلاج.
</details>

## 2. Anatomy & Biomechanics

### Rotator cuff (4 muscles)
| Muscle | Main action | Nerve |
|---|---|---|
| **Supraspinatus** | **initiates abduction** | **suprascapular nerve** |
| **Infraspinatus** | **external rotation** | **suprascapular nerve** |
| **Teres minor** | **external rotation** | **axillary nerve** |
| **Subscapularis** | **internal rotation** | **upper/lower subscapular nerves** |

- الـ **supraspinatus tendon** يمر **تحت الـ acromion** قبل ما يدخل على الـ **greater tuberosity** → لهذا هو الأكثر إصابة.
- الـ **subacromial bursa** موجودة بين **rotator cuff** و **acromion/deltoid**.

### Normal biomechanics
- رفع اليد الطبيعي يحتاج تنسيق بين: **glenohumeral joint، scapula، clavicle، thoracic spine**.
- الـ **rotator cuff** وظيفته يخلي **humeral head centered within the glenoid** بينما العضلات الكبيرة تسوي الحركة.
- **Scapulohumeral rhythm** ≈ **2° glenohumeral : 1° scapular** أثناء الرفع الكامل (الرقم يتغير خلال الحركة).
- أي خلل بحركة الـ scapula، ضعف، أو **altered motor control** ممكن يسبب أعراض.

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Supraspinatus (initiates abduction) & infraspinatus (ER) — suprascapular n.; teres minor (ER) — axillary n.; subscapularis (IR) — subscapular nn.
- Supraspinatus tendon passes under the acromion → greater tuberosity.
- Subacromial bursa lies between cuff and acromion/deltoid.
- Rotator cuff centers the humeral head; scapulohumeral rhythm ≈ 2:1.

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. Tabulate the rotator cuff muscles with their actions and nerve supply.</summary>

**Answer:** Supraspinatus — initiates abduction — suprascapular nerve. Infraspinatus — external rotation — suprascapular nerve. Teres minor — external rotation — axillary nerve. Subscapularis — internal rotation — upper and lower subscapular nerves.

**ليش؟** جدول الأعصاب يجي كسؤال مباشر.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the scapulohumeral rhythm?</summary>

**Answer:** The coordinated movement of the glenohumeral joint and scapula during elevation: approximately 2° of glenohumeral movement for every 1° of scapular movement during full elevation, although the ratio varies through the range.

**ليش؟** الرقم 2:1 هو المطلوب.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is the supraspinatus most frequently involved?</summary>

**Answer:** Its tendon passes beneath the acromion before inserting on the greater tuberosity, so it is frequently involved in rotator-cuff-related shoulder pain.

**ليش؟** الموقع التشريحي هو السبب.
</details>

## 3. Pathophysiology & Risk factors

### النموذج القديم
الـ rotator cuff (خصوصاً **supraspinatus**) ينضغط بين **humeral head ↔ acromion/coracoacromial arch** أثناء الرفع.

### الفهم الحديث (أعقد)
عوامل مساهمة:
- **Tendon overload** و **reduced tendon capacity**
- **Age-related tendon degeneration**
- **Altered load management**
- **Rotator-cuff weakness**
- **Scapular motor-control abnormalities**
- **Thoracic posture/movement**
- **Subacromial bursal nociception**
- **Central/peripheral pain sensitization**
- **Occupational or sporting repetitive loading**

⭐ الخلاصة: **Shoulder pain ≠ simply a narrow subacromial space**.

### Risk factors
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Occupational** | repetitive overhead work، manual labor، heavy lifting، repetitive reaching |
| **Sporting** | swimming، volleyball، tennis، baseball، throwing sports، weightlifting |
| **Patient-related** | increasing age، **smoking**، **diabetes**، metabolic disease، poor conditioning، previous injury |
| **Biomechanical** | rotator-cuff weakness، **scapular dyskinesis**، reduced thoracic mobility، repetitive overhead loading |

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Old model: cuff compressed between humeral head and acromion/coracoacromial arch.
- Modern: multifactorial — overload, degeneration, weakness, scapular control, posture, bursal nociception, pain sensitization.
- Shoulder pain ≠ simply a narrow subacromial space.
- Risks: overhead work/sports, age, smoking, diabetes, scapular dyskinesis.

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the traditional and modern understanding of impingement pathophysiology.</summary>

**Answer:** Traditionally, the rotator cuff (especially supraspinatus) was thought to be compressed between the humeral head and the acromion/coracoacromial arch during elevation. Modern understanding is multifactorial: tendon overload, reduced tendon capacity, age-related degeneration, altered load management, cuff weakness, scapular motor-control abnormalities, thoracic posture, subacromial bursal nociception, pain sensitization and repetitive loading — so pain is not simply a narrow subacromial space.

**ليش؟** السؤال يريد الانتقال من mechanical لـ multifactorial.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the risk factors for subacromial pain syndrome.</summary>

**Answer:** Occupational (repetitive overhead work, manual labor, heavy lifting, repetitive reaching); sporting (swimming, volleyball, tennis, baseball, throwing sports, weightlifting); patient-related (increasing age, smoking, diabetes, metabolic disease, poor conditioning, previous injury); biomechanical (cuff weakness, scapular dyskinesis, reduced thoracic mobility, repetitive overhead loading).

**ليش؟** قسّمها لأربع مجموعات حتى ما تنسى.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What biomechanical factors contribute to shoulder impingement?</summary>

**Answer:** Rotator-cuff weakness, scapular dyskinesis, reduced thoracic mobility and repetitive overhead loading.

**ليش؟** أربع نقاط قصيرة.
</details>

## 4. Clinical presentation, History & Red flags

### المريض النموذجي
**Middle-aged adult** عنده ألم كتف **يتقدم تدريجياً**، أسوأ مع **overhead activity**.

- **مكان الألم**: **anterolateral shoulder**، **lateral upper arm/deltoid region**، أحياناً anterior.
- ممكن ينزل **باتجاه الـ elbow** بس ⭐ **عادةً ما ينزل تحت الـ elbow**.
- **Aggravating**: reaching overhead، لبس الملابس، **تمشيط الشعر**، reaching behind the back، lifting، **النوم على الجهة المصابة**.
- **Night pain**: شائع ويأثر على النوم.

### History — اسأل عن
- **Onset**: acute أو gradual؟ trauma؟ repetitive activity؟
- **Site**: anterior/lateral/superior/posterior؟
- **Character**: mechanical، constant، burning، neuropathic؟
- **Functional limitation**: يلبس؟ يمشط؟ يوصل للرف؟ ورا ظهره؟ يشيل أشياء؟
- **Associated**: weakness، neck pain، numbness، paraesthesia، instability، fever، weight loss.

### 🚩 Red flags — لا تشخّص impingement تلقائياً
significant trauma، **acute inability to actively elevate the arm**، severe unremitting pain، fever، constitutional symptoms، history of malignancy، neurological deficit، vascular symptoms، deformity، suspected fracture/dislocation، **significant true weakness**.

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Typical: middle-aged, gradual pain, worse overhead; anterolateral shoulder/deltoid region; does not go below the elbow; night pain.
- Aggravated by overhead reaching, dressing, combing hair, reaching behind back, sleeping on that side.
- Red flags: trauma, acute inability to elevate arm, fever, malignancy, neuro deficit, deformity, true weakness.

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the typical clinical presentation of subacromial pain syndrome.</summary>

**Answer:** A middle-aged adult with gradually progressive shoulder pain worse with overhead activity, located in the anterolateral shoulder or lateral upper arm/deltoid region, sometimes radiating toward but not below the elbow; aggravated by reaching overhead, dressing, combing hair, reaching behind the back, lifting and lying on the affected side; night pain is common.

**ليش؟** وصف كامل للمريض النموذجي.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the red flags that should prevent automatically diagnosing impingement.</summary>

**Answer:** Significant trauma, acute inability to actively elevate the arm, severe unremitting pain, fever, constitutional symptoms, history of malignancy, neurological deficit, vascular symptoms, deformity, suspected fracture/dislocation and significant true weakness.

**ليش؟** الـ red flags دائماً سؤال مهم بالـ clinical.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What should be asked in the history of a patient with shoulder pain?</summary>

**Answer:** Onset (acute/gradual, trauma, repetitive activity), site, character (mechanical, constant, burning, neuropathic), functional limitation (dressing, combing hair, reaching a shelf or behind the back, lifting) and associated symptoms (weakness, neck pain, numbness, paraesthesia, instability, fever, weight loss).

**ليش؟** ترتيب الـ history حسب الملزمة.
</details>

## 5. Physical examination & Special tests

### Step 1 — Inspection
muscle wasting، asymmetry، **scapular winging**، swelling، deformity، scars — **وقارن الجهتين**.

### Range of movement
- افحص **active** و **passive** (flexion، extension، abduction، IR، ER).
- ⭐ **Key distinction**: بالـ **impingement** الـ **passive ROM usually preserved**؛ بالـ **adhesive capsulitis** الـ **active + passive restricted**.

### Special tests
| Test | Technique | Positive / meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Painful arc** | active abduction | **pain 60–120°** ويتحسن بعدها → subacromial pain (**not pathognomonic**) |
| **Neer** | **stabilize scapula، internally rotate، passively elevate forward** | يطلع نفس ألم المريض → subacromial pain (مو دليل على mechanical impingement) |
| **Hawkins–Kennedy** | **shoulder 90° flexion + elbow 90°**، الفاحص يسوي **internal rotation** | يطلع الألم → يدعم subacromial pain |
| **Jobe / Empty-can** | **90° abduction، slight forward flexion، thumb down**، يقاوم الضغط لتحت | يفحص **supraspinatus**: **pain only** → tendinopathy؛ **marked weakness** → **cuff tear** |
| **External rotation test** | قارن قوة الـ ER بالجهتين | **infraspinatus + teres minor**؛ ضعف واضح → structural pathology |
| **Lift-off** | اليد ورا الظهر ويحاول يبعدها | **subscapularis** |
| **Belly-press** | يضغط الكف على البطن والكوع ثابت | **subscapularis** |

### Tests للـ differential
- **AC joint**: **cross-body adduction test** (ألم بمكان الـ AC joint).
- **Biceps**: **Speed test**، **Yergason test**.
- **Cervical spine**: neck + neurological exam + **Spurling-type test** — لأن **cervical radiculopathy may mimic shoulder disease**.

> ⚠️ **انتبه:** كل tests الـ impingement (Neer، Hawkins) **تدعم** الـ subacromial pain بس **ما تثبت** lesion معين. والـ Empty-can: **ألم = tendinopathy**، **ضعف = tear**.

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Passive ROM preserved in impingement; restricted in adhesive capsulitis.
- Painful arc 60–120°; Neer (scapula stabilized, IR, passive forward elevation); Hawkins–Kennedy (90°/90°, IR).
- Empty-can (supraspinatus): pain → tendinopathy, weakness → tear. ER test: infraspinatus/teres minor. Lift-off & belly-press: subscapularis.
- Cross-body adduction: AC joint. Speed/Yergason: biceps. Examine the neck (radiculopathy mimics).

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the Neer and Hawkins–Kennedy tests and their interpretation.</summary>

**Answer:** Neer: stabilize the scapula, internally rotate the arm and passively elevate it forward; reproduction of the patient's familiar pain is positive. Hawkins–Kennedy: shoulder and elbow flexed to about 90°, then the examiner internally rotates the shoulder; reproduction of pain is positive. Both support subacromial pain but are not proof of a specific mechanical lesion.

**ليش؟** الطريقة + التفسير الصحيح (supporting، مو proof).
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain the Jobe (empty-can) test and how to interpret pain versus weakness.</summary>

**Answer:** It assesses the supraspinatus: shoulder at about 90° abduction with slight forward flexion and thumb down while the patient resists downward pressure. Pain only may reflect tendinopathy/subacromial pain; marked weakness raises suspicion of a rotator-cuff tear.

**ليش؟** التمييز بين الألم والضعف هو جوهر السؤال.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is the subscapularis examined?</summary>

**Answer:** By the lift-off test (the patient places the hand behind the back and tries to lift it away) and the belly-press test (the patient presses the palm into the abdomen while maintaining elbow position).

**ليش؟** اسم الاختبارين + الطريقة.
</details>

## 6. Differential diagnosis (مقارنات مهمة)

### Impingement vs Rotator-cuff tear — ⭐ "major postgraduate examination issue"
| Feature | Subacromial pain | Rotator-cuff tear |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | common | common |
| Painful arc | common | may occur |
| Passive ROM | usually preserved | usually preserved initially |
| Active elevation | painful | **may be markedly impaired** |
| True weakness | usually limited by pain | **often prominent** |
| Trauma | not necessary | may be present |
| Atrophy | usually absent | may occur |
| **Drop-arm** | usually negative | **may be positive** |

⭐ **Key point**: مريض **فجأة ما يكدر يرفع يده بعد trauma** → قيّمه لـ **acute rotator-cuff tear** مو "impingement".

### Impingement vs Adhesive capsulitis
| Feature | Impingement | Adhesive capsulitis |
|---|---|---|
| Active ROM | painful | **restricted** |
| **Passive ROM** | **usually preserved** | **restricted** |
| **External rotation** | usually preserved | **markedly restricted** |
| **Diabetes** | possible | **strong association** |

- Classic adhesive capsulitis pattern: **External rotation > abduction > internal rotation** restriction.

### Other differentials (من الأهداف)
rotator-cuff tear، adhesive capsulitis، **cervical radiculopathy**، **AC-joint disease**، **glenohumeral arthritis**، **calcific tendinopathy**.

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cuff tear: marked active elevation impairment, prominent true weakness, trauma, atrophy, positive drop-arm.
- Acute traumatic inability to elevate → assess for acute cuff tear, not "impingement".
- Adhesive capsulitis: active AND passive ROM restricted, ER markedly restricted, strong diabetes link; ER > abduction > IR.

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. Differentiate subacromial pain syndrome from a rotator-cuff tear.</summary>

**Answer:** Both cause pain and passive ROM is usually preserved (initially in tears). In a tear, active elevation may be markedly impaired, true weakness is often prominent, trauma may be present, atrophy may occur and the drop-arm test may be positive; in subacromial pain, weakness is usually limited by pain, trauma is not necessary, atrophy is absent and drop-arm is negative. Sudden inability to elevate the arm after trauma should be assessed as an acute cuff tear.

**ليش؟** الأستاذ نفسه كتب إنها "major examination issue".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate impingement from adhesive capsulitis.</summary>

**Answer:** In impingement, active ROM is painful but passive ROM and external rotation are usually preserved. In adhesive capsulitis, both active and passive ROM are restricted, external rotation is markedly restricted, there is a strong association with diabetes, and the classic pattern of restriction is external rotation > abduction > internal rotation.

**ليش؟** الـ passive ROM هو المفتاح.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the differential diagnoses of subacromial pain syndrome.</summary>

**Answer:** Rotator-cuff tear, adhesive capsulitis, cervical radiculopathy, AC-joint disease, glenohumeral arthritis and calcific tendinopathy.

**ليش؟** ست تشخيصات من الـ learning objectives.
</details>

## 7. Imaging

الـ imaging **يكمّل** الفحص السريري، مو بديله.

| Modality | When | What it shows / advantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Plain X-ray** | trauma، persistent symptoms، suspected arthritis or **calcific tendinopathy**، older patient with unexplained symptoms، alternative diagnosis | AC arthritis، glenohumeral OA، **calcific deposits**، fracture، **acromial morphology**، **high-riding humeral head** (advanced cuff disease) |
| **Ultrasound** | cuff tears، tendinopathy، bursitis، biceps، **dynamic assessment** | **no radiation**، **inexpensive**، **dynamic**، يقارن الجهتين؛ **operator-dependent** |
| **MRI** | significant weakness (tear؟)، trauma، فشل العلاج المحافظ، قبل الجراحة، تشخيص غير واضح، intra-articular pathology | tendinopathy، partial/full tear، **atrophy**، **fatty infiltration**، bursitis، labral، AC pathology |

⭐ **Important postgraduate point**: الـ MRI abnormalities **شائعة خصوصاً مع العمر** → **"Do not treat the MRI; treat the patient"** وربط الصورة بالأعراض والفحص.

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- X-ray: trauma, persistent symptoms, arthritis, calcific tendinopathy; shows calcific deposits, acromial morphology, high-riding humeral head.
- Ultrasound: tears, tendinopathy, bursitis; no radiation, cheap, dynamic; operator-dependent.
- MRI: weakness/trauma/failed conservative care/surgery planning; shows tears, atrophy, fatty infiltration.
- MRI abnormalities are common with age — treat the patient, not the MRI.

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. What are the indications and findings of plain X-ray in shoulder pain?</summary>

**Answer:** Indications: trauma, persistent symptoms, suspected arthritis or calcific tendinopathy, older patient with unexplained symptoms, suspected alternative diagnosis. Findings: AC arthritis, glenohumeral OA, calcific deposits, fracture, acromial morphology and a high-riding humeral head in advanced cuff disease.

**ليش؟** قائمتين: متى + شنو يبين.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the advantages of ultrasound in shoulder assessment?</summary>

**Answer:** It is very useful for cuff tears, tendinopathy, bursitis and biceps pathology; it has no radiation, is relatively inexpensive, allows dynamic assessment and comparison of both shoulders; however, operator experience matters.

**ليش؟** المميزات + العيب الوحيد.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Explain the statement "Do not treat the MRI; treat the patient".</summary>

**Answer:** MRI abnormalities (such as tendinopathy or partial tears) are common, especially with increasing age, even without symptoms; therefore imaging findings must be correlated with the patient's symptoms and examination before deciding treatment.

**ليش؟** نقطة postgraduate صريحة بالملزمة.
</details>

## 8. Management

### Treatment hierarchy (بالترتيب)
**Education + activity modification → Exercise-based rehabilitation → Analgesia/NSAID when appropriate → Corticosteroid injection (selected patients) → Reassess diagnosis & adherence → Specialist if persistent/structural**

### Education
- الحالة **usually not dangerous**.
- **الألم مو معناه progressive structural damage**.
- **Complete rest is generally unnecessary**؛ **gradual loading** مهم.
- الشفاء ياخذ **أسابيع لأشهر**؛ الالتزام بالتمارين أساسي.

### Activity modification
تجنب مؤقتاً الـ repetitive painful overhead lifting والأوزان الثقيلة، **بس بدون immobilization طويل**. الهدف: ⭐ **"Modify load, not eliminate movement."**

### Exercise therapy — ⭐ **the cornerstone**
- **Rotator cuff**: ER و IR strengthening، supraspinatus loading، progressive resistance.
- **Scapular muscles**: **serratus anterior**، **middle & lower trapezius**، **rhomboids**.
- **Mobility**: posterior shoulder، thoracic mobility، stretching.
- **Progression**: load → repetitions → resistance → functional activity.

### Analgesics
- **Paracetamol** حسب الحاجة.
- **NSAIDs**: فائدة **short-term**؛ انتبه للموانع: **peptic ulcer، CKD، heart failure، significant CVD، anticoagulation، NSAID hypersensitivity**. ⭐ **NSAIDs support rehabilitation rather than replace it**.

### Corticosteroid injection
- ينعطى لما الألم **يمنع الـ rehabilitation**.
- فائدته: **rapid short-term relief** — **adjunct** مو علاج نهائي.
- ⭐ دراسة: injection + exercise أحسن بالمدى القصير، بس **بعد 12 أسبوع ماكو فرق** مقارنة بالتمارين وحدها.
- انتبه من **repeated injections** خصوصاً إذا اكو **cuff tear** أو ممكن جراحة.

### Physiotherapy modalities
manual therapy، taping، education، mobility — بس الأساس يبقى **progressive exercise**؛ الأدلة للـ passive modalities أضعف.

### Surgery
- قبل كانوا يسوون **arthroscopic subacromial decompression / acromioplasty**.
- ⭐ هسه **الأدلة ما تدعم الـ routine decompression** للـ uncomplicated SAPS. **BMJ guideline strongly recommended against** لأن الـ trials ما بينت تحسن مهم بالألم أو الوظيفة أو الـ quality of life مقارنة بالـ non-operative/placebo.

### When to refer
acute traumatic weakness، suspected **full-thickness cuff tear**، persistent severe symptoms رغم الـ rehab، **progressive weakness**، significant disability، diagnostic uncertainty، suspected cervical/neurological disease، suspected arthritis/structural disease.

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hierarchy: education + activity modification → exercise → analgesia → injection (selected) → reassess → specialist.
- "Modify load, not eliminate movement"; complete rest unnecessary.
- Exercise is the cornerstone: rotator cuff + scapular muscles (serratus anterior, middle/lower trapezius, rhomboids) + mobility.
- NSAIDs short-term; they support, not replace, rehab.
- Steroid injection: short-term adjunct; no difference vs exercise alone at 12 weeks.
- Routine subacromial decompression NOT recommended (BMJ guideline).

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. Outline the management hierarchy of subacromial pain syndrome.</summary>

**Answer:** Education and activity modification → exercise-based rehabilitation → analgesia/NSAIDs when appropriate → corticosteroid injection for selected patients → reassessment of diagnosis and adherence → specialist assessment if symptoms persist or structural pathology is suspected.

**ليش؟** الترتيب هو السؤال.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the role and limitation of subacromial corticosteroid injection?</summary>

**Answer:** It can be considered when pain is substantial and prevents rehabilitation, giving relatively rapid short-term relief; it is an adjunct, not definitive treatment. In a randomized trial, injection plus exercise gave greater short-term improvement but no significant difference from exercise alone at 12 weeks. Repeated injections need caution, especially with a cuff tear or possible surgery.

**ليش؟** الفائدة + الـ 12 weeks + التحذير.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is routine subacromial decompression no longer recommended?</summary>

**Answer:** Current evidence does not support it for uncomplicated SAPS; a BMJ clinical practice guideline strongly recommended against it because trials showed no clinically important improvement in pain, function or quality of life compared with non-operative or placebo approaches.

**ليش؟** هذا "updated concept" صريح بالملزمة.
</details>

## 9. Practical algorithm

1. **Trauma?** → exclude fracture/dislocation/acute cuff tear.
2. **Red flags?** → investigate.
3. **Neck/neurological symptoms?** → examine cervical spine + neuro.
4. **Passive ROM restricted?** → think **adhesive capsulitis** or glenohumeral pathology.
5. **Passive ROM preserved + painful elevation?** → **rotator-cuff-related pain / SAPS**.
6. **Marked weakness?** → investigate for **cuff tear**.
7. **No major structural concern** → education + progressive exercise ± analgesia.
8. **Severe pain prevents rehab** → selected subacromial **corticosteroid injection**.
9. **Persistent symptoms** → reassess diagnosis، adherence، loading؛ imaging/referral.

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Trauma → exclude fracture/dislocation/tear; red flags → investigate; neck symptoms → examine spine.
- Passive ROM restricted → adhesive capsulitis/GH pathology; preserved + painful elevation → SAPS; marked weakness → cuff tear.
- Otherwise: education + exercise ± analgesia; injection if pain blocks rehab; reassess if persistent.

## 🔥 الخلاصة النهائية — احفظها قبل الامتحان

1. **SAPS** preferred over impingement: الألم **multifactorial**، مو compression بس.
2. **Supraspinatus** الأكثر إصابة — يمر تحت الـ **acromion** → **greater tuberosity**.
3. Cuff nerves: **supra/infraspinatus = suprascapular**، **teres minor = axillary**، **subscapularis = subscapular nn.**
4. **Scapulohumeral rhythm 2:1**.
5. Pain: **anterolateral**، **not below elbow**، **night pain**، overhead activities.
6. **Painful arc 60–120°**؛ **Neer** (stabilize scapula + IR + forward elevation)؛ **Hawkins–Kennedy** (90/90 + IR).
7. **Empty-can** (supraspinatus): **pain = tendinopathy، weakness = tear**.
8. **Lift-off & belly-press = subscapularis**؛ **ER test = infraspinatus + teres minor**؛ **cross-body = AC joint**؛ **Speed/Yergason = biceps**.
9. **Passive ROM preserved** = impingement؛ **restricted (ER most)** = **adhesive capsulitis** (diabetes).
10. **Acute traumatic inability to elevate = cuff tear** مو impingement.
11. Imaging: X-ray (calcific، high-riding head)، **US (no radiation، dynamic)**، MRI (tear، atrophy)؛ **treat the patient, not the MRI**.
12. **Exercise = cornerstone**؛ **modify load, not eliminate movement**.
13. **Steroid injection**: short-term adjunct؛ **no difference at 12 weeks**.
14. **No routine subacromial decompression** (BMJ guideline).
