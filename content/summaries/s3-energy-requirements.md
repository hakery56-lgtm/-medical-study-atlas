## 1. Energy needs, balance and fuel stores

- The body needs **continuous energy** for all processes, growth and activity; even at rest for **muscle contraction, active transport and synthesis of macromolecules**.
- **Forms:** **chemical** (metabolic products), **electrical** (brain and nerves), **mechanical** (muscle contraction), **thermal** (body temperature).
- ⭐ **Energy balance = energy in − energy out.** **Energy out = BMR + thermic effect of food + physical activity.** Positive balance → **gain**; neutral → **maintain**; negative → **loss**.
- **Fuels:** **carbohydrate = primary**, **fat = storage**, **protein = back-up**; all converted to **ATP**.

| Fasting stage | Main source | Pathway | Tissues |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 (0–24 h)** | **Glycogen** | Glycogenolysis | Liver, muscle |
| **2 (1–3 days)** | **Fat** | Lipolysis, β-oxidation | Muscle, liver, heart |
| **3 (> 3 days)** | **Protein + ketone bodies** | Proteolysis, ketogenesis | **Brain (ketones), RBCs (glucose)** |

- **Glycogen** (liver and muscle): a **12–48 h reserve**, keeps glucose normal during sleep. **Adipose tissue** is used in prolonged fasting. **Protein** gives **~5%** of daily energy, up to **15%** in strenuous activity with depleted glycogen.
- **Calorie:** 1 **kcal (Calorie)** = 1000 calories.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Energy balance = in − out; out = BMR + TEF + activity.
- Fasting: glycogen → fat → protein + ketones.

</div>

## 2. Basal metabolic rate and its measurement

- ⭐ **BMR:** the **minimum calories** needed to sustain basic life functions (breathing, circulation) over **24 h of rest**.
- **Measurement:** **direct calorimetry** (heat, chamber surrounded by water; accurate but expensive, rarely used), **indirect calorimetry** (**O₂ consumption and CO₂ production**; metabolic cart or spirometer), **predictive equations**.
- **Conditions:** complete **physical and mental rest**, **relaxed but not asleep**, **≥ 12 h after last meal**, hours after exercise, comfortable temperature.
- **Estimation:** men **1 kcal × IBW × 24 h**; women slightly lower (worked examples use **0.95**). **Mifflin-St Jeor:** **(10 × kg) + (6.25 × cm) − (5 × age)**, then **+5 (male)** or **−161 (female)**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- BMR measured at rest, post-absorptive; indirect calorimetry uses O₂ and CO₂.
- Mifflin-St Jeor: +5 male, −161 female.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define BMR and the conditions of its measurement.</summary>

**Answer:** The minimum calories needed to sustain basic life functions during 24 hours of rest; the person must be at complete physical and mental rest, relaxed but not asleep, at least 12 hours after the last meal, several hours after strenuous exercise and in a comfortable environment.

**Why:** Slides "Basal metabolic rate" and "Conditions to measure BMR".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare direct and indirect calorimetry.</summary>

**Answer:** Direct calorimetry measures heat produced by the body in a chamber surrounded by water (accurate but expensive, rarely used); indirect calorimetry calculates energy expenditure from oxygen consumption and carbon dioxide production using a metabolic cart or spirometer.

**Why:** Slide "Methods of measuring BMR".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Write the Mifflin-St Jeor equation.</summary>

**Answer:** BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5 for males, or − 161 for females.

**Why:** Slide "Mifflin-St Jeor equation".
</details>

## 3. Factors affecting BMR

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Surface area** | Higher in taller people |
| **Gender** | Female **5–10% lower** |
| **Age** | Higher in **children < 2 y** and at puberty; falls with age |
| **Thyroid** | Hypothyroid **↓ 30–50%**; hyperthyroid **↑ 50–75%** |
| **GH, sympathetic stimulation** | **↑ BMR** |
| **Sex hormones** | Fluctuate in the cycle (↓ mid-cycle, **↑ 7.7%** after ovulation) |
| **Body composition** | **↑** with more muscle |
| **Pregnancy** | **↑ 20%** in the 3rd trimester |
| **Starvation / malnutrition** | **↓ 50%** (adaptive) |
| **Sleep** | **↓ 10%** |
| **Fever** | **↑ 13%** per 1 °C above 37 °C |
| **Muscle tone (athletes)** | **↑** |
| **Climate** | Cold: **↑ 5%** per 10 °C fall; hot: ↓ (but sweating raises expenditure) |
| **Caffeine** | **↑** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Thyroid: −30–50% / +50–75%; fever +13% per °C; sleep −10%; starvation −50%.
- Female 5–10% lower than male.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do thyroid hormones affect BMR?</summary>

**Answer:** Hypothyroidism decreases BMR by 30-50%; hyperthyroidism increases it by 50-75%.

**Why:** Slide "Factors affecting BMR".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the secondary factors that change BMR.</summary>

**Answer:** Nutritional status (starvation lowers it by 50%), sleep (decreases 10%), fever (increases 13% per °C above 37), muscle tone, climate (cold raises 5% per 10 °C fall) and caffeine (increases).

**Why:** Slides "Secondary factors".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do gender, age and pregnancy affect BMR?</summary>

**Answer:** Female BMR is 5-10% lower than male for the same weight and height; BMR is higher in children under 2 years and at puberty and declines with age; pregnancy raises BMR by 20% in the 3rd trimester.

**Why:** Slides "Factors affecting BMR".
</details>

## 4. Physical activity, thermic effect and macronutrients

- **Fuel factors:** **carbohydrate 4, fat 9, protein 4 kcal/g**. **Ranges:** **CHO 45–65%**, **protein 10–35%**, **fat 20–35%**.
- **Energy expenditure split:** **BMR ~55–60%**, **physical activity ~20–30%**, **TEF ~10–15%**.
- **Physical activity = BMR × activity factor:** **light 20–30%** of BMR (office worker, doctor, teacher), **moderate 40%** (fast walking, level cycling), **heavy 50%** (running, swimming, carrying loads). **PAL:** **very light 1.2, light 1.37, moderate 1.55, heavy 1.72.**
- **Exercise** raises metabolism during and after; **lean muscle** raises BMR long-term; **intensity** matters most; **mental activity** does **not** appreciably change energy needs.
- **Thermic effect of food (SDA)** = energy to **digest, absorb and metabolize** food; **peaks 3–5 h** after eating: **protein 25–30%**, **CHO 6%**, **fat 4%**, **mixed diet 6–10%**.
- **Fat** should be **≤ 25–30%** of calories with only **10% saturated**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 4-9-4 kcal/g; PAL 1.2 / 1.37 / 1.55 / 1.72.
- TEF: protein highest (25–30%), mixed diet 6–10%.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is the energy of physical activity calculated?</summary>

**Answer:** Physical activity = BMR × activity factor; by occupation light activity is 20-30% of BMR, moderate 40% and heavy 50%; PAL factors are 1.2 (very light), 1.37 (light), 1.55 (moderate) and 1.72 (heavy).

**Why:** Slides "Physical activity" and "Physical activity level".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the thermic effect of food and how does it vary?</summary>

**Answer:** The energy the body spends to digest, absorb and metabolize food, maximal 3-5 hours after ingestion; protein 25-30%, carbohydrate 6%, fat 4%, mixed diet 6-10%.

**Why:** Slide "Thermic effect of food".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. State the energy value and recommended range of each macronutrient.</summary>

**Answer:** Carbohydrate 4 kcal/g (45-65%), protein 4 kcal/g (10-35%), fat 9 kcal/g (20-35%).

**Why:** Slide "Fuel factors".
</details>

## 5. Calculating total energy requirement (TER)

- **Formula:** **TER = [BMR − 10% (sleep)] + TEF + physical activity** (+ **300** pregnancy / **500** lactation).
- **Overweight:** use **adjusted weight = ideal + 0.25 × (observed − ideal)**. **IBW (Devine):** men **50 + 0.9 × (height cm − 152.4)**; women **45.5 + 0.9 × (height cm − 152.4)**.

| Worked example | BMR | Sleep (−10%) | Activity | TEF (6%) | TER |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Male student, IBW 60 kg, moderate (40%)** | 60 × 1 × 24 = **1440** | 144 → 1296 | **576** | **86** | **1958** |
| **Housewife 60 kg, moderate (40%)** | 60 × 0.95 × 24 = **1368** | 136 → 1232 | **547** | **82** | **1861** |
| **Woman 85 kg (ideal 68; adjusted 72), light (30%)** | 0.95 × 72 × 24 = **1641** | 164 → 1477 | **492** | **98** | **2067** |

- **Student's macros (TER 1958):** **fat 25% → 489 kcal ÷ 9 = 54 g**; **CHO 50% → 979 kcal ÷ 4 = 245 g**; **protein 20% → 392 kcal ÷ 4 = 98 g**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- TER = (BMR − sleep 10%) + TEF 6% + activity.
- Grams = kcal ÷ 9 for fat, ÷ 4 for CHO and protein.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Calculate the TER of a 60 kg moderately active male (BMR 1 kcal/kg/h).</summary>

**Answer:** BMR 60 × 1 × 24 = 1440; minus 10% for sleep = 1296; activity 40% = 576; TEF 6% = 86; TER = 1296 + 576 + 86 = 1958 kcal/day.

**Why:** Worked example slide.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How many grams of fat, carbohydrate and protein does he need?</summary>

**Answer:** Fat 25% of 1958 = 489 kcal ÷ 9 = 54 g; carbohydrate 50% = 979 kcal ÷ 4 = 245 g; protein 20% = 392 kcal ÷ 4 = 98 g.

**Why:** Slides on fat, CHO and protein calculations.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is the energy requirement of an overweight patient estimated?</summary>

**Answer:** Use an adjusted weight = ideal weight + 25% of the difference between observed and ideal weight (e.g. 68 + 0.25 × 17 = 72 kg).

**Why:** Slide "Energy requirement in overweight".
</details>

## 6. Growth, pregnancy, lactation and age

- **Growth:** extra energy for increasing weight and height; an infant may store **12–15%** of expenditure for growth; later growth need falls but **TER rises with body size**.
- **Pregnancy:** extra energy for the mother's tissues, **fat stores for milk**, the baby and placenta: **+300 kcal/day** (2nd and 3rd trimesters). **Lactation: +500 kcal/day** (first 6 months).
- **Adulthood:** needs **plateau**, then BMR **declines**; **rapid decline at 40 (men) and 50 (women)**.

| Group | Need |
|---|---|
| **Infant (0–1 y)** | **100–120 kcal/kg** |
| **Child (1–10 y)** | **80–100 kcal/kg** |
| **Adult male** | **2500–3000 kcal** |
| **Adult female** | **2000–2200 kcal** |
| **Pregnancy / lactation** | **+300 / +500 kcal/day** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pregnancy +300; lactation +500.
- Infant 100–120 kcal/kg; male 2500–3000; female 2000–2200 kcal.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Why does pregnancy increase energy needs, and by how much?</summary>

**Answer:** To build the mother's tissues, fat stores for breast milk, and the baby's tissues and placenta; about 300 kcal/day, especially in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters.

**Why:** Slides "Pregnancy and lactation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How much extra energy does lactation need?</summary>

**Answer:** 500 kcal/day, during the first 6 months.

**Why:** Slides on energy requirements by physiological state.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do energy needs change in adulthood?</summary>

**Answer:** Needs plateau when full growth is achieved, then BMR declines (rapidly at age 40 in men and 50 in women), reducing energy needs.

**Why:** Slide "Adulthood".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Fasting: glycogen (0–24 h) → fat (1–3 days) → protein + ketones (> 3 days).**
- **Thyroid: hypo −30–50%, hyper +50–75%; fever +13% per °C; sleep −10%.**
- **TER subtracts 10% of BMR for sleep before adding TEF and activity.**
- **Fat = 9 kcal/g; CHO and protein = 4 kcal/g; convert kcal to grams by dividing.**
- **Protein has the highest TEF (25–30%).**
- **Mental activity does not change energy requirement.**
- **Pregnancy +300; lactation +500.**
