## 1. Digestion in the stomach

- When **protein enters the stomach** it stimulates the secretion of the hormone ⭐ **gastrin** from the **gastric mucosal cells**, which stimulates the release of **gastric juice** containing **HCl, the proenzyme pepsinogen and rennin (in infants)**.

### 1) Hydrochloric acid
- Secreted by the **gastric parietal cells**.
- Makes the **pH optimum for pepsin** and **activates pepsin**; **denatures the protein**.

### 2) Rennin (infants)
- **Clots milk**: slight hydrolysis of **casein → paracasein**, which **coagulates in the presence of Ca²⁺** → insoluble **calcium paracaseinate curd**, then acted on by **pepsin**.
- **Purpose:** convert milk to a **more solid form** to **prevent the rapid passage of milk** from the infant's stomach.

### 3) Pepsin
- Secreted as the inactive **pepsinogen**; activated in gastric juice **by pepsin itself (autocatalysis) or by high H⁺ concentration (pH 1.5–2.5)**.
- Hydrolyses **peptide bonds adjacent to amino acids with bulky side chains** — **aromatic and branched** amino acids.
- Cleaves long polypeptides into **smaller peptides and some free amino acids**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Protein in the stomach → gastrin → HCl (parietal cells), pepsinogen, rennin (infants).
- HCl: optimum pH for pepsin, activates pepsinogen, denatures protein.
- Rennin: casein → paracasein → calcium paracaseinate curd (slows gastric emptying of milk).
- Pepsin: from pepsinogen (autoactivation or H⁺); cleaves bonds next to aromatic/branched amino acids.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe protein digestion in the stomach.</summary>

**Answer:** Protein entering the stomach stimulates gastric mucosal cells to secrete gastrin, which stimulates release of gastric juice containing hydrochloric acid, pepsinogen and (in infants) rennin. HCl from the parietal cells provides the optimum pH for pepsin, activates pepsinogen and denatures proteins. Pepsin cleaves peptide bonds adjacent to aromatic and branched amino acids, producing smaller peptides and some free amino acids.

**Why:** Hormone → juice → three agents.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the action and purpose of rennin?</summary>

**Answer:** Rennin hydrolyses casein slightly to paracasein, which coagulates with calcium ions into an insoluble calcium paracaseinate curd that pepsin then digests. This converts milk into a more solid form and prevents its rapid passage out of the infant's stomach.

**Why:** Why rennin is vital in infants.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is pepsinogen activated?</summary>

**Answer:** In the gastric juice, by the action of pepsin itself (autocatalysis) or by the high hydrogen ion concentration (HCl, pH 1.5–2.5).

**Why:** Zymogen activation.
</details>

## 2. Pancreatic digestion of proteins

- **Optimum pH of the pancreatic enzymes = 8** (bicarbonate neutralizes the chyme).
- Pancreatic juice contains the proteases ⭐ **trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase and carboxypeptidases** (the slide lists them as endopeptidases; carboxypeptidases are strictly exopeptidases).
- They are secreted as **inactive zymogens**, converted to the active form **in the intestinal lumen** → **prevents autodigestion of the pancreatic acini**.

### Zymogen activation cascade (lecture figure)
```
Trypsinogen ──(enteropeptidase / enterokinase)──► TRYPSIN
Trypsin ──► activates: chymotrypsinogen → chymotrypsin
                       proelastase → elastase
                       procarboxypeptidase A, B → carboxypeptidase A, B
Pepsinogen ──(pepsin, H⁺ pH 1.5–2.5)──► PEPSIN        (stomach)
```
- **Result:** **polypeptides → oligopeptides (2–10 amino acids) + free amino acids**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pancreatic enzymes work at pH 8: trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase, carboxypeptidases.
- All are secreted as zymogens activated in the lumen (prevents autodigestion of the pancreas).
- Enterokinase (enteropeptidase) activates trypsinogen → trypsin activates the others.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the pancreatic proteolytic enzymes and explain how they are activated.</summary>

**Answer:** Trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase and carboxypeptidases, secreted as inactive zymogens (trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen, proelastase and procarboxypeptidases). Enterokinase (enteropeptidase) of the intestinal brush border converts trypsinogen into trypsin, and trypsin then activates the other zymogens in the intestinal lumen.

**Why:** Trypsin is the master activator.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why are the pancreatic proteases secreted as zymogens?</summary>

**Answer:** To prevent autodigestion of the pancreatic secretory acini; they become active only in the intestinal lumen.

**Why:** Protects the pancreas.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the optimum pH of the pancreatic enzymes and the products of their action?</summary>

**Answer:** The optimum pH is 8. They cleave the polypeptides into oligopeptides (2–10 amino acids) and free amino acids.

**Why:** pH 8 vs pepsin's pH 1.5–2.5.
</details>

## 3. Intestinal digestion & absorption of amino acids

### Intestinal (brush-border) digestion
- The **luminal surface of the intestinal epithelial cells** contains **aminopeptidases and dipeptidases/tripeptidases**.
- They complete the digestion → **di- or tripeptides + free amino acids**; other peptidases inside the cells hydrolyse the remaining di/tripeptides.

### Absorption of amino acids
- Most amino acids are absorbed by **active transport requiring ATP and specific transport proteins** in the mucosal cells (**Na⁺-dependent symporter** at the brush border; the **Na⁺/K⁺ pump (3Na⁺/2K⁺)** removes the Na⁺ → **secondary active transport**).
- **Dipeptides and tripeptides** are absorbed by an **H⁺-dependent symporter** and **hydrolysed to amino acids inside the cell**.
- Amino acids **leave the cell by facilitated diffusion** (basolateral transporter) → **portal vein → liver**.

### The Meister cycle (γ-glutamyl cycle)
- **Glutathione** participates in the absorption of amino acids in the **intestine, kidneys and brain** — the cycle is the **gamma-glutamyl cycle (Meister cycle)**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Brush border: aminopeptidases, dipeptidases and tripeptidases → di/tripeptides + amino acids.
- Amino acids: Na⁺-dependent symporter (secondary active, ATP through the Na⁺/K⁺ pump); di/tripeptides: H⁺-dependent symporter, then hydrolysed inside the cell.
- Exit by facilitated diffusion → portal vein.
- Meister (γ-glutamyl) cycle: glutathione-dependent amino acid absorption in intestine, kidney, brain.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the intestinal digestion and absorption of amino acids.</summary>

**Answer:** Brush-border aminopeptidases and di-/tripeptidases complete the digestion to free amino acids and small peptides. Most amino acids are absorbed by active transport requiring ATP and specific transport proteins (a Na⁺-dependent symporter, with the Na⁺ removed by the Na⁺/K⁺ pump); di- and tripeptides enter through an H⁺-dependent symporter and are hydrolysed inside the cell. Amino acids leave by facilitated diffusion into the portal vein.

**Why:** Luminal → cell → blood.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the Meister cycle?</summary>

**Answer:** The gamma-glutamyl cycle, in which glutathione participates in the absorption of amino acids in the intestine, kidneys and brain.

**Why:** Named in the objectives.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why does amino acid absorption need ATP?</summary>

**Answer:** Because the symporter uses the Na⁺ gradient (secondary active transport), and the Na⁺/K⁺ pump uses ATP to maintain that gradient by pumping Na⁺ out of the cell.

**Why:** Indirect energy use, like glucose.
</details>

## 4. Summary of protein digestion & comparison with carbohydrates

### Summary table
| Location | Agent | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Stomach** | **Acid (HCl)** | **denaturation** |
| **Stomach** | **Pepsin** | **large peptides + some free amino acids** |
| **Small intestine lumen** (pancreatic enzymes) | **Trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase, carboxypeptidases** | **oligopeptides (2–10 amino acids) + free amino acids** |
| **Brush-border surface** | **Endopeptidases, aminopeptidases (dipeptidases)** | **di- or tri-peptides + free amino acids** |

### Protein vs carbohydrate digestion
| | **Carbohydrate** | **Protein** |
|---|---|---|
| **Enzymes** | salivary amylase; pancreatic amylase; brush-border enzymes (dextrinase, glucoamylase, lactase, maltase, sucrase) | pepsin (stomach, with HCl); pancreatic trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase; brush-border aminopeptidase, carboxypeptidase, dipeptidases |
| **Sites** | mouth; small intestine; small intestine | stomach; small intestine; small intestine |
| **Absorption** | **glucose and galactose** by **Na⁺ cotransport**; **fructose** by **facilitated diffusion**; all leave the cell by facilitated diffusion | **amino acids** by **Na⁺ cotransport**; some **di/tripeptides** by **H⁺ cotransport** and hydrolysed in the cell; leave by facilitated diffusion |
| **Route** | capillaries of the villi → **hepatic portal vein → liver** | capillaries of the villi → **hepatic portal vein → liver** |

### Practice MCQs from the lecture
1. **Trypsinogen is converted to active trypsin by:** **enterokinase** (enteropeptidase).
2. **Inactive zymogens are precursors of all the following GI enzymes EXCEPT:** **aminopeptidase** (it is an active brush-border enzyme, not a zymogen).

### Location exercise (answers)
- **Trypsin is active:** **small intestine lumen**; **large polypeptides produced:** **stomach** (pepsin); **protein denatured by HCl:** **stomach**; **active transport of amino acids into the bloodstream:** **small intestine (enterocytes)**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Stomach: HCl denatures, pepsin → large peptides; lumen of small intestine: pancreatic proteases → oligopeptides; brush border: peptidases → di/tripeptides + amino acids.
- Both carbohydrates and proteins are absorbed with Na⁺ cotransport and reach the liver via the hepatic portal vein.
- Enterokinase activates trypsinogen; aminopeptidase is not a zymogen.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Summarize protein digestion by location, agent and outcome.</summary>

**Answer:** Stomach: HCl denatures protein and pepsin produces large peptides and some free amino acids. Small intestine lumen: pancreatic trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase and carboxypeptidases give oligopeptides (2–10 amino acids) and free amino acids. Brush border: endopeptidases and aminopeptidases give di- or tripeptides and free amino acids, which are absorbed.

**Why:** The summary table of the lecture.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare the absorption of carbohydrates and proteins.</summary>

**Answer:** Glucose and galactose, like amino acids, are absorbed by cotransport with sodium; fructose uses facilitated diffusion; some di- and tripeptides are absorbed by H⁺ cotransport and hydrolysed in the cell. All leave the epithelial cells by facilitated diffusion, enter the villous capillaries and are carried to the liver by the hepatic portal vein.

**Why:** Same route, similar mechanisms.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which enzyme activates trypsinogen and which listed enzyme is NOT derived from a zymogen?</summary>

**Answer:** Enterokinase (enteropeptidase) activates trypsinogen; aminopeptidase is not derived from a zymogen (trypsin, carboxypeptidase, pepsin and chymotrypsin all are).

**Why:** The two MCQs of the lecture.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Gastrin** is released by **gastric mucosal cells** when protein enters the stomach; **HCl** comes from **parietal cells**.
- **Rennin** works in **infants** (casein → paracasein → curd with Ca²⁺).
- **Pepsin**: bulky **aromatic and branched** amino acids; **pH 1.5–2.5**. **Pancreatic enzymes**: **pH 8**.
- **Trypsinogen → trypsin by enterokinase**; trypsin activates the other zymogens.
- **Aminopeptidase** is **not** a zymogen.
- **Oligopeptides = 2–10 amino acids.**
- Amino acids: **Na⁺-dependent symporter**; di/tripeptides: **H⁺-dependent symporter**.
- **Meister cycle = γ-glutamyl cycle** (glutathione).

## 🔥 Final review — memorize before the exam

1. Protein in stomach → **gastrin** → **HCl (parietal cells), pepsinogen, rennin (infants)**.
2. **HCl:** pH for pepsin, activates pepsinogen, denatures. **Pepsin:** aromatic and branched amino acids. **Rennin:** casein → paracasein → Ca-paracaseinate curd.

6. Summary: HCl denatures → pepsin (large peptides) → pancreatic proteases (oligopeptides) → brush border (di/tripeptides + amino acids).
