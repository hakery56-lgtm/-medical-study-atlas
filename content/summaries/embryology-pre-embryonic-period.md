## 1. Development and week 1: fertilisation to morula

- ⭐ **Development** = **growth + morphogenesis (form and structure) + differentiation (specialisation for function).**
- **Periods:** **pre-embryonic (weeks 1–3 after fertilisation), embryonic (to week 8), fetal (to week 38).** Clinical pregnancy weeks run from the **LMP** (conception weeks + 2; term = 40 weeks).
- **Week 1 sequence:** **fertilisation → cleavage → compaction → morula → blastocyst → hatching → implantation.**
- **Day 1 – fertilisation:** oocyte released, travels along the tube, **fertilised in the ampulla** → **zygote**; oocyte viable **1 day**, sperm **up to 3 days**; ideal implantation site = **posterior uterine wall.**
- **Day 2 – cleavage:** from the **2-cell stage**, mitotic divisions produce smaller **blastomeres** (loose clump up to the 8-cell stage).
- **Day 3 – compaction:** after the 8-cell stage; inner cells communicate by **gap junctions**; **cell outlines become indistinct.**
- **Day 4 – morula:** **16-cell mulberry**; **inner cell mass** → embryo proper; **outer cell mass** → **trophoblast** (later placenta).
- Slide note: adult tissue stem cells are **multipotent** (restricted in the cell types they form).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pre-embryonic weeks 1–3; embryonic to week 8; fetal to week 38; pregnancy weeks from LMP.
- Fertilisation in the ampulla; cleavage from 2-cell stage; compaction after 8-cell stage; morula = 16 cells.
- Inner cell mass = embryo; outer cell mass = trophoblast.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the events of week 1 in order.</summary>

**Answer:** Fertilisation, cleavage, compaction, morula, blastocyst, hatching and implantation.

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe cleavage and compaction.</summary>

**Answer:** From the two-cell stage the zygote divides mitotically into smaller blastomeres (loose clump to the eight-cell stage); at about day 3 compaction maximises cell-cell contacts, inner cells communicate by gap junctions and outlines become indistinct.

**Why:** Slides 6–7.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What does the morula give rise to?</summary>

**Answer:** A 16-cell mulberry (day 4): the inner cell mass forms the tissues of the embryo proper, the outer cell mass forms the trophoblast which contributes to the placenta.

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>

## 2. Blastocyst, hatching and implantation (week 1)

- ⭐ **Blastocyst formation:** as the morula enters the uterus, **fluid enters through the zona pellucida** → single cavity = **blastocele**; **inner cell mass = embryoblast** at one pole; **trophoblast** flattens to form the wall; the **zona pellucida disappears** so implantation can begin (**days 4–5**).
- **Day 5 – hatching:** the blastocyst **hatches from the zona**, enlarges and can **interact with the uterine surface.**
- **Day 6 – implantation begins:** conceptus has **107 cells**: **8 → embryo**, **99 → fetal membranes.**
- **Day 7:** **L-selectin on trophoblast** and carbohydrate receptors on the uterine epithelium mediate **initial attachment**; implantation = **mutual trophoblastic and endometrial action.**
- **"Two things happening at once":** making an embryo and making a placenta; **the placenta has priority** at the earliest stages.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Blastocyst: blastocele + embryoblast + trophoblast; zona pellucida lost.
- Day 5 hatching; day 6 implantation begins (107 cells: 8 embryo, 99 membranes); day 7 L-selectin attachment.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the formation of the blastocyst.</summary>

**Answer:** Fluid passes through the zona pellucida into the inner cell mass, forming the blastocele; the inner cell mass (embryoblast) is at one pole and the trophoblast forms the epithelial wall; the zona disappears so implantation can begin.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What happens at hatching and on day 6?</summary>

**Answer:** The blastocyst hatches from the zona pellucida and can interact with the uterine surface; on day 6 the conceptus has 107 cells, 8 for the embryo and 99 for the fetal membranes.

**Why:** Slides 11–12.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What mediates the initial attachment to the uterus?</summary>

**Answer:** L-selectin on trophoblast cells binding carbohydrate receptors on the uterine epithelium (day 7), as a result of mutual trophoblastic and endometrial action.

**Why:** Slide 13.
</details>

## 3. Week 2: the bilaminar germ disc

- ⭐ **Week 2 = "week of twos":** trophoblast → **cytotrophoblast + syncytiotrophoblast**; embryoblast → **hypoblast + epiblast** (**bilaminar disc**); **two cavities:** **amniotic cavity** (in the epiblast, day 8) and **yolk sac.**

| Day | Event |
|---|---|
| **8** | **Bilaminar disc**; small cavity in the epiblast → **amniotic cavity** |
| **9** | **Lacunar stage:** rapid syncytiotrophoblast growth at the **embryonic pole**; **primitive yolk sac** at the **abembryonic pole** |
| **11** | Yolk sac membrane pushed away by **extraembryonic reticulum** → later **extraembryonic mesoderm**; surface epithelium almost covers the uterine defect |
| **12** | **Maternal sinusoids invaded**; lacunae continuous with sinusoids → **uteroplacental circulation begins** |
| **13** | **Secondary (definitive) yolk sac**; **exocoelomic cysts**; **primary villi** |
| **14** | **Chorionic cavity** forms; embryo suspended by the **connecting stalk** (future umbilical cord); bleeding may mimic **menstruation** |

- **hCG:** made by the **syncytiotrophoblast**; detectable by radioimmunoassay by the end of week 2 = basis of the **pregnancy test.**
- **End of week 2:** conceptus implanted; embryo with **amniotic cavity and yolk sac**, **suspended by the connecting stalk** in the **chorionic cavity.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Week of twos: 2 trophoblast layers, 2 disc layers, 2 cavities.
- Day 9 lacunar; day 12 uteroplacental circulation; day 13 secondary yolk sac and primary villi; day 14 chorionic cavity.
- hCG from syncytiotrophoblast.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Why is week 2 called the week of twos?</summary>

**Answer:** The trophoblast forms two layers (cytotrophoblast, syncytiotrophoblast), the embryoblast forms the bilaminar disc (epiblast, hypoblast), and two cavities appear (amniotic cavity and yolk sac).

**Why:** Slides 15 and 21.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Summarise days 9 to 14.</summary>

**Answer:** Day 9 lacunar stage and primitive yolk sac; day 11 extraembryonic mesoderm forms; day 12 maternal sinusoids invaded and uteroplacental circulation begins; day 13 secondary yolk sac, exocoelomic cysts, primary villi; day 14 chorionic cavity and connecting stalk.

**Why:** Slides 16–20.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How does the pregnancy test work?</summary>

**Answer:** The syncytiotrophoblast produces hCG, which by the end of week 2 is sufficient to be detected by radioimmunoassay.

**Why:** Slide 24.
</details>

## 4. Implantation defects, cavities and early pregnancy loss

- ⭐ **Implantation defects = inappropriate site.** **Ectopic pregnancy:** outside the uterine body (most commonly the **Fallopian tube**; also peritoneal or ovarian) → can quickly become a **life-threatening emergency.** **Placenta praevia:** **lower uterine segment** → **haemorrhage in pregnancy**, requires **C-section.**

### Cavities, coeloms, sacs and spaces
| Structure | Origin |
|---|---|
| **Blastocoele** | First cavity, formed as a result of **compaction** |
| **Amniotic sac** | From spaces within the **epiblast** |
| **Primitive yolk sac** (= exocoelomic cavity) | Formed by **hypoblast** lining the blastocoele |
| **Secondary yolk sac** (= definitive) | Formed **within** the primitive yolk sac |
| **Extraembryonic coelom** (= chorionic cavity) | From spaces within the extraembryonic reticulum and mesoderm |

- **Early pregnancy loss:** **~50% of zygotes** lost in the first 2–3 weeks; **15%** of diagnosed pregnancies miscarry; **1%** of women have **recurrent miscarriage** (3 consecutive).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Ectopic = outside uterine body (tube); placenta praevia = lower segment, C-section.
- Primitive yolk sac = exocoelomic cavity; chorionic cavity = extraembryonic coelom.
- 50% of zygotes lost early; 15% miscarry; 1% recurrent.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare ectopic pregnancy and placenta praevia.</summary>

**Answer:** Ectopic: implantation outside the uterine body, most often the Fallopian tube, a possible life-threatening emergency. Placenta praevia: implantation in the lower uterine segment, may cause haemorrhage and requires C-section.

**Why:** Slide 23.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Name the cavities of the early conceptus and their origins.</summary>

**Answer:** Blastocoele (compaction); amniotic sac (epiblast); primitive yolk sac or exocoelomic cavity (hypoblast lining the blastocoele); secondary yolk sac (within the primitive yolk sac); extraembryonic coelom or chorionic cavity (extraembryonic reticulum and mesoderm).

**Why:** Slide 25.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How common is early pregnancy loss?</summary>

**Answer:** About 50% of zygotes are lost in the first 2–3 weeks, 15% of diagnosed pregnancies miscarry and 1% of women have recurrent miscarriage (three consecutive).

**Why:** Slide 26.
</details>
