## 1. Lab safety

### Areas covered by the safety diagram
- **Chemical hygiene, fire/life safety, radiation, lab waste, blood-borne agents, lab animals, hazardous compounds (MSDS).**

### Risks associated with lab items
- **Pipettes, pH/pH meter, gel electrophoresis, UV light/gel-doc system, centrifuge, microscopy, clinical analyzer, sharps (needles, blades), electrical equipment, autoclave** (and computers/laptops — posed as a question).

### Specific guidelines (1)
- Place **extra clothing and books** in an appropriate place.
- **Eating, drinking and smoking are forbidden.**
- **Return reagents, cultures and glassware** to their places.
- **Wear a laboratory coat.**
- **Do not place anything in your mouth.**
- **Clean your work area.**

### Specific guidelines (2)
- **Spilled infectious material → cover immediately with disinfectant and notify the instructor at once.**
- **Flame wire loops and needles BEFORE and AFTER transfer of cultures.**
- **Wash hands thoroughly before and after each experiment.**
- **Blood/body-fluid specimens go in a well-constructed container.**
- Use **biological safety cabinets.**
- **Mouth pipetting must NOT be done.**
- Everyone **washes hands after completing laboratory activities.**

### Do's and don'ts
- No eating/drinking, no smoking, clean the work area, **proper disposal of biohazard waste**, hand washing.

### Aseptic technique
- Must be observed **at all times** when working with infectious materials (blood, pathogens, microorganisms); use **safety cabinets and fume hoods.**

**Key points**
- Safety areas: chemical hygiene, fire, radiation, waste, blood-borne agents, animals, MSDS.
- No eating, drinking, smoking or mouth pipetting; wear a lab coat; wash hands before and after.
- Spill → cover with disinfectant + inform instructor. Flame loops before and after transfer.
- Aseptic technique at all times; biological safety cabinets/fume hoods.

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List five general rules of laboratory safety.</summary>

**Answer:** Wear a lab coat; no eating, drinking or smoking; no mouth pipetting or putting anything in the mouth; keep extra clothes and books in an appropriate place; clean the work area; wash hands before and after experiments.

**Why:** Specific guidelines (1) and (2).
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What should you do if infectious material is spilled?</summary>

**Answer:** Cover it immediately with a disinfectant and notify the instructor at once.

**Why:** Prevents spread of infection.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why must wire loops and needles be flamed and what is aseptic technique?</summary>

**Answer:** They are flamed before and after transferring cultures to sterilize them and prevent contamination; aseptic technique means working at all times with infectious materials (blood, pathogens, microorganisms) in a way that avoids contamination, using safety cabinets and fume hoods.

**Why:** Protects the culture and the worker.
</details>
