## 1. Bones, joints and surface anatomy

- ⭐ **27 bones:** **8 carpals + 5 metacarpals + 14 phalanges** (excluding thumb sesamoids). **Fingers: 3 phalanges; thumb: 2 and one IP joint.** Metacarpal: **base, shaft, head.**
- ⭐ **Carpals:** **proximal row (lateral → medial): scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform; distal row: trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate.** Mnemonic: **"Some Lovers Try Positions That They Can't Handle."**
- **Clinical points:** **scaphoid = most commonly fractured (fall on outstretched hand; snuffbox tenderness; AVN of the proximal pole);** **lunate dislocation → median nerve compression;** **pisiform = sesamoid in FCU tendon;** **hook of hamate near the ulnar nerve (Guyon canal);** **trapezium = saddle joint with the 1st metacarpal.**
- **Anatomical snuffbox:** **radial border APL + EPB; ulnar border EPL; floor scaphoid and trapezium; contents radial artery and superficial radial nerve branches;** tenderness = scaphoid fracture.
- **Wrist joint:** **synovial ellipsoid;** proximal **radius + articular disc;** distal **scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum;** the **ulna does not articulate with the carpus;** movement at **radiocarpal + midcarpal** joints; **distal radio-ulnar joint = pronation/supination.**

| Wrist movement | Muscles | Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Flexion** | **FCR, FCU, palmaris longus** | **~80°** |
| **Extension** | **ECRL, ECRB, ECU** | **~70°** |
| **Radial deviation** | **FCR, ECRL, ECRB** | **~20°** |
| **Ulnar deviation** | **FCU, ECU** | **~30°** |

| Joint | Type |
|---|---|
| **Intercarpal** | **Plane (gliding)** |
| **Thumb CMC** | **Saddle (opposition)** |
| **MCP** | **Condyloid** |
| **PIP, DIP** | **Hinge** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 27 bones; scaphoid most fractured; snuffbox tenderness.
- Wrist: ellipsoid; flexion 80°, extension 70°, radial 20°, ulnar 30°.

</div>

**Answer:** Proximal row: scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform; distal row: trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate. The scaphoid is the most commonly fractured carpal bone (fall on outstretched hand), tender in the snuffbox, with a risk of avascular necrosis of the proximal pole.

**Why:** Slide "Surface anatomy: dorsal aspect".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the wrist joint.</summary>

**Answer:** A synovial ellipsoid joint between the distal radius with its articular disc and the scaphoid, lunate and triquetrum; the ulna does not articulate with the carpus; movement occurs at the radiocarpal and midcarpal joints.

**Why:** Slide "Wrist joint".
</details>

## 2. Muscles, nerves and vessels

- **Extrinsic flexors:** **FDS → PIP (median); FDP → DIP (lateral half AIN, medial half ulnar); FPL → thumb IP (AIN).** **Extrinsic extensors (radial nerve, posterior interosseous branch):** extensor digitorum (MCP), extensor indicis, extensor digiti minimi, **EPL (thumb IP), EPB (thumb MCP), APL (thumb abduction at CMC).**
- **Thenar:** **abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis (superficial head median, deep head ulnar), opponens pollicis (median); adductor pollicis (ulnar deep branch).** **Hypothenar (ulnar deep branch):** abductor, flexor, opponens digiti minimi; **palmaris brevis** (superficial branch).
- ⭐ **Lumbricals:** **from FDP tendons to the extensor expansion; flex MCP, extend IP; lateral two median, medial two ulnar** (medial paralysis → claw hand).
- ⭐ **Interossei (all ulnar deep branch):** **4 dorsal ABduct; 3 palmar ADduct (DAB / PAD).**
- ⭐ **Sensory:** **median: palmar lateral 3½ digits + distal dorsal tips; ulnar: little finger and medial half of ring finger, medial palm and dorsum; radial: dorsolateral hand (first dorsal web space).**
- **Median:** **LOAF muscles;** **palmar cutaneous branch arises before the tunnel → thenar eminence spared in CTS;** late: thenar wasting, weak thumb abduction.
- ⭐ **Ulnar:** **interossei, hypothenar, medial two lumbricals, adductor pollicis, deep head of FPB, FCU, medial half FDP;** **claw hand (MCP hyperextension, IP flexion of ring and little), weak finger abduction/adduction, Froment sign (FPL substitutes for adductor pollicis), hypothenar wasting, guttering of interossei;** **through Guyon canal;** **ulnar paradox: distal lesion → more claw.**
- **Radial:** **dorsolateral sensation; wrist and finger extensors; not the intrinsic muscles; injury → wrist drop.**
- ⭐ **Vessels:** **radial artery: snuffbox floor, mainly deep palmar arch, pulse lateral to FCR;** **ulnar artery: through Guyon canal, mainly superficial arch, pulse medial to FCU.** **Allen test:** compress both arteries, clench then open the hand (pale), release one and watch colour return; **slow return = the released artery is inadequate.**
- ⭐ **Carpal tunnel:** **roof = flexor retinaculum; floor and sides = carpal bones; 10 structures = median nerve + 4 FDS + 4 FDP + 1 FPL.** **FCR is in its own compartment; ulnar nerve and artery pass through Guyon canal.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Interossei DAB/PAD; lumbricals lateral two median, medial two ulnar.
- Carpal tunnel: 10 structures (median nerve + 9 tendons).
- Ulnar: claw, Froment, Guyon canal.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the contents of the carpal tunnel.</summary>

**Answer:** Ten structures: the median nerve, 4 FDS tendons, 4 FDP tendons and 1 FPL tendon; the roof is the flexor retinaculum; FCR and the ulnar nerve and artery are not inside.

**Why:** Slide "Tendon sheaths and carpal tunnel".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the features of an ulnar nerve lesion.</summary>

**Answer:** Claw hand (MCP hyperextension, IP flexion of ring and little fingers), weak finger abduction and adduction, Froment sign, hypothenar wasting and interosseous guttering; the nerve passes through Guyon canal; a distal lesion causes more claw than a high one (ulnar paradox).

**Why:** Slide "Nerve supply: ulnar nerve".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is the Allen test performed?</summary>

**Answer:** Compress both radial and ulnar arteries, have the patient clench the fist then open the hand (pale); release one artery and watch the colour return; slow return suggests the released artery is not supplying the hand adequately.

**Why:** Slide "Blood supply".
</details>

## 3. History: SOCRATES and clues

- ⭐ **SOCRATES:** **Site, Onset, Character, Radiation, Associated symptoms, Timing, Exacerbating/relieving, Severity.** **Introduction:** wash hands, introduce, confirm name and age, explain, consent, **hand dominance and occupation.**
- **Character:** **burning or tingling = neuropathic.** **Radiation proximally → think cervical spine or nerve root.**
- **Additional history:** **PMH (arthritis, diabetes, injuries, thyroid, psoriasis, gout), drugs, allergies, family history, occupation (repetitive movements, vibrating tools), smoking, alcohol, function (buttons, writing, jar).**

| Feature | Think of |
|---|---|
| **Thumb-index-middle numbness, nocturnal, relieved by shaking** | **Carpal tunnel syndrome** |
| **Finger catches/locks in flexion, morning, painful snap** | **Trigger finger** |
| **Radial wrist pain on gripping/thumb movement, new mothers** | **De Quervain tenosynovitis** |
| **Morning stiffness > 30-60 min; symmetrical small joint swelling** | **Rheumatoid (inflammatory) arthritis** |
| **Worse with use, DIP and thumb base, short stiffness** | **Osteoarthritis** |
| **Painless palm thickening, finger cannot straighten** | **Dupuytren contracture** |
| **Sudden hot red swollen joint ± fever** | **Gout or septic arthritis (urgent)** |
| **Fall on outstretched hand, snuffbox pain** | **Scaphoid fracture** |

- **Intracapsular:** **fracture, DRUJ subluxation, ligament tear, arthritis, neoplasm, ganglia, AVN, osteochondromatosis, carpal coalition.** **Extracapsular:** **neuropathy (median/ulnar), tendinopathy (De Quervain, RSI, multisystem disorders, infections).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- SOCRATES; neuropathic = burning/tingling.
- Learn the history-to-diagnosis table.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the components of SOCRATES.</summary>

**Answer:** Site, onset, character, radiation, associated symptoms, timing, exacerbating/relieving factors and severity.

**Why:** Slides "Task and introduction", "SOCRATES".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which histories suggest carpal tunnel syndrome, trigger finger and De Quervain?</summary>

**Answer:** CTS: nocturnal numbness of thumb, index and middle fingers relieved by shaking; trigger finger: finger locks in flexion with a painful snap, worse in the morning; De Quervain: radial wrist pain on gripping or thumb movement, often in new mothers.

**Why:** Slide "Clues from the history".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Classify causes of hand pain as intracapsular or extracapsular.</summary>

**Answer:** Intracapsular: fracture, DRUJ subluxation, ligament tear, arthritis, neoplasm, ganglia, AVN; extracapsular: median or ulnar neuropathy and tendinopathy (De Quervain, repetitive strain injury, multisystem disorders, infections).

**Why:** Slide "Intracapsular vs extracapsular causes".
</details>

## 4. Examination: preparation and LOOK

- **Preparation:** **wash hands, introduce, identity, consent; ask about pain and dominance; seated, both arms exposed to the elbows on a pillow; compare both hands; examine dorsum and palm.** **Order: LOOK → FEEL → MOVE → special tests.** **Finish:** **sensation (median, ulnar, radial), radial and ulnar pulses, capillary refill, elbow/shoulder/cervical spine, thank patient, suggest X-rays.**
- ⭐ **Look:** **first posture and movement, then distal → proximal: nails, skin, subcutaneous tissue, muscles, joints.**
- **Posture:** **claw hand = ulnar palsy; wrist drop = radial palsy; tremor: rest (parkinsonian), postural, flapping (liver, CO₂), fine (thyrotoxicosis).**

| Nail finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| **Nail-fold infarcts** | **Vasculitis (RA, SLE)** |
| **Capillary changes** | **Systemic sclerosis, dermatomyositis** |
| **Clubbing** | **Lung (cancer, bronchiectasis, abscess, ILD), heart (endocarditis, cyanotic), gut (coeliac, IBD, cirrhosis), thyroid acropachy** |
| **Splinter haemorrhage** | **Infective endocarditis, SLE vasculitis, trauma** |
| **Pitting** | **Psoriasis, lichen planus, alopecia areata** |
| **Leukonychia** | **Cirrhosis, hypoalbuminaemia, idiopathic** |
| **Half-and-half** | **Chronic kidney disease** |
| **Onycholysis** | **Psoriasis, lichen planus, trauma, thyroid, fungal** |

- **Skin:** **traumatic scars; fasciotomy and carpal tunnel decompression scars (palm up);** **palmar erythema (liver disease, RA, thyrotoxicosis, polycythaemia, pregnancy); tophi (gout); gangrene (Raynaud, vasculitis).**
- **Subcutaneous:** **rheumatoid nodules (extensor forearm/elbow); gouty tophi (fingers, ears, elbows); Heberden (DIP) and Bouchard (PIP) nodes; ganglion (dorsal wrist).**
- ⭐ **Muscle wasting:** **thenar = median (carpal tunnel); hypothenar = ulnar; interosseous grooves = ulnar, C8-T1, RA.**
- ⭐ **Dupuytren:** **palmar fascia: nodule → cord; ring then little finger; fixed MCP and PIP flexion; painless, often bilateral; table-top test; associations: familial, alcohol/liver, diabetes, smoking, epilepsy, manual work; grades I-III.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Inspect first: posture and nails often give the diagnosis.
- Thenar wasting = median; hypothenar = ulnar.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the roadmap of inspection.</summary>

**Answer:** First look for abnormal posture (claw hand, wrist drop) and movement (tremor), then inspect distal to proximal: nails, skin, subcutaneous tissue, muscles and joints.

**Why:** Slides "LOOK: inspection roadmap" and "First look".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What do the nail changes suggest?</summary>

**Answer:** Nail-fold infarcts: vasculitis; clubbing: lung, heart or gut disease; splinter haemorrhages: endocarditis, vasculitis or trauma; pitting: psoriasis; leukonychia: cirrhosis or hypoalbuminaemia; half-and-half: chronic kidney disease; onycholysis: psoriasis, trauma, thyroid or fungal disease.

**Why:** Slides "Nails".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe Dupuytren contracture.</summary>

**Answer:** Palmar fascia thickening with nodule then cord, usually ring then little finger, fixed MCP and PIP flexion, painless and often bilateral; associated with family history, alcohol or liver disease, diabetes, smoking, epilepsy and manual work; table-top test; grades I-III.

**Why:** Slide "Dupuytren contracture".
</details>

## 5. LOOK: joints, FEEL and MOVE

- ⭐ **Rheumatoid deformities:** **swan neck (PIP extension, DIP flexion); boutonnière (PIP flexion, DIP extension); Z thumb (MCP flexion, IP extension); ulnar deviation at MCP;** **mallet finger = flexed DIP after trauma.** **SSc:** fixed flexion, tapered fingers, tight skin, digital ulcers. **SLE:** Jaccoud arthropathy, reducible swan neck. **Acromegaly:** large hands. **Marfan:** spider fingers.
- **Joint swelling:** **name, count, symmetry; fist end-on for loss of hill-valley-hill contour.**

| | **Distribution** | **Other** |
|---|---|---|
| **Osteoarthritis** | **DIP (Heberden), PIP (Bouchard), thumb CMC; spares MCP and wrist** | **Bony hard, squaring of thumb base** |
| **Rheumatoid** | **MCP, PIP, wrist; symmetrical; spares DIP** | **Soft synovial, swan neck, boutonnière, ulnar deviation, nodules** |
| **Psoriatic** | **DIP, asymmetric; dactylitis** | **Nail pitting, onycholysis, plaques** |
| **Gout** | **Any joint, acute** | **Tophi, hot red joint** |

- **Rotational deformity:** **fingers normally point to the scaphoid tubercle; deviation suggests previous metacarpal/phalangeal fracture.**
- ⭐ **FEEL:** **warmth (back of hand), tenderness (watch face); hard = bone (osteophytes), soft = synovitis/ganglion; hot red tender = gout, infection, inflammation.** **IP joints:** above and below each joint for sponginess. **MCP:** sponginess and gentle squeeze (**pain = synovitis, early RA**). **Flexor sheaths:** trigger finger (**tender nodule at the A1 pulley, locks in flexion, snaps open**). **De Quervain:** swelling, tenderness, crepitus over APL/EPB sheaths (first dorsal compartment, radial styloid). **Wrist crepitus → osteoarthritis (often post scaphoid/radius fracture).**
- ⭐ **MOVE:** **fist then full extension (lag, pain, locking).** **FDP:** hold PIP extended, flex DIP. **FDS:** hold other fingers extended, flex PIP. **Extensor digitorum:** extend with neutral wrist. **FPL:** hold proximal phalanx, flex IP. **EPL:** hitch-hiker (palm flat). **Resisted thumb extension/abduction → De Quervain pain.** **Grip:** squeeze two fingers; **pinch and key grip:** median, ulnar, thumb muscles; **function:** buttons, writing, coin, jar, key.
- **Wrist:** **prayer sign (extension), reverse prayer (flexion).** **Passive:** reduced active, normal passive = weakness/tendon; both reduced = stiffness/contracture/block; painful end-range = synovitis/arthritis.
- ⭐ **Motor nerve screen:**

| Nerve | Test | Abnormal |
|---|---|---|
| **Radial** | **Paper (extend wrist and fingers)** | **Wrist drop** |
| **Ulnar** | **Scissors (spread/close fingers)** | **Claw, weak abduction/adduction, Froment** |
| **Median** | **Stone (fist, thumb abduction to ceiling)** | **Hand of benediction, thenar wasting, weak abduction** |
| **AIN** | **OK sign (tip-to-tip pinch)** | **Cannot form circle; pulp-to-pulp pinch** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- RA spares DIP; OA spares MCP; psoriatic involves DIP with dactylitis.
- Paper (radial), scissors (ulnar), stone (median), OK (AIN).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare OA, RA, psoriatic arthritis and gout.</summary>

**Answer:** OA: DIP, PIP and thumb base with bony swellings, sparing MCP and wrist; RA: symmetrical MCP, PIP and wrist with soft swelling and deformities, sparing DIP; psoriatic: asymmetric DIP with dactylitis and nail changes; gout: acute, any joint, tophi, hot red joint.

**Why:** Slide "Joints: pattern recognition".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How do you test FDP, FDS and EPL?</summary>

**Answer:** FDP: PIP held in extension, flex the DIP; FDS: other fingers held extended, flex the PIP; EPL: palm flat on a table, lift the thumb like a hitch-hiker.

**Why:** Slides "Move".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the motor nerve screen.</summary>

**Answer:** Paper tests radial (wrist drop), scissors tests ulnar (claw, weak abduction/adduction, Froment), stone tests median (benediction hand, thenar wasting, weak abduction) and the OK sign tests the anterior interosseous nerve (cannot form the circle).

**Why:** Slide "Motor nerve screen".
</details>

## 6. Special tests

| Test | Detects | Method | Positive |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Finkelstein** | **De Quervain (APL, EPB)** | **Thumb inside the fist, then ulnar deviation of the wrist** | **Pain over the radial styloid** |
| **Trigger finger** | **A1 pulley stenosing tenosynovitis** | **Active flexion/extension, palpate the A1 pulley** | **Triggering or locking, tender nodule** |
| **Phalen** | **CTS** | **Wrists fully flexed, backs of hands together, 60 s** | **Tingling/numbness in median distribution** |
| **Reverse Phalen** | **CTS** | **Wrists fully extended, palms together (prayer), 60 s** | **Same symptoms** |
| **Tinel** | **Median nerve irritability** | **Tap at the wrist crease (between palmaris longus and FCR)** | **Tingling in median distribution** |
| **Table-top** | **Dupuytren** | **Palm flat on a table; palpate nodules and cords** | **Palm cannot lie flat** |

- **CTS features:** **nocturnal tingling in thumb, index, middle and half of the ring finger; late thenar wasting and weak abduction; Phalen, reverse Phalen and Tinel positive; confirm with nerve conduction studies.**
- **Key points:** **inspect first; always name, count and compare joints; test median, ulnar and radial nerves with paper, scissors, stone and OK; complete with sensation, pulses and proximal joints.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Finkelstein = De Quervain; Phalen/reverse Phalen/Tinel = CTS; table-top = Dupuytren.
- CTS confirmed by nerve conduction studies.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is Finkelstein's test performed and what does it detect?</summary>

**Answer:** The patient makes a fist over the thumb and the examiner ulnarly deviates the wrist; pain over the radial styloid indicates De Quervain tenosynovitis (APL and EPB).

**Why:** Slide "Finkelstein test".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare Phalen's and reverse Phalen's tests.</summary>

**Answer:** Phalen: backs of hands together with fully flexed wrists for 60 seconds; reverse Phalen: palms together (prayer) with wrists fully extended for 60 seconds; both are positive with numbness or tingling in the median distribution.

**Why:** Slides "Phalen test" and "Reverse Phalen test".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the trigger finger test and the Dupuytren table-top test.</summary>

**Answer:** Trigger finger: active flexion and extension with palpation of the A1 pulley, positive with triggering or locking and a tender nodule; table-top: the palm is placed flat on a table and cannot lie flat in Dupuytren contracture because of fixed finger flexion.

**Why:** Slides "Trigger finger test" and "Dupuytren contracture".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Hamate hook = ulnar nerve; lunate dislocation = median nerve.**
- **Carpal tunnel = 10 structures; FCR is NOT inside; ulnar nerve is in Guyon canal.**
- **Thenar eminence is spared in CTS (palmar cutaneous branch).**
- **OA spares MCP; RA spares DIP.**
- **Swan neck = PIP extension + DIP flexion; boutonnière = PIP flexion + DIP extension.**
- **Paper = radial; scissors = ulnar; stone = median; OK = AIN.**
