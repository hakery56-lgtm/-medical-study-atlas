## 1. Chromosomes and the purpose of a pedigree

- **Human cell = 46 chromosomes = 23 pairs.** **22 pairs = autosomes** (same in both sexes); the **23rd pair = sex chromosomes** (differ between male and female).
- To know whether a trait is **genetic and hereditary** we observe its **transmission across generations** or study its **frequency among relatives**.
- ⭐ The purpose of studying inheritance in families is **genetic counseling**: advising relatives about the **likelihood of developing the disorder or passing it on** to their children.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 46 chromosomes: 22 autosome pairs + 1 sex pair.
- Pedigree analysis = observe transmission or frequency among relatives.
- Aim = genetic counseling.

</div>

## 2. Family pedigree and symbols

- A **family tree (pedigree)** = shorthand system of recording information about a family; it begins with the **index case** (propositus), the person through whom the family came to attention, marked by an **arrow**.
- **Affected** = **completely colored** square (male) or circle (female); **carriers** = **half-colored** square or circle.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Circle** | **Female** |
| **Square** | **Male** |
| **Diamond** | **Sex unknown** |
| **Single bar** (line between partners) | **Mating** |
| **Double bar** | **Consanguineous mating** (close relatives) |
| **Roman numerals (I, II)** / **numbers under siblings** | **Generations** / **birth order** |
| **Single parent shown** | Partner is **normal or of no significance** to the analysis |
| **Number inside a symbol** | **Number of children of that sex** |
| **Half-shaded symbol** | **Autosomal heterozygous recessive** (carrier) |
| **Circle with central dot** | **X-linked carrier** |
| **Slash through symbol** | **Dead** |
| **Stillborn / twins** | Separate symbols for **stillborn**, **fraternal (non-identical)** and **identical** twins |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Circle = female, square = male, diamond = unknown sex.
- Filled = affected; half-filled = carrier; arrow = index case.
- Double bar = consanguinity; slash = dead; dot in circle = X-linked carrier.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

</details>

## 3. Autosomal dominant disorders

- The changed gene is **dominant** and lies on an **autosome**; **one changed gene is enough** to be affected.
- ⭐ **Affected parent + normal partner: each child has a 50% (1 in 2) risk** of being affected and 50% unaffected.
- **Pedigree: vertical transmission, no sex differences.**
- **Both parents affected (rare): 75% of offspring affected**, with **variable severity**.
- **Examples:** **Huntington's disease** (progressive brain disorder: uncontrolled movements, emotional problems, later loss of thinking ability); **Marfan syndrome** (connective tissue disorder affecting **heart, eyes, blood vessels and skeleton**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- One mutated allele is enough; risk 50% per child.
- Vertical transmission, both sexes equal.
- Both parents affected: 75% affected.
- Huntington's disease and Marfan syndrome.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the inheritance of an autosomal dominant disorder and the risk to children of an affected parent.</summary>

**Answer:** One changed (dominant) autosomal gene is enough to cause the disease. When one parent is affected and the other normal, each child has a 50% chance of being affected and 50% of being unaffected.

**Why:** Slides on autosomal dominant disorders.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the pedigree pattern of autosomal dominant inheritance and what if both parents are affected?</summary>

**Answer:** Vertical transmission with no sex differences. If both parents are affected, 75% of the offspring are affected, with a variable degree of severity.

**Why:** Slide "Any child born to a person affected with a dominant trait...".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give examples of autosomal dominant conditions.</summary>

**Answer:** Huntington's disease (progressive brain disorder with uncontrolled movements, emotional problems and loss of thinking ability) and Marfan syndrome (connective tissue disorder affecting the heart, eyes, blood vessels and skeleton).

**Why:** Slide "Common examples of autosomal dominant conditions".
</details>

## 4. Autosomal recessive diseases

- Humans have **two copies of each autosomal gene**; the disease appears only when **both copies carry a pathogenic variant**.
- **Pedigree: horizontal transmission** (affected individuals usually in **one generation**).
- ⭐ **Two heterozygous carriers:** **25% homozygous affected**, **50% heterozygous unaffected (carriers)**, **25% homozygous normal**.
- **Homozygous affected × carrier:** **50% affected, 50% carriers**.

| Parents | Affected | Carriers | Normal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carrier × carrier** | **25%** | **50%** | **25%** |
| **Affected × carrier** | **50%** | **50%** | **0%** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Both gene copies must be mutated.
- Horizontal pattern; one generation affected.
- Carrier × carrier: 25 / 50 / 25.
- Affected × carrier: 50% affected, 50% carriers.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. When does an autosomal recessive disease manifest and what is its pedigree pattern?</summary>

**Answer:** When both copies of the gene have a pathogenic variant. The pedigree shows horizontal transmission: affected individuals are usually in a single generation.

**Why:** Slides on autosomal recessive diseases.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the risks for the children of two heterozygous carriers?</summary>

**Answer:** 25% homozygous affected, 50% heterozygous unaffected (carriers) and 25% homozygous unaffected (normal).

**Why:** Slide "all the affected individuals in a family...".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the risks if a homozygous affected person marries a carrier?</summary>

**Answer:** 50% of the children are affected and the other 50% are carriers.

**Why:** Same slide.
</details>

## 5. X-linked disease

- **X-linked disease** = condition caused by a **mutation on the X chromosome**.
- A **male gives his X to every daughter and his Y to every son.**
- ⭐ **Affected male × normal female:** **all daughters are obligate carriers; no son is affected.**
- **Carrier female × normal male:** **each son 50% affected; each daughter 50% carrier.**

| Mating | Sons | Daughters |
|---|---|---|
| **Affected male × normal female** | **None affected** | **All obligate carriers** |
| **Carrier female × normal male** | **50% affected** | **50% carriers** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Mutation on the X chromosome.
- Father passes X to all daughters and Y to all sons.
- Affected father: daughters all carriers, sons unaffected.
- Carrier mother: 50% of sons affected, 50% of daughters carriers.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is X-linked disease?</summary>

**Answer:** A genetic condition caused by a mutation on the X chromosome.

**Why:** Slide "X-linked disease".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What happens when an X-linked recessive affected male has children with a normal female?</summary>

**Answer:** A male transmits his X to every daughter and his Y to every son, so all daughters are obligate carriers and none of the sons are affected.

**Why:** Slide "A male transmits his X chromosome...".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the risks when a carrier female has children with a normal male?</summary>

**Answer:** Each son has a 50% chance of being affected and each daughter has a 50% chance of being a carrier.

**Why:** Slide "For a carrier female of an X-linked recessive disorder...".
</details>

## 6. Karyotype nomenclature

- Written as: **total number of chromosomes, comma, sex chromosomes** → **46,XX = normal female; 46,XY = normal male.**
- **Symbols:** **+ extra chromosome, - missing chromosome, t translocation**; the **chromosome number goes between brackets**.
- **More than one chromosome:** list **lowest number first.** **Numerical + structural change in the same chromosome:** **numerical designated first.**
- **Location:** between brackets give the **arm (q = long, p = short)**, then **region number**, then **band**.
- ⭐ **Example: 46,XX,t(1;2)(p32;q22)** = female with a **translocation between chromosome 1 (short arm, region 3, band 2) and chromosome 2 (long arm, region 2, band 2).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Order: number, sex chromosomes, abnormality symbol, chromosomes in brackets, arm/region/band.
- p = short arm, q = long arm.
- Lowest chromosome number first; numerical before structural.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is a karyotype written, and what do 46,XX and 46,XY mean?</summary>

**Answer:** Total chromosome number, a comma, then the sex chromosomes; 46,XX is a normal female and 46,XY a normal male. A symbol then shows the abnormality (+ extra, - missing, t translocation) followed by the chromosome numbers in brackets.

**Why:** Slide "Karyotypes are abbreviated by...".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the rules when several chromosomes or both numerical and structural changes are present?</summary>

**Answer:** Start with the chromosome with the lowest number; if the same chromosome has both a numerical and a structural change in the same cell, the numerical abnormality is designated first.

**Why:** Slide "If more than one chromosome is affected...".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Interpret 46,XX,t(1;2)(p32;q22).</summary>

**Answer:** A female with a translocation between chromosome 1 (short arm, region 3 band 2) and chromosome 2 (long arm, region 2 band 2); p = short arm, q = long arm.

**Why:** Slide "Then between brackets specify the arm involved...".
</details>

## 7. Numerical chromosomal abnormalities

- **Normal meiosis:** gametes **n + n**, fertilization gives **2n** (normal). **Anaphase lag or non-disjunction:** gametes **n+1 and n-1**; fusion with a normal gamete gives **2n+1 = trisomy** or **2n-1 = monosomy**.

| Abnormality | Example |
|---|---|
| **Triploidy** | **69,XXX; 69,XXY; 69,XYY** |
| **Trisomy** | **47,XX,+21** |
| **Monosomy** | **45,X** |
| **Mosaicism** | **47,XXX / 46,XX** |

- **Mosaicism** = one individual with **two or more genetically distinct cell populations, all from a single zygote.**

| Syndrome | Karyotype |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Down syndrome (trisomy 21)** | **47,XX,+21** |
| **Patau syndrome (trisomy 13)** | **47,XX,+13** |
| **Edwards syndrome (trisomy 18)** | **47,XY,+18** |
| **Turner syndrome (monosomy X)** | **45,XO** |
| **Klinefelter syndrome** | **47,XXY** (two X chromosomes plus a Y) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Non-disjunction or anaphase lag gives n+1 and n-1 gametes → trisomy or monosomy.
- Triploidy = 69 chromosomes.
- Mosaicism = distinct cell lines from one zygote.
- Trisomy 21, 13, 18 = Down, Patau, Edwards; 45,X = Turner; 47,XXY = Klinefelter.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do trisomy and monosomy arise?</summary>

**Answer:** Anaphase lag or non-disjunction during meiosis gives abnormal gametes (n+1 and n-1). When these fuse with normal gametes they produce 2n+1 (trisomy) or 2n-1 (monosomy).

**Why:** Slide with the meiosis diagram.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Define mosaicism and triploidy and give notation examples.</summary>

**Answer:** Mosaicism: one individual with two or more genetically distinct cell populations from a single zygote (e.g. 47,XXX / 46,XX). Triploidy: 69 chromosomes (69,XXX; 69,XXY; 69,XYY).

**Why:** Slide "Numerical abnormalities".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Match the syndromes with their karyotypes.</summary>

**Answer:** Down 47,XX,+21; Patau 47,XX,+13; Edwards 47,XY,+18; Turner 45,XO; Klinefelter 47,XXY.

**Why:** Karyotype slides (trisomy 21, 13, 18, monosomy X, Klinefelter).
</details>

## 8. Structural chromosomal abnormalities

| Abnormality | Example notation |
|---|---|
| **Deletion** | **46,XY,del(4)(p16.3)** |
| **Inversion** | **46,XY,inv(11)(p11p15)** |
| **Reciprocal translocation** | **46,XX,t(2;6)(q35;p21.3)** |
| **Robertsonian translocation** | **46,XX,der(14;21)(q10;q10),+21** |

- **Deletion types:** **terminal** (at the chromosome end; **single break**, loss of the end segment) and **interstitial / intercalary** (inside the chromosome; **two breaks**, loss of the middle segment). Example: **46,XX,del(1)(q24q31)** = interstitial deletion of part of the long arm of chromosome 1.
- **Inversion types:** **pericentric** (centromere involved) and **paracentric** (one side of the centromere).
- **Reciprocal translocation:** two chromosomes **swap pieces**; both are hybrid (**der**) and each has lost a piece to the other.
- **Robertsonian translocation:** occurs **exclusively between two acrocentric chromosomes** (**Group D = 13, 14, 15; Group G = 21, 22**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Four structural changes: deletion, inversion, reciprocal and Robertsonian translocation.
- Terminal = one break; interstitial = two breaks.
- Pericentric includes the centromere; paracentric does not.
- Robertsonian: acrocentric chromosomes 13, 14, 15, 21, 22 only.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare terminal and interstitial deletions.</summary>

**Answer:** Terminal deletion occurs at the end of a chromosome from a single break with loss of the end segment; interstitial (intercalary) deletion occurs inside the chromosome from two breaks with loss of the middle segment (e.g. 46,XX,del(1)(q24q31)).

**Why:** Slide "Types of chromosomal deletion".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare pericentric and paracentric inversions.</summary>

**Answer:** In a pericentric inversion the centromere is involved in the inverted segment; in a paracentric inversion the inversion lies at one side of the centromere.

**Why:** Slide "Chromosomal inversion".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare reciprocal and Robertsonian translocations.</summary>

**Answer:** Reciprocal: two chromosomes exchange segments so both become hybrid (der) and each loses a piece to the other. Robertsonian: occurs exclusively between two acrocentric chromosomes (13, 14, 15 and 21, 22), e.g. 46,XX,der(14;21)(q10;q10),+21.

**Why:** Slides "Reciprocal translocation" and "Robertsonian translocation".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Circle = female, square = male; filled = affected, half-filled = carrier; arrow = index case.**
- **Autosomal dominant = vertical transmission, 50% risk; both parents affected = 75%.**
- **Autosomal recessive = horizontal; carrier × carrier = 25% / 50% / 25%; affected × carrier = 50% affected, 50% carriers.**
- **X-linked recessive: affected father → all daughters carriers, no sons affected; carrier mother → 50% sons affected, 50% daughters carriers.**
- **p = short arm, q = long arm; lowest chromosome first; numerical before structural.**
- **Triploidy = 69 chromosomes; trisomy = 2n+1; monosomy = 2n-1.**
- **Terminal deletion = 1 break; interstitial = 2 breaks.**
- **Robertsonian translocation = acrocentric chromosomes only (13, 14, 15, 21, 22).**
