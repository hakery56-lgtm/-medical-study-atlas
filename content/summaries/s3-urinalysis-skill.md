## 1. Midstream urine collection: definition, purpose and indications

- ⭐ **Midstream (clean-catch) specimen** = urine from the **middle of the stream** to **minimize contamination from external genitalia.**
- **Purpose:** **diagnose UTI (identify pathogens); detect hematuria or proteinuria; monitor renal and metabolic disorders.**
- **Indications:** **suspected UTI; routine prenatal screening; pre-surgical assessments; monitoring chronic kidney disease.**
- **Importance:** **accurate results (less contamination); guides antimicrobial therapy; early detection of urinary pathology.**

- Used for UTI diagnosis, screening and monitoring kidney disease.

## 2. Collection procedure

- **Preparation:** **wash hands with soap and water; sterile container.**
- ⭐ **Females:** **sit with legs apart → spread labia with two fingers → cleanse front to back with towelettes → start urinating into the toilet, then collect the midstream without touching the skin → remove the container before the flow ends.**
- ⭐ **Males:** **retract foreskin if uncircumcised → cleanse the glans → start urinating into the toilet, then collect midstream without touching the skin → remove container before flow ends.**
- **After:** **secure the lid without touching the inside; label with patient details and collection time; wash hands.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Start into the toilet, collect the middle, remove before the end.
- Females wipe front to back; males retract the foreskin.

</div>

## 3. Handling, transport, pitfalls and best practices

| Topic | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Storage** | **Room temperature if delivered within 2 hours;** **2-8 °C for up to 24 hours if delayed** |
| **Transport** | **Labeled container in a biohazard bag; complete the requisition form with clinical information; prompt delivery** |
| **Pitfalls** | **Skin flora contamination (false positives); incomplete cleansing; touching inside of container or lid; delayed transport → bacterial overgrowth** |
| **Best practices** | **Clear verbal and written instructions; appropriate cleansing agents and sterile containers; educate patients; timely processing** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- ≤ 2 h room temperature; delay → refrigerate 2-8 °C up to 24 h.
- Contamination gives false-positive results; delay causes overgrowth.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

## 4. Urinalysis with Multistix

- ⭐ **Quick dipstick test** for **multiple parameters; semi-quantitative (color changes).** Useful for **UTI, kidney disease, diabetes mellitus, liver function.**
- ⭐ **Steps:** (1) **fresh midstream sample in a clean container;** (2) **dip the strip briefly;** (3) **remove and tap off excess urine;** (4) **wait 30-120 s depending on the parameter;** (5) **compare pads with the color chart;** (6) **interpret each parameter (glucose, protein, blood, leukocytes, nitrites).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Dip briefly, tap off excess, wait 30-120 s, compare with chart.
- Parameters include glucose, protein, blood, leukocytes and nitrites.

</div>

## 5. Urine colour clues

| Urine | Cause | Dipstick |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Frothy, foam persists** | **Nephrotic syndrome: heavy proteinuria (> 3.5 g/day)** | **Protein +++** |
| **B. Dark brown, cola-colored** | **Acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis; also rhabdomyolysis** | **Blood + and protein +** |
| **C. Red or pink, sometimes clots** | **Hematuria: stones, UTI, trauma, tumours** | **Blood +, RBCs on microscopy** |
| **D. Dark red-brown after fava beans or oxidant drugs** | **G6PD deficiency: hemolysis → hemoglobinuria** | **Blood + but no RBCs on microscopy** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Frothy = nephrotic; cola = PSGN or rhabdomyolysis.
- Hematuria has RBCs; hemoglobinuria (G6PD) does not.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What are the causes and dipstick findings of frothy and cola-coloured urine?</summary>

**Answer:** Frothy urine: heavy proteinuria in nephrotic syndrome (protein +++); cola-coloured urine: acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis or rhabdomyolysis (blood + and protein +).

**Why:** Slides "Frothy urine" and "Cola-coloured urine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe hematuria and its causes.</summary>

**Answer:** Red or pink urine, sometimes with clots, from stones, UTI, trauma or tumours; blood positive with RBCs on microscopy.

**Why:** Slide "Bloody urine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How does G6PD deficiency change the urine and how do you differentiate it from hematuria?</summary>

**Answer:** Hemolysis after fava beans or oxidant drugs releases hemoglobin giving dark red-brown urine; the dipstick is blood positive but microscopy shows no RBCs, unlike hematuria.

**Why:** Slide "G6PD deficiency".
</details>
