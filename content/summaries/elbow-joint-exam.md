## 1. Anatomy and approach

- **Articulations:** **humeroulnar, radiocapitellar, superior radioulnar.**
- **Medial epicondyle = common flexor origin; lateral epicondyle = common extensor origin.**
- **Elbow pain:** local or **referred from the neck**; **inflammatory arthritis and epicondylitis** are common.
- **Approach:** wash hands, greet, introduce, explain, get **permission**; patient **standing with both elbows and shoulders exposed**; ask about **pain, tingling and weakness**; **compare sides**; steps **Look, Feel, Move**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Medial = flexors; lateral = extensors.
- Look, Feel, Move; compare sides.

</div>

## 2. Look and Feel

- **Inspect** from **front, sides, back**; **carrying angle** = angle between arm and forearm axes in **full extension and supination**, **normal valgus 11–13°**; **cubitus valgus > 15°**, **cubitus varus < 5°**; extend both elbows and **compare**.
- **Look for:** swelling, skin changes (**psoriasis, tophi, nodules, olecranon bursitis**), scar, erythema, deformity, bruising; **rheumatoid nodules** along the extensor surface and olecranon bursa.
- **Feel:** **temperature, tenderness**; palpate **lateral and medial epicondyles and olecranon tip**; **focal epicondylar tenderness**; **olecranon bursa swelling, nodules, tophi, rheumatoid nodules**.
- **Landmarks:** posterior view — epicondyles, olecranon, radial head; anterior — common flexor origin and cubital fossa.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Carrying angle 11–13°.
- Cubitus valgus > 15°; varus < 5°.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the carrying angle and its limits.</summary>

**Answer:** The angle between arm and forearm in full extension and supination; normal valgus 11–13°, cubitus valgus above 15°, cubitus varus below 5°.

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What do you look for on inspection?</summary>

**Answer:** Swelling, skin changes (psoriasis, tophi, nodules, olecranon bursitis), scars, erythema, deformity and bruising.

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What do you palpate?</summary>

**Answer:** Temperature and tenderness; epicondyles and olecranon tip; focal tenderness; bursa swelling, nodules and tophi.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

## 3. Move and epicondylitis

- **Active and passive:** **flexion–extension** (touch same-side shoulder then straighten; **normal 0–145°; below 30–110° causes functional problems**); **supination 0–90°, pronation 0–85°** (elbows by the sides, flexed 90°).
- **Tennis elbow** (**lateral epicondylitis**): outer elbow pain, lateral tendons, overuse of **forearm extensors**. **Test:** elbow 90°, **pronate**, flex wrist fully, support the elbow, patient **extends wrist against resistance**; pain at **lateral epicondyle**, may radiate down the extensor side.
- **Golfer's elbow** (**medial epicondylitis**): inner elbow pain, overuse of **forearm flexors**. **Test:** elbow 90°, **supinate** and extend the wrist fully, patient **flexes wrist against resistance**; pain at **medial epicondyle**, may radiate down the flexor side.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Tennis = lateral/extensors; golfer = medial/flexors.
- Flexion 0–145°; supination 0–90°; pronation 0–85°.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give the normal elbow ranges.</summary>

**Answer:** Flexion-extension 0–145°, supination 0–90° and pronation 0–85°; function is impaired below 30–110°.

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the tennis elbow test.</summary>

**Answer:** Elbow at 90°, forearm pronated, wrist fully flexed, elbow supported; the patient extends the wrist against resistance; pain at the lateral epicondyle is positive.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the golfer's elbow test.</summary>

**Answer:** Elbow at 90°, forearm supinated with wrist extended, elbow supported; the patient flexes the wrist against resistance; pain at the medial epicondyle is positive.

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

## 4. Neurological assessment

| Nerve | Motor test | Sensation |
|---|---|---|
| **Radial** | **Thumb's up sign** | **Dorsal web space between thumb and forefinger** |
| **Median (anterior interosseous branch)** | **OK sign** | **Palmar index finger** |
| **Ulnar** | **Finger spread against resistance, or paper held between middle and ring fingers** | **Little finger** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- OK sign = anterior interosseous nerve.
- Little finger = ulnar.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give the motor tests of the three nerves.</summary>

**Answer:** Radial: thumb's up; median (anterior interosseous): OK sign; ulnar: finger spread against resistance or paper held between middle and ring fingers.

**Why:** Slide 12.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give the sensory test areas.</summary>

**Answer:** Radial: dorsal thumb-forefinger web space; median: palmar index finger; ulnar: little finger.

**Why:** Slide 13.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which branch does the OK sign test?</summary>

**Answer:** The anterior interosseous nerve, a branch of the median nerve.

**Why:** Slide 12.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Tennis elbow is lateral (extensors); golfer's elbow is medial (flexors).**
- **Tennis test: pronation + wrist extension against resistance; golfer test: supination + wrist flexion against resistance.**
- The OK-sign caption names the radial nerve together with the anterior interosseous nerve; remember it as the **anterior interosseous (median branch)** test.
