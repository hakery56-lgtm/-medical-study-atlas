## 1. RA at a glance, immunity recap and the multistep model

- **RA:** **0.5–1%** of adults; **2–3× more in women**; **~2/3 seropositive** (RF and/or ACPA); **40–65% genetic** risk. A **chronic, symmetric, erosive polyarthritis with systemic features**: synovitis → **pannus** → cartilage and bone erosion → disability and systemic disease.
- **Innate** immunity: fast, same every time, **no memory** (neutrophils, macrophages, DCs, mast cells, NK; phagocytosis, complement, cytokines). **Adaptive:** slower, antigen-specific, needs **HLA**, **memory**, can turn against self (T cells, B cells). **In RA the adaptive arm makes autoantibodies; the innate arm drives much of the inflammation.**
- **Abbreviations:** **RF** (antibody against Fc of IgG), **ACPA** (anti-citrullinated protein antibodies; very specific; measured as **anti-CCP**), **PAD** (arginine → citrulline), **HLA-DRB1** (strongest gene), **SE** (shared epitope), **Tfh/Tph** (help B cells), **Treg** (calms), **FLS**, **NETs**.

| Step | Event |
|---|---|
| **1 Genetic risk** | HLA-DRB1 shared epitope, PTPN22 |
| **2 Mucosal trigger** | Smoking, microbiome, periodontitis → **citrullination** |
| **3 Tolerance lost** | **ACPA and RF** appear, often **years before symptoms** |
| **4 Second hit** | Cytokines, vascular leak, **synovitis begins** |
| **5 Chronic RA** | Pannus, erosions, systemic disease |

Steps 1–3 = **pre-arthritis (preclinical)** phase; steps 4–5 = **clinical** phase.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Adaptive = autoantibodies; innate = inflammation.
- Autoantibodies precede arthritis by years; a second hit triggers synovitis.

</div>

## 2. Genetic and environmental risk

- **HLA-DRB1 shared epitope:** **strongest genetic risk**; a short sequence in the HLA-DR groove that **holds citrullinated peptides well**. **PTPN22 (R620W):** T and B cells react more easily, so self-reactive cells **escape deletion**. **PADI4** (citrullination), **STAT4** (cytokine signalling), **CTLA4** (T-cell activation), **TRAF1–C5**.
- **Gene × environment:** **shared epitope + smoking** → greatly ↑ risk of **ACPA-positive RA only**.
- **Mucosal triggers:** **smoking/lung** (strongest environmental risk; airway inflammation induces PAD); **periodontitis** (**P. gingivalis** carries its own PAD); **gut microbiome** dysbiosis (**Prevotella copri**); silica and dust, obesity, infections, hormones.
- **Mucosal origin hypothesis:** tolerance may break at **lung, gum or gut** years before joint involvement.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- HLA-DRB1 shared epitope is the top gene; smoking is the top environmental factor.
- Smoking × shared epitope → ACPA-positive RA.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which genes raise RA risk and how?</summary>

**Answer:** HLA-DRB1 shared epitope (strongest; holds citrullinated peptides), PTPN22 R620W (self-reactive cells escape deletion), PADI4 (citrullination), STAT4 (cytokine signalling), CTLA4 (T-cell activation).

**Why:** Slide "Genetic risk".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the mucosal environmental triggers.</summary>

**Answer:** Smoking and the lung (airway inflammation induces PAD), periodontitis with P. gingivalis carrying its own PAD enzyme, and gut dysbiosis such as Prevotella copri expansion; silica, obesity, infections and hormones also modify risk.

**Why:** Slide "Environmental triggers start at mucosal sites".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the gene-environment interaction?</summary>

**Answer:** Shared epitope alleles together with smoking greatly increase the risk of ACPA-positive RA, and this interaction is seen only in ACPA-positive RA.

**Why:** Slide "Genetic risk".
</details>

## 3. Citrullination and loss of tolerance

- **Citrullination:** **PAD2/PAD4 + Ca²⁺** convert **positively charged arginine** to **neutral citrulline** (**deimination**) → protein folds differently, looks **"new"**. PAD is normally **off**; **cell death, stress and NETosis** let **Ca²⁺** in and switch it on.
- **Targets:** **vimentin, fibrinogen, α-enolase, type II collagen, histones.** Citrullination also occurs in normal inflammation, but with the **shared epitope** the peptides **fit HLA-DR tightly**, so **CD4+ T cells** treat them as foreign.
- **Lymphoid tissue:** APC (HLA-DR SE) shows the **citrullinated peptide** to an **autoreactive CD4+ T cell (Tfh/Tph)** via the TCR; **CD40L–CD40 and IL-21** give **B-cell help** → B cell → plasma cell → **ACPA**. **Germinal centres:** class switch and **affinity maturation**. **Epitope spreading** widens the response; **long-lived plasma cells** sustain it for years.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- PAD + Ca²⁺: arginine → citrulline; triggered by cell death, stress, NETosis.
- Shared epitope + citrullinated peptide → T-cell help → ACPA.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Explain citrullination and what switches PAD on.</summary>

**Answer:** PAD2/PAD4 with calcium convert peptidyl-arginine (positive) to peptidyl-citrulline (neutral), changing protein folding so it looks new. PAD is normally off; cell death, stress and NETosis let calcium into the cell and switch it on.

**Why:** Slide "Citrullination creates new epitopes".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How do citrullinated proteins lead to ACPA?</summary>

**Answer:** In people with the shared epitope the citrullinated peptide is presented on HLA-DR to autoreactive CD4+ T cells (Tfh/Tph), which give B-cell help through CD40L–CD40 and IL-21; B cells become plasma cells producing ACPA, which improve through germinal-centre affinity maturation.

**Why:** Slide "Loss of tolerance in lymphoid tissue".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are epitope spreading and durable memory?</summary>

**Answer:** Epitope spreading: the response widens to more citrullinated and modified proteins before clinical onset. Durable memory: long-lived plasma cells sustain autoantibody production for years.

**Why:** Slide "Loss of tolerance in lymphoid tissue".
</details>

## 4. RF and ACPA, and from autoimmunity to arthritis

| | **RF** | **ACPA (anti-CCP)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Target** | Fc portion of IgG | Citrullinated proteins |
| **Plasma cells** | Short-lived | Long-lived |
| **Germinal-centre rounds** | Few | Many; affinity-matured |
| **Specificity** | Moderate (~85%) | **High (~95%)** |
| **Sensitivity** | ~69% | ~67% |
| **2010 ACR/EULAR score** | Negative 0, low-positive 2, high-positive 3 | Same |

- **ACPA are more than a biomarker** (necessary but probably not sufficient; second hit needed): **immune complexes** bind **FcγR and complement** → macrophage **TNF**; **NETs** expose more citrullinated antigen (**vicious circle**); bind **osteoclast precursors** (bone resorption); experimentally sensitize **pain** nerves (**IL-8**).
- **Before symptoms:** broader, more potent autoantibodies; **cytokine surge** (IL-6, IL-12, IL-21, TGF-β) expanding Tfh/Tph; **injury, infection or stress** → leaky vessels → cells and antibodies enter the synovium.
- **Window of opportunity:** treatment in the **first months** gives the best chance of remission and preventing erosions.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- ACPA: ~95% specific, long-lived plasma cells; RF: Fc of IgG, moderate specificity.
- Early treatment = window of opportunity.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare RF and ACPA.</summary>

**Answer:** RF targets the Fc portion of IgG with short-lived plasma cells, few germinal-centre rounds and moderate specificity; ACPA target citrullinated proteins with long-lived plasma cells, many affinity-matured rounds and high specificity (~95%).

**Why:** Slide "Two autoantibodies: RF and ACPA".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How do ACPA contribute to disease beyond being a marker?</summary>

**Answer:** They form immune complexes that engage FcγR and complement so macrophages release TNF, drive NET release exposing more citrullinated antigen, bind osteoclast precursors to promote bone resorption, and may sensitize pain nerves via IL-8.

**Why:** Slide "ACPA are more than a biomarker".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the window of opportunity?</summary>

**Answer:** Treatment begun within the first months of symptoms gives the best chance of remission and of preventing erosions.

**Why:** Slide "From autoimmunity to clinical arthritis".
</details>

## 5. The inflamed joint: cells

| Cell | Role in RA |
|---|---|
| **Tfh / Tph** | Help B cells (**IL-21, CXCL13**); Tph ↑ in seropositive synovium |
| **Th1** | **IFN-γ** → activates macrophages and fibroblasts |
| **Th17** | **IL-17, IL-22, GM-CSF**; develop with **IL-6 and IL-23** |
| **Treg** | Calm response but **impaired**; too many Th17, too few Treg = inflammation |
| **CD8+ T** | Expanded memory cells; kill cells, release cytokines |
| **γδ, MAIT, NKT** | Link innate and adaptive immunity |
| **B / plasma cells** | Present antigen, secrete **RF and ACPA**, make **IL-6, TNF, lymphotoxin**; **rituximab (anti-CD20)** depletes B cells but **most plasma cells lack CD20** |
| **Macrophages** | **Main source of TNF, IL-1β, IL-6**; lining macrophages lost, inflammatory ones ↑; osteoclast precursors |
| **Neutrophils** | ROS, proteases, **NETs** |
| **Dendritic cells** | Antigen presentation; **IL-12/IL-23** → Th1/Th17 |
| **Mast cells** | Histamine, TNF, proteases; ↑ vascular permeability |
| **FLS** | Aggressive: **pannus**, apoptosis resistance, **IL-6, chemokines, RANKL, MMPs**; epigenetically imprinted; can migrate to other joints |

- **TLRs/DAMPs** switch on inflammatory genes through **NF-κB**.
- **Synovial pathotypes:** **lympho-myeloid** (aggregates, plasma cells, often seropositive), **diffuse-myeloid** (macrophage-rich), **pauci-immune/fibroid** (fibroblast-dominated); may predict response (B-cell depletion vs IL-6 blockade); investigational.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Th1 → IFN-γ; Th17 → IL-17; Tfh/Tph → IL-21 and CXCL13.
- Macrophages = main TNF source; FLS = pannus, RANKL, MMPs.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the roles of T-cell subsets in RA synovitis.</summary>

**Answer:** Tfh/Tph help B cells via IL-21 and CXCL13; Th1 make IFN-γ activating macrophages and fibroblasts; Th17 make IL-17, IL-22 and GM-CSF (driven by IL-6 and IL-23); Treg are impaired; CD8+ memory cells kill cells and release cytokines.

**Why:** Slide "T cells: orchestrators of synovitis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why can autoantibodies persist after rituximab?</summary>

**Answer:** Rituximab depletes CD20+ B cells, but most plasma cells (the antibody factories) lack CD20.

**Why:** Slide "B cells and plasma cells".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What do FLS do in RA?</summary>

**Answer:** They grow aggressively forming lining hyperplasia and pannus (resistant to apoptosis), release IL-6, chemokines, RANKL and MMPs, keep an epigenetically imprinted activated phenotype and can migrate to unaffected joints experimentally.

**Why:** Slide "Synoviocytes and synovial pathotypes".
</details>

## 6. Cytokines, JAK-STAT and joint destruction

- **Sources:** macrophages (**TNF, IL-1β, IL-6**), Th17 (**IL-17, IL-22, GM-CSF**), Tfh/Tph (**IL-21, CXCL13**), DCs (**IL-12, IL-23**).
- **Hub:** **TNF-α** induces IL-1, IL-6, chemokines (**TNFR1** inflammation, **TNFR2** may protect); **IL-6** drives Th17, helps B cells, acute-phase response (**CRP**; **hepcidin → anaemia**); **IL-1β** smaller clinical role.
- **Targets:** synovial fibroblasts (IL-6, MMPs, RANKL), endothelium (adhesion, **angiogenesis**), osteoclasts (erosion), liver (CRP, hepcidin).
- **JAK–STAT:** cytokine binds receptor → **JAK** phosphorylates **STAT** → nucleus → genes. **TNF, IL-1, IL-17 do NOT use JAK–STAT** (IL-6 induces them).

| JAKs | Cytokines |
|---|---|
| JAK1, JAK2, TYK2 | IL-6 |
| JAK2, TYK2 | IL-12, IL-23 |
| JAK1, TYK2 | Type I interferons |
| JAK1, JAK3 | IL-2, IL-7, IL-15, IL-21 |
| JAK2 | GM-CSF |

- **JAK inhibitors:** **tofacitinib (JAK1/3), baricitinib (JAK1/2), upadacitinib (JAK1).**
- **Cartilage:** FLS and chondrocytes release **MMPs and aggrecanases** (IL-1, TNF, IL-17); pannus invades at the cartilage–bone junction. **Bone:** **RANKL** (from FLS, Th17) → **RANK** on precursors → **osteoclasts**; **OPG** is a **decoy**; TNF, IL-6, IL-17 amplify; repair blocked by **DKK1 and sclerostin**. **Denosumab (anti-RANKL)** slows erosions without suppressing inflammation.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- TNF, IL-1, IL-17 do not signal via JAK–STAT.
- RANKL → osteoclasts; OPG blocks RANKL; denosumab = anti-RANKL.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which cytokine does not signal through JAK-STAT: TNF, IL-6, GM-CSF or IL-23?</summary>

**Answer:** TNF. IL-6, IL-23 and GM-CSF are JAK-dependent; TNF, IL-1 and IL-17 do not use JAK–STAT.

**Why:** Slides "JAK–STAT: the intracellular hub" and "Discussion questions".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain the RANKL pathway and bone destruction.</summary>

**Answer:** FLS and Th17 cells express RANKL, which binds RANK on osteoclast precursors to form mature osteoclasts causing erosion; OPG is a decoy receptor; TNF, IL-6 and IL-17 amplify RANKL; Wnt inhibitors (DKK1, sclerostin) block repair; denosumab (anti-RANKL) slows erosions without suppressing inflammation.

**Why:** Slide "How the joint is destroyed".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the actions of TNF-α and IL-6.</summary>

**Answer:** TNF-α is the core hub inducing IL-1, IL-6 and chemokines and activating the vessel lining (TNFR1 inflammatory, TNFR2 may protect). IL-6 drives Th17 cells, helps B cells and triggers the acute-phase response (fever, CRP), with hepcidin causing anaemia.

**Why:** Slide "The cytokine network".
</details>

## 7. Systemic disease, targeted therapy and the future

- **Systemic:** **cardiovascular** (accelerated atherosclerosis; leading cause of death), **lung** (ILD, airway disease), **anaemia of chronic disease** (IL-6 → hepcidin traps iron), **osteoporosis**, **nodules and rare vasculitis** (seropositive), **fatigue, low mood, sarcopenia**.

| Class | Examples | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **csDMARD** | Methotrexate, leflunomide, hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine | Broad immune suppression |
| **bDMARD** | Adalimumab, etanercept, infliximab, golimumab, certolizumab | **TNF-α** |
| **bDMARD** | Tocilizumab, sarilumab | **IL-6 receptor** |
| **bDMARD** | Abatacept | **CD80/CD86–CD28** co-stimulation |
| **bDMARD** | Rituximab | **CD20** (B cells) |
| **tsDMARD** | Tofacitinib, baricitinib, upadacitinib | **JAK1/2/3** |

- **Treat-to-target:** start **methotrexate**, then add or switch.
- **Emerging (mostly investigational):** **prevention** (abatacept in ACPA-positive arthralgia), **antigen-specific tolerance** (tolerogenic DCs, nanoparticles with citrullinated peptides), **CAR-T anti-CD19** (drug-free remission in small studies), **precision medicine** (synovial signatures).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Each pathway node is a drug target: TNF, IL-6R, CD28 co-stimulation, CD20, JAK.
- Treat-to-target: methotrexate first.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Link pathogenic pathways to targeted therapies.</summary>

**Answer:** TNF — adalimumab, etanercept, infliximab, golimumab, certolizumab; IL-6 receptor — tocilizumab, sarilumab; T-cell co-stimulation (CD80/CD86–CD28) — abatacept; B cells (CD20) — rituximab; JAK–STAT — tofacitinib, baricitinib, upadacitinib; broad suppression — csDMARDs.

**Why:** Slide "From mechanism to targeted therapy".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the systemic manifestations of RA.</summary>

**Answer:** Cardiovascular disease, interstitial lung and airway disease, anaemia of chronic disease, osteoporosis, nodules and vasculitis in seropositive disease, and fatigue, low mood and sarcopenia.

**Why:** Slide "Beyond the joint: systemic disease".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the emerging approaches in RA?</summary>

**Answer:** Prevention trials in ACPA-positive at-risk people, antigen-specific tolerance, CAR-T cells against CD19, and precision medicine using synovial signatures and biomarkers; most remain investigational.

**Why:** Slide "Open questions and emerging approaches".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **TNF, IL-1 and IL-17 do NOT signal through JAK–STAT; IL-6, IL-12/23, IFNs and GM-CSF do.**
- **ACPA are more specific (~95%) than RF (~85%); RF targets Fc of IgG, ACPA target citrullinated proteins.**
- **Rituximab depletes B cells but most plasma cells lack CD20, so autoantibodies persist.**
- **Shared epitope + smoking → ACPA-positive RA only.**
- **PAD needs Ca²⁺; cell death, stress and NETosis switch it on.**
- **OPG is a decoy for RANKL; denosumab blocks RANKL and does not suppress inflammation.**
- **Abatacept blocks CD80/CD86–CD28 co-stimulation.**
