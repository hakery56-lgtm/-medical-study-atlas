## 1. Definition, goals and fields of pathology

- **Pathology = pathos (suffering) + logos (study)** → the **study of disease**.
- The pathology of any disease involves **structural changes** in tissues → reflected as **functional disturbances** → perceived as **clinical features**.
- ⭐ **Goal:** identify the **cause (etiology)** and the **mechanisms of disease evolution (pathogenesis)** to help **prevention and/or therapy**.
- **Clinical medicine and clinical pathology are complementary and cannot be separated.**

### Fields of pathology
- **Experimental pathology.**
- **Clinical pathology** — the approach to the patient: **history → examination → investigations → diagnosis → treatment**.

### Subdivisions of clinical pathology
| Subdivision | Concerned with |
|---|---|
| **Histopathology** | disease from **examination of tissues** |
| **Cytopathology** | disease from **isolated cells**: **(i) exfoliative** (spontaneous shedding, e.g. **sputum** for cancer/TB, **urine**); **(ii) fine needle aspiration (FNA)** (negative-pressure aspiration, e.g. **breast, thyroid, salivary glands**) |
| **Hematopathology** | disorders of **blood cells and the coagulation system** |
| **Microbiology** | **infectious diseases** and the organisms responsible |
| **Immunopathology** | disturbances of **defense mechanisms** |
| **Chemical pathology** | **chemical changes** in tissues and fluids |
| **Medical genetics** | **abnormal chromosomes and genes** |
| **Toxicology** | effects of **known or suspected poisons** |
| **Forensic pathology** | pathology for **legal purposes** (e.g. death in suspicious circumstances) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pathology = pathos (suffering) + logos (study of disease); goal = etiology + pathogenesis → prevention/therapy.
- Clinical approach: history → examination → investigations → diagnosis → treatment.
- Cytopathology: exfoliative (sputum, urine) vs FNA (breast, thyroid, salivary glands).
- Subdivisions: histo-, cyto-, hemato-, micro-, immuno-, chemical pathology, medical genetics, toxicology, forensic.

</div>

## 2. Techniques of pathology

| # | Technique | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Gross (macroscopic) pathology** | changes seen by the **naked eye**; often so characteristic that an experienced pathologist can give a confident diagnosis (e.g. **"honeycomb" lung**) |
| 2 | **Light microscopy** | examines **tissue structure and cells** with the light microscope |
| 3 | **Histochemistry** | **specific reagents (stains)** detect chemical substances made by cells — **PAS (glycogen)**, **Perl's stain (iron)** |
| 4 | **Immunohistochemistry (IHC) and immunofluorescence (IF)** | **antibodies linked to enzymes** (IHC: colored deposit, seen by light microscope) or to **fluorescent dyes (IF)**; expanded by **monoclonal antibodies**; e.g. **Her2/neu** in breast cancer |
| 5 | **Electron microscopy** | **subcellular/organelle level**, demonstrates **viruses**; commonest use = **renal biopsy in glomerulonephritis** |
| 6 | **Biochemical techniques** | tissues and fluids: **fluid/electrolytes**, **serum enzymes** (e.g. **cardiac enzymes ↑ in MI**) |
| 7 | **Hematological techniques** | blood disorders: from **cell counts** to **coagulation factor assays** |
| 8 | **Cell cultures** | research (easy to modify environment); diagnostically for **chromosome spreads (cytogenetics)** |
| 9 | **Medical microbiology** | diseases caused by **bacteria, fungi, viruses, parasites** |

### Specimen preparation for light microscopy
- Solid tissue (liver, kidney…) must be **embedded in wax** (to **harden** it and allow cutting) and **thinly sectioned** (to **transmit light** and **minimize superimposition** of components); cutting is done with a **microtome**.

### Molecular pathology
1. **In situ hybridization (ISH):** shows specific **genes (DNA) or their mRNA** in tissue sections or cell preparations.
2. **DNA microarrays:** **many gene-expression patterns** (mRNA) at the same time.
3. **PCR:** **amplifies minute quantities of nucleic acids**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Techniques: gross, light microscopy, histochemistry (PAS, Perl's), IHC/IF, EM, biochemical, hematological, cell culture, microbiology.
- IHC = antibody + enzyme → colored deposit; IF = antibody + fluorescent dye.
- EM: organelles, viruses, renal biopsy in glomerulonephritis. Biochemistry: cardiac enzymes in MI.
- Tissue → wax embedding + thin sectioning (microtome).
- Molecular: ISH (gene/mRNA localisation), microarrays (expression patterns), PCR (amplification).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the techniques used in pathology.</summary>

**Answer:** Gross pathology, light microscopy, histochemistry, immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence, electron microscopy, biochemical techniques, hematological techniques, cell cultures and medical microbiology.

**Why:** List slide.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate immunohistochemistry from immunofluorescence and give a use of each.</summary>

**Answer:** Both use antibodies against specific antigens. In IHC the antibody is chemically linked to an enzyme that produces a colored deposit visible with an ordinary light microscope (e.g. Her2/neu in breast cancer); in IF the antibody is linked to a fluorescent dye (e.g. immunofluorescence of glomerulonephritis).

**Why:** The label differs: enzyme vs fluorescent dye.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why must a tissue be embedded in wax and thinly sectioned?</summary>

**Answer:** Wax hardens the tissue so that sections can be cut; thin sections allow light to pass through and reduce superimposition of tissue components.

**Why:** Preparation for light microscopy.
</details>

## 3. How to build knowledge of a disease

For **each disease entity** the student should list the chief characteristics:
1. **Epidemiology**
2. **Etiology**
3. **Pathogenesis**
4. **Pathological and clinical features**
5. **Complications and sequelae**
6. **Prognosis**

### Evolution of disease (Figure 2.1)
- **Etiology** (causes: immunologic abnormalities, genetic abnormalities (inherited/acquired), nutritional imbalances, infections, toxins, trauma) → **pathogenesis** (biochemical and structural changes) → **molecular, functional and morphologic abnormalities** in cells and tissues → **clinical manifestations (signs and symptoms)**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- For each disease know: epidemiology, etiology, pathogenesis, pathological/clinical features, complications, prognosis.
- Etiology → pathogenesis → molecular/functional/morphologic changes → signs and symptoms.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What should you know about each disease entity?</summary>

**Answer:** Epidemiology, etiology, pathogenesis, pathological and clinical features, complications and sequelae, and prognosis.

**Why:** Slide "Building knowledge and understanding".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Outline the steps in the evolution of disease.</summary>

**Answer:** Etiology (immunologic, genetic, nutritional, infectious, toxic, traumatic causes) → pathogenesis (biochemical and structural changes) → molecular, functional and morphologic abnormalities in cells and tissues → clinical manifestations (signs and symptoms).

**Why:** Figure 2.1.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Define etiology and pathogenesis.</summary>

**Answer:** Etiology is the cause of the disease; pathogenesis is the mechanism by which the disease develops (evolves).

**Why:** Both are the goals of pathology.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Etiology = cause; pathogenesis = mechanism of development.**
- **Cytopathology (isolated cells) ≠ histopathology (tissue).**
- **Exfoliative = spontaneous shedding (sputum, urine); FNA = needle aspiration (breast, thyroid, salivary glands).**
- **IHC = enzyme → colored deposit; IF = fluorescent dye.**
- **PAS = glycogen; Perl's = iron.**
- **EM main diagnostic use = renal biopsy (glomerulonephritis).**
- **Cardiac enzymes ↑ = MI** (biochemical technique).
- **ISH = locate gene/mRNA in tissue; PCR = amplify; microarray = many genes at once.**
