## 3. Results: spirograms and parameters

- Results are presented as **spirograms** (the **spirograph** and the **flow-volume loop**) and **parameters**.
- **Flow-volume loop:** **flow (L/s)** against **volume (L)**; **expiration = upper part** (**PEF** at the top, then **FEF25%, FEF50%, FEF75%**); **inspiration = lower part** (**FIF25%, FIF50%, FIF75%**).

| Parameter | What it assesses |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **FVC** | **Restriction and its severity** |
| ⭐ **FEV1** | **Severity of obstruction, reversibility and follow-up** |
| ⭐ **FEV1/FVC** | **Diagnosis of obstruction** |
| **PEF** | Peak expiratory flow |
| **MEF (FEF25-75%)** | Mid-expiratory flow |

- The example reports also list **FET, FIVC, predicted value, LLN, % predicted and Z-score** (predicted values: **Knudson**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Spirograms: spirograph + flow-volume loop; plus numerical parameters.
- FVC → restriction; FEV1 → severity/reversibility/follow-up; FEV1/FVC → diagnosis of obstruction.
- Expiration is the upper limb of the loop (PEF, FEF25/50/75); inspiration the lower (FIF25/50/75).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What does each spirometric parameter assess?</summary>

**Answer:** FVC assesses restriction and its severity; FEV1 the severity of obstruction, reversibility and follow-up; FEV1/FVC the diagnosis of obstruction; PEF and MEF (FEF25-75%) are flow measures.

**Why:** Slide "Spirometric Parameters".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the flow-volume loop.</summary>

**Answer:** Flow (L/s) is plotted against volume (L); the upper part is expiration (PEF, FEF25%, FEF50%, FEF75%) and the lower part is inspiration (FIF25%, FIF50%, FIF75%).

**Why:** Slide "Spirograph — Flow Volume Loop".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. In what forms are spirometry results presented?</summary>

**Answer:** As spirograms (spirograph and flow-volume loop) and as numerical parameters.

**Why:** Slide "Results".
</details>

## 4. Flow-volume loop patterns

| Pattern | Loop appearance |
|---|---|
| **Normal** (age 20 and age 50 examples) | Normal shape for age |
| **Mild obstruction** (asthma, COPD) | **Scooped (concave) expiratory limb**, reduced flows |
| **Moderate / severe obstruction** (asthma, COPD) | More marked scooping |
| ⭐ **Severe obstruction** (COPD, emphysema) | **Very low expiratory flows after a small early peak** |
| **Restriction** (pulmonary fibrosis, kyphoscoliosis) | **Smaller, narrower loop** (reduced volume) |
| **Bronchodilator pre- and post-** | **Improved curve after the bronchodilator = reversibility** |

- ⭐ Obstruction → **flows reduced** (FEV1/FVC low); restriction → **volumes reduced** (FVC low, FEV1/FVC normal or high).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Obstruction: scooped expiratory limb (asthma, COPD, emphysema); severity increases the scooping.
- Restriction: small loop (pulmonary fibrosis, kyphoscoliosis).
- Improvement after bronchodilator = reversibility.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How does the flow-volume loop differ in obstruction and restriction?</summary>

**Answer:** In obstruction the expiratory limb is scooped with reduced flows (more marked in severe disease such as COPD and emphysema); in restriction the whole loop is smaller (reduced volume), as in pulmonary fibrosis and kyphoscoliosis.

**Why:** Slides "Normal / Restriction / Obstruction", "Normal & abnormal FV Loops" and the spirogram grid.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give examples of obstructive and restrictive diseases shown on the loops.</summary>

**Answer:** Obstructive: asthma, COPD and emphysema. Restrictive: pulmonary fibrosis and kyphoscoliosis.

**Why:** Slides "Normal & abnormal FV Loops" and "Spirograms".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What does the pre- and post-bronchodilator loop show?</summary>

**Answer:** The post-bronchodilator curve is improved compared with the pre-bronchodilator curve, showing reversibility of the obstruction.

**Why:** Spirogram grid slide ("Bronchodilator, Pre- & Post-").
</details>

## 5. Worked examples (spirometry reports)

| | **Case 1 (27 Dec 2025)** | **Case 2 (22 Dec 2025)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Patient effort** | Good | Good |
| **FVC** | 4.49 L (**89%** pred) | 2.24 L (**47%** pred) |
| **FEV1** | 2.61 L (**60%**) | 2.05 L (**53%**) |
| **FEV1/FVC** | **58.1%** (LLN 74.9) | **91.5%** (LLN 71.0) |
| **PEF** | 5.81 L/s (66%) | 7.42 L/s (83%) |
| **FEF25-75** | 1.50 L/s (**31%**) | 2.59 L/s (65%) |
| **Post 5 mg salbutamol** | FEV1 3.86 L (**+48%**), FVC +12%, FEV1/FVC 76.4% | FEV1 **0%**, FVC +1% |
| **Report** | ⭐ **Moderate obstructive defect (FEV1 60% pred); significant reversibility** | ⭐ **Severe restrictive defect (FVC 48% pred); air trapping could not be excluded; borderline FEF25-75%; reversibility not significant** |

- Note: the slides print "Quality Control Grade: F" and "0 acceptable trials" on both reports although the written conclusion says "good patient effort"; and Case 2's conclusion quotes FVC 48% (the post-bronchodilator %pred) while the pre-bronchodilator best is 47%. Keep the slide wording but do not build exam questions on these.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Case 1: low FEV1/FVC (58.1%) + FEV1 60% → moderate obstruction; FEV1 +48% after salbutamol = significant reversibility.
- Case 2: FVC 47-48% with FEV1/FVC 91.5% → severe restriction; no reversibility (FEV1 0%).
- Both used 5 mg salbutamol nebulizer; predicted values by Knudson.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Interpret Case 1.</summary>

**Answer:** Good effort; FEV1/FVC 58.1% (below LLN 74.9%) and FEV1 60% predicted indicate a moderate obstructive defect; FEV1 rose 48% (2.61 to 3.86 L) after 5 mg salbutamol nebulizer, so reversibility is significant.

**Why:** Slide with the 27 Dec 2025 report.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Interpret Case 2.</summary>

**Answer:** Good effort; FVC about 48% predicted with a normal-to-high FEV1/FVC (91.5%) indicates a severe restrictive defect; air trapping could not be excluded, FEF25-75% was borderline and there was no significant reversibility (FEV1 0%, FVC +1%).

**Why:** Slide with the 22 Dec 2025 report.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do the two reports show obstruction versus restriction?</summary>

**Answer:** Obstruction (Case 1) lowers FEV1/FVC and the flows (FEF25-75 31%), whereas restriction (Case 2) lowers FVC with a normal-to-high FEV1/FVC and near-normal PEF.

**Why:** Slides with the two spirometry reports.
</details>

## 6. Upper airway obstruction (UAO)

| Type | Flow-volume loop |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Fixed UAO** | **Both expiratory and inspiratory limbs flattened** |
| ⭐ **Variable intrathoracic UAO** | **Expiratory limb flattened**; inspiration preserved |
| ⭐ **Variable extrathoracic UAO** | **Inspiratory limb flattened**; expiration preserved with a sharp peak |

- Variable extrathoracic and intrathoracic stenosis are listed as **rare** causes.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Fixed: both limbs flat; variable intrathoracic: expiratory limb flat; variable extrathoracic: inspiratory limb flat.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name the three types of upper airway obstruction and their loop patterns.</summary>

**Answer:** Fixed UAO flattens both limbs; variable intrathoracic UAO flattens the expiratory limb; variable extrathoracic UAO flattens the inspiratory limb.

**Why:** Slides "Upper Airway Obstruction UAO" and the spirogram grid.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which limb is flattened in extrathoracic obstruction?</summary>

**Answer:** The inspiratory limb.

**Why:** Slide "Upper Airway Obstruction UAO".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which limb is flattened in intrathoracic obstruction?</summary>

**Answer:** The expiratory limb.

**Why:** Slide "Upper Airway Obstruction UAO".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Flows → OBSTRUCTION; volumes → RESTRICTION.**
- **FEV1/FVC diagnoses obstruction; FEV1 grades severity and follows up; FVC assesses restriction.**
- **Contraindications: recent surgery, acute MI, pneumothorax.**
- **Restriction: low FVC with NORMAL or HIGH FEV1/FVC (Case 2: 91.5%).**
- **Reversibility: Case 1 FEV1 +48% (significant); Case 2 FEV1 0% (not significant).**
- **Variable extrathoracic → INSPIRATORY limb flat; variable intrathoracic → EXPIRATORY limb flat; fixed → BOTH.**
- **IOS is for poorly compliant patients; PSG = sleep study; CPET = cardio-pulmonary fitness.**
