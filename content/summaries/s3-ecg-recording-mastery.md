## 1. What is an ECG and how to prepare the patient

- ⭐ **ECG** = recording the **electrical activity of the heart over time** using **electrodes on the skin**. **10 electrodes (4 limb + 6 chest) → 12 leads** (views of the heart); usual recording **10 s**. **P = atria, QRS = ventricles, T = recovery.**
- **Preparing the patient (6 steps):** **1 identify and explain** (identity, consent, privacy) → **2 hand hygiene** → **3 remove jewellery, hospital gown** → **4 lie down and relax** (supine, arms by the sides) → **5 prepare the skin** (shave if needed, clean and dry) → **6 earth the machine** (avoid electrical interference).
- Learning objectives: place electrodes, record an adequate ECG and recognise artefacts, label and escalate, calculate rate and check rhythm and axis. **Interpretation is taught in UGY6.**

**Key points**
- 10 electrodes (4 limb + 6 chest) give 12 leads; 10 s recording.
- P atria, QRS ventricles, T recovery.
- Prepare: identify, hand hygiene, jewellery off, supine, skin, earth.

## 2. Limb and chest electrodes

- **Limb colours:** **RA red, LA yellow, LL green, RL black (earth).** Mnemonic: ⭐ **Ride Your Green Bike** (Red, Yellow, Green, Black). Some machines use **American (AHA) colours**: **always read the label on the lead.**
- **Limb rules:** **wrists and ankles, or near shoulders and hips; left and right at the same level; over flesh, not bone.**

| Lead | Position |
|---|---|
| **C1 / V1** | **4th ICS, right sternal edge** |
| **C2 / V2** | **4th ICS, left sternal edge** |
| **C3 / V3** | **Midway between C2 and C4** |
| **C4 / V4** | **5th ICS, mid-clavicular line** |
| **C5 / V5** | **Anterior axillary line, level with C4** |
| **C6 / V6** | **Mid-axillary line, level with C4** |

- **Finding landmarks:** **angle of Louis (bump on the sternum) = 2nd rib → 2nd ICS below it → count down to the 4th space (C1, C2) → 5th space at MCL (C4) → same level at AAL and MAL (C5, C6).** **Women: place C4-C6 under the breast.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- RA red, LA yellow, LL green, RL black (earth); Ride Your Green Bike.
- V1 right and V2 left of sternum at the 4th ICS; V4 at the 5th ICS, MCL; V5, V6 level with V4.
- Angle of Louis = 2nd rib; count down.

</div>

## 3. Recording, artefacts, labelling and escalation

- **Recording (6 steps):** **connect all 10 leads** (check colour and label) → **enter details** (name, ID, age, sex) → **settings: 25 mm/s, 10 mm/mV** → **patient still** (relaxed, quiet breathing, not talking) → **record and review** (clear trace, all 12 leads, rhythm strip) → **remove electrodes**, clean skin, hand hygiene.
- **Real ECG:** **3 rows x 4 columns** (I, II, III | aVR, aVL, aVF | V1-V3 | V4-V6) with a **long lead II rhythm strip**; **calibration box 10 mm = 1 mV**; **25 mm/s.** Before handover: **all 12 leads clear, no artefact, correctly labelled.**

| Artefact | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Movement or shivering** | Ask to relax; keep warm |
| **Wandering baseline** (breathing, poor contact) | Re-stick electrodes |
| **Electrical (50 Hz) noise** | Earth the machine; move away from devices |
| **Leads reversed** (lead I upside down, aVR upright) | Recheck colours |

- **Label every ECG:** **name and ID, date and time, symptoms at the time, your name, any non-standard placement.**
- ⭐ **Show a doctor now if:** **chest pain (ECG within 10 min), very fast or very slow rate, irregular or new abnormal trace, patient unwell (breathless, faint, low BP).**
- Note: the slide heading "Wrong connection" in the artefact table has no detail on the slide.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Settings 25 mm/s and 10 mm/mV; patient still, relaxed, not talking.
- Artefacts: movement, wandering baseline, 50 Hz noise, reversed leads, each with a fix.
- Chest pain: ECG within 10 min and show a doctor now.

</div>

## 4. ECG paper, heart rate, rhythm and axis

- **Paper:** **1 small box = 1 mm = 0.04 s; 1 large box = 5 mm = 0.20 s; 5 large boxes = 1 s; height 10 mm = 1 mV.**
- **Heart rate:** **300 method = 300 ÷ large boxes between two R waves (regular).** **1500 method = 1500 ÷ small boxes (regular, most accurate).** **6-second method = R waves in 30 large boxes × 10 (irregular).** Examples: **4 large boxes → 75 bpm; 14 R waves on a 10 s strip × 6 = 84 bpm.** **Normal 60-100 bpm.**
- **Rhythm:** use the **lead II rhythm strip (10 s)**, mark two R waves on paper, slide along; **same distance = regular (sinus), varying = irregular (arrhythmia, e.g. atrial fibrillation).**

| Lead I | aVF | Axis |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | **Positive** | **Normal** |
| **Negative** | **Positive** | **Right axis** |
| **Positive** | **Negative** | **Left axis (check lead II)** |

- ⭐ **Normal axis −30° to +90°.** If lead I is positive and aVF negative but **lead II is positive, the axis is still normal;** if lead II is also negative, it is truly left. Lecture examples: **right axis = lead I negative, aVF positive; left axis = lead I positive, aVF and lead II negative.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 5 large boxes = 1 s; 1 small box 0.04 s; 10 mm = 1 mV.
- 300 and 1500 methods for regular rhythm; 6-second method for irregular; normal 60-100 bpm.
- Axis: I+ aVF+ normal; I− aVF+ right; I+ aVF− left (check lead II).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the three methods of calculating heart rate.</summary>

**Answer:** 300 method: 300 divided by the large boxes between two R waves (regular). 1500 method: 1500 divided by the small boxes between two R waves (regular, most accurate). 6-second method: R waves in 30 large boxes multiplied by 10 (irregular rhythm).

**Why:** Slide "Heart Rate: Three Easy Methods".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How do you decide whether the rhythm is regular or irregular?</summary>

**Answer:** Use the lead II rhythm strip, mark two R waves on the edge of a paper and slide it along the strip; if the marks match every R-R the rhythm is regular (sinus rhythm), if the distance varies it is irregular (arrhythmia, e.g. atrial fibrillation).

**Why:** Slide "Rhythm: Regular or Irregular?".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Use leads I and aVF to classify the axis.</summary>

**Answer:** Lead I positive and aVF positive: normal; lead I negative and aVF positive: right axis; lead I positive and aVF negative: left axis (only truly left if lead II is also negative, otherwise still normal). Normal axis is −30° to +90°.

**Why:** Slides "Cardiac Axis: Quick Method" and "Axis: Two Real Examples".
</details>

## 5. Other types of ECG

- **Continuous monitoring:** live display on a bedside monitor (usually **lead II**) with heart rate and alarms; for **chest pain, arrhythmia risk, CCU/ICU, critically ill, after cardiac procedures**; **3 or 5 electrodes** over flat areas, clean skin, set alarm limits. ⭐ **Check the patient, not just the screen; for diagnosis record a 12-lead ECG.**
- **Ambulatory (Holter):** portable recorder worn **24-48 h at home** for symptoms that come and go (palpitations, dizziness, fainting).
- **Exercise ECG (stress test):** ECG **before, during and after exercise** (treadmill or bicycle, workload slowly increased) to **find ischaemia in suspected angina, measure exercise capacity, show arrhythmia with effort.** **Stop if:** chest pain or dizziness, marked ST changes, serious arrhythmia, fall in BP. **ECG, BP and symptoms watched; a doctor and resuscitation kit must be present.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Monitoring: lead II, 3 or 5 electrodes, alarm limits; check the patient.
- Holter: 24-48 h at home for intermittent symptoms.
- Exercise ECG: stop for pain, ST changes, arrhythmia, BP fall; doctor and resuscitation kit present.

</div>
