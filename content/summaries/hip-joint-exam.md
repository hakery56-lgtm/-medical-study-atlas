## 1. Surface anatomy of the groin and hip

- ⭐ **Groin (inguinal region)** = where the **lower anterior abdominal wall meets the upper thigh.**
- **Inguinal ligament:** from the **ASIS** (lateral attachment) to the **pubic tubercle** (medial attachment); lower border of the anterior abdominal wall. **Pubic symphysis** = midline cartilaginous joint.
- **Iliac crest:** palpated from ASIS back to PSIS; its highest points ≈ **L4**. **Greater trochanter:** lateral proximal femur; moves in rotation.
- **Posterior:** **PSIS** (≈ skin dimples above the buttocks), **PIIS** (below PSIS, hard to palpate), **sacrum** (between the two PSIS), **ischial tuberosity** (sitting bone; palpable when sitting with weight shifted away).

### Femoral triangle
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Position** | Below the **inguinal ligament**, upper anterior thigh |
| **Boundaries** | **Superior:** inguinal ligament; **Lateral:** medial border of **sartorius**; **Medial:** medial border of **adductor longus** |
| **Contents (lateral → medial)** | **NAVEL:** **N**erve (femoral), **A**rtery, **V**ein, **E**mpty space (femoral canal), **L**ymphatics |

### Clinical importance of landmarks
| Landmark | Importance |
|---|---|
| **ASIS** | Inguinal ligament attachment |
| **Pubic tubercle** | **Hernia examination** |
| **Iliac crest** | **L4** vertebral level |
| **PSIS** | Posterior pelvic landmark |
| **Greater trochanter** | Hip examination and movement |
| **Ischial tuberosity** | Sitting bone; **hamstring attachment** |
| **Femoral pulse** | Femoral artery assessment |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Inguinal ligament = ASIS → pubic tubercle.
- Femoral triangle: inguinal ligament, sartorius, adductor longus; NAVEL contents.
- Iliac crest = L4; pubic tubercle = hernia exam; ischial tuberosity = hamstrings.

</div>

## 2. Before you start, Look and Feel

- ⭐ **Preparation:** wash hands, introduce yourself, **confirm name and date of birth**, explain and obtain **verbal consent**; undress to underwear (shoes and socks off), **both iliac crests visible**, offer a **chaperone**, ensure privacy; **standing, then supine** (prone for extension); ask about pain and stop if significant. **Equipment:** measuring tape, couch, blocks, marker pen.
- **Finish:** compare both hips; examine the **joint above and below** (lumbar spine, sacroiliac joints, knee) and **neurovascular status**.
- **Look:** exposure, **gait**, then general inspection **front** (stance, shoulders, deformity of hips/knees/ankles/feet, wasting), **side** (stooped posture, ↑ lumbar lordosis, scars, sinuses, skin changes) and **behind** (scoliosis, shoulder/pelvic levels, leg length, gluteal atrophy).
- **Important points:** **asymmetric iliac crests = pelvic tilt**; **↑ lumbar lordosis = limited hip extension**; **wasting = neuromuscular disease or disuse in arthritis.**
- **Feel:** **greater trochanter** tenderness → **greater trochanteric pain syndrome**; **ASIS** tenderness → **enthesitis.**

### Leg length
| | **Measured from** | **Detects** |
|---|---|---|
| **True** | **ASIS → medial malleolus** | Actual anatomical discrepancy |
| **Apparent** | **Umbilicus → medial malleolus** | Shortening due to **pelvic tilt or deformity** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Always: consent, chaperone, expose both iliac crests, compare both sides, examine joint above and below.
- Look: gait + front/side/behind; Feel: trochanter (GTPS) and ASIS (enthesitis).
- True = ASIS to medial malleolus; apparent = umbilicus to medial malleolus.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Outline the preparation for a hip examination.</summary>

**Answer:** Hand hygiene, introduction, confirm name and date of birth, explain and obtain verbal consent; undress to underwear with both iliac crests visible; offer a chaperone; ask about pain; start standing then supine (prone for extension); prepare tape, couch, blocks and marker pen.

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What do you look for from the front, side and behind?</summary>

**Answer:** Front: stance, shoulders, deformities, muscle wasting. Side: stooped posture, lumbar lordosis, scars, sinuses, skin changes. Behind: scoliosis, shoulder and pelvic levels, leg length symmetry, gluteal atrophy.

**Why:** Slides 12–13.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Distinguish true from apparent leg length.</summary>

**Answer:** True: ASIS to medial malleolus, detects actual shortening. Apparent: umbilicus to medial malleolus, detects shortening due to pelvic tilt or deformity. Measure both legs and compare.

**Why:** Slide 14.
</details>

## 3. Move: range of movement

- ⭐ **Position:** supine, pelvis straight and stable. **Always compare both hips** for pain, restriction and asymmetry.
- **Flexion:** bring the knee towards the chest. **Abduction:** hand on the **opposite iliac crest**, move leg laterally, **normal 45°.** **Adduction:** move the leg medially across the other, **normal 25°.**
- **Rotation:** first with the leg **extended** (roll it), then with hip and knee **flexed to 90°**; normal **45° each**. ⭐ Moving the foot **medially = external rotation; laterally = internal rotation.**
- **Extension:** **prone**, stabilise the pelvis, lift the leg backwards; **0–20°.**
- **Note:** the figure "Normal range of movements" shows slightly different values (flexion 0–140°, extension 0–10°, adduction 0–30°, rotation about 0–40°/0–50°); the **text wording** is used above and no exam question is built on the differing numbers.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Abduction 45° (stabilise the opposite iliac crest); adduction 25°; rotation 45° each; extension (prone) 0–20°.
- Foot medially = external rotation; foot laterally = internal rotation.
- Always compare both sides.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do you examine hip abduction and adduction?</summary>

**Answer:** Supine, pelvis stabilised with a hand on the opposite iliac crest; move the leg laterally for abduction (normal 45°) and medially across the opposite leg for adduction (normal 25°).

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How are rotations tested and what does foot direction mean?</summary>

**Answer:** First with the leg extended by rolling it on the couch, then with the hip and knee flexed to 90°; normal 45° each. Foot moved medially = external rotation; laterally = internal rotation.

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is hip extension examined?</summary>

**Answer:** The patient lies prone, the pelvis is stabilised with one hand and the leg is lifted backwards; normal 0–20°.

**Why:** Slide 17.
</details>

## 4. Special tests

| Test | Main purpose | Positive finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Thomas** | **Fixed flexion deformity** | Hip **cannot fully extend**, remains flexed |
| **Leg length** | **Limb shortening** | Difference in measured length |
| **Trendelenburg** | **Gluteal muscle function** | **Opposite pelvis drops** |
| **Patrick (FABER)** | **Hip / SI joint pathology** | **Pain** during the manoeuvre |

- ⭐ **Thomas test:** supine; hand under the lumbar spine; flex **both** hips and knees; keep the **non-test hip maximally flexed** (removes lordosis); extend the test hip. **Do NOT perform** if there is a **hip replacement on the opposite side** (forced flexion may dislocate it).
- **Block test:** stand with feet flat; **blocks under the shorter leg** until both iliac crests are level. **True shortening** = anatomical; **apparent** = deformity (**adduction or flexion**).
- **Causes of true shortening:** hip: **fractures (neck of femur), total hip arthroplasty, slipped upper femoral epiphysis, Perthes' disease, unreduced dislocation, septic arthritis, loss of articular cartilage, congenital coxa vara, missed congenital dislocation**; femur/tibia: growth disturbance (**JIA, polio, cerebral palsy, fractures, osteomyelitis, septic arthritis, growth-plate injury, congenital**).
- ⭐ **Trendelenburg:** assesses **gluteus medius and minimus**; stand in front, palpate both iliac crests, **single-leg stance for 30 s**. **Normal:** unsupported side **rises**; **positive:** unsupported side **drops**. **Causes:** gluteal weakness, hip OA with pain inhibition, coxa vara, developmental hip dysplasia.
- **Patrick (FABER):** **F**lexion, **AB**duction, **E**xternal **R**otation; ankle over opposite knee (**figure-of-four**), stabilise opposite pelvis, push the knee down. **Groin pain = hip pathology; posterior pelvic/SI pain = sacroiliac pathology.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Thomas = fixed flexion deformity (hip stays flexed); contraindicated with opposite hip replacement.
- Trendelenburg positive = opposite pelvis drops (weak gluteus medius/minimus).
- FABER: groin pain = hip; SI/posterior pain = sacroiliac joint.
- Block test: blocks under the shorter leg until the iliac crests are level.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the Thomas test and its contraindication.</summary>

**Answer:** Supine; hand under the lower back; flex both hips and knees; keep the non-test hip maximally flexed; extend the test hip. If it cannot fully extend, there is fixed flexion deformity. Do not perform if the opposite hip has a replacement (dislocation risk).

**Why:** Slides 20–21.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the Trendelenburg test and its interpretation.</summary>

**Answer:** Stand in front of the patient, palpate both iliac crests, ask the patient to stand on one leg for 30 seconds and watch the opposite pelvis. Normal: it rises. Positive: it drops below the horizontal, implying gluteus medius/minimus weakness, hip OA with pain inhibition, coxa vara or developmental hip dysplasia.

**Why:** Slide 23.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe Patrick's (FABER) test.</summary>

**Answer:** Supine; flex the affected hip and knee; place the ankle over the opposite knee (figure-of-four); stabilise the opposite pelvis; push the flexed knee down. Groin pain suggests hip pathology; posterior pelvic or sacroiliac pain suggests sacroiliac joint pathology.

**Why:** Slide 24.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **True leg length = ASIS → medial malleolus; apparent = umbilicus → medial malleolus.**
- **Foot moved medially = EXTERNAL rotation (hip and knee flexed 90°).**
- **Thomas test: hip stays flexed = fixed flexion deformity; avoid if the opposite hip is replaced.**
- **Trendelenburg positive = the UNSUPPORTED side of the pelvis DROPS (normal: it rises).**
- **FABER: groin pain = hip joint; posterior/SI pain = sacroiliac joint.**
- **Extension is tested PRONE; the other movements supine.**
- **Greater trochanter tenderness = GTPS; ASIS tenderness = enthesitis.**
