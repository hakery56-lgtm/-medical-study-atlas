# Anatomy — Root of the Neck — Exam Night Review

Anatomy lecture (Al-Ameed 2nd stage): **boundaries, subclavian and carotid arteries, veins, nerves (cervical plexus, phrenic, vagus, sympathetic trunk, Horner's syndrome) and lymphatics.** After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer).

## 1. Definition and subclavian artery

- **Root of the neck:** area **immediately superior to the superior thoracic aperture and axillary inlets**; **anterior:** manubrium and superior margin of clavicle; **posterior:** **T1** and superior margin of scapula to the coracoid process; contains structures between **neck, thorax and upper limb** and the **cervical pleura**.
- **Right subclavian:** from the **brachiocephalic trunk**; **left subclavian:** direct from the **aortic arch** (lower in the thorax). Both pass **anterior to the pleura, posterior to the anterior scalene**, and become **axillary at the lateral border of rib I**.
- **Three parts** by **anterior scalene:** **1st medial, 2nd posterior, 3rd lateral**.
- **Five branches:** **vertebral, thyrocervical trunk, internal thoracic, costocervical trunk, dorsal scapular.**

| Branch | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Vertebral** | **First branch; through transverse foramina C6–C1; crosses posterior arch of C1; foramen magnum** |
| **Thyrocervical trunk** | **Inferior thyroid (→ ascending cervical), transverse cervical, suprascapular** |
| **Costocervical trunk** | **Deep cervical + supreme intercostal (first two spaces)** |
| **Internal thoracic** | **Behind costal cartilage I, 1 fingerbreadth lateral to sternum; divides in 6th space into superior epigastric and musculophrenic** |
| **Dorsal scapular** | **Deep to levator scapulae and rhomboids** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Subclavian: 3 parts, 5 branches.
- Vertebral: C6 to C1, foramen magnum.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the root of the neck and give its boundaries.</summary>

**Answer:** The area above the superior thoracic aperture and axillary inlets, bounded anteriorly by the manubrium and clavicle and posteriorly by T1 and the scapula to the coracoid process.

**Why:** Slide 3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Name the branches of the subclavian artery.</summary>

**Answer:** Vertebral artery, thyrocervical trunk, internal thoracic artery, costocervical trunk and dorsal scapular artery.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the vertebral artery.</summary>

**Answer:** First branch of the subclavian; ascends through the transverse foramina of C6 to C1, crosses the posterior arch of C1 and enters the posterior cranial fossa through the foramen magnum.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

## 2. Carotid arteries

- **Right common carotid:** from **brachiocephalic trunk**, wholly in the neck; **left:** from **aortic arch**. Ascend in the **carotid sheath** lateral to trachea and esophagus; **no branches in the neck**; **divide at the upper border of the thyroid cartilage**.
- **Carotid sinus** (baroreceptors, **glossopharyngeal**) and **carotid body** (chemoreceptors, **glossopharyngeal + vagus**) at the bifurcation.
- **Internal carotid:** **no neck branches; enters the carotid canal**; supplies **cerebral hemispheres, eyes/orbit, forehead**.
- **External carotid (8 branches):** **superior thyroid, ascending pharyngeal, lingual, facial, occipital, posterior auricular, superficial temporal, maxillary** — terminal: **superficial temporal and maxillary** (in the parotid, behind the mandibular neck); supplies **neck, face, scalp, tongue, maxilla**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Common carotid: no branches; splits at thyroid cartilage.
- External carotid: 8 branches.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the origin of the two common carotids.</summary>

**Answer:** The right arises from the brachiocephalic trunk and is wholly in the neck; the left arises directly from the aortic arch.

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the carotid sinus and carotid body?</summary>

**Answer:** The sinus is a dilation with baroreceptors (glossopharyngeal); the body contains chemoreceptors for oxygen (glossopharyngeal and vagus).

**Why:** Slide 13.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the external carotid branches.</summary>

**Answer:** Superior thyroid, ascending pharyngeal, lingual, facial, occipital, posterior auricular, superficial temporal and maxillary.

**Why:** Slide 17.
</details>

## 3. Veins

- **Internal jugular vein:** continuation of the **sigmoid sinus** (superior bulb, receives **inferior petrosal sinus**); exits the **jugular foramen** with **IX, X, XI**; in the **carotid sheath**; **joins the subclavian vein to form the brachiocephalic vein**; tributaries: inferior petrosal sinus, **facial, lingual, pharyngeal, occipital, superior and middle thyroid veins**.
- **Subclavian vein:** continues the axillary vein at the lateral border of rib I, **anterior to anterior scalene**; **only tributaries: external and anterior jugular veins**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- IJV exits via the jugular foramen.
- Subclavian vein: only external and anterior jugular tributaries.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the internal jugular vein.</summary>

**Answer:** It continues the sigmoid sinus, exits the skull by the jugular foramen with cranial nerves IX, X and XI, runs in the carotid sheath and joins the subclavian vein to form the brachiocephalic vein.

**Why:** Slide 21.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give tributaries of the internal jugular vein.</summary>

**Answer:** Inferior petrosal sinus and the facial, lingual, pharyngeal, occipital, superior thyroid and middle thyroid veins.

**Why:** Slide 21.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the tributaries of the subclavian vein?</summary>

**Answer:** Only the external and anterior jugular veins.

**Why:** Slide 22.
</details>

## 4. Nerves

- **Cervical plexus:** **anterior rami C1–C4 (+ minor C5)**, deep to **sternocleidomastoid**. **Cutaneous:** **lesser occipital (C2), great auricular (C2–3), transverse cervical (C2–3), supraclavicular (C3–4).** **Motor:** **ansa cervicalis (C1–3)** and **phrenic (C3–5, mainly C4)**.
- **Ansa cervicalis:** **superior root (C1 with hypoglossal)** + **inferior root (C2, C3)**, in the carotid sheath.
- **Phrenic nerve:** around the upper lateral border of **anterior scalene**, in the **prevertebral fascia**, then **between subclavian vein and artery** into the thorax.
- **Vagus:** in the **carotid sheath** between **common carotid and IJV**; in the root of the neck **anterior to the subclavian artery, posterior to the subclavian vein**. **Right recurrent laryngeal** hooks the **subclavian artery**; **left recurrent laryngeal** hooks the **aortic arch**.
- **Sympathetic trunk:** anterior to **longus colli/capitis**, posterior to the carotid arteries; **3 ganglia:** **superior (C1–C2, large)**, **middle (C4)**, **inferior → with T1 = stellate (cervicothoracic) ganglion, anterior to neck of rib I**.
- **Horner's syndrome (ipsilateral):** **miosis, partial ptosis, apparent anhidrosis, apparent enophthalmos**; causes: **Pancoast tumor**, thyrocervical venous dilatation.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Phrenic C3–5 passes between subclavian vein and artery.
- Horner's = miosis, ptosis, anhidrosis, enophthalmos.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the cervical plexus branches.</summary>

**Answer:** Cutaneous: lesser occipital, great auricular, transverse cervical and supraclavicular nerves; motor: ansa cervicalis and phrenic nerve.

**Why:** Slides 24–25.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the course of the phrenic nerve in the neck.</summary>

**Answer:** It forms from C3–C5, passes around the upper lateral border of anterior scalene in the prevertebral fascia, then between the subclavian vein and artery into the thorax.

**Why:** Slide 27.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is Horner's syndrome?</summary>

**Answer:** Ipsilateral miosis, partial ptosis, apparent anhidrosis and enophthalmos from sympathetic trunk damage, for example a Pancoast tumor.

**Why:** Slide 32.
</details>

## 5. Lymphatics

- **Superficial ring (5 groups):** **occipital, mastoid, pre-auricular/parotid, submandibular, submental.**
- **Drainage:** **occipital + mastoid → superficial cervical nodes (along external jugular vein)**; **pre-auricular/parotid, submandibular, submental → deep cervical nodes**; superficial cervical → deep cervical.
- **Deep cervical nodes:** chain along the **internal jugular vein**, upper and lower groups divided by the **omohyoid intermediate tendon**. **Jugulodigastric node → tonsils; jugulo-omohyoid node → tongue.**
- **Final drainage:** **jugular trunks → right lymphatic duct (right) / thoracic duct (left).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Jugulodigastric = tonsil; jugulo-omohyoid = tongue.
- Jugular trunks → right duct / thoracic duct.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name the superficial lymph node groups.</summary>

**Answer:** Occipital, mastoid, pre-auricular and parotid, submandibular and submental nodes.

**Why:** Slide 33.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe their drainage.</summary>

**Answer:** Occipital and mastoid nodes drain to superficial cervical nodes; the others drain to deep cervical nodes.

**Why:** Slide 34.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which nodes drain the tonsil and the tongue?</summary>

**Answer:** The jugulodigastric node drains the tonsils; the jugulo-omohyoid node drains the tongue.

**Why:** Slide 35.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Right subclavian ← brachiocephalic; left ← aortic arch.**
- **Common and internal carotids give NO neck branches.**
- **Phrenic passes between subclavian vein and artery; vagus is anterior to the artery, posterior to the vein.**
- **Right recurrent laryngeal hooks the subclavian; left hooks the aortic arch.**
- Slide 8 says the costocervical trunk arises from the second part on the right and first part on the left; keep that distinction.
