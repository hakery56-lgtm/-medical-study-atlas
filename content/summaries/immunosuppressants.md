# Immunosuppressants — Exam Night Review

A pharmacology lecture (2026-27) on **immunosuppressant drugs: the three-signal model of T-cell activation, their role in transplantation (induction, maintenance, rejection), and each class — antibodies, calcineurin inhibitors, costimulation blocker, mTOR inhibitors, anti-proliferatives, adrenocorticoids and other agents.** After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer).

---

## 1. Classification, the three-signal model and transplant therapy

| Class | Drugs |
|---|---|
| **Antibodies** | **Alemtuzumab, anti-thymocyte globulins, basiliximab, rituximab** |
| **Calcineurin inhibitors** | **Cyclosporine, tacrolimus** |
| **Costimulation blocker** | **Belatacept** |
| **mTOR inhibitors** | **Sirolimus, everolimus** |
| **Anti-proliferatives** | **Azathioprine, mycophenolate mofetil** |
| **Adrenocorticoids** | **Methylprednisolone, prednisolone, prednisone** |
| **Others** | **Belimumab, bortezomib, eculizumab, IVIG, tofacitinib** |

- ⭐ **Immunosuppressants** reduce immune activation to treat **autoimmune disease** or prevent **graft rejection**.
- **Three signals:** **(1)** antigen on APC triggers the **CD3/TCR complex** (not enough alone); **(2) costimulation:** **CD80/CD86 on APC + CD28 on T cell**; both activate pathways including **calcium–calcineurin** → **IL-2**; **(3)** **IL-2 binds IL-2R (CD25)** → **mTOR** → cell cycle and **T-cell proliferation**.
- **Transplant phases:** **induction** (about **2 weeks**, **antibodies and/or higher doses**; highest rejection risk), **maintenance** (**lifelong**, lower-dose **2–4 drug** combinations with different mechanisms) and **treatment of rejection** (attenuate immune response, prevent irreversible graft injury). **Acute rejection** 1 week–3 months (any time); **chronic** months–years (may need **plasmapheresis**). Choice depends on **target organ** and **type of rejection**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Signal 1 = CD3; signal 2 = CD80/86–CD28; signal 3 = IL-2 → mTOR.
- Induction (antibodies, ~2 weeks), maintenance (2–4 drugs), rejection treatment.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Explain the three-signal model.</summary>

**Answer:** Signal 1 is T-cell triggering at the CD3 complex by antigen on an APC; signal 2 (costimulation) is CD80/CD86 on the APC binding CD28; both activate pathways such as calcium-calcineurin producing IL-2; signal 3 is IL-2 binding CD25 on T cells, activating the cell cycle via mTOR and proliferation.

**Why:** Slides "Immune activation cascade".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate induction and maintenance therapy.</summary>

**Answer:** Induction is short-term (about 2 weeks) powerful immunosuppression at transplant, using antibodies and/or higher doses; maintenance is long-term, lower-dose combination therapy of two to four drugs with different mechanisms started at surgery and continued indefinitely.

**Why:** Slides "Immunosuppressants for induction and rejection".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Differentiate acute and chronic rejection.</summary>

**Answer:** Acute rejection occurs 1 week to 3 months post-transplant (any time) and early treatment minimizes damage; chronic rejection occurs months to years later and may not respond, needing options such as plasmapheresis.

**Why:** Slide "Acute and chronic rejection".
</details>

## 2. Antibodies and other biologic agents

| Drug | Nature / mechanism | Uses | Key adverse effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alemtuzumab** | **Humanized mAb**; **depletes T and B cells** (T recover 6–12 months, B ≤ 6 months) | **CLL, multiple sclerosis**, transplant **induction and anti-rejection** (acute cellular and **AMR**) | Prolonged immunosuppression → **Pneumocystis and herpes prophylaxis** |
| **Anti-thymocyte globulin** | **Polyclonal**; **depletes T cells**, apoptosis of activated T cells | Prevent early rejection; **severe or steroid-resistant rejection**; **3–10 days**, lymphopenia > 1 year | Infusion reactions (premedicate); **opportunistic infection, PTLD** |
| **Basiliximab** | **Chimeric mAb** against **IL-2R α chain**; **not depleting** | **Induction in renal transplant** with cyclosporine + steroids | Well tolerated; hypertension, GI distress, infections, PTLD |
| **Rituximab** | **Chimeric anti-CD20**; **B-cell depletion** | **B-cell lymphoma, PTLD, RA**; B cells normal in 9–12 months | **Infusion reactions**; **hepatitis B reactivation, CMV, zoster** |
| **Bortezomib** | **Proteasome inhibitor** → apoptosis of plasma cells | **Myeloma, lymphomas, AMR** | — |
| **IVIG** | Pooled human plasma; ↑ **B-cell apoptosis**, blocks antibody binding and complement | **Autoimmune disease, desensitization, AMR**; half-life **3–4 weeks** | Headache, fever, chills, myalgia, BP changes (slow infusion, premedicate) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Basiliximab = IL-2R blocker, not depleting; rituximab = anti-CD20; alemtuzumab depletes T and B.
- Bortezomib and IVIG are used for AMR/desensitization.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare alemtuzumab, anti-thymocyte globulin and basiliximab.</summary>

**Answer:** Alemtuzumab is a humanized anti-lymphocyte antibody depleting T and B cells (induction and anti-rejection, also CLL and MS); anti-thymocyte globulin is polyclonal and depletes T cells for early rejection prevention and severe or steroid-resistant rejection; basiliximab is a chimeric anti-IL-2 receptor alpha chain antibody that does not deplete T cells and is used for induction in renal transplantation.

**Why:** Slides "Antibodies".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe rituximab.</summary>

**Answer:** A chimeric anti-CD20 antibody causing rapid, sustained B-cell depletion and blocking maturation to plasma cells; used for B-cell lymphomas, PTLD and rheumatoid arthritis; adverse effects are infusion reactions and infections such as hepatitis B reactivation, CMV and herpes zoster.

**Why:** Slide "Rituximab".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are bortezomib and IVIG used for?</summary>

**Answer:** Bortezomib (proteasome inhibitor, plasma-cell apoptosis) for multiple myeloma, lymphomas and antibody-mediated rejection; IVIG (pooled plasma, B-cell apoptosis, blocks antibody binding and complement) for autoimmune diseases, pretransplant desensitization and AMR.

**Why:** Slides "Others" and "IVIG".
</details>

## 3. Calcineurin inhibitors and costimulation blocker

| Drug | Mechanism / features | Adverse effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Cyclosporine** | Binds **cyclophilin** → inhibits **calcineurin** → ↓ T-cell activation; **kidney, liver, heart** transplant, **GVHD** | **Hirsutism, gingival hyperplasia**, nephrotoxicity |
| **Tacrolimus** | Binds **FKBP** → inhibits calcineurin; **preferred** (↓ rejection), mainstay of maintenance, **GVHD**; switch if cyclosporine fails/intolerance | **Nephrotoxicity** (main limitation), infections, **hypertension, headache, tremor** |
| **Belatacept** | **Costimulation blocker** — inhibits **signal 2**; **kidney transplant** with basiliximab, **MMF**, steroids; can **replace calcineurin inhibitors** to avoid nephrotoxic, cardiovascular and metabolic effects | **↑ PTLD (CNS)** → **contraindicated in EBV-seronegative** patients |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cyclosporine → cyclophilin; tacrolimus → FKBP; both inhibit calcineurin.
- Belatacept blocks signal 2; contraindicated in EBV-seronegative.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the mechanism of calcineurin inhibitors.</summary>

**Answer:** Cyclosporine binds cyclophilin and tacrolimus binds FK-binding protein; these drug-protein complexes inhibit calcineurin enzymatic activity, preventing T-cell activation.

**Why:** Slide "Calcineurin inhibitors".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is tacrolimus preferred and what are its adverse effects?</summary>

**Answer:** It gives a lower rate of allograft rejection than cyclosporine and is a mainstay of maintenance therapy; adverse effects: nephrotoxicity (main limitation), infections, hypertension, headache and tremor (cyclosporine: hirsutism and gingival hyperplasia).

**Why:** Slide "Tacrolimus".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is belatacept and its contraindication?</summary>

**Answer:** A costimulation blocker inhibiting signal 2 of T-cell activation, used in kidney transplantation to spare calcineurin inhibitor toxicity; it increases PTLD risk (especially CNS), so it is contraindicated in EBV-seronegative patients.

**Why:** Slide "Costimulation blocker".
</details>

## 4. mTOR inhibitors and anti-proliferatives

- **mTOR inhibitors (sirolimus = rapamycin, everolimus):** inhibit **mTOR** → block **signal 3** → no cell-cycle progression/**T-cell proliferation**; used to **minimize calcineurin inhibitor doses** and spare **nephrotoxicity**; **rejection prophylaxis**; **sirolimus** also **GVHD**; **everolimus** also **oncology**. **Adverse effects:** **hypercholesterolemia, hypertriglyceridemia**, **leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia**, GI distress, **new-onset diabetes** (long term).
- **Anti-proliferatives:** block lymphocyte proliferation by **inhibiting nucleic acid synthesis**; **adjuncts with calcineurin inhibitors ± steroids**.
- **Azathioprine:** **prodrug → 6-mercaptopurine** → blocks **DNA elongation**; **bone marrow suppression** (dose-limiting); **allopurinol/febuxostat (xanthine oxidase inhibitors) ↑ toxicity**.
- **Mycophenolate:** blocks **T and B proliferation** by eliminating **de novo GMP** synthesis; **GI distress, bone marrow suppression, infections**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- mTOR inhibitors: hyperlipidemia, cytopenias, diabetes; spare nephrotoxicity.
- Azathioprine + allopurinol = toxicity; mycophenolate blocks GMP synthesis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the mTOR inhibitors.</summary>

**Answer:** Sirolimus (rapamycin) and everolimus inhibit mTOR, blocking signal 3 and T-cell proliferation; used to reduce calcineurin inhibitor doses and for rejection prophylaxis (sirolimus also in GVHD, everolimus in oncology); adverse effects include hyperlipidemia, cytopenias, GI distress and new-onset diabetes.

**Why:** Slide "mTOR inhibitors".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe azathioprine.</summary>

**Answer:** A prodrug converted to 6-mercaptopurine, incorporated into nucleic acid chains and blocking DNA elongation; dose-limiting bone marrow suppression; xanthine oxidase inhibitors (allopurinol, febuxostat) increase its toxicity.

**Why:** Slide "Azathioprine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe mycophenolate.</summary>

**Answer:** Blocks T- and B-cell proliferation by eliminating de novo guanosine monophosphate production; adverse effects are GI distress, bone marrow suppression and increased infection risk.

**Why:** Slide "Mycophenolate".
</details>

## 5. Adrenocorticoids and other agents

- **Corticosteroids:** the **first** immunosuppressants; **mainstay for attenuating rejection**; **transplantation: prednisone, methylprednisolone**; **autoimmune: prednisone, prednisolone**; used with other agents for **acute solid-organ rejection** and **chronic GVHD**; also **refractory RA, SLE, temporal arteritis, asthma**. **Mechanism unclear:** **T cells most affected**; rapid ↓ lymphocytes (**lysis/redistribution**); bind **glucocorticoid receptor** → nucleus → regulate transcription. **Adverse effects:** **GI distress, fluid retention, hypertension, hyperglycemia, mood swings, insomnia, confusion**; long term: **impaired wound healing, osteoporosis, myopathy, cataracts**.

| Drug | Mechanism | Uses | Adverse effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Belimumab** | Human mAb vs **BLyS** → ↓ B-cell survival (also autoreactive) and plasma-cell differentiation | **SLE**; renal/heart AMR | **Serious infections (PML)**, **depression/suicidality**, hypersensitivity; **no live vaccines** |
| **Eculizumab** | Humanized mAb vs **C5** → blocks C5a/C5b and **C5b-9** | **aHUS**; AMR in kidney transplant | **Meningococcal infection** → vaccine ≥ 2 weeks before or antibiotic prophylaxis; headache, nasopharyngitis, nausea |
| **Tofacitinib** | **JAK inhibitor** | **RA, psoriatic arthritis, ulcerative colitis**; transplant role under study | **URTI, nasopharyngitis, diarrhea, headache** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Prednisone/methylprednisolone for transplant; prednisone/prednisolone for autoimmune disease.
- Eculizumab = C5 → meningococcal risk; belimumab = BLyS → SLE; tofacitinib = JAK.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the role and adverse effects of corticosteroids.</summary>

**Answer:** The first immunosuppressants, still a mainstay for attenuating rejection and treating autoimmune disease (refractory RA, SLE, temporal arteritis, asthma); they rapidly reduce T lymphocytes via the glucocorticoid receptor regulating gene transcription; adverse effects: GI distress, fluid retention, hypertension, hyperglycemia, mood swings, insomnia, confusion and, long term, impaired wound healing, osteoporosis, myopathy and cataracts.

**Why:** Slides "Adrenocorticoids".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe eculizumab and its precaution.</summary>

**Answer:** A humanized monoclonal antibody binding complement C5, preventing C5a/C5b formation and the terminal complex C5b-9; used for atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome and AMR; it raises the risk of fatal meningococcal infection, so patients get the meningococcal vaccine at least 2 weeks before the first dose or prophylactic antibiotics.

**Why:** Slide "Eculizumab".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe belimumab and tofacitinib.</summary>

**Answer:** Belimumab is a human antibody against BLyS inhibiting B-cell survival, approved for SLE, with infection, depression and hypersensitivity risks and live-vaccine contraindication; tofacitinib is a JAK inhibitor approved for RA, psoriatic arthritis and ulcerative colitis, causing URTI, nasopharyngitis, diarrhea and headache.

**Why:** Slides "Belimumab" and "Tofacitinib".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Basiliximab does not deplete T cells (IL-2R α chain); alemtuzumab depletes T and B; rituximab depletes B.**
- **Cyclosporine → cyclophilin; tacrolimus → FKBP; tacrolimus is preferred.**
- **Belatacept is contraindicated in EBV-seronegative patients (PTLD).**
- **mTOR inhibitors cause hyperlipidemia and spare nephrotoxicity.**
- **Allopurinol/febuxostat raise azathioprine toxicity.**
- **Eculizumab needs meningococcal vaccination ≥ 2 weeks before.**
- **Belimumab: no live vaccines.**

## 🔥 Final review — memorize before the exam

1. Signals: CD3; CD80/86–CD28; IL-2 → mTOR.
2. Phases: induction (~2 weeks, antibodies), maintenance (2–4 drugs, lifelong), rejection treatment.
3. Antibodies: alemtuzumab (T+B), ATG (polyclonal T), basiliximab (IL-2Rα), rituximab (CD20).
4. Calcineurin inhibitors: cyclosporine (hirsutism, gum hyperplasia), tacrolimus (nephrotoxicity, tremor).
5. Belatacept: signal 2, kidney, EBV-seronegative contraindicated.
6. mTOR: sirolimus, everolimus; metabolic and blood adverse effects.
7. Azathioprine (6-MP, marrow), mycophenolate (GMP, GI).
8. Steroids: prednisone, methylprednisolone; belimumab (BLyS), eculizumab (C5), IVIG, bortezomib, tofacitinib (JAK).
