## 1. Tumor-like lesions and benign tumors

- **Hamartoma:** **well-circumscribed, often encapsulated mass of normal but disorganized components**; **pulmonary hamartoma** = **subpleural** lung mass with **cartilage, respiratory epithelium, fat**.
- **Choristoma:** **ectopic pancreas in the jejunal wall** (heterotopic normal tissue).
- **Thyroid adenoma:** **encapsulated** nodule with **whitish fibrous capsule**, compressing normal parenchyma; follicles.
- **Ovarian mature cystic teratoma (dermoid cyst):** **germ cell tumor** with skin, hair, fat (several germ layers).
- **Fibroadenoma of the breast:** **mixed tumor** — **epithelial** (dilated branched glands, myoepithelial cells) + **mesenchymal** (spindle cells); **both benign, no pleomorphism**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hamartoma = disorganized native tissue; choristoma = ectopic tissue.
- Fibroadenoma = benign mixed tumor.

</div>

## 2. Dysplasia and carcinoma in situ

- **Dysplasia grades:** **normal → CIN I → CIN II → CIN III** (cervical intraepithelial neoplasia; severity increases).
- **DCIS** (**ductal carcinoma in situ**) in **duct or lobule**, may show necrosis; contrasted with **invasive ductal carcinoma** (invades stroma).
- **Urethral carcinoma in situ:** **full-thickness atypia without basement membrane invasion**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- CIN III ≈ severe dysplasia / in situ.
- In situ = no basement membrane invasion.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the grades of cervical dysplasia.</summary>

**Answer:** Normal epithelium, CIN I, CIN II and CIN III.

**Why:** Slide 9.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What distinguishes DCIS from invasive ductal carcinoma?</summary>

**Answer:** DCIS stays within the duct or lobule; invasive carcinoma invades the surrounding stroma.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Define carcinoma in situ.</summary>

**Answer:** Malignant-looking epithelial change confined to the epithelium without invasion of the basement membrane.

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

## 3. Squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma

- **SCC of skin:** **central ulcer with rolling edges**, **exophytic growth**. **SCC of lung:** **massive necrosis with central cavitation**.
- **Well-differentiated SCC:** **nests of cohesive squamous cells, keratin pearls, intercellular bridges (desmosomes)**, variable cell and nuclear size; **poorly differentiated SCC** lacks keratinization.
- **Colonic polyp:** **stalked** growth.
- **Adenocarcinoma of colon:** **exophytic**, **ulcerating**, **deeply penetrating** the wall; **well-differentiated** = **gland-forming**, **pleomorphic hyperchromatic** cells vs normal glands; **poorly differentiated** = sheets, few glands.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Keratin pearls = well-differentiated SCC.
- Glands = adenocarcinoma.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe well-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.</summary>

**Answer:** Nests of cohesive squamous cells with keratin pearls and intercellular bridges, with variable cell and nuclear size and shape.

**Why:** Slide 14.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe adenocarcinoma of the colon.</summary>

**Answer:** Grossly exophytic or ulcerating and deeply penetrating the wall; microscopically gland-forming, pleomorphic, hyperchromatic malignant cells contrasting with normal glands.

**Why:** Slides 18–19.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How does poor differentiation look?</summary>

**Answer:** Loss of keratinization in SCC and loss of gland formation in adenocarcinoma, with sheets of anaplastic cells.

**Why:** Slides 15 and 20.
</details>

## 4. Leiomyoma vs leiomyosarcoma and mitoses

| Feature | **Uterine leiomyoma** | **Leiomyosarcoma** |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross** | **Multiple well-defined gray firm homogeneous masses** | **Single large irregular mass with hemorrhage and necrosis** |
| **Micro** | **Interlacing muscle fibers; uniform normochromic spindle nuclei** | **Pleomorphic spindle cells, highly cellular, frequent mitoses, necrosis, tripolar mitosis** |

- **Mitotic figures:** **Class 1 normal** (prometaphase, metaphase, ring, anaphase/telophase); **Class 2 atypical** (multipolar, bipolar asymmetry, lagging chromosome, bridging chromosome, dispersed chromosome).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Leiomyoma: multiple, uniform.
- Leiomyosarcoma: single, necrotic, pleomorphic.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare leiomyoma and leiomyosarcoma.</summary>

**Answer:** Leiomyoma gives multiple well-defined firm masses with uniform spindle nuclei; leiomyosarcoma is a single large irregular necrotic mass with pleomorphic cells and abnormal mitoses.

**Why:** Slides 22–24.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give atypical mitotic figures.</summary>

**Answer:** Multipolar, bipolar asymmetry, lagging chromosome, bridging chromosome and dispersed chromosome.

**Why:** Slide 21.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What does necrosis with tripolar mitosis suggest?</summary>

**Answer:** A high-grade malignant sarcoma.

**Why:** Slide 24.
</details>

## 5. Invasion, metastasis and anaplasia

- **Lymphovascular invasion (LVI):** **tumor emboli within lymphatic spaces** (with small vessels); **associated with lymph node metastasis and recurrence**.
- **Metastasis:** **in lymphoid tissue**; **vascular metastases in lungs and liver**; **vascular invasion**; **perineural invasion**.
- **Paget's disease of the breast:** **intraepithelial spread** of cancer within the nipple epidermis.
- **Undifferentiated (anaplastic) cancer:** **complete loss of differentiation, frequent abnormal mitoses, marked pleomorphism with giant cells, extreme hyperchromasia, N:C up to 1:1, coarse chromatin, prominent nucleoli**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- LVI predicts nodal metastasis and recurrence.
- Anaplastic = primitive cells, giant cells, 1:1 N:C.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define lymphovascular invasion and its significance.</summary>

**Answer:** Tumor emboli within lymphatic spaces (and small vessels); it is linked to lymph node metastasis and recurrence.

**Why:** Slide 25.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give the features of anaplastic cancer.</summary>

**Answer:** Complete loss of differentiation, frequent abnormal mitoses, marked pleomorphism and giant cells, extreme hyperchromasia, N:C up to 1:1, coarse chromatin and prominent nucleoli.

**Why:** Slide 31.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is Paget's disease of the breast?</summary>

**Answer:** Intraepithelial spread of cancer cells within the nipple epidermis.

**Why:** Slide 30.
</details>
