## 1. Chemical transmission

- ⭐ **Cholinergic = secrete ACh; adrenergic = secrete norepinephrine** (named after adrenalin).
- ⭐ **All preganglionic neurons (SNS and PNS) are cholinergic.** **All or almost all parasympathetic postganglionic neurons are cholinergic.** **Most sympathetic postganglionic neurons are adrenergic;** exception: **fibers to sweat glands and a very few muscle blood vessels are cholinergic.**

| | **Acetylcholine** | **Norepinephrine** |
|---|---|---|
| **Synthesis** | **Nerve endings; mostly in axoplasm outside vesicles** | **Starts in axoplasm, completed inside vesicles; NE → E in adrenal medulla (~80%)** |
| **Clearance** | **Acetylcholinesterase → acetate + choline (choline recycled); persists a few seconds; not found in circulation** | **Reuptake into nerve endings; diffusion; less by enzymes, especially MAO** |
| **Duration** | **Localized, temporary** | **Adrenal NE/E remain active longer (10-30 s; inactive after minutes)** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Preganglionic = cholinergic; postganglionic sympathetic = mostly adrenergic (sweat glands cholinergic).
- ACh destroyed by acetylcholinesterase; NE mainly by reuptake.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which autonomic neurons are cholinergic and which are adrenergic?</summary>

**Answer:** All preganglionic neurons and almost all parasympathetic postganglionic neurons are cholinergic; most sympathetic postganglionic neurons are adrenergic, except fibers to sweat glands and a few muscle vessels, which are cholinergic.

**Why:** Slides "Chemical transmission".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the synthesis and clearance of ACh.</summary>

**Answer:** Synthesized in terminal nerve endings (mostly in the axoplasm); after release it lasts seconds and is split into acetate and choline by acetylcholinesterase; choline is taken back to make new ACh.

**Why:** Slide "Synthesis and catabolism of acetylcholine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is norepinephrine removed?</summary>

**Answer:** Mainly by reuptake into noradrenergic nerve endings and diffusion into body fluids, and to a lesser degree by enzymatic destruction (MAO).

**Why:** Slide "Synthesis and clearance of norepinephrine".
</details>

## 2. Receptors on effector organs

- **Transmitters bind receptors on the outside of the cell membrane → conformational change → excitation or inhibition**, most often by **changing ion permeability** or **activating/inactivating an enzyme** inside the cell.
- ⭐ **ACh receptors:** **muscarinic** (effector cells of postganglionic PNS neurons and postganglionic cholinergic SNS neurons) and **nicotinic** (**ganglia between pre- and postganglionic neurons of SNS and PNS** and the **neuromuscular junction**). **Both found in large numbers in the brain.**
- ⭐ **Adrenergic receptors:** **alpha (α1, α2)** and **beta (β1, β2, β3);** some functions excitatory, others inhibitory.

| **Alpha** | **Beta** |
|---|---|
| **Vasoconstriction; iris dilation; intestinal relaxation; intestinal and bladder sphincter contraction; pilomotor contraction; α2: inhibits neurotransmitter release** | **Vasodilation (β2); cardioacceleration (β1); ↑ myocardial strength (β1); intestinal and uterine relaxation (β2); bronchodilation (β2); calorigenesis (β2); glycogenolysis (β2); lipolysis (β1); bladder wall relaxation (β2); thermogenesis (β3)** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Muscarinic = effector organs; nicotinic = ganglia and neuromuscular junction.
- Alpha mostly constricts; beta-1 heart, beta-2 relaxes, beta-3 thermogenesis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare muscarinic and nicotinic receptors.</summary>

**Answer:** Muscarinic receptors are on effector cells of postganglionic PNS (and cholinergic SNS) neurons; nicotinic receptors are at autonomic ganglia and the neuromuscular junction; both occur in the brain.

**Why:** Slide "ACh receptors".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the types of adrenergic receptors?</summary>

**Answer:** Alpha (α1, α2) and beta (β1, β2, β3); some functions are excitatory and others inhibitory.

**Why:** Slide "Adrenergic receptors".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do receptors act on the effector cell?</summary>

**Answer:** Binding changes the receptor's conformation, which excites or inhibits the cell by changing membrane ion permeability or activating/inactivating an attached intracellular enzyme.

**Why:** Slide "Receptors on the effector organs".
</details>

## 3. Norepinephrine, epinephrine and the adrenal medullae

- ⭐ **NE excites mainly alpha receptors and, less, beta.** **E excites alpha and beta equally.** **E is more effective if all receptors in the organ are beta.** Effects depend on **receptor types in the organ.**
- **Sympathomimetics** mimic NE or E; some are **selective** (**isoprenaline: strong beta, essentially no alpha action**).
- ⭐ **Adrenal medullae:** **sympathetic stimulation releases E and NE into blood;** same general effects as nerve-released but **5 to 10 times longer** (**removed slowly over 2-4 minutes**). **E: greater cardiac stimulation and 5-10 times greater metabolic effect; NE: stronger muscle vessel constriction.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- NE: alpha > beta; E: alpha = beta.
- Adrenal hormones act 5-10 times longer than nerve-released transmitter.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the receptor effects of NE and E.</summary>

**Answer:** NE excites mainly alpha and less beta receptors; E excites both equally and is more effective where all receptors are beta; the organ's receptor types determine the response.

**Why:** Slide "Norepinephrine and epinephrine effect".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are sympathomimetic drugs? Give an example.</summary>

**Answer:** Drugs mimicking NE or E; some are selective, e.g. isoprenaline strongly acts on beta receptors with essentially no alpha action.

**Why:** Slide "Sympathomimetic drugs".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the functions of the adrenal medullae.</summary>

**Answer:** Sympathetic stimulation releases E and NE into the blood, with similar effects to nerve-released ones lasting 5 to 10 times longer; E has greater cardiac and metabolic effect, NE greater muscle vessel constriction.

**Why:** Slide "Functions of adrenal medullae".
</details>

## 4. Effects and functions of the SNS and PNS

- **Each system excites some organs and inhibits others; many organs receive both (sometimes reciprocal); most are dominantly controlled by one;** **no generalization** predicts the effect on an organ.

| | **SNS** | **PNS** |
|---|---|---|
| **Sweating** | **↑** | **↓** |
| **Peristalsis** | **↓** | **↑** |
| **Heart rate** | **↑ (↑ conduction speed, ↓ refractory period)** | **↓ (↓ conduction speed, ↑ refractory period)** |
| **Blood pressure** | **↑ (↑ contractility, ↑ relaxation and filling)** | **↓ (↓ contractility, ↓ relaxation and filling)** |
| **Pupil** | **Dilation** | **Constriction** |

- ⭐ **SNS (fight or flight):** **hypothalamus triggers massive discharge: ↑ HR, BP, mental activity;** can be isolated (**heat regulation**). **Widespread and prolonged effects due to long postganglionic fibers, incomplete NE reuptake, adrenal medullae.**
- ⭐ **PNS (rest and digest):** **conserves energy, slows HR, favors digestion;** **mainly reflex-activated, localized, discrete;** **short postganglionic neurons; ACh rapidly destroyed.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- SNS: fight or flight, widespread; PNS: rest and digest, localized.
- Three reasons SNS effects are prolonged: long postganglionic fibers, incomplete reuptake, adrenal medulla.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the effects of SNS and PNS activation.</summary>

**Answer:** SNS: more sweating, less peristalsis, faster heart rate, higher BP, pupil dilation; PNS: less sweating, more peristalsis, slower heart rate, lower BP, pupil constriction.

**Why:** Slide "Effects of SNS and PNS activation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why are sympathetic effects widespread and prolonged?</summary>

**Answer:** Because of long postganglionic fibers, incomplete neuronal reuptake of NE and activation of the adrenal medullae.

**Why:** Slide "Summary of the sympathetic nervous system".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why are parasympathetic effects localized?</summary>

**Answer:** Its postganglionic neurons are short and control effectors locally, and ACh is rapidly destroyed; it is mainly activated by reflex activity.

**Why:** Slide "Summary of the parasympathetic nervous system".
</details>
