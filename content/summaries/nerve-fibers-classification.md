## 1. Neuron, nerve fiber and myelin

- **Neuron** = structural and functional unit of the nervous system (**~10¹² neurons**). **Nerve fiber** = thread-like extension of a nerve cell: **axon + myelin sheath (if present).**
- ⭐ **Myelin sheath:** formed by **Schwann cells (PNS)** and **oligodendroglia (CNS)**; composed of **proteins and lipids (cholesterol, lecithin, sphingomyelin).**
- **Nodes of Ranvier:** gaps without myelin (**2-3 µm**), about **once every 1-3 mm.**
- **Myelin:** **prevents ion leakage 5000-fold**, **raises conduction velocity 5-50-fold** by **saltatory conduction** (~**100 m/s** myelinated vs **0.5-1 m/s** unmyelinated); **saves energy** because **only the nodes depolarize.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Myelin: Schwann cells (PNS), oligodendroglia (CNS); nodes every 1-3 mm.
- Saltatory conduction: faster (100 vs 0.5-1 m/s) and energy-saving.

</div>

**Answer:** Schwann cells in the PNS and oligodendroglia in the CNS; it is made of proteins and lipids (cholesterol, lecithin, sphingomyelin).

</details>

## 2. Classification by structure and distribution

| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| **Structure** | **Myelinated** (covered by myelin) / **Unmyelinated** |
| **Distribution** | **Somatic** (skeletal muscle) / **Visceral (autonomic)** (internal organs) |

- **Somatic fibers:** **α-type motor**; transmitter **acetylcholine**; **always excite muscle** (inhibition is central via interneurons).
- **Autonomic fibers:** supply **smooth muscle, cardiac muscle and glands**; maintain **homeostasis**; can **excite or inhibit.**

| | **Preganglionic** | **Postganglionic** |
|---|---|---|
| **Transmitter** | **ACh** (both systems) | **ACh** (parasympathetic); **ACh or norepinephrine** (sympathetic) |
| **Fiber** | **Myelinated B** | **Unmyelinated C** (sympathetic) |
| **Ends on** | **Postganglionic cell body** | **Visceral effector** |
| **Length** | Shorter in **sympathetic** | Shorter in **parasympathetic** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Somatic = α motor, ACh, excitatory; autonomic = excite or inhibit.
- Preganglionic = B, ACh; postganglionic sympathetic = C, ACh or NE.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe somatic nerve fibers.</summary>

**Answer:** Alpha motor fibers to skeletal muscle releasing acetylcholine; they always excite muscle, inhibition occurring centrally via interneurons.

**Why:** Slide "Somatic nerve fibers".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare preganglionic and postganglionic autonomic fibers.</summary>

**Answer:** Preganglionic: ACh, myelinated B, end on the postganglionic cell body; postganglionic: ACh (parasympathetic) or ACh/NE (sympathetic), unmyelinated C in sympathetics, end on the effector.

**Why:** Slide "Sympathetic and parasympathetic nerve fibers".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which autonomic fiber is longer in each division?</summary>

**Answer:** Sympathetic: the postganglionic fiber is longer; parasympathetic: the preganglionic fiber is longer.

**Why:** Same slide.
</details>

## 3. Erlanger-Gasser classification (A, B, C)

- **Erlanger and Gasser** recorded a **mixed nerve** with a **cathode ray oscilloscope** (compound spike) and found **diameter** is the main difference. **As diameter ↑: velocity ↑, response magnitude ↑, duration ↓, refractory period ↓.**

| Fiber | Function | Diameter (µm) | Velocity (m/s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aα** | **Proprioception, somatic motor** | **12-20** | **70-120** |
| **Aβ** | **Touch, pressure** | **5-12** | **30-70** |
| **Aγ** | **Motor to muscle spindles** | **3-6** | **15-30** |
| **Aδ** | **Pain, cold, touch** | **2-5** | **12-30** |
| **B** | **Preganglionic autonomic** | **< 3** | **3-15** |
| **C (dorsal root)** | **Pain, temperature, some mechanoreception** | **0.4-1.2** | **0.5-2** |
| **C (sympathetic)** | **Postganglionic sympathetic** | **0.3-1.3** | **0.7-2.3** |

- **A:** largest, **myelinated**, sensory and motor (sciatic, saphenous); **Aα, Aβ** afferent + efferent, **Aγ efferent**, **Aδ afferent.** **B:** myelinated, **preganglionic autonomic only.** **C:** smallest, **unmyelinated**, visceral/cutaneous and postganglionic sympathetic.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Bigger fiber = faster, shorter response and refractory period.
- A (myelinated, 4 subgroups), B (myelinated, preganglionic), C (unmyelinated, smallest).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How did Erlanger and Gasser classify nerve fibers?</summary>

**Answer:** By recording a mixed nerve with a cathode ray oscilloscope; they found diameter the main difference and defined A, B and C groups.

**Why:** Slide "Erlanger and Gasser's classification".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How do properties vary with fiber diameter?</summary>

**Answer:** Larger fibers conduct faster, with larger responses, shorter response duration and shorter refractory periods.

**Why:** Slide "Properties correlated with diameter".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give the functions and velocities of Aα, Aβ, Aγ, Aδ, B and C fibers.</summary>

**Answer:** Aα proprioception/motor 70-120; Aβ touch/pressure 30-70; Aγ muscle spindle motor 15-30; Aδ pain/cold/touch 12-30; B preganglionic autonomic 3-15; C pain/temperature and postganglionic sympathetic 0.5-2.3 m/s.

**Why:** Table of fiber types.
</details>

## 4. Susceptibility, numerical classification and neuropathies

| Susceptibility to | Most | Intermediate | Least |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hypoxia** | **B** | A | **C** |
| **Pressure** | **A** | B | **C** |
| **Local anesthetics** | **C** | B | **A** |

| Group | Origin | Fiber type |
|---|---|---|
| **Ia** | **Muscle spindle (annulospiral)** | **Aα** |
| **Ib** | **Golgi tendon organ** | **Aα** |
| **II** | **Muscle spindle (flower-spray), touch, pressure** | **Aβ** |
| **III** | **Pain and cold, some touch** | **Aδ** |
| **IV** | **Pain, temperature, others** | **Dorsal root C** |

- **Peripheral neuropathy** = damage to peripheral nerves (disease or trauma). **Mononeuropathy:** one nerve, usually **compression.** **Mononeuritis multiplex:** **asymmetric**, non-contiguous nerves. **Polyneuropathy:** **symmetric**, more serious. **Autonomic neuropathy:** involves autonomic nerves, commonly **long-standing diabetes.** **Neuritis:** inflammation of a nerve.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hypoxia: B most; pressure: A most; local anesthetics: C most.
- Ia/Ib = Aα; II = Aβ; III = Aδ; IV = C.
- Mononeuropathy = one nerve; polyneuropathy = symmetric; autonomic neuropathy = diabetes.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which fibers are most susceptible to hypoxia, pressure and local anesthetics?</summary>

**Answer:** Hypoxia: B; pressure: A; local anesthetics: C (least: C, C and A respectively).

**Why:** Slide "Facts about the nerve fibers".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give the numerical classification of sensory fibers.</summary>

**Answer:** Ia (muscle spindle, Aα), Ib (Golgi tendon organ, Aα), II (muscle spindle, touch, pressure, Aβ), III (pain, cold, Aδ) and IV (pain, temperature, C).

**Why:** Slide "Numerical classification".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Distinguish mononeuropathy, mononeuritis multiplex and polyneuropathy.</summary>

**Answer:** Mononeuropathy affects one nerve (often by compression); mononeuritis multiplex affects several non-contiguous nerves asymmetrically; polyneuropathy is a symmetric, more widespread, more serious pattern.

**Why:** Slides "Peripheral neuropathy".
</details>
