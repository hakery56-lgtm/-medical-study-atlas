## 1. Vertebrae, discs, joints and ligaments

- ⭐ **33 vertebrae:** **7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 fused sacral, 4 fused coccygeal.** Column length: **vertebrae 4/5, discs 1/5.**
- **Vertebra = body + vertebral arch** (**pedicles, laminae, spinous process, transverse processes, superior and inferior articular processes**); pedicles bear the **superior and inferior vertebral notches.**
- ⭐ **Intervertebral disc:** **nucleus pulposus (inner gelatinous core) + annulus fibrosus (collagen fibers and fibrocartilage).**
- ⭐ **Articulated column:** **intervertebral foramina, joints, vertebral canal.** **Foraminal narrowing/stenosis compresses the spinal nerve → shooting (radiating) pain along its dermatome.**
- **Joints:** **intervertebral = bodies, secondary cartilaginous (symphyses); zygapophyseal (facet) = articular processes, synovial; craniovertebral = 5 joints: atlanto-occipital + 3 atlanto-axial (two lateral, one medial).**
- ⭐ **Five ligaments:** **anterior longitudinal, posterior longitudinal, ligamentum flavum, interspinous, supraspinous.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 7 C, 12 T, 5 L, 5 S (fused), 4 Co (fused) = 33.
- Disc = nucleus pulposus + annulus fibrosus.
- Facet joints are synovial; intervertebral joints are symphyses.

</div>

## 2. Regional vertebrae, sacrum and coccyx

| Region | Distinguishing features |
|---|---|
| **Cervical** | **Bifid spinous process; transverse foramina; triangular vertebral foramen** |
| **Atlas (C1)** | **No body and no spinous process; lateral masses, anterior and posterior arches and tubercles** |
| **Axis (C2)** | **Dens (odontoid process)** |
| **C7** | **Longer, non-bifid spinous process** |
| **Thoracic** | **Demi-facets on the bodies; costal facets on transverse processes; heart-shaped body; long, inferiorly slanting spinous processes** |
| **Lumbar** | **Short broad spinous processes; large kidney-shaped bodies (largest); no transverse foramen, costal facets or bifid spine; triangular vertebral foramen** |
| **Sacrum** | **Curved triangle of 5 fused vertebrae; fixes the column to the pelvis, stabilizes the pelvic girdle** |
| **Coccyx** | **4 (sometimes 3 or 5) fused vertebrae; attachment of pelvic floor muscles and part of gluteus maximus** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cervical: bifid spine + transverse foramina; thoracic: costal facets; lumbar: kidney-shaped body.
- Atlas has no body; axis has the dens.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. State the distinguishing features of cervical vertebrae.</summary>

**Answer:** Bifid spinous process, transverse foramina and a triangular vertebral foramen; C1 lacks a body and spinous process, C2 has the dens and C7 has a longer non-bifid spine.

**Why:** Slides "Cervical vertebra".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. State the distinguishing features of thoracic and lumbar vertebrae.</summary>

**Answer:** Thoracic: demi-facets, costal facets on transverse processes, heart-shaped body, long inferiorly slanting spines; lumbar: short broad spines, large kidney-shaped bodies, no costal facets or transverse foramina and a triangular foramen.

**Why:** Slides "Thoracic vertebrae" and "Lumbar vertebrae".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the sacrum and coccyx.</summary>

**Answer:** The sacrum is a curved triangle of 5 fused vertebrae anchoring the column to the pelvis; the coccyx is 4 (3-5) fused vertebrae giving attachment to pelvic floor muscles and part of gluteus maximus.

**Why:** Slides "Sacrum" and "Coccyx".
</details>

## 3. Curvatures

- ⭐ **Primary (forward concavity): thoracic and sacral.** **Secondary (forward convexity): cervical and lumbar.**
- **Cervical** curvature develops when the child holds the head up (**~3 months**). **Lumbar** curvature develops when the child **sits (~6-7 months)** and is more marked on **walking.**
- ⭐ **Kyphosis:** **exaggerated thoracic concavity;** predisposed by **osteoporosis and disc degeneration** in old age. **Lordosis:** **exaggerated lumbar curve;** **increased in pregnancy.** **Scoliosis:** **lateral curvature;** from vertebral maldevelopment (**hemivertebra**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Primary = thoracic, sacral; secondary = cervical, lumbar.
- Kyphosis (thoracic), lordosis (lumbar), scoliosis (lateral).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Classify the curvatures of the column.</summary>

**Answer:** Primary curvatures with forward concavity: thoracic and sacral; secondary curvatures with forward convexity: cervical and lumbar.

**Why:** Slide "Curvatures".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. When do the secondary curvatures develop?</summary>

**Answer:** The cervical curve when the child holds the head upright (about 3 months); the lumbar curve when the child sits (6-7 months), becoming more marked with walking.

**Why:** Slide "Curvatures".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Define kyphosis, lordosis and scoliosis.</summary>

**Answer:** Kyphosis: exaggerated thoracic concavity; lordosis: exaggerated lumbar curve (increased in pregnancy); scoliosis: lateral curvature from vertebral maldevelopment such as a hemivertebra.

**Why:** Slides "Abnormal curvatures".
</details>

## 4. Radiological anatomy

- **Cervical X-ray (lateral):** **vertebrae 1-7, intervertebral (neural) foramina, facet joints (parallel lines), laminae (parallel lines);** axial MRI shows **adenoid and C1.**
- **Lumbar X-ray landmarks:** **body, pedicles, laminae, vertebral foramen, intervertebral foramina, spinous and transverse processes, superior and inferior articular facets, pars interarticularis;** views: **frontal, lateral, oblique.**
- ⭐ **"Scotty dog" (oblique lumbar view):** **transverse process = nose; pedicle = eye; superior articular facet = ear; inferior articular facet = front leg; lamina = body; spinous process = hind leg; pars interarticularis = neck.**
- **MRI and age:** **discs become darker, lose height and bulge** (30 vs 70 vs 90 years). **Normal lumbar MRI:** vertebral bodies, intervertebral discs, spinal canal; **herniated disc compresses nerves.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Scotty dog: nose = transverse process, eye = pedicle, ear = superior facet, neck = pars interarticularis.
- Disc degeneration: dark, thin, bulging.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the Scotty dog sign.</summary>

**Answer:** On an oblique lumbar X-ray: transverse process is the nose, pedicle the eye, superior articular facet the ear, inferior articular facet the front leg, lamina the body, spinous process the hind leg and pars interarticularis the neck.

**Why:** Slide "Look for the Scotty dog".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which structures are identified on a lateral cervical X-ray?</summary>

**Answer:** Vertebral bodies 1-7, intervertebral (neural) foramina, facet joints and laminae.

**Why:** Slide "Anatomy of cervical X-ray".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do discs look on MRI with ageing?</summary>

**Answer:** Darker, reduced in vertical height and bulging.

**Why:** Slide "Age-related intervertebral disc degeneration".
</details>

## 5. Spinal cord, coverings and nerve exits

- ⭐ **Cord:** starts at the **upper border of C1;** ends at **L1-L2 disc (L3 in infants);** **31 segments: 8 C, 12 T, 5 L, 5 S, 1 Co.** **Segment = cord part giving a pair of spinal nerves.** **Segment levels do not match vertebral levels.**
- **Conus medullaris** = lower expanded end. **Cauda equina** = lower lumbar and sacral roots around the filum terminale, floating in **CSF of the lumbar cistern.** **Filum terminale** = pial extension attached to the **back of the coccyx.** **Lumbar cistern** = subarachnoid space **L2-S2** (safer site for lumbar puncture). **Terminal ventricle** = lower end of the central canal. **Denticulate ligaments** = lateral pial extensions attached to the canal.
- ⭐ **Canal contents:** **to the lower border of L1: cord with 3 meninges and 3 spaces;** **L1 to S2: cauda equina, arachnoid, dura;** **below S2: filum terminale + S5 and coccygeal roots (exit via sacral hiatus).**
- **Spaces:** **epidural (outside dura); subdural (dura-arachnoid); subarachnoid (arachnoid-pia, CSF).**
- ⭐ **Nerve exits:** **C1-C7 above the vertebra; C8-L5 below; S1-S4 via sacral foramina; S5 + coccygeal via sacral hiatus.**
- **Sensory pathways:** **posterior funiculus (uncrossed): conscious proprioception, tactile localization, discrimination, stereognosis; lateral and anterior spinothalamic tracts (crossed).** **Three neurons: 1st = dorsal root ganglion; 2nd = posterior horn or medulla (by modality); 3rd = thalamus.**
- **Cord section labels:** **arachnoid mater, dorsal (sensory) root, dorsal root ganglion, ventral (motor) root, mixed spinal nerve;** gray matter (anterior and posterior horns, commissure); white matter funiculi; central canal.
- **Lumbar puncture:** needle between **L3/4 and L4/5** (around the L4 spinous process).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cord ends L1-L2 (L3 in infants); lumbar cistern L2-S2 for lumbar puncture.
- C1-C7 exit above; C8 onward exit below the vertebra.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the extent of the cord and what lies below it.</summary>

**Answer:** The cord runs from the upper border of C1 to the L1-L2 disc (L3 in infants); below it lie the conus medullaris, cauda equina in the lumbar cistern (L2-S2), and the filum terminale attached to the coccyx.

**Why:** Slides "Spinal cord".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Name the three spaces around the cord.</summary>

**Answer:** Epidural (outside the dura), subdural (between dura and arachnoid) and subarachnoid (between arachnoid and pia, containing CSF).

**Why:** Slide "Spinal cord" (spaces).
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Where do the spinal nerves exit?</summary>

**Answer:** C1-C7 above the corresponding vertebra; C8-L5 below it; S1-S4 through the sacral foramina; S5 and the coccygeal nerve via the sacral hiatus.

**Why:** Slide "Exit of spinal nerves".
</details>

## 6. Intervertebral disc herniation

- **Relations:** **lumbar disc posteriorly: exiting root (laterally), traversing root (posterolateral), cauda equina (posteriorly).** **Cervical disc posteriorly: exiting root (posterolateral), spinal cord (posteriorly).**
- ⭐ **Level:** **95% in the lumbosacral spine (L4-L5 or L5-S1);** **cervical: most frequently C6-C7 (also C5-C6).**
- **Cervical herniation:** **posterolateral → root compression (neck pain radiating to the ipsilateral arm, numbness, paresthesia, weakness); central → cord compression.** **A bone spur or disc herniation in the neural foramen impinges the nerve there.**
- ⭐ **Lumbar example L4-5:** **exiting = L4; traversing = L5.**

| Direction | Affects |
|---|---|
| **Posterolateral** | **Traversing nerve root** |
| **Foraminal (lateral)** (e.g. L4-L5 far lateral, ~10%) | **Exiting nerve root (L4)** |
| **Central** | **Cauda equina: rare, most dangerous; back pain, bladder/bowel symptoms, saddle anaesthesia, perineal numbness; MRI and surgery within 48 h** |

- **Lab review questions** (picture-based, no answers on the slides) cover earlier labs: **shoulder dislocation, clavicle and humerus fractures, fall on outstretched hand, carpal tunnel, snuffbox, hand X-ray age, rheumatoid tendon rupture, quick motor and sensory testing of the hand.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Posterolateral → traversing root; foraminal → exiting root; central → cauda equina.
- Central herniation: emergency (MRI, surgery within 48 h).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What are the three directions of lumbar disc herniation?</summary>

**Answer:** Posterolateral (affects the traversing root), foraminal or lateral (affects the exiting root) and central (affects the cauda equina).

**Why:** Slides "Direction of herniation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which nerves relate to the L4-L5 disc?</summary>

**Answer:** The exiting nerve is L4 and the traversing nerve is L5.

**Why:** Slide "Lumbar IVD related to 2 nerves".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe central disc herniation.</summary>

**Answer:** It affects the cauda equina; rare and the most dangerous, causing back pain, bladder and bowel symptoms, saddle anaesthesia and perineal numbness; MRI and surgery within 48 hours improve bladder and motor recovery.

**Why:** Slide "Central IVD herniation".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Cervical spinal nerves C1-C7 exit ABOVE their vertebra; C8 onward exit BELOW.**
- **Cord ends L1-L2 (L3 in infants); lumbar cistern L2-S2.**
- **Posterolateral herniation = traversing root; foraminal = exiting root.**
- **Primary curvatures = thoracic and sacral (concave forward).**
- **Costal facets = thoracic; no transverse foramen = lumbar.**
