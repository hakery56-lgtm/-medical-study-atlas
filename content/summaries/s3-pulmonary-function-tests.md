# Pulmonary Function Tests — Exam Night Review

A student's guide (adapted from the ATS Patient Education Series, 2024) to **pulmonary function tests: spirometry, diffusion studies and body plethysmography, why they are ordered, how each is done, how to prepare, and what "predicted values" mean.** After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer).

---

## 1. What PFTs are and why they are done

- ⭐ **PFTs** = breathing tests that show **how well you move air in and out of your lungs**.

| Test | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **Spirometry** | **How much air you can breathe in and how fast you can blow it out** |
| **Diffusion studies** | **How well air transfers across the lungs into the blood** |
| **Body plethysmography** | **How much air the lungs hold and how much is left after breathing out** |

- Sometimes only one or two tests are needed; sometimes all three on the same day.
- **Why done:** compare your lungs with **expected values**; measure effects of **asthma, COPD, cystic fibrosis**; catch **early changes** needing new treatment; detect **airway narrowing**; decide if a **medicine could help**; check whether **home or work exposures** harmed the lungs; judge ability to **tolerate surgery** and procedures.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Three tests: spirometry (flow), diffusion (gas transfer), plethysmography (volumes).
- Used for asthma, COPD, cystic fibrosis, exposures and pre-surgery assessment.

</div>

## 2. Spirometry

- **Most commonly ordered** lung function test; ~**30 minutes**; **at least 3 blows** to find the best result.
- **Steps:** (1) **nose clips**, lips sealed on the **mouthpiece**; (2) **deep breath in**; (3) **blow out all the air as fast as you can**; (4) **rest and repeat ≥ 3 times**; (5) may **repeat after a bronchodilator** to see improvement.
- **Curve:** air leaves **fast at first, then levels off** as the lungs empty.
- **Flow-volume loops:**

| Pattern | Volume-time curve | PEF |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | Normal plateau at FVC | Normal |
| **Obstruction** | **Late plateau, FVC preserved**; scooped (coved) expiratory limb | **Mildly reduced** |
| **Restriction** | **Normal plateau, FVC reduced** (smaller loop) | **Normal or mildly reduced** |

- **Before:** may be asked **not to take breathing medicines**. **During:** effort needed, **tiredness expected**; if **dizzy or light-headed, stop blowing and tell staff**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- ≥ 3 blows, ~30 min, bronchodilator repeat optional.
- Obstruction: FVC preserved, late plateau; restriction: FVC reduced.

</div>

## 3. Diffusion studies

- Measure **how well air crosses the lungs into the blood**, using a **safe low level of carbon monoxide** that crosses from the air sac (alveolus) into the blood vessel.
- **10 s breath hold**; **repeated ≤ 5 times**; **~30 minutes** in total.
- **Steps:** (1) **breathe out** gently as much as you can; (2) **quick deep breath in** containing the low-level carbon monoxide; (3) **hold 10 seconds**; (4) **breathe out** as instructed; **repeat** with a few minutes between tests.
- **Before:** **no smoking** and avoid smokers that day; if on **oxygen**, come off it for a few minutes first.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Carbon monoxide, 10-second hold, up to 5 repeats, ~30 minutes.
- No smoking that day; off oxygen beforehand.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What do diffusion studies measure and what gas is used?</summary>

**Answer:** How well air transfers across the lungs into the blood, using a safe low level of carbon monoxide.

**Why:** Slide "What are diffusion studies?".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the steps of a diffusion test.</summary>

**Answer:** Breathe out gently, take a quick deep breath containing low-level carbon monoxide, hold for 10 seconds, breathe out as instructed, and repeat (usually at most five times).

**Why:** Slide "Diffusion test: step by step".

## 4. Body plethysmography

- Done sitting in a **see-through box (like a telephone booth)** with a **nose clip**; **~15 minutes**.
- Measures **total air in the lungs and air left after breathing out**.
- **Steps:** (1) sit in the box with a nose clip; (2) follow instructions for breathing through the mouthpiece; (3) **short, shallow breaths**; the mouthpiece is **blocked for a few seconds** (may be uncomfortable).
- **Oxygen:** usually **off oxygen** during the test. **Tell staff** if you have **difficulty in closed spaces** or **recent surgery or heart attack**.
- Some laboratories use **other tests** instead to measure total lung volume.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- See-through box, short shallow breaths, ~15 minutes.
- Report claustrophobia, recent surgery or heart attack.

</div>

## 5. Preparation, predicted values and key terms

- **Prepare:** **dress loosely**; ask which **medicines to skip** and for how long.
- **Tell the staff:** **cold or flu** (may reschedule), **claustrophobia**, **recent eye, sinus, ear, chest or abdominal surgery or heart attack** (tell ordering provider), **medicines**, **oxygen use**, and **dizziness during a test**.
- **Predicted values** = based on **height, age and sex at birth**; **taller people and males** have **larger lungs**; **shorter people and females** smaller; lung function **falls slightly with age**; measured values are compared with predicted.
- **Key terms:** **spirometry**, **spirometer** (machine on the mouthpiece), **diffusion studies**, **body plethysmography**, **bronchodilator**, **predicted values**.
- **Quiz facts:** avoid alcohol **at least 8 hours** before; diffusion hold **10 seconds**; plethysmography = **see-through box**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Predicted values = height + age + sex at birth.
- Tell staff about cold/flu, claustrophobia, recent surgery/heart attack, medicines and oxygen.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Expected numbers based on your height, age and sex at birth; the measured values are compared with them.

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Spirometry = flow (how fast); diffusion = gas transfer (carbon monoxide); plethysmography = lung volume (box).**
- **Obstruction keeps FVC; restriction reduces FVC.**
- **Diffusion: 10-second hold, ≤ 5 repeats.**
- **Plethysmography: short shallow breaths, not deep blows.**
- **Predicted values depend on height, age and sex at birth, not weight.**
- **If dizzy while blowing: stop and tell the staff.**

## 🔥 Final review — memorize before the exam

1. PFTs: spirometry, diffusion studies, body plethysmography.
2. Spirometry: ~30 min, ≥ 3 blows, deep breath then fast blow, bronchodilator repeat.
3. Obstruction: late plateau, FVC preserved; restriction: FVC reduced.
4. Diffusion: low-level carbon monoxide, 10-second hold, ≤ 5 repeats, ~30 min.
5. Plethysmography: see-through box, nose clip, short shallow breaths, ~15 min.
6. Tell staff: cold/flu, closed spaces, recent surgery or heart attack, medicines, oxygen.
7. Predicted values = height + age + sex at birth.
