## 1. The dermis and skin circulation

- ⭐ **Dermis** = connective tissue supporting the epidermis and binding it to the subcutaneous tissue; **max 4 mm on the back**; irregular surface with **dermal papillae** interdigitating with **epidermal pegs/ridges**; **basement membrane** between stratum basale and dermis.
- **Two sublayers (indistinct boundary):** **papillary** (thin, **loose** connective tissue, includes papillae) and **reticular** (thicker, **irregular dense** connective tissue, **more fibers, fewer cells**).
- **Bullous pemphigoid:** blistering due to abnormalities of the **dermal-epidermal junction.**
- **Circulation:** **nutritive + thermoregulatory** (**arteriovenous anastomoses**). **Subpapillary plexus** (between papillary and reticular layers; capillaries into papillae) and **deep (cutaneous) plexus** (larger blood and lymphatic vessels near dermis-subcutaneous interface).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Papillary = loose; reticular = dense irregular.
- Two plexuses: subpapillary and cutaneous.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the papillary and reticular layers.</summary>

**Answer:** The papillary layer is thin loose connective tissue including the dermal papillae; the reticular layer is thicker irregular dense connective tissue with more fibers and fewer cells.

**Why:** Slide 3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the skin circulation.</summary>

**Answer:** It is nutritive and thermoregulatory (arteriovenous shunts) with a subpapillary plexus feeding capillaries in the papillae and a deep cutaneous plexus with larger blood and lymphatic vessels.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is bullous pemphigoid?</summary>

**Answer:** A blistering disorder due to abnormalities of the dermal-epidermal junction.

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>

## 2. Hair and the hair follicle

- ⭐ **Hairs** = elongated keratinized structures from **invaginations of the epidermis (hair follicles)**; color, size, shape, texture vary with **age, genetics, region.**
- **Hairless:** **palms, soles, lips, glans penis, clitoris, labia minora.** **Density:** face **≈ 600/cm2**, rest of body **≈ 60/cm2.**
- **Hair bulb** = terminal dilatation; **dermal papilla** with capillaries sustains it (**loss of blood flow kills the hair**); epidermal cells over the papilla = **hair root.**
- **Arrector pili** = smooth muscle (midpoint of the fibrous sheath to the papillary layer) → **erects the hair.**
- **Layers:** **shaft** = medulla, cortex, cuticle; **internal and external root sheaths** (continuous with epidermis); **glassy membrane** (continuous with the epidermal basement membrane); **connective tissue sheath.**
- **Alopecia totalis and areata:** **autoimmune** attack on hair follicles.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Dermal papilla blood supply is essential for hair survival.
- Shaft = medulla, cortex, cuticle.
- Hairless sites: palms, soles, lips and some genital areas.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the hair follicle.</summary>

**Answer:** A bulb at its base receives a dermal papilla with a capillary network (loss of flow kills the hair); epidermal cells over it form the hair root; the arrector pili muscle erects the hair.

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the layers of a hair.</summary>

**Answer:** Medulla, cortex and cuticle (shaft), internal and external root sheaths, the glassy membrane and the connective tissue sheath.

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which skin areas lack hair?</summary>

**Answer:** The palms, soles, lips, glans penis, clitoris and labia minora.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

## 3. Sebaceous glands

- ⭐ **Holocrine, branched acinar glands**; several acini converge on a **short duct opening into the upper hair follicle.**
- In the dermis **everywhere except thick skin**; **100/cm2** on average, **400-900/cm2 on face and scalp.**
- **Sebum production:** basal flattened cells proliferate → move to the middle → become large **lipid-producing sebocytes** → nuclei shrink and undergo **autophagy** → near the duct they **disintegrate and release lipids.**
- **Sebum:** maintains the **stratum corneum and hair shafts**; **weak antibacterial and antifungal** action.
- **Acne:** chronic inflammation of **obstructed** glands from disturbed sebum flow.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Holocrine secretion = the whole cell disintegrates.
- Absent in thick skin; densest on face and scalp.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the sebaceous glands.</summary>

**Answer:** Holocrine branched acinar glands whose acini converge on a short duct emptying into the upper hair follicle; present in the dermis except in thick skin, densest on the face and scalp.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How is sebum formed?</summary>

**Answer:** Basal cells become large lipid-producing sebocytes, shrink their nuclei, and near the duct disintegrate and release lipids.

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the function of sebum and what is acne?</summary>

**Answer:** Sebum maintains the stratum corneum and hair and is weakly antibacterial and antifungal; acne is chronic inflammation of obstructed sebaceous glands.

**Why:** Slides 11-12.
</details>

## 4. Sweat glands

- ⭐ **Epidermal derivatives in the dermis:** **eccrine** (open on the **skin surface**) and **apocrine** (open into **hair follicles**).

| Feature | Eccrine | Apocrine |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | **Simple coiled tubular**, small lumen | **Much larger lumen**; simple cuboidal **eosinophilic** cells |
| **Distribution** | **Widespread**, most on **soles (620/cm2)**; **3 million**, mass of a kidney, up to **10 L/day** | **Axillary and perineal** |
| **Development** | From birth | **Sex-hormone dependent; functional after puberty** |
| **Secretion** | Watery | **Slightly viscous, odorless until bacteria act** |

- **Eccrine secretory cells:** **clear (pale) cells** make sweat; **dark cells** (eosinophilic granules) have **bactericidal activity**; **myoepithelial cells** contract to move secretion. **Ducts:** two layers of stratified cuboidal epithelium.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Eccrine to the surface; apocrine to hair follicles.
- Clear cells produce sweat; myoepithelial cells propel it.
- Apocrine odor is from bacteria.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare eccrine and apocrine glands.</summary>

**Answer:** Eccrine glands open on the surface, are widespread, simple coiled tubular with small lumens; apocrine glands open into hair follicles, are confined to axilla and perineum, have larger lumens and function after puberty.

**Why:** Slides 13-16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Name the cells of the eccrine secretory portion.</summary>

**Answer:** Clear cells (produce sweat), dark cells (bactericidal granules) and myoepithelial cells (contract to move secretion).

**Why:** Slide 15.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give the quantitative facts of eccrine glands.</summary>

**Answer:** About 3 million glands (kidney mass), up to 10 L/day, and 620/cm2 on the soles.

**Why:** Slide 14.
</details>

## 5. Nails

- ⭐ **Hard keratin plates** on the dorsal surface of each **distal phalanx.**
- **Nail root** (proximal) is covered by a skin fold; the **stratum corneum extends as the cuticle (eponychium).**
- **Nail bed** under the plate has **only basal and spinous layers.** The **nail matrix** forms the root: cells divide, move distally and keratinize.
- **Clinical window:** transparent plate and thin bed show **blood color in the vascular dermis** → **blood oxygen.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Nail bed = basal + spinous layers only.
- Matrix produces the nail.
- Nail color reflects blood oxygenation.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the nail.</summary>

**Answer:** A hard keratin plate on the dorsal distal phalanx, with a root covered by a skin fold (cuticle or eponychium) and a nail bed of basal and spinous layers; the root forms from the nail matrix.

**Why:** Slide 17.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What does the nail bed contain?</summary>

**Answer:** Only the basal and spinous epidermal layers.

**Why:** Slide 17.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why are nails clinically useful?</summary>

**Answer:** The transparent plate shows the color of blood in the vascular dermis of the nail bed, indicating blood oxygenation.

**Why:** Slide 17.
</details>

## 6. Sensory receptors of the skin

| Receptor | Location and function |
|---|---|
| **Tactile (Merkel) discs** | **Unencapsulated**; epidermal; **light touch** |
| **Free nerve endings** | **Unencapsulated**; papillary dermis into lower epidermis; **temperature, pain, itching**, also touch |
| **Root hair plexuses** | **Unencapsulated**; around follicle bases; **hair movement** |
| **Meissner corpuscles** | **Encapsulated**; dermal papillae of **fingertips, palms, soles**; **light touch** |
| **Pacinian corpuscles** | **Encapsulated**; **deep reticular dermis or hypodermis**; ~0.5 × 1 mm, **15-50 lamellae** around an unmyelinated axon; **coarse touch, pressure, vibration** |
| **Krause and Ruffini** | **Encapsulated** pressure-sensing mechanoreceptors, poorly characterized |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Meissner = light touch (papillae); Pacinian = pressure and vibration (deep).
- Free endings = temperature, pain, itch.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the unencapsulated receptors.</summary>

**Answer:** Merkel tactile discs, free nerve endings and root hair plexuses.

**Why:** Slide 18.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare Meissner and Pacinian corpuscles.</summary>

**Answer:** Meissner corpuscles lie in dermal papillae of fingertips, palms and soles and detect light touch; Pacinian corpuscles lie deep in the reticular dermis or hypodermis, have 15-50 lamellae and sense coarse touch, pressure and vibration.

**Why:** Slide 19.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What do free nerve endings detect?</summary>

**Answer:** High and low temperatures, pain and itching, and also touch.

**Why:** Slide 18.
</details>

## 7. Aging skin

- **Epidermis thins and becomes transparent**; **fewer melanocytes.**
- **Connective tissue:** **thickened collagen fibers, less collagen synthesis, loss of elastic fibers** → less strength and elasticity.
- **Fragile dermal blood vessels**; **less sebum**; **less sweat**; **fewer and shallower dermal papillae**; **fragile, less supple, wrinkled skin.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Aging reduces melanocytes, glands, collagen synthesis and elastin.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the changes of aging skin.</summary>

**Answer:** Thinner transparent epidermis, fewer melanocytes, altered collagen and elastic fiber loss, fragile vessels, less sebum and sweat, fewer dermal papillae and wrinkling.

**Why:** Slide "Aging skin".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why does aging skin lose strength?</summary>

**Answer:** Collagen fibers thicken with less collagen synthesis and elastic fibers are lost.

**Why:** Slide "Aging skin".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What happens to the dermal papillae with age?</summary>

**Answer:** They decrease in number and depth.

**Why:** Slide "Aging skin".
</details>
