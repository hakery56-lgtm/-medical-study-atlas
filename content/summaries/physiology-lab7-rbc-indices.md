## 1. RBC indices and MCV

- **RBC indices** are part of the **complete blood count (CBC)** and give information about the **hemoglobin content and size** of red cells. **Abnormal values indicate anemia and its type**; used for the **classification of anemia**.
- Three indices: **MCV, MCH, MCHC** - derived from the **hematocrit (PCV), hemoglobin and RBC count** by formulas.
- ⭐ **MCV** = average **volume (size)** of a red cell; measured by the automated analyzer or **MCV = PCV (%) / RBC count (million/mm³) × 10**; unit **femtoliters (fl)**; **normal 80-100 fl**.
- Example: PCV 45%, RBC 5 million/mm³ → MCV = 45/5 × 10 = **90 fl**.

| MCV | Cell size | Anemia | Causes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal (80-100 fl)** | Normal | **Normocytic** | Sudden blood loss, kidney failure, hemolytic anemia |
| **High (> 100 fl)** | Larger | **Macrocytic** | Folate deficiency, vitamin B12 deficiency, chemotherapy |
| **Low (< 80 fl)** | Smaller | **Microcytic** | Iron deficiency, lead poisoning, thalassemia |

- You can have a **normal MCV and still be anemic** if there are too few red cells or other indices are abnormal.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- MCV = PCV / RBC count × 10 (fl); normal 80-100 fl.
- High MCV = macrocytic (folate/B12 deficiency, chemotherapy); low MCV = microcytic (iron deficiency, lead, thalassemia).
- Normal MCV = normocytic (blood loss, kidney failure, hemolysis).

</div>

## 2. MCH

- ⭐ **MCH** = average **amount (weight) of hemoglobin in a red cell**; **MCH = Hb (g/dl) / RBC count (million/mm³) × 10**; unit **picograms (pg)**; **normal 27-33 pg**.
- Example: Hb 15 g/dl, RBC 5 million/mm³ → MCH = 15/5 × 10 = **30 pg**.
- **Macrocytic** cells → **higher MCH** (larger, more Hb); **microcytic** cells → **lower MCH** (smaller, less Hb).
- MCH expresses Hb amount **per cell without taking size into account**, so it is **less useful than MCHC** in classifying anemia.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- MCH = Hb / RBC count × 10 (pg); normal 27-33 pg.
- High in macrocytic, low in microcytic cells.
- Ignores cell size, so less useful than MCHC.

</div>

**Why:** Slide "Mean corpuscular hemoglobin (MCH)".
</details>

## 3. MCHC

- ⭐ **MCHC** = average **concentration of hemoglobin per unit volume of red cells**; **MCHC = Hb (g/dl) / PCV (%) × 100**; unit **% (g/dl)**; **normal 30-37 %**.
- Example: Hb 15, PCV 45 → MCHC = 15/45 × 100 = **33 g%**.
- **Normal** → **normochromic**; **below normal** → **hypochromic** (low Hb concentration; cells **lighter in color**). Causes of low MCHC = those of low MCV: **iron deficiency, lead poisoning, thalassemia**.
- **Low MCV + low MCHC** are generally found together = **microcytic hypochromic anemia**.
- **High MCHC** (seen in **hereditary spherocytosis, sickle cell anemia**) - ⭐ **there is no hyperchromic anemia**; in spherocytosis the MCHC rises because of **loss of membrane and the spherical shape** of the cell.
- Note: the slide states that there is no hyperchromic anemia, yet lists conditions with an elevated MCHC; keep the slide wording ("hyperchromia" is not a true anemia class here).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- MCHC = Hb / PCV × 100 (%); normal 30-37 %.
- Low MCHC = hypochromic (iron deficiency, lead, thalassemia); high MCHC in spherocytosis and sickle cell anemia.
- No hyperchromic anemia; spherocytosis raises MCHC through membrane loss.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** The average concentration of hemoglobin per unit volume of red cells; MCHC = Hb (g/dl) / PCV (%) × 100, in %; normal 30-37 %.

**Why:** Slide "Mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC)".

**Why:** Slide on high MCHC.
</details>

## 4. Indices in common anemias and cases

| Anemia | MCV | MCH | MCHC |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normocytic normochromic** (e.g. **hemolytic anemia**) | Normal | Normal | Normal |
| **Microcytic hypochromic** (e.g. **iron deficiency anemia**) | Decrease | Decrease | Decrease |
| **Macrocytic normochromic** (e.g. **megaloblastic / vitamin B12 deficiency anemia**) | Increase | Increase | Normal |

| Case | Hb | MCV | MCH | MCHC | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 11 g/dl | 87 fl | 30 pg | 33 % | **Normocytic normochromic** (e.g. hemolytic) |
| **2** | 9 g/dl | 115 fl | 35 pg | 30 % | **Macrocytic normochromic** (e.g. megaloblastic) |
| **3** | 8 g/dl | 62 fl | 19 pg | 27 % | **Microcytic hypochromic** (e.g. iron deficiency) |

- Note: the case slides give Hb reference "13-18 g/dl" and RBC "< 4.5 million/mm³", and the case values are illustrative (they do not all follow the formulas exactly); learn the pattern of the indices rather than recalculating them.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Normal/normal/normal = normocytic normochromic (hemolytic).
- All decreased = microcytic hypochromic (iron deficiency).
- High MCV and MCH with normal MCHC = macrocytic normochromic (megaloblastic).

</div>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **MCV uses PCV ÷ RBC × 10 (fl); MCH uses Hb ÷ RBC × 10 (pg); MCHC uses Hb ÷ PCV × 100 (%).**
- **Normal MCV does not exclude anemia.**
- **Megaloblastic anemia = macrocytic NORMOchromic (MCHC normal).**
- **"There are no hyperchromic anemias" despite a raised MCHC in spherocytosis.**
- **MCH is less useful than MCHC for classification.**

## 🔥 Final review — memorize before the exam

1. Normal ranges: MCV 80-100 fl; MCH 27-33 pg; MCHC 30-37 %.
2. MCV = PCV / RBC count × 10 (example 45/5 × 10 = 90 fl).
3. MCH = Hb / RBC count × 10 (example 15/5 × 10 = 30 pg).
4. MCHC = Hb / PCV × 100 (example 15/45 × 100 = 33 g%).
5. Macrocytic: folate/B12 deficiency, chemotherapy.
6. Microcytic: iron deficiency, lead poisoning, thalassemia.
7. Normocytic: sudden blood loss, kidney failure, hemolytic anemia.
8. Low MCV + low MCHC = microcytic hypochromic anemia.
9. High MCHC: hereditary spherocytosis, sickle cell anemia.
10. Hemolytic = normo/normo; iron deficiency = micro/hypo; megaloblastic = macro/normo.
