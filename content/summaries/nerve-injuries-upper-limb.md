## 1. Median nerve: course and branches

- **Root:** **C5, C6, C7, C8, T1**; **origin:** **medial and lateral cords** of the brachial plexus.
- **No branches in the axilla or the arm**; branches begin in the **forearm**.
- **Forearm, motor:** **all the muscles of the flexor compartment except 1½**: **flexor carpi ulnaris and the medial part of FDP**. **Sensory:** **palmar cutaneous branch** → skin over the **thenar eminence**.
- ⭐ **Palm, motor — recurrent branch (LOAF):** **L**ateral two lumbricals (the first two), **O**pponens pollicis, **A**bductor pollicis brevis, **F**lexor pollicis brevis.
- **Palm, sensory:** **palmar surface of the lateral 3½ fingers** and the **dorsal surface of the distal phalanx of the lateral 3½ fingers**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Median nerve: C5–T1; no branches in the axilla and arm.
- Forearm flexors except FCU and the medial half of FDP; palmar cutaneous branch to the thenar skin.
- Palm: LOAF + lateral 3½ fingers.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the branches of the median nerve in the forearm and palm.</summary>

**Answer:** Forearm: motor to all the flexor compartment muscles except flexor carpi ulnaris and the medial part of FDP, and the palmar cutaneous branch to the skin over the thenar eminence. Palm: the recurrent branch (abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis, opponens pollicis and the first two lumbricals) and sensory branches to the palmar surface of the lateral 3½ fingers and the dorsal surface of their distal phalanges.

**Why:** Slides "Median nerve" and "Branches in the palm".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the root value and origin of the median nerve?</summary>

**Answer:** C5, C6, C7, C8 and T1; it arises from the medial and lateral cords of the brachial plexus.

**Why:** Slide "Median nerve".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What does LOAF stand for?</summary>

**Answer:** Lateral two lumbricals, Opponens pollicis, Abductor pollicis brevis and Flexor pollicis brevis, all supplied by the recurrent branch of the median nerve.

**Why:** Slide "Branches in the palm".
</details>

## 2. Median nerve injury and carpal tunnel syndrome

- **Common sites:** **elbow** (supracondylar fracture of the humerus); **wrist** (Colles' fracture, stab wound, **carpal tunnel syndrome**).

| Deformity / sign | Description | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Injury at the elbow** | Forearm in **supination**, **weak flexion**, wrist flexion with wrist adduction | **Pronators paralyzed**, long flexors paralyzed except 1½, **FCU unopposed by FCR** |
| **Benediction hand** (elbow) | Making a fist: **index and middle fingers stay straight**, ring and little flex weakly; MP flexion still attempted; **thumb distal phalanx flexion lost, OK sign negative** | **FDS and lateral half of FDP** paralyzed; **interossei spared** (ulnar); **FPL (AIN)** paralysis |
| **Ape-like hand** (elbow or wrist) | **Flattened thenar eminence**, **adducted thumb**, opposition and abduction impossible → **pincer action lost** | **Thenar muscle paralysis and wasting** (recurrent branch); **adductor pollicis unopposed** (ulnar) |
| **Sensory loss** (elbow or wrist) | Skin **warmer and drier** | **Arterial dilatation and absence of sweating** from loss of sympathetic control |

### Carpal tunnel syndrome
- **Compression** of the median nerve in the tunnel, tightly packed by the **flexor tendons with their synovial sheaths**.
- **Pain and tingling** along the sensory distribution **excluding the thenar eminence skin**, because the **palmar cutaneous branch passes superficial to the flexor retinaculum** and is not caught.
- Other signs and symptoms are like injury at the wrist.
- **Diagnosis:** **forced palmar flexion**, **tapping** over the tunnel, **EMG** (delayed conduction velocity), **ultrasound**.
- **Treatment:** **decompression through an incision along the flexor retinaculum** or **endoscopic release**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Elbow injury: benediction hand (FDS and lateral FDP lost), OK sign negative, ape hand, supinated forearm.
- Ape hand = thenar wasting + adducted thumb (adductor pollicis unopposed).
- CTS spares the thenar skin (palmar cutaneous branch); treat by releasing the flexor retinaculum.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the deformities of median nerve injury at the elbow and their causes.</summary>

**Answer:** The forearm lies in supination with weak flexion and wrist flexion with adduction (pronators and long flexors paralyzed, FCU unopposed by FCR); the benediction hand (index and middle fingers stay straight on making a fist, from paralysis of FDS and the lateral half of FDP, with a negative OK sign from FPL paralysis); and the ape-like hand (thenar wasting with an adducted thumb).

**Why:** Slides "Median nerve injury at elbow".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the ape-like hand and what causes it?</summary>

**Answer:** Flattening of the thenar eminence with adduction of the thumb; opposition and abduction are impossible so the pincer action is lost. It is caused by paralysis and wasting of the thenar muscles supplied by the recurrent branch (opponens pollicis, abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis) with adductor pollicis (ulnar) unopposed; it occurs in injury at the elbow or the wrist.

**Why:** Slide "Median nerve injury at elbow or at wrist — ape-like hand".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is the thenar skin spared in carpal tunnel syndrome, and how is it treated?</summary>

**Answer:** The palmar cutaneous branch passes superficial to the flexor retinaculum, so it is not caught by compression of the main nerve. Treatment is decompression of the tunnel by an incision along the flexor retinaculum or endoscopic release.

**Why:** Slides "Carpal tunnel syndrome".
</details>

## 3. Ulnar nerve: course, branches and interossei actions

- **Origin:** **medial cord**; **root C7, C8, T1**.
- **No branches in the axilla or arm**; passes **behind the medial epicondyle** into the forearm **between the two heads of FCU**.
- **Forearm:** **motor** to **1½ muscles** (**FCU + medial half of FDP**); **sensory:** **dorsal cutaneous** and **palmar cutaneous** branches.
- **Hand, motor:** **palmaris brevis, hypothenar muscles, medial two lumbricals, adductor pollicis, ALL interossei**; **sensory:** **palmar surface of the medial 1½ fingers**.
- **Interossei actions:** isolated: **PAD** (palmar adduct) and **DAB** (dorsal abduct); together with the lumbricals: **adduction cancels abduction**, acting through the **extensor expansion** to **flex the MP joints and extend the IP joints**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Ulnar nerve: medial cord, C7–T1, behind the medial epicondyle, between the heads of FCU.
- Forearm: FCU + medial half of FDP; hand: hypothenars, medial 2 lumbricals, adductor pollicis, all interossei.
- PAD and DAB; with lumbricals → MP flexion, IP extension.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the course and branches of the ulnar nerve.</summary>

**Answer:** It arises from the medial cord (C7, C8, T1), has no branches in the axilla or arm, passes behind the medial epicondyle and between the two heads of FCU. In the forearm it supplies FCU and the medial half of FDP and gives dorsal and palmar cutaneous branches; in the hand it supplies palmaris brevis, the hypothenar muscles, the medial two lumbricals, adductor pollicis and all interossei, and the medial 1½ fingers.

**Why:** Slides "Ulnar nerve".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the actions of the interossei?</summary>

**Answer:** Isolated: the palmar interossei adduct (PAD) and the dorsal interossei abduct (DAB). Together with the lumbricals, adduction cancels abduction and they act through the extensor expansion to flex the MP joints and extend the IP joints.

**Why:** Slide "Action of interossei".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the ulnar nerve branches in the hand.</summary>

**Answer:** Motor: palmaris brevis, hypothenar muscles, medial two lumbricals, adductor pollicis and all interossei. Sensory: the palmar surface of the medial 1½ fingers.

**Why:** Slide "Branches in hand".
</details>

## 4. Ulnar nerve injury and Guyon's canal syndrome

- **Common sites:** **elbow** (fracture or dislocation; improper positioning of the arm on the operating table); **wrist** (stab wound, **Guyon's canal syndrome**, **fracture of the hook of the hamate**).
- **Guyon's canal syndrome:** entrapment of the ulnar nerve in the tunnel at the wrist between the **pisiform and hamate** and the **ligament connecting them**; similar to carpal tunnel syndrome but involves the **ulnar nerve**.

| | **Injury at the elbow and above** | **Injury at the hand** |
|---|---|---|
| **Claw** | **Partial claw hand:** MP extension, IP flexion, most prominent in the **5th and 4th fingers** (lumbricals also ulnar) | **Partial claw hand (ulnar paradox):** ring and little fingers **flex more** because the branch to the **medial half of FDP is not lost** → **greater functional disability** |
| **Muscles** | **Atrophy of the interossei** (best seen on the **dorsum** of the hand) | Atrophy of the small muscles / interossei (dorsum) |
| **Thumb** | **Abduction of the thumb** | **Abduction of the thumb** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sites: elbow (fracture, dislocation, operating table) and wrist (stab, Guyon's canal, hook of hamate).
- Ulnar claw hand is most obvious in the 4th and 5th fingers; interossei wasting shows on the dorsum.
- Ulnar paradox: the claw is worse with a low (hand) lesion because medial FDP survives.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is Guyon's canal syndrome?</summary>

**Answer:** Entrapment of the ulnar nerve as it passes through the tunnel at the wrist between the pisiform and the hamate and the ligament connecting them; it is similar to carpal tunnel syndrome but involves the ulnar nerve.

**Why:** Slide "Guyon's canal syndrome".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the claw hand in ulnar nerve injury at the elbow.</summary>

**Answer:** Partial claw hand: extension of the MP joints and flexion of the IP joints, most prominent in the 5th and 4th fingers whose lumbricals are also supplied by the ulnar nerve, with atrophy of the interossei (best seen on the dorsum of the hand) and abduction of the thumb.

**Why:** Slide "Ulnar nerve injury — at elbow and above".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the ulnar paradox?</summary>

**Answer:** In injury at the hand the claw is more pronounced because the branch to the medial half of FDP is not lost, so the ring and little fingers flex more, causing greater functional disability.

**Why:** Slide "Ulnar nerve injury — when injury occurs at the hand".
</details>

## 5. Radial nerve: course, branches, injury

- **Origin:** in the axilla, the **larger terminal branch of the posterior cord**; **root C5, C6, C7, C8, T1**.
- **Termination:** at the level of the **lateral epicondyles**, dividing into the **superficial radial nerve (sensory)** and the **posterior interosseous nerve (motor)**.

| Level | Branches |
|---|---|
| **Axilla** | Muscular: **long head of triceps**; cutaneous: **posterior cutaneous nerve of the arm** |
| **Spiral groove** | Muscular: **medial and lateral heads of triceps and anconeus**; cutaneous: **lower lateral cutaneous nerve of the arm** and **posterior cutaneous nerve of the forearm** |
| **Between brachialis and brachioradialis** | **Lateral half of brachialis, brachioradialis, ECRL** |
| **PIN** | **Pure motor** to all the muscles of the back of the forearm **except brachioradialis, ECRL and anconeus** |
| **Superficial radial** | Cutaneous: **lateral 2/3 of the dorsum of the hand** and the dorsal aspect of the **proximal half of the lateral 3½ fingers** |

- **Causes of injury:** **crutch palsy, Saturday night palsy, fracture of the shaft or supracondylar fracture of the humerus**.
- **Results:** **sensory loss** limited to a **triangular area** on the dorsum of the base of the thumb and the **1st dorsal interosseous space** (overlap by surrounding nerves); **motor loss:** paralysis and atrophy of the muscles it supplies; **deformity:** **flexion of the elbow**, **finger drop and wrist drop**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Radial nerve: posterior cord, C5–T1; ends as the superficial radial (sensory) and PIN (motor).
- PIN supplies the posterior forearm muscles except brachioradialis, ECRL and anconeus.
- Injury: crutch palsy, Saturday night palsy, humeral fractures → wrist and finger drop; small triangular sensory loss.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the origin, course and termination of the radial nerve.</summary>

**Answer:** It begins in the axilla as the larger terminal branch of the posterior cord (C5–T1), runs in the spiral groove and between brachialis and brachioradialis, and ends at the level of the lateral epicondyles by dividing into the superficial radial nerve (sensory) and the posterior interosseous nerve (motor).

**Why:** Slides "Radial nerve — beginning" and "Branches".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What muscles does the radial nerve supply at each level?</summary>

**Answer:** Axilla: long head of triceps. Spiral groove: medial and lateral heads of triceps and anconeus. Between brachialis and brachioradialis: lateral half of brachialis, brachioradialis and ECRL. PIN: all muscles of the back of the forearm except brachioradialis, ECRL and anconeus.

**Why:** Slide "Radial nerve — branches".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the causes and results of radial nerve injury?</summary>

**Answer:** Causes: crutch palsy, Saturday night palsy, fracture of the shaft or supracondylar fracture of the humerus. Results: sensory loss limited to a triangular area on the dorsum of the base of the thumb and the 1st dorsal interosseous space (overlap by neighbouring nerves); paralysis and atrophy of the muscles supplied; deformity with flexion of the elbow, finger drop and wrist drop.

**Why:** Slide "Radial nerve injury".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Median nerve has no branches in the axilla or arm.**
- **Median nerve supplies all forearm flexors except FCU and the medial half of FDP.**
- **Adductor pollicis is ulnar**, not median; its unopposed action contributes to the ape-like hand.
- **Benediction hand is a median lesion at the elbow; the ulnar claw is most obvious in the 4th and 5th fingers.**
- **Ulnar paradox:** the claw is worse with a hand-level injury because medial FDP is spared.
- **CTS spares the thenar skin** because the palmar cutaneous branch passes superficial to the flexor retinaculum.
- **PIN spares brachioradialis, ECRL and anconeus** (supplied before the division).
- **Radial nerve sensory loss is only a small triangular area** because of overlap.
