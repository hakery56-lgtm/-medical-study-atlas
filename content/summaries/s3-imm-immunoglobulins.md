## 1. Antibodies, serum electrophoresis and valence

- ⭐ **Antibodies** = **glycoprotein molecules produced by plasma cells** in response to immunogens; they **recognize and neutralize** toxins and foreign substances (bacteria, viruses). **Only B lymphocytes make antibodies.**
- **Two forms:** **membrane-bound** (receptors for antigen on B cells) and **soluble** (blood and connective tissues; inhibit entry and spread of pathogens).
- **Serum electrophoresis bands:** **albumin** (formed in the liver, **60%** of total protein) → **α1** (α1-antitrypsin, thyroid-binding globulin) → **α2** (caeruloplasmin, α2-macroglobulin, haptoglobin) → **β1** (transferrin) → **β2** (β-lipoprotein) → **γ-globulin** (immunoglobulins).
- **General functions:** **effector functions** (complement fixation, binding to cells; usually need Ag binding) and **Ag binding** (can give protection; **valence**).
- ⭐ **Valence** = number of antigenic determinants an individual antibody molecule can bind; **at least 2**. **Avidity** depends on **binding affinity** (strength at one site), **valency** (total number of binding sites) and **structural arrangement**. **Monovalent = low, bivalent (IgG) = high, polyvalent (IgM) = very high avidity.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Antibodies are glycoproteins made only by B cells (plasma cells); membrane-bound or soluble.
- Albumin 60% of serum protein; antibodies are in the gamma band.
- Valency ≥ 2; avidity depends on affinity, valency and arrangement (IgM highest).

</div>

## 2. Immunoglobulin structure and fragments

- **Immunoglobulins are heterogeneous; myeloma proteins are homogeneous** immunoglobulins.
- ⭐ **Typical antibody** = **2 identical light chains + 2 identical heavy chains**; light chains **κ or λ**; heavy chains **α, γ, μ, δ, ε**.
- **Regions:** **Fab** (fragment antigen binding) and **Fc** (fragment crystallizable; contains only constant regions of the heavy chains); **VL/CL**, **VH/CH1-CH3 (or CH4)**; **hinge region**; carbohydrate and disulfide bonds.
- **Fab:** Ag binding, **valence = 1**, specificity by **VH and VL**. **Fc:** **effector functions** (complement binding, placental transfer, Fc receptor binding). **CDRs** = **hypervariable** regions complementary to the epitope.
- ⭐ **Enzymes:** **Papain** cuts **just above** the hinge interchain disulfide bond → **2 Fab + 1 Fc**. **Pepsin** cuts **just below** it → **F(ab')2** (the other fragment is not completely digested).
- **Multiple myeloma:** bone marrow cancer; one antibody clone grows out of control → **monoclonal protein (M-protein/M-spike)**; the **serum free light chain** test predicts outcome better than M-protein (more free light chains = more aggressive). **Bence-Jones protein:** monoclonal light chains (**22-24 kDa**, mostly **κ**) in blood or urine; myeloma cells are neoplastic plasma cells.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 2 light + 2 heavy chains; Fab binds antigen (valence 1), Fc does the effector work.
- Papain = 2 Fab + Fc (above the hinge bonds); pepsin = F(ab')2 (below).
- Myeloma: M-protein; Bence-Jones = free light chains (22-24 kDa) in blood/urine.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the structure of a typical antibody molecule.</summary>

**Answer:** Two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains (κ or λ). Each chain has variable (VL, VH) and constant (CL, CH1-CH3) domains; a hinge region links the Fab arms to the Fc portion; the Fab binds antigen and the Fc has effector functions.

**Why:** Slides "The structure of typical antibody molecule" and "Immunoglobulin Structure".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare papain and pepsin digestion.</summary>

**Answer:** Papain cleaves just above the interchain disulfide bond of the hinge giving two Fab fragments and one Fc fragment; pepsin cleaves just below it giving an F(ab')2 fragment while the rest is not completely digested.

**Why:** Slide "Pepsin and Papain digestion of antibody molecules".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is Bence-Jones protein and why is the free light chain test important?</summary>

**Answer:** A monoclonal protein of immunoglobulin light chains (22-24 kDa, kappa most of the time) found in the blood or urine of myeloma patients. The higher the free light chains the more aggressive the disease, so the serum free light chain test predicts outcome better than the M-protein level.

**Why:** Slides "Kappa/Lambda Light Chains in Multiple Myeloma" and "Bence-Jones protein".
</details>

## 3. Classes, subclasses and the five immunoglobulins

- **Classes:** **IgG (γ), IgM (μ), IgA (α), IgD (δ), IgE (ε)**; they differ in the **number of basic subunits** and the **heavy chain constant region sequence**; all share the basic structure. **Subclasses:** IgG1-4 (γ1-γ4), IgA1-2. **Light chains:** κ, λ (λ1-λ4).

| Property | **IgG** | **IgM** | **IgA** | **IgD** | **IgE** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| % of serum Ig | **80%** | **5-10%** | **10-15%** | **0.2%** | **0.002%** |
| Location | Blood, lymph, intestine | Blood, lymph, B-cell surface | Secretions: tears, saliva, mucus, milk | Blood, lymph, B-cell surface | Bound to mast and basophil cells |
| MW | 150,000 | 970,000 | 405,000 | 175,000 | 190,000 |
| Complement fixation | **Yes** | **Yes** | No | No | No |
| Placental transfer | **Yes** | No | No | No | No |
| Function | Enhance phagocytosis, neutralize toxin and virus, protect fetus and newborn | First Ab in response to initial infection | Localized protection on mucosal surface | Serum function not known | Allergic reaction |

- ⭐ **IgG:** systemic immunity; basic **monomer**; subclasses **IgG1 70%, IgG2 20%, IgG3 8%, IgG4 2%**; life span **21 days**; predominant Ab of the **secondary response**; **extravascular**; complement fixation **IgG3 > IgG1 > IgG2 > IgG4**; **opsonization**; placental transfer **IgG1, 3, 4**; **NK cell ADCC**; binds Fc receptors on lymphocytes and monocytes.
- ⭐ **IgM:** **extra CH4 domain**; **3rd highest serum Ig**; **first Ig made by the fetus and B cells**; **fixes complement**; **agglutinating** Ig; **B cell surface Ig** (with Ig-α/Ig-β in the BcR). **Hyper IgM syndrome:** ↓**CD40L** on Th2 CD4 cells → ↓ co-stimulation → ↓ class switching → ↑ **IgM**, ↓ IgG, IgA, IgE; ↑ **Pneumocystis, Cryptosporidium, CMV**. A single IgG does not activate C1; clustered antibodies/IgM do.
- ⭐ **IgA:** **2nd highest serum Ig**; **major secretory Ig** (mucosal/local immunity: tears, saliva, gastric and pulmonary secretions); **does not fix complement unless aggregated**; **Neisseria** destroy **IgA1** with a protease. Secretory IgA is a **dimer** with **J chain** and **secretory component** (polymeric Ig receptor).
- ⭐ **IgD:** serum level **≥ 1000-fold lower than IgG (< 1%)**; monomer; on the surface of circulating B cells, often with IgM; **life span 3 days**; **no complement binding**; surface Ig allows antigen binding and triggers B cell activation.
- ⭐ **IgE:** **allergic reactions and helminth infections**; **binds Fc receptors** (eosinophils; mast cells and basophils via FcεRI); **no complement fixation**; with antigen triggers mast cell release of **histamine, PGD2, proteases, heparin, TNF-α, IL-4**.
- Note: the IgG slide gives **75%** of serum Ig while the class table gives **80%**; the IgE slide names eosinophils whereas the table and mast-cell slide name mast cells and basophils. Know both statements.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Serum order: IgG (80%) > IgA > IgM > IgD > IgE; only IgG crosses the placenta; IgG and IgM fix complement.
- IgM = first antibody and the agglutinating Ig; IgA = secretory; IgD = B cell receptor; IgE = allergy and helminths.
- Hyper IgM: CD40L deficiency, high IgM, low IgG/IgA/IgE.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the five immunoglobulin classes.</summary>

**Answer:** IgG 80%, 150,000, fixes complement and crosses the placenta; IgM 5-10%, 970,000, first antibody, fixes complement; IgA 10-15%, secretions, mucosal protection; IgD 0.2%, B-cell surface, function in serum unknown; IgE 0.002%, bound to mast cells and basophils, allergic reactions.

**Why:** Slide "Immunoglobulin classes and biological activities".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the features of IgG.</summary>

**Answer:** A monomer with four subclasses (70/20/8/2%), life span 21 days, predominant antibody of the secondary response, extravascular, fixes complement (IgG3 > IgG1 > IgG2 > IgG4), opsonizes, crosses the placenta (IgG1, 3, 4), mediates NK-cell ADCC and binds Fc receptors.

</details>

## 4. Isotype, allotype and idiotype

| | **Isotype** (iso = same) | **Allotype** (allo = different) | **Idiotype** (idio = own) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Defined by** | Classes/subclasses of heavy chains and types/subtypes of light chains | **Allelic forms** of Ig genes | Unique determinants of each antibody's variable regions |
| **Location** | **Constant** region of heavy and light chains | **Constant** region of heavy and light chains | **Variable** region of heavy and light chains |
| **Seen** | In **all normal individuals** of a species | **Blood transfusion, pregnancy** | Injecting antibody from a genetically identical donor |
| **Importance** | Measure Ig levels, immunodeficiency, B cell tumors | Bone marrow grafts, paternity testing, forensics | Treatment of B cell tumors, vaccines |

- Antibody from one species injected into another → **anti-isotypic** antibodies. In **genetically identical twins** isotype and allotype are the same but **idiotype differs**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Isotype = class/subclass (constant, same in all); allotype = allelic variants (constant, individual); idiotype = variable region (unique to each antibody).
- Twins share isotype and allotype, not idiotype.
- Uses: isotype → Ig levels; allotype → paternity/forensics; idiotype → B cell tumors/vaccines.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define isotype, allotype and idiotype.</summary>

**Answer:** Isotype: antigenic determinants of the classes/subclasses of heavy chains and types of light chains, present in all individuals of a species. Allotype: determinants from allelic forms of Ig genes, differing between individuals. Idiotype: unique determinants of the variable regions of an individual antibody.

**Why:** Slides "Isotype, Allotype & Idiotype".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Where is each located and what is its importance?</summary>

**Answer:** Isotype and allotype: constant regions (isotype: measuring Ig levels, checking immunodeficiency, B cell tumors; allotype: bone marrow grafts, paternity testing, forensics). Idiotype: variable regions (treatment of B cell tumors, vaccines).

**Why:** Slides 35-38.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What differs and what is the same in identical twins?</summary>

**Answer:** Their isotype and allotype are the same, but their idiotype is different.

**Why:** Slide 38.
</details>

## 5. Roles of antibodies and monoclonal antibodies

- ⭐ **Seven roles:** **neutralization** (block infectivity by interfering with attachment to host cell receptors); **precipitation** (soluble reactants → insoluble product); **agglutination** (clumping of red cells or bacteria); **complement activation** (indirectly by pathogen-bound antibody → cascade on the pathogen surface); **opsonization** (particles made readily ingestible by phagocytes); **ADCC** (lytic mechanism by cells with Fc receptors for IgG); **allergy** (IgE on mast cells → degranulation).
- **Monoclonal antibodies (Mab):** produced by a clone of single plasma cells against a **single epitope**; made by **hybridoma technology**: a **plasma cell is fused with a myeloma cell** using **PEG**; the hybridoma combines **antibody specificity** (plasma cell) and **immortality** (myeloma cell).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Antibody roles: neutralization, precipitation, agglutination, complement activation, opsonization, ADCC, allergy.
- Mab = single epitope, one clone; hybridoma = plasma cell + myeloma cell (PEG).
- Specificity from the plasma cell, immortality from the myeloma cell.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List and define the roles of antibody in the immune response.</summary>

**Answer:** Neutralization; precipitation; agglutination; complement activation; opsonization; ADCC; allergy (IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation) - each as defined above.

**Why:** Slides "Neutralization ... Allergy".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is ADCC?</summary>

**Answer:** Antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity: a lytic mechanism mediated by mononuclear cells (e.g. NK cells) bearing receptors for the Fc portion of IgG; antibody bound to the target cell allows lysis.

**Why:** Slide "ADCC".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How are monoclonal antibodies produced?</summary>

**Answer:** By hybridoma technology: an immunized plasma cell is fused with a myeloma cell using polyethylene glycol; the hybridoma, which has antibody specificity and immortality, is selected and cloned to secrete antibody of a single specificity.

**Why:** Slide "Monoclonal antibodies (Mab)".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Papain = ABOVE the hinge bonds (2 Fab + Fc); pepsin = BELOW (F(ab')2).**
- **IgM is the first antibody; IgG alone crosses the placenta; IgA, IgD and IgE do not fix complement.**

- **Isotype and allotype are in the constant region; idiotype is in the variable region.**
- **Hyper IgM: IgM ↑ but IgG/IgA/IgE ↓ (CD40L defect).**
