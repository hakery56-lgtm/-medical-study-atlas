# Muscles of the Thigh — Exam Night Review

Anatomy lecture (Dr. Ahmed Al-Baghdadi, Anatomy of the Thigh, Part I): **the compartments of the thigh, the medial (adductor) compartment, the proximal hip flexors, the anterior compartment (sartorius and quadriceps femoris), and the posterior compartment (hamstrings).** After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer).

---

## 1. Compartments of the thigh

- The **deep fascia** divides the thigh into **3 compartments** (+ a proximal group): **anterior (quadriceps; extensors) — femoral nerve**; **medial (adductors) — obturator nerve**; **posterior (hamstrings; flexors) — sciatic nerve**.
- ⭐ The 12 muscles are classified into **4 groups**: **proximal part (2)** = hip flexors; **anterior (2)** = hip flexor and knee extensor; **medial (5)** = adductors and medial rotators of the hip; **posterior (3)** = hip extensors and knee flexors.
- **Watershed muscles:** one muscle from each compartment may be classified in another; they share a common insertion on the **medial aspect of the upper tibia = SGS (Sartorius, Gracilis, Semitendinosus) = pes anserinus ("goose foot")**.
- **Guy ropes:** sartorius, gracilis and semitendinosus form a tent-like arrangement that **steadies the pelvis on the femur and tibia** during standing and walking.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Anterior = femoral n.; medial = obturator n.; posterior = sciatic n.
- 12 muscles in 4 groups: 2 + 2 + 5 + 3.
- SGS (sartorius, gracilis, semitendinosus) = pes anserinus = guy ropes.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name the compartments of the thigh with their nerve supply and main actions.</summary>

**Answer:** Anterior (femoral nerve): flexes the hip and extends the knee. Medial (obturator nerve): adducts and medially rotates the hip. Posterior (sciatic nerve): extends the hip and flexes the knee. The proximal part (psoas major, iliacus) flexes the hip.

**Why:** Slides "Compartments of the thigh" and "Introduction".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are watershed muscles and which ones form the goose foot?</summary>

**Answer:** One muscle from each compartment that may be classified in another compartment; they share an insertion on the medial upper tibia. They are sartorius, gracilis and semitendinosus (SGS = pes anserinus).

**Why:** Slide "Introduction" (notes, goose foot).
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why are SGS called the guy ropes?</summary>

**Answer:** They are arranged like a tent and steady the pelvis on the femur and tibia during standing and walking, increasing the strength and stability of the pelvis.

**Why:** Slide "Guy ropes".
</details>

## 2. Medial (adductor) compartment

- **Supply:** **obturator nerve** for all, **except pectineus (dual: femoral + obturator)** and the **ischial part of adductor magnus (sciatic)**.
- **Common origin:** **body of the pubis and inferior pubic ramus** (not pectineus and adductor magnus). **Common action:** **adduction + medial rotation** of the hip.

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion |
|---|---|---|
| **Gracilis** | Body of pubis, inferior pubic ramus | Upper medial surface of tibia, behind sartorius |
| **Pectineus** | Superior pubic ramus | Pectineal line of femur |
| **Adductor longus** | Body of pubis just below pubic tubercle | Linea aspera |
| **Adductor brevis** | Body of pubis, inferior pubic ramus | Linea aspera |
| **Adductor magnus** | Pubic part: conjoint ramus; ischial part: ischial tuberosity | Pubic part: gluteal tuberosity, linea aspera, medial supracondylar ridge; ischial part: **adductor tubercle** |

- **Layers** (V formed by gracilis and femoral shaft): **superficial = pectineus + adductor longus; intermediate = obturator externus + adductor brevis; deepest = adductor magnus.**
- ⭐ **Gracilis:** most superficial; the **only adductor not attached to the femur**; weak adductor; flexes and medially rotates the leg; **used as a muscle graft**.
- **Pectineus:** part of the **floor of the femoral triangle**; dual innervation.
- **Adductor longus:** landmark for the **pubic tubercle**; floor and medial boundary of the femoral triangle; "**horse riding muscle**" (rider's strain); posterior relations: profunda femoris vessels, anterior branch of the obturator nerve, adductor brevis.
- **Adductor brevis:** between longus (front) and magnus (behind); **sandwiched between the anterior and posterior divisions of the obturator nerve**.
- **Obturator externus:** anatomically medial but **functionally a short lateral rotator**; posterior division of the obturator nerve; its tendon spirals behind the femoral neck and acts like a **ligament steadying the femoral head**.
- **Adductor magnus:** adductor part = posterior division of obturator n. (adduction + medial rotation); hamstring part = tibial part of sciatic n. (extends the hip). **Five openings** in its insertion: **upper 4 = perforating branches of profunda femoris; lowest = adductor hiatus** (femoral vessels into the popliteal fossa; at the junction of the middle and lower thirds of the thigh; covered by the middle third of sartorius).
- Note: the slide also lists knee flexion for the hamstring part of adductor magnus; most textbooks give it as a hip extensor only.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Obturator n. except pectineus (dual) and ischial part of magnus (sciatic).
- Gracilis = only adductor not attached to femur; graft muscle.
- Adductor hiatus = lowest opening in magnus; upper 4 = perforators.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the layers of the adductor muscles.</summary>

**Answer:** Gracilis and the femoral shaft form a V containing 3 layers: superficial (pectineus, adductor longus), intermediate (obturator externus, adductor brevis) and deepest (adductor magnus).

**Why:** Slide "Introduction" (layers).
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is adductor magnus said to have two functionally different parts?</summary>

**Answer:** The adductor part (obturator nerve, adducts and medially rotates) and hamstring part (sciatic nerve, extends the hip) differ in origin, insertion and nerve supply. The adductor hiatus lies between them.

**Why:** Slide "Adductor magnus".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is special about obturator externus?</summary>

**Answer:** It belongs anatomically to the medial compartment but functionally to the short lateral rotators; its tendon passes behind the femoral neck and steadies the femoral head in the acetabulum like a ligament.

**Why:** Slide "Obturator externus".
</details>

## 3. Proximal part and anterior compartment

### Hip flexors (proximal part)
- ⭐ **Psoas major** (T12–L5 bodies/transverse processes; nerve **L2, 3, 4**) + **iliacus** (iliac fossa; **femoral nerve**) → **iliopsoas tendon → lesser trochanter**; main hip flexor; both sides flex the vertebral column, one side laterally flexes it.
- Psoas relations: **lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh and femoral nerve** emerge from its lateral border; **obturator nerve** from the medial border; **genitofemoral nerve** from the anterior surface. **Psoas abscess** (TB spine) presents as a groin lump. In neck-of-femur fracture, iliopsoas **laterally rotates** the thigh.

### Sartorius
- Origin **ASIS**; insertion upper medial tibia (**SGS**); **longest muscle**; the **only muscle that flexes both hip and knee**; hip: flexion, abduction, lateral rotation (tailor position); knee: flexion, medial rotation.
- **Upper third:** lateral boundary of the femoral triangle; **middle third:** roof of the adductor canal; **lower third:** pierced by the **intermediate cutaneous nerve of the thigh and the saphenous nerve**.

### Quadriceps femoris (femoral nerve)
| Component | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Rectus femoris** | **Straight head: AIIS; reflected head: above the acetabulum**; the **only one that flexes the hip** |
| **Vastus lateralis** | **Largest**; intertrochanteric line, greater trochanter root, lateral lip of linea aspera |
| **Vastus medialis** | Thickest nerve (proprioception from knee); **lowest horizontal fibers stop lateral patellar displacement** |
| **Vastus intermedius** | Anterior/lateral femoral shaft; lowest fibers = **articularis genu** |

- **Insertion:** **quadriceps tendon → superior patella → ligamentum patellae → tibial tuberosity**; medial and lateral **patellar retinacula** to the tibial condyles; articularis genu to the synovial membrane of the suprapatellar bursa.
- **Actions:** **only extensor of the knee**; rectus femoris flexes the hip; **articularis genu retracts the synovial membrane** (protects the suprapatellar bursa); **main muscle for knee stability**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Iliopsoas: lesser trochanter, main hip flexor; psoas abscess in the groin.
- Sartorius: longest; only hip + knee flexor; roof of the adductor canal (middle third).
- Quadriceps: only knee extensor; rectus femoris also flexes the hip; vastus medialis stops lateral patellar slip.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the relations of psoas major to lumbar plexus branches and its clinical importance.</summary>

**Answer:** Lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh and femoral nerve emerge from the lateral border, obturator nerve from the medial border, genitofemoral nerve from the anterior surface. Pus from lumbar disc disease (TB spine) tracks along it as a psoas abscess presenting in the groin.

**Why:** Slide "Psoas muscle".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe sartorius.</summary>

**Answer:** Longest muscle, from the ASIS to the upper medial tibia; the only muscle flexing both hip and knee; forms the lateral boundary of the femoral triangle (upper third), the roof of the adductor canal (middle third) and is pierced by two nerves in its lower third.

**Why:** Slides "Sartorius".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the components, insertion and actions of quadriceps femoris.</summary>

**Answer:** Rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, vastus medialis and vastus intermedius (with articularis genu), all from the femoral nerve. Common quadriceps tendon to the patella, then ligamentum patellae to the tibial tuberosity. It is the only knee extensor, stabilizes the knee, and rectus femoris also flexes the hip.

**Why:** Slides "Quadriceps femoris", "Insertion / Actions".
</details>

## 4. Posterior compartment (hamstrings)

- **Hamstrings:** **semitendinosus, semimembranosus, biceps femoris (long head), ischial head of adductor magnus.**
- ⭐ **Features:** all arise from the **ischial tuberosity**; all insert into one of the **leg bones**; all supplied by the **tibial part of the sciatic nerve**; all **flex the knee and extend the hip**. (Short head of biceps arises from the **linea aspera** and is supplied by the **common peroneal part**.)

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Biceps femoris** | Long head: ischial tuberosity; short head: linea aspera | **Head of fibula** (only muscle inserted there), by 2 slips | Long head extends hip + flexes knee; short head flexes knee only; **lateral rotator** of the semiflexed knee; long head overlies the **sciatic nerve** |
| **Semitendinosus** | Ischial tuberosity (with long head of biceps) | Upper medial tibial shaft (**SGS**) | **Medial rotator** |
| **Semimembranosus** | Upper lateral part of the ischial tuberosity | Groove behind the **medial tibial condyle** | Expansions: **oblique popliteal ligament**, fascia over popliteus, slip to leg fascia; **semimembranosus bursa/cyst** |

- Muscles inserted into the upper tibia (**SGS, semimembranosus, popliteus**) **flex and medially rotate the leg** (unlock the knee).
- **Three muscles from three compartments** (sartorius, gracilis, semitendinosus) insert on the upper medial tibia.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hamstrings: ischial tuberosity origin; hip extension + knee flexion; tibial part of sciatic.
- Biceps femoris = only muscle to the fibular head; short head from the linea aspera, common peroneal supply.
- Semimembranosus → oblique popliteal ligament; its bursa may form a cyst.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the hamstring muscles and their 4 characteristic features.</summary>

**Answer:** Semitendinosus, semimembranosus, long head of biceps femoris and the ischial head of adductor magnus. All arise from the ischial tuberosity, insert into a leg bone, are supplied by the tibial part of the sciatic nerve and flex the knee and extend the hip.

**Why:** Slide "Hamstring muscles".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe biceps femoris.</summary>

**Answer:** Long head from the ischial tuberosity (tibial nerve), short head from the linea aspera (common peroneal nerve); inserts on the fibular head by two slips; long head extends the hip and flexes the knee, short head only flexes the knee; lateral rotator of the semiflexed knee; long head overlies the sciatic nerve.

**Why:** Slides "Posterior compartment table", "Biceps femoris".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the expansions and clinical relation of semimembranosus?</summary>

**Answer:** Its tendon expands to form the oblique popliteal ligament, the fascia covering popliteus and a slip to the deep fascia of the leg; a bursa lies between the tendon and bone and may form a semimembranosus cyst.

**Why:** Slide "Semimembranosus".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Pectineus = dual supply (femoral + obturator); ischial part of adductor magnus = sciatic.**
- **Gracilis is the only adductor not attached to the femur.**
- **Sartorius is the only muscle flexing BOTH hip and knee; rectus femoris is the only quadriceps part that crosses the hip.**
- **Biceps femoris is the only muscle inserted into the fibula; its short head is supplied by the common peroneal nerve.**
- **Obturator externus is in the medial compartment anatomically but acts as a lateral rotator.**
- **Psoas: obturator n. = medial border; genitofemoral n. = anterior surface; femoral + lateral cutaneous n. = lateral border.**
