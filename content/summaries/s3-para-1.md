## 1. Introduction to parasitology

- ⭐ **Parasite** = a **living (eukaryotic) organism adapted to live in or on another living organism (the host)**, **at the expense of the host**, obtaining its **food and shelter** from it.
- **Host** = human, animal, insect or snail.
- **Parasitology** = the science dealing with **medical parasites, their morphology, transmission and the diseases they cause**.
- **Three classes of parasites causing human disease:** (1) **protozoa** (endoparasite), (2) **helminths** (endoparasite), (3) **ectoparasites** (live outside the body, e.g. insects, ticks, mites, snails — which also act as hosts in which the parasite completes its life cycle).
- **Two major groups of animal parasites:** **protozoa** and **helminths (metazoa)**.

| Group | Characteristics | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Protozoa** | **unicellular** (one cell) | ***Entamoeba*, *Giardia lamblia*, *Trichomonas*, *Trypanosoma*, *Leishmania*, *Plasmodium*** |
| ⭐ **Helminths** | **multicellular** | ***Taenia saginata, T. solium, E. granulosus, H. nana*** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Parasite = eukaryotic organism living at the expense of a host; parasitology studies morphology, transmission and disease.
- Protozoa = unicellular (Entamoeba, Giardia, Trichomonas, Trypanosoma, Leishmania, Plasmodium); helminths = multicellular (Taenia, Echinococcus, H. nana).
- Endoparasites (protozoa, helminths) vs ectoparasites (insects, ticks, mites, snails).

</div>

## 2. Biology of protozoa

### Structure
- **Complete cellular organelles:** **nucleus**, **mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus**; each protozoan performs **all life activities**.
  - **Uninucleate** trophozoite: ***Entamoeba histolytica***.
  - **Multinucleate** trophozoites: ***Giardia lamblia*, *Balantidium coli***.

### Reproduction
- **Sexual or asexual**; commonest asexual = **binary fission** (Amoeba, Giardia).
- **Both** in **Plasmodium** (malaria): **asexual = schizogony in the human body**; **sexual = sporogony (gametocytes) in the female Anopheles**, union of male and female cells = **zygote**.

### Locomotion
| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **Pseudopodia** | **Amoeba** (*Entamoeba histolytica*) |
| **Cilia** (short, hair-like, covering the body) | ***Balantidium coli*** |
| **Flagella** (one or few, longer than cilia) | ***Giardia lamblia*, *Trichomonas vaginalis*** |
| **Undulating membrane** | ***Trichomonas*, *Trypanosoma*, *Leishmania*** |

### Respiration and nutrition
- **Respiration:** most **free-living protozoa respire aerobically**; **contractile vacuole** excretes liquid wastes by diffusion.
- **Nutrition:** **saprophytic** (absorb simple molecules through the cell wall) or **holozoic** (engulf whole cells through a mouth, digest in **food vacuoles**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Protozoa have organelles; uninucleate (E. histolytica) vs multinucleate (Giardia, Balantidium).
- Reproduction: binary fission; Plasmodium = schizogony (human) + sporogony (Anopheles).
- Locomotion: pseudopodia (Entamoeba), cilia (Balantidium), flagella (Giardia, Trichomonas), undulating membrane (Trichomonas, Trypanosoma, Leishmania).
- Nutrition: saprophytic vs holozoic.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the reproduction of protozoa.</summary>

**Answer:** Sexual or asexual; the commonest asexual method is binary fission (Amoeba, Giardia). Plasmodium uses both: asexual schizogony in the human and sexual sporogony (gametocytes and zygote) in the female Anopheles.

**Why:** Malaria life cycle.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Classify protozoa by locomotion with examples.</summary>

**Answer:** Pseudopodia (Entamoeba histolytica), cilia (Balantidium coli), flagella (Giardia lamblia, Trichomonas vaginalis) and undulating membrane (Trichomonas, Trypanosoma, Leishmania).

**Why:** Motile structures differ among species.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Define saprophytic and holozoic nutrition.</summary>

**Answer:** Saprophytic nutrition: absorption of simple molecules through the cell wall. Holozoic nutrition: engulfing of whole cells through a mouth with digestion in food vacuoles within the endoplasm.

**Why:** Two modes in the lecture.
</details>

## 3. Types of parasites and zoonosis

| Type | Meaning / examples |
|---|---|
| **Endoparasite** | lives **inside** the human host and causes disease |
| **Ectoparasite** | lives **outside** the body: **insects, ticks, snails, mites**; multiplies/develops in the insect or snail host to complete the life cycle |
| ⭐ **Obligate parasite** | **cannot survive without the human host**: **(1) intracellular** — ***Plasmodium* in liver parenchymal cells; *Toxoplasma gondii* in reticuloendothelial cells (REC)**; **(2) in human tissue** — ***Entamoeba histolytica* trophozoite (large intestine)**, ***Schistosoma* (liver, grows into male and female)** |
| ⭐ **Accidental parasite** | infects humans **accidentally by inhalation**: eggs of ***Echinococcus granulosus* and *Enterobius vermicularis*** |

- **Plasmodium (malaria) cycle:** sporozoite → hepatocyte → **hypnozoite (reactivation; *P. vivax*, *P. ovale*)** → schizont → merozoite → erythrocyte → ring; species: ***P. falciparum, P. vivax, P. ovale, P. malariae, P. knowlesi***.

### Zoonosis
- Introduced by **Rudolf Virchow in 1880**: diseases **naturally transmitted from infected animals to humans**.
  - **Helminth zoonosis:** **hydatidosis (*Echinococcus granulosus*)**, **taeniasis (*T. saginata*, *T. solium*)**.
  - **Protozoan zoonosis:** **toxoplasmosis (*Toxoplasma gondii*)**, **leishmaniasis (*Leishmania*)**, **balantidiasis (*Balantidium coli*)**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Endo- vs ecto-parasite; obligate (Plasmodium, Toxoplasma, E. histolytica, Schistosoma) vs accidental (Echinococcus eggs, Enterobius eggs inhaled).
- Zoonosis (Virchow, 1880): hydatidosis, taeniasis, toxoplasmosis, leishmaniasis, balantidiasis.
- Hypnozoite = reactivation stage (P. vivax, P. ovale).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

Obligate parasites cannot survive without the human host: Plasmodium (liver parenchymal cells), Toxoplasma gondii (reticuloendothelial cells), Entamoeba histolytica (large-intestine tissue) and Schistosoma.

**Why:** Slide "Types of parasites".
</details>

**Answer:** A parasite that infects humans accidentally, e.g. by inhalation of eggs of Echinococcus granulosus or Enterobius vermicularis.

**Why:** Slide "Accidental parasite".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Define zoonosis and give helminthic and protozoal examples.</summary>

**Answer:** Diseases naturally transmitted from infected animals to humans (term introduced by Rudolf Virchow in 1880). Helminthic: hydatidosis (E. granulosus), taeniasis (T. saginata, T. solium). Protozoal: toxoplasmosis, leishmaniasis, balantidiasis.

## 4. Source of infection and transmission

### Source of infection
| Source | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Anthropophilic** | **patients or carriers** | ***Trichomonas vaginalis*** |
| ⭐ **Zoophilic** | **infected animals** (intermediate or reservoir host) | ***Balantidium coli*, *Trichinella spiralis*** |
| ⭐ **Geophilic** | **contaminated soil or water** | **hookworm** (soil); ***Schistosoma* cercaria** (water) |

### Ways of transmission
- **Direct:** infective stage invades the body directly — **through skin, or sexual intercourse (vaginal trichomoniasis)**, or **kissing (*Trichomonas tenax*)**.
- **Indirect:** through **ingestion or inhalation** via contaminated clothes, wounds, **fingers, flies, food, fluids, feces (the "F5")**, or **water contaminated with infective stages or an intermediate host such as *Cyclops* carrying *Dracunculus medinensis* (guinea worm)** larvae.

### Modes of infection
| Route | Examples |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Ingestion** | **cysts of *E. histolytica*, *Giardia lamblia*, *Balantidium coli***; **eggs of *Ascaris lumbricoides* and *Trichuris trichiura***; **fish with metacercaria of *Paragonimus westermani*** (paragonimiasis) or **plerocercoid of *D. latum*** (diphyllobothriasis); **undercooked pork/beef with cysticercus of *T. solium/T. saginata*** and **cysts of *Trichinella spiralis*** in pork |
| ⭐ **Inhalation of dust** | **eggs of *Enterobius vermicularis* and *Echinococcus granulosus*** |
| ⭐ **Skin penetration** | **filariform larva of hookworm or *Strongyloides stercoralis*** in soil; **cercaria of *Schistosoma*** in water (swimming/washing) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sources: anthropophilic (Trichomonas), zoophilic (Balantidium, Trichinella), geophilic (hookworm, Schistosoma cercaria).
- Direct (skin, sexual, kissing) vs indirect (F5: fingers, flies, food, fluids, feces; Cyclops → guinea worm).
- Ingestion (cysts, eggs, metacercaria, plerocercoid, cysticercus); inhalation (Enterobius, Echinococcus eggs); skin (filariform larva, cercaria).

</div>

**Answer:** Anthropophilic (patients/carriers, e.g. Trichomonas vaginalis); zoophilic (infected animals, e.g. Balantidium coli, Trichinella spiralis); geophilic (contaminated soil or water, e.g. hookworm, Schistosoma cercaria).

**Why:** Slide "Source of infection".
</details>

**Answer:** Direct: the infective stage enters directly through skin or by person-to-person contact (sexual intercourse in vaginal trichomoniasis, kissing in Trichomonas tenax). Indirect: through ingestion or inhalation via contaminated clothes, wounds, fingers, flies, food, fluids, feces (F5) or water containing an intermediate host such as Cyclops carrying Dracunculus larvae.

**Why:** Slide "Transmission".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the modes of infection with examples.</summary>

**Answer:** Ingestion (cysts of E. histolytica/Giardia/Balantidium, Ascaris and Trichuris eggs, metacercaria in fish, plerocercoid, cysticercus in pork/beef, Trichinella cysts); inhalation of dust (Enterobius and Echinococcus eggs); skin penetration (filariform larva of hookworm and Strongyloides, Schistosoma cercaria).

**Why:** Three routes.
</details>

## 5. Vectors and other transmission routes

### Vector transmission
- **Mechanical:** carried on **legs, hair and appendages of insects** (e.g. **house flies** carry **Ascaris eggs, *E. histolytica* cysts, *Giardia* cysts**).
- **Biological:** the insect is an **intermediate/reservoir host**: **helminth** develops only (e.g. **microfilaria in *Culex* or *Aedes***); **protozoa multiply** (e.g. ***Leishmania* in the sand fly**).

### Vector skin transmission
| Disease | Vector → infective stage |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Malaria** | **female *Anopheles* bite → sporozoite** |
| ⭐ **Sleeping sickness** | **tsetse fly bite → metacyclic trypomastigote** |
| ⭐ **Kala-azar** | **sand fly bite → promastigote** |
| ⭐ **Chagas disease** | **reduviid bug feces on skin → metacyclic trypomastigote** |
| **L. braziliensis** | **sand-fly feces → promastigote** |

### Other routes
- **Direct contact:** vaginal trichomoniasis (sexual), *T. tenax* (kissing); **scabies** (*Sarcoptes scabiei* mite invades adult skin).
- ⭐ **Congenital (transplacental):** ***Toxoplasma gondii* tachyzoite** (congenital toxoplasmosis) and ***Trypanosoma cruzi*** (congenital Chagas).
- **Sexual:** ***Trichomonas vaginalis* trophozoite** → vaginitis/urethritis.
- **Iatrogenic:** via **blood transfusion or transplantation**.
- **Auto-infection:** infective eggs carried from the **perianal area to the mouth** or larva invading the rectum through the anus = **external**; **internal** also occurs — ***H. nana* and *Strongyloides stercoralis*** (both internal and external), ***E. vermicularis*** (external).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Mechanical (flies: Ascaris eggs, E. histolytica and Giardia cysts) vs biological vectors (Culex/Aedes–filaria; sand fly–Leishmania).
- Malaria: Anopheles/sporozoite; sleeping sickness: tsetse/metacyclic trypomastigote; kala-azar: sand fly/promastigote; Chagas: reduviid bug feces.
- Congenital: Toxoplasma tachyzoite, T. cruzi. Iatrogenic: transfusion/transplant. Auto-infection: H. nana, Strongyloides (internal + external), Enterobius (external).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Differentiate mechanical from biological transmission by vectors.</summary>

**Answer:** In mechanical transmission insects (e.g. house flies) carry infective stages on their legs, hair and appendages (Ascaris eggs, E. histolytica and Giardia cysts); in biological transmission the insect is an intermediate or reservoir host where the parasite develops (microfilaria in Culex/Aedes) or multiplies (Leishmania in the sand fly).

**Why:** Development occurs only in biological vectors.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Mention the vector and infective stage of malaria, sleeping sickness, kala-azar and Chagas disease.</summary>

**Answer:** Malaria: female Anopheles injects sporozoites. Sleeping sickness: tsetse fly injects metacyclic trypomastigotes. Kala-azar: sand fly injects promastigotes. Chagas disease: reduviid bug deposits feces containing metacyclic trypomastigotes on the skin.

**Why:** High-yield table.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are congenital, iatrogenic transmission and auto-infection?</summary>

**Answer:** Congenital: transplacental transmission from mother to fetus (Toxoplasma tachyzoites, Trypanosoma cruzi). Iatrogenic: through blood transfusion or transplantation. Auto-infection: reinfection from the perianal area to the mouth or larvae invading the rectum (external), and internal auto-infection, e.g. H. nana and Strongyloides stercoralis.

**Why:** Other transmission routes slides.
</details>
