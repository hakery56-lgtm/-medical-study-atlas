## 1. Air pollution

- ⭐ **Environmental diseases** = conditions caused by exposure to **chemical or physical agents** in the ambient, workplace or personal environment (diet, drugs, alcohol, tobacco), including **nutritional diseases (over- or undernutrition)**. **Two million people die yearly from occupation-related injury/illness; malnutrition affects 1 in 7 people and causes > 50% of global childhood mortality.**
- **Outdoor air pollution** hits the **lung**: **inflammation, increased airway reactivity, diminished mucociliary clearance, increased infections** (worst in preexisting lung/heart disease).

| Pollutant | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Ozone** | **Free radicals** injure alveolar epithelium → inflammatory mediators → **cough, chest discomfort**; worse in **asthma/emphysema** |
| **Sulfur dioxide** | From **coal and oil combustion, copper smelting, paper manufacture** → **sulfuric acid + sulfur trioxide** → burning, dyspnea, airway hyper-reactivity |
| **Particulates (soot)** | From **coal, oil, diesel**; **< 10 µm most harmful** (reach alveoli; taken up by macrophages and neutrophils → mediators); larger particles trapped in nasal mucus |
| **Carbon monoxide** | **Colorless, odorless, tasteless**; incomplete combustion (engines, wood, cigarettes); **200× affinity for Hb**; death by **CNS depression and hypoxia**; **cherry-red** skin (carboxyhemoglobin) |

### Indoor air pollution
- Increased by **improved insulation, fewer air leaks, air conditioning.** Pollutants: **tobacco smoke, CO, nitrogen dioxide, carcinogenic polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (cooking).**
- **Wood smoke** (particulates, polycyclic hydrocarbons: irritant, infections, carcinogenic); **bioaerosols** (**Legionella**; pet dander, dust mites, molds); **radon** (radioactive gas from uranium decay; ↑ cancer risk in **smokers**); **formaldehyde** (eye/upper-airway irritant, **carcinogen**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Air pollution → inflammation, ↑ airway reactivity, ↓ mucociliary clearance, infections.
- Ozone (free radicals), SO₂ (sulfuric acid), soot < 10 µm, CO (200× Hb affinity, cherry-red).
- Indoor: tobacco smoke, wood smoke, bioaerosols (Legionella), radon, formaldehyde.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the toxicity of carbon monoxide.</summary>

**Answer:** CO is a colorless, odorless, nonirritating gas from incomplete combustion; it binds hemoglobin with 200-fold greater affinity than oxygen, so acute poisoning causes CNS depression and systemic hypoxia with a cherry-red color from carboxyhemoglobin; chronic exposure in confined spaces can cause serious injury.

**Why:** Slide "CO".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain how particulates and sulfur dioxide injure the lung.</summary>

**Answer:** SO₂ (from coal/oil combustion, smelting, paper manufacture) forms sulfuric acid and sulfur trioxide causing burning, dyspnea and hyper-reactivity; particles < 10 µm reach the alveoli, are phagocytosed by macrophages and neutrophils and trigger mediator release and inflammation.

**Why:** Slides "Sulfur dioxide" and "Particulate matter".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the major indoor air pollutants.</summary>

**Answer:** Tobacco smoke, CO, nitrogen dioxide, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons from cooking, wood smoke, bioaerosols (Legionella, allergens), radon and formaldehyde.

**Why:** Slides "Indoor air pollution".
</details>

## 2. Metals as environmental pollutants

### Lead
- **Sources:** **air** (spray painting, foundries, gasoline), **soil/dust with leaded paint**, **water from lead plumbing.** **Inhalation = most important occupational route**; oral intake also toxic. **Children are more vulnerable** (↑ GI absorption, more permeable blood–brain barrier).
- **Mechanism:** binds protein **sulfhydryl groups** and **competes with calcium**; **80–85% accumulates in bones and teeth.**
- **Toxic effects:** **neurotoxicity** (altered calcium homeostasis; **children: psychomotor impairment; adults: peripheral demyelinating neuropathy**); **inhibition of ALA dehydratase and ferrochelatase** → **microcytic hypochromic anemia with punctate basophilic stippling**; **skeletal:** radiodense **"lead lines"** in epiphyses and impaired bone healing; **GIT:** abdominal pain, anorexia (**lead colic**).
- **Figure:** gingival lead line; chronic tubulointerstitial kidney disease; occupational sources (**spray painting, foundry work, mining/extracting lead, battery burning**); non-occupational (**water supply, paint dust and flakes, automotive exhaust, urban soil**).

### Mercury
- Binds **sulfhydryl groups** → damage to **CNS, GIT, kidneys.** **Vapor:** **tremor, gingivitis, bizarre behavior.** **Main sources:** **contaminated fish and dental amalgams.** **Ingested:** gut ulcerations, bloody diarrhea. **Kidney:** **acute tubular necrosis.**
- **Methyl mercury** accumulates in the developing brain and **blocks ion channels**; fetal exposure → **Minamata disease: cerebral palsy, deafness, blindness.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Lead: inhalation (occupational), children vulnerable; binds SH, mimics Ca; neuro, anemia (stippling), lead lines, colic.
- Mercury: SH binding; vapor → tremor/gingivitis/behavior; fish + amalgam; ATN; methyl-Hg → Minamata.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the toxic effects of lead.</summary>

**Answer:** Neurotoxicity (psychomotor impairment in children, peripheral demyelinating neuropathy in adults); inhibition of heme synthesis enzymes (ALA dehydratase, ferrochelatase) causing microcytic hypochromic anemia with basophilic stippling; skeletal changes (lead lines, impaired bone healing); GI lead colic.

**Why:** Slide "Toxic effects of lead".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why are children especially vulnerable to lead?</summary>

**Answer:** They have greater GI absorption and a more permeable blood–brain barrier.

**Why:** Slide "Lead".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe mercury poisoning and Minamata disease.</summary>

**Answer:** Mercury binds sulfhydryl groups, injuring the CNS, GI tract and kidneys (tremor, gingivitis, bizarre behavior from vapor; ulcers and bloody diarrhea if ingested; acute tubular necrosis); methyl mercury from contaminated fish accumulates in the developing brain and blocks ion channels, so fetal exposure causes Minamata disease (cerebral palsy, deafness, blindness).

**Why:** Slide "Mercury".
</details>

## 3. Occupational health risks

- Occupational exposures range from **mild respiratory irritation to lung cancer and leukemia**; all organ systems can be affected.

| Agent | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Organic solvents** (chloroform, carbon tetrachloride) | Absorbed via **lungs, skin, GIT**; **acute CNS depression, liver and kidney toxicity** |
| **Benzene** (aromatic hydrocarbon) | **CYP2E1 → toxic metabolites** disrupt marrow → **dose-dependent aplasia**, ↑ **acute myeloid leukemia** |
| **Polycyclic hydrocarbons** | From fossil-fuel combustion → **lung and bladder cancer** |
| **Organochlorines** | Lipophilic, degradation-resistant: **DDT** (pesticide), **PCB and dioxin** → **chloracne** (acne, cysts, hyperpigmentation, hyperkeratosis around face and behind ears), liver/CNS abnormalities; PCBs **induce P-450** → altered drug metabolism |
| **Bisphenol A (BPA)** | Polycarbonate containers, epoxy resin lining of cans; **endocrine disrupter (antiestrogen, antiandrogenic)** |
| **Mineral dusts** (coal, silica, asbestos, beryllium) | **Chronic, non-neoplastic, diffusely fibrosing pneumoconioses**; workplace exposure (**coal worker's pneumoconiosis: progressive massive fibrosis**) |
| **Vinyl chloride** | → **angiosarcoma of the liver** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Solvents: CNS depression, liver/kidney damage; benzene → marrow aplasia and AML.
- PCB/dioxin: chloracne, P-450 induction; BPA: endocrine disrupter.
- Mineral dusts → pneumoconioses (progressive massive fibrosis); vinyl chloride → hepatic angiosarcoma.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What are the effects of benzene exposure?</summary>

**Answer:** CYP2E1 converts benzene into toxic metabolites that disrupt marrow hematopoiesis, causing dose-dependent aplasia and an increased risk of acute myeloid leukemia.

**Why:** Slide "Organic solvents".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is chloracne and which compounds cause it?</summary>

**Answer:** A skin disorder caused by PCBs and dioxin (organochlorines) with acne, cysts, hyperpigmentation and hyperkeratosis, generally around the face and behind the ears; it may be accompanied by liver and CNS abnormalities.

**Why:** Slide "Organochlorines".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Name the mineral dusts and the diseases they cause.</summary>

**Answer:** Coal, silica, asbestos and beryllium cause chronic, non-neoplastic, diffusely fibrosing pneumoconioses (e.g. coal worker's pneumoconiosis with progressive massive fibrosis); vinyl chloride causes hepatic angiosarcoma.

**Why:** Slide "Mineral dust inhalation".
</details>

## 4. Effects of tobacco and alcohol

### Tobacco
- ⭐ **Most common exogenous cause of human cancer; 90% of lung cancers.** **Passive smoke** can cause lung cancer in non-smokers (**relative risk ≈ 1.3**); **cessation greatly reduces risk**; lung-cancer risk depends on the **number of cigarettes**.
- **Nicotine:** alkaloid, **highly addictive but not a direct cause** of the diseases; via **catecholamines** → ↑ heart rate, BP, contractility, output. **Commonest diseases: emphysema, chronic bronchitis, lung cancer.**
- **Mechanisms:** **I) direct irritant effect** (inflammation, ↑ mucus = bronchitis; leukocyte recruitment → ↑ **elastase** → **emphysema**); **II) carcinogenesis** (**polycyclic hydrocarbons, nitrosamines** → lung, oral cavity, esophagus, pancreas, bladder; **multiplies other risks: 10-fold lung carcinoma in asbestos workers/uranium miners who smoke; tobacco + alcohol multiplicative for oral, laryngeal, esophageal cancer**); **III) atherosclerosis/MI** (↑ platelet aggregation, ↓ myocardial O₂ supply with ↑ demand, ↓ threshold for ventricular fibrillation; multiplicative with hypertension and hypercholesterolemia); **IV) maternal smoking** → **spontaneous abortions, preterm births, intrauterine growth retardation**; **V) passive smoke.**

### Alcohol (ethanol)
- **Absorbed unaltered in stomach and small intestine**, distributed in proportion to blood levels; **most is converted to acetaldehyde by alcohol dehydrogenase in the liver**; **< 10% excreted unchanged** (urine, sweat, breath = **breath test**). **CYP induction** competes with metabolism of other drugs (**acetaminophen, cocaine**), anesthetics, carcinogens and industrial solvents.
- **Acute:** **hepatic steatosis, gastritis/ulceration, CNS depression.**
- **Chronic:** **liver** (fatty change, alcoholic hepatitis, **cirrhosis**); **GI** bleeding (gastritis, ulcer, **esophageal varices** from portal hypertension); **nervous system** (**thiamine deficiency** → peripheral neuropathy, **Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome**, cerebral atrophy, cerebellar degeneration, optic neuropathy); **cardiovascular** (**dilated cardiomyopathy**, hypertension); **pancreatitis** (acute and chronic); **fetal alcohol syndrome** (most harmful in the **first trimester**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Tobacco: 90% of lung cancer; bronchitis, emphysema (elastase), cancers (PAH, nitrosamines), atherosclerosis/MI, maternal risks; passive RR 1.3.
- Alcohol: ADH → acetaldehyde; < 10% unchanged; CYP induction; liver > GI > nervous (thiamine) > heart > pancreas > fetus.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Explain the mechanisms of tobacco-induced disease.</summary>

**Answer:** Direct irritation of the tracheobronchial mucosa (inflammation, mucus; leukocyte recruitment and elastase causing emphysema); carcinogenesis by polycyclic hydrocarbons and nitrosamines (lung, oral cavity, esophagus, pancreas, bladder), multiplied by asbestos or alcohol; atherosclerosis and MI (platelet aggregation, reduced myocardial oxygen supply, lower fibrillation threshold); and harm to the fetus (abortion, preterm birth, growth retardation).

**Why:** Slides "Effects of tobacco".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the metabolism of alcohol.</summary>

**Answer:** Ethanol is absorbed unaltered in the stomach and small intestine, distributed in proportion to blood level, and mostly converted to acetaldehyde by hepatic alcohol dehydrogenase; less than 10% is excreted unchanged (basis of the breath test); CYP induction competes with the metabolism of other drugs such as acetaminophen.

**Why:** Slide "Effect of alcohol".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the adverse effects of chronic alcoholism.</summary>

**Answer:** Fatty liver, alcoholic hepatitis and cirrhosis; GI bleeding from gastritis, ulcers or esophageal varices; thiamine-deficiency neuropathy and Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome, cerebral atrophy, cerebellar degeneration, optic neuropathy; dilated cardiomyopathy and hypertension; pancreatitis; fetal alcohol syndrome.

**Why:** Slide "Adverse alcohol effects".
</details>

## 5. Injury by physical agents: radiation

- **Radiation** = energy traveling as waves or high-speed particles. **Non-ionizing:** **UV and infrared, radio waves, microwaves, sound waves** (longer wavelength, lower frequency). **Ionizing:** **X-rays, gamma rays, high-energy neutrons, alpha particles, beta particles** (short wavelength, high frequency).

### Determinants of the biologic effects of ionizing radiation
1. **Rate of delivery:** a **single dose** injures more than **divided (fractionated)** doses of the same total.
2. **Field size:** a single low **whole-body** dose can be more lethal than higher **regional** doses with shielding.
3. **Cell proliferation:** DNA is the main target → **rapidly dividing cells** are more susceptible.
4. **Oxygen effects/hypoxia:** **reactive oxygen species from water ionization** start DNA damage.
5. **Vascular damage:** moderately sensitive endothelium → proinflammatory cytokines and healing with **luminal narrowing → progressive ischemia.**

### Acute effects and DNA damage
- **High dose, large field:** **peripheral lymphopenia; spleen and lymph node atrophy within hours; marrow aplasia.** **Very high doses** kill stem cells → **permanent aplastic anemia**; **granulocytes first, then platelets, then erythrocytes**; recovery takes **months.** **Fibrosis** in the field **weeks to months** later (scar replacing dead tissue + vascular injury).
- **DNA damage:** **single-base damage, single- and double-strand breaks, DNA–protein cross-links.** **Double-strand breaks** may persist or be repaired imprecisely → **mutations (deletions, duplications, inversions, translocations)**; without checkpoint control, abnormal clones survive and form **tumors.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Non-ionizing: UV, IR, radio, microwave, sound; ionizing: X-ray, gamma, neutrons, alpha, beta.
- 5 determinants: delivery rate, field size, cell proliferation, oxygen/ROS, vascular damage.
- Acute: lymphopenia, marrow aplasia (granulocytes → platelets → RBC); late: fibrosis; DNA double-strand breaks → mutations → cancer.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Distinguish ionizing from non-ionizing radiation.</summary>

**Answer:** Non-ionizing radiation (UV, infrared, radio waves, microwaves, sound waves) has longer wavelengths and lower frequency; ionizing radiation (X-rays, gamma rays, neutrons, alpha and beta particles) has short wavelength and high frequency.

**Why:** Slide "Radiation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the determinants of the biologic effects of ionizing radiation.</summary>

**Answer:** Rate of delivery, field size, cell proliferation, oxygen effects/hypoxia (ROS from water ionization) and vascular damage.

**Why:** Slide "Main determinants".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the acute hematologic effects and the DNA damage of radiation.</summary>

**Answer:** Lymphopenia, spleen/lymph node atrophy and marrow aplasia (granulocytes then platelets then erythrocytes; permanent aplastic anemia at very high doses); DNA single-base damage, strand breaks and cross-links, with unrepaired or misrepaired double-strand breaks causing mutations and eventually tumors.

**Why:** Slides "Acute effects" and "DNA damage and carcinogenesis".
</details>

## 6. Nutrient deficiencies

### Classification and PEM
- **Type 1:** **specific signs/symptoms** from reduced tissue concentration of **iron, iodine, vitamins.** **Type 2:** **reduced growth rate** as in **protein-energy malnutrition (PEM)**, more severe in **infancy and early childhood.**
- **PEM:** loss of **adipose tissue and tissue proteins**, **thin inelastic skin, dry hair that falls easily, lowered BMR, ↓ heart rate and BP, amenorrhoea.** **Causes:** inadequate intake, chronic diseases, **anorexia nervosa, alcoholism.** In children: **marasmus, kwashiorkor, intermediate forms.**

| | **Marasmus** | **Kwashiorkor** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Diet **severely lacking in calories** | **18–24 months, after weaning to a high-carbohydrate, low-protein diet** |
| Fat / muscle | **Loss of muscle and subcutaneous fat**; emaciated limbs, head looks too large | **Loss of skeletal muscle, subcutaneous fat preserved** |
| Albumin | **Normal or slightly reduced** (visceral protein spared) | Generalized **edema, moon face** |
| Other | Anemia, multivitamin deficiency, **↓ T-cell immunity**, infections | **Skin exfoliation, straight friable depigmented hair, fatty enlarged liver**, anemia, vitamin deficiency, **apathy and irritability** |

### Cachexia
- **PEM in chronically ill patients (cancer, AIDS).** ≈ **50% of cancer patients**; **cause of death in a third** (respiratory-muscle atrophy). **Proteolysis-inducing factor (PIF)** and **lipid-mobilizing factor** drive **TNF and IL-6** → **skeletal-muscle catabolism via NF-κB-induced proteasome activation.**

### Vitamins
- **Fat-soluble: A, D, E, K; water-soluble: B, C.** Deficiency from **reduced intake, wrong cooking/storage, impaired GI absorption, increased demand, iatrogenic (isoniazid → B6).**
- **Vitamin A:** keeps **mucus-secreting epithelium** and **vision**; deficiency = **xerophthalmia** (dry, wrinkled cornea → ulceration and liquefaction = **keratomalacia** → scarring, blindness), **recurrent respiratory infections, renal stones (keratin debris)**, **keratinizing metaplasia** (may potentiate neoplasia).
- **Vitamin D:** maintains **Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻** with **PTH**; from **diet and endogenous synthesis**; deficiency from **inadequate intake, little sun exposure, altered absorption/metabolism (renal disease)** → poor gut calcium/phosphate absorption → ↓ serum levels → **PTH mobilizes bone calcium**, urinary phosphate wasting → **low phosphate impairs mineralization.** Result: **rickets (children)** and **osteomalacia (adults)** = excess **unmineralized matrix.**
  - **Rickets:** poor calcification of epiphyseal cartilage → **frontal bossing, chest deformity, lumbar lordosis, bowing legs.** **Osteomalacia:** inadequately mineralized osteoid → weak bone, **fractures.**
  - **Non-skeletal:** **1,25(OH)₂D made by macrophages and epithelia**; receptors in many tissues (innate immunity; genes for proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, angiogenesis); chronic insufficiency → **30–50% higher incidence of colon, prostate and breast cancers.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Type 1 = specific (iron, iodine, vitamins); Type 2 = growth failure (PEM).
- Marasmus = calorie lack, wasting, normal albumin; kwashiorkor = low protein, edema, fatty liver, hair changes.
- Cachexia = PEM in cancer/AIDS (PIF, TNF, IL-6, NF-κB). Vit A: xerophthalmia/keratomalacia; vit D: rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare marasmus and kwashiorkor.</summary>

**Answer:** Marasmus: severe calorie deficiency with growth retardation, loss of muscle and subcutaneous fat, emaciated limbs, large-looking head, normal or slightly low albumin, anemia and impaired T-cell immunity. Kwashiorkor: after weaning at 18–24 months to a low-protein, high-carbohydrate diet, with edema and moon face, preserved subcutaneous fat, skin exfoliation, friable depigmented hair, fatty enlarged liver, anemia, apathy and irritability.

**Why:** Slides "Marasmus" and "Kwashiorkor".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is cachexia and how do tumors cause it?</summary>

**Answer:** PEM in chronically ill patients (cancer, AIDS), present in about 50% of cancer patients and causing death in a third; tumors release proteolysis-inducing factor and lipid-mobilizing factor and drive TNF and IL-6, causing skeletal-muscle catabolism through NF-κB-induced proteasome activation.

**Why:** Slide "Cachexia".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the consequences of vitamin A and vitamin D deficiency.</summary>

**Answer:** Vitamin A deficiency: xerophthalmia with corneal drying, ulceration and keratomalacia, scarring and blindness, recurrent respiratory infections, renal stones and keratinizing metaplasia. Vitamin D deficiency: poor calcium/phosphate absorption and low phosphate, causing rickets in children (frontal bossing, chest deformity, lumbar lordosis, bowed legs) and osteomalacia in adults (weak bones, fractures).

**Why:** Slides "Vitamin A" and "Vitamin D".
</details>
