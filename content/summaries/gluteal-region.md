## 1. Contents and muscle groups

- ⭐ **Contents:** **10 muscles** (gluteus maximus, medius, minimus, tensor fasciae latae, piriformis, obturator internus, obturator externus, quadratus femoris, superior and inferior gemelli), **7 nerves** (superior gluteal, inferior gluteal, n. to obturator internus, n. to quadratus femoris, pudendal, sciatic, posterior cutaneous n. of the thigh) and **3 vessels** (superior gluteal, inferior gluteal, internal pudendal).
- **Three groups:** **superficial extensor** (gluteus maximus); **abductors and medial rotators** (gluteus medius, minimus, tensor fasciae latae); **deep lateral rotators** (quadratus femoris, piriformis, gemelli, obturator externus and internus).
- **Supply:** **superior and inferior gluteal arteries** from the **internal iliac**; nerves from the **sacral plexus** except **obturator externus**.

**Key points**
- 10 muscles, 7 nerves, 3 vessels.
- Three muscle groups: extensor, abductors, lateral rotators.

</div>

## 2. Superficial muscles

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action | Nerve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gluteus maximus** (largest, buttock contour) | Posterior ilium, sacrum, sacrotuberous ligament, coccyx | **¾ iliotibial tract; ¼ gluteal tuberosity** | **Powerful hip extensor** (rising, climbing, running); lateral rotation and abduction; extends the knee via the tract | **Inferior gluteal** |
| **Gluteus medius** | Gluteal surface of ilium | **Lateral side of the greater trochanter** | **Middle fibers abduct; anterior fibers medially rotate; stabilize the pelvis** | **Superior gluteal** |
| **Gluteus minimus** (deepest, smallest) | Ilium | **Anterior aspect of the greater trochanter** | As medius | **Superior gluteal** |
| **Tensor fasciae latae** | **ASIS** | **Iliotibial tract** → lateral tibial condyle | Tightens the tract; weak abductor and medial rotator; stabilizes hip and knee | **Superior gluteal** |

- **Pelvic stability:** when the other leg is raised, **medius and minimus** pull on the **same-side hip bone** and **prevent the pelvis tilting to the opposite side.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Maximus: inferior gluteal nerve; medius, minimus, TFL: superior gluteal nerve.
- Medius and minimus keep the pelvis level.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe gluteus maximus.</summary>

**Answer:** Largest, most superficial; arises from the posterior ilium, sacrum, sacrotuberous ligament and coccyx; inserts into the iliotibial tract (¾) and gluteal tuberosity (¼); powerful hip extensor; inferior gluteal nerve.

**Why:** Slide 9.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the role of gluteus medius and minimus in walking?</summary>

**Answer:** They abduct the thigh and keep the pelvis level by pulling on the same-side hip bone when the other leg is lifted.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe tensor fasciae latae.</summary>

**Answer:** From the ASIS to the iliotibial tract; tightens the tract and stabilizes hip and knee; weak abductor and medial rotator; superior gluteal nerve.

**Why:** Slide 13.
</details>

## 3. Deep muscles (lateral rotators)

- **General action:** **lateral rotation** (cross behind the hip) and **stabilization** by pulling the femoral head into the acetabulum.

| Muscle | Origin → insertion | Nerve |
|---|---|---|
| **Piriformis** (**landmark**) | **Anterior sacrum** → through **greater sciatic foramen** → **greater trochanter** (upper border) | **Nerve to piriformis (S1, S2)** |
| **Obturator internus** (lateral pelvic wall) | **Ischiopubic ramus and obturator membrane** → **lesser sciatic foramen** → **medial greater trochanter** | **Nerve to obturator internus** |
| **Gemelli** | **Superior:** ischial spine; **inferior:** ischial tuberosity (flank obturator internus tendon) → greater trochanter | **Superior: n. to obturator internus; inferior: n. to quadratus femoris** |
| **Quadratus femoris** (most inferior) | **Lateral ischial tuberosity** → **quadrate tubercle** | **Nerve to quadratus femoris** |
| **Obturator externus** | — | **Posterior division of obturator nerve** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Piriformis exits through the greater foramen; obturator internus through the lesser foramen.
- Quadratus femoris is the lowest.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the general action of the deep gluteal muscles?</summary>

**Answer:** Lateral rotation of the hip and stabilization of the joint by drawing the femoral head into the acetabulum.

**Why:** Slide 14.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the piriformis.</summary>

**Answer:** From the anterior sacrum through the greater sciatic foramen to the greater trochanter; lateral rotator and abductor when flexed; nerve to piriformis; the key landmark.

**Why:** Slide 15.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give the nerve supply of the deep muscles.</summary>

**Answer:** Piriformis S1-S2; obturator internus and superior gemellus: nerve to obturator internus; quadratus femoris and inferior gemellus: nerve to quadratus femoris; obturator externus: posterior division of the obturator nerve.

**Why:** Slides 15-18 and the table.
</details>

## 4. Arteries and foramina

- **Superior gluteal artery:** exits the **greater sciatic foramen above piriformis**, runs deep to gluteus maximus, joins the **trochanteric anastomosis** (with the **inferior gluteal** and **lateral and medial circumflex femoral** arteries) = **chief supply of the femoral head.**
- **Inferior gluteal artery:** exits **below piriformis**, descends medial to the sciatic nerve, supplies the **trochanteric and cruciate anastomoses.**
- **Anastomoses** link **internal and external iliac (femoral)** systems: **collateral circulation** if the femoral artery is blocked proximally.

| Foramen | Structures |
|---|---|
| **Greater sciatic, above piriformis** | **Superior gluteal vessels and nerve** |
| **Greater sciatic, below piriformis** | **Inferior gluteal vessels and nerve, sciatic nerve, perforating cutaneous nerve, posterior femoral cutaneous nerve, nerve to quadratus femoris, nerve to obturator internus, pudendal nerve, internal pudendal vessels** |
| **Lesser sciatic** | **Tendon of obturator internus, nerve to obturator internus, internal pudendal vessels, pudendal nerve** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Superior gluteal structures pass above piriformis; everything else below.
- Trochanteric anastomosis supplies the femoral head.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the superior and inferior gluteal arteries.</summary>

**Answer:** Both exit the greater sciatic foramen, the superior above and the inferior below piriformis, deep to gluteus maximus, and join the trochanteric anastomosis.

**Why:** Slides 23-24.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is the trochanteric anastomosis important?</summary>

**Answer:** It is the chief source of blood to the head of the femur.

**Why:** Slide 23.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the structures passing below piriformis.</summary>

**Answer:** Inferior gluteal vessels and nerve, sciatic nerve, perforating and posterior femoral cutaneous nerves, nerves to quadratus femoris and obturator internus, pudendal nerve and internal pudendal vessels.

**Why:** Slide 21 figure.
</details>

## 5. Lumbar and sacral plexuses and the sciatic nerve

- **Lumbar plexus:** **anterior rami of L1-L3 + upper L4**, within **psoas major**; **6 branches:** **iliohypogastric (T12, L1), ilioinguinal (L1), genitofemoral (L1, 2), lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh (L2, 3), femoral (dorsal divisions L2-4), obturator (ventral divisions L2-4).** **L4** is the **link** (lower L4 + L5 = **lumbosacral trunk**).
- **Sacral plexus:** **5 roots:** **lumbosacral trunk (L4, 5), S1-S3 anterior rami, upper S4**; lies **in front of the sacrum and piriformis.**

| Group | Branches |
|---|---|
| **2 pelvic** | **Nerve to piriformis (S1, 2); pelvic splanchnic (S2-4, parasympathetic)** |
| **2 gluteal** | **Superior gluteal (L4, 5, S1); inferior gluteal (L5, S1, 2)** |
| **2 muscular** | **Nerve to quadratus femoris (L4, 5, S1); nerve to obturator internus (L5, S1, 2)** |
| **2 cutaneous** | **Perforating cutaneous (S2, 3); posterior cutaneous of thigh (S1, 2, 3)** |
| **2 terminal** | **Sciatic (L4, 5, S1-3); pudendal (S2-4)** |

- **Sciatic nerve:** the **largest nerve**; **tibial + common peroneal** bound together; usually divide **above the popliteal fossa** (sometimes in the pelvis: **tibial below, common peroneal through or above piriformis**). **Motor:** **hamstrings, ischial part of adductor magnus, all leg and foot muscles.** **Sensory:** **all of the leg and foot except the medial side (saphenous).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sacral plexus: 2 pelvic, 2 gluteal, 2 muscular, 2 cutaneous, 2 terminal.
- Sciatic = tibial + common peroneal; L4-S3.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the branches of the lumbar plexus.</summary>

**Answer:** Iliohypogastric, ilioinguinal, genitofemoral, lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh, femoral and obturator nerves.

**Why:** Slide 26.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the branches of the sacral plexus.</summary>

**Answer:** Nerve to piriformis and pelvic splanchnics; superior and inferior gluteal nerves; nerves to quadratus femoris and obturator internus; perforating and posterior cutaneous nerves; sciatic and pudendal nerves.

**Why:** Slide 27.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the sciatic nerve and its supply.</summary>

**Answer:** L4-S3, the largest nerve, composed of tibial and common peroneal parts; motor to hamstrings, ischial adductor magnus and all leg and foot muscles; sensory to the leg and foot except the medial side.

**Why:** Slides 28-29.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Gluteus maximus: inferior gluteal nerve; medius, minimus and TFL: superior gluteal nerve.**
- **Piriformis: greater foramen; obturator internus: lesser foramen.**
- **Superior gluteal vessels and nerve are the only ones above piriformis.**
- **Obturator externus is not supplied by the sacral plexus.**
- **Femoral nerve = dorsal divisions; obturator nerve = ventral divisions (L2-L4).**
