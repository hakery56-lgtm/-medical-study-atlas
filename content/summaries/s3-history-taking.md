## 1. Objectives, principles and set-up

- ⭐ **Why take a history?** To **confirm the diagnosis** — **in more than 60 % of patients the diagnosis is made from the history alone.**
- **What we need:** available patient, appropriate setting, a **comfortable place and time**, **patience and understanding**, **knowledge**, **good communication skills**, **experience**.

### Set-up of history taking
| Setting | Type of history |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Outpatient clinic** | **short but effective, focused and informative** |
| ⭐ **Inpatient clinic** | **long classical history** |
| ⭐ **Emergency room** | **chief complaint → vital signs → secondary survey** |

### Don't forget to
- **Introduce yourself** and explain what you will do.
- Ask what the patient **likes to be called**.
- Get a **chaperon when interviewing a female patient**.
- Be a **good listener** but **take control of the session**.
- Use a lot of **body language**.

**Key points**
- > 60 % of diagnoses come from history alone.
- Outpatient = short focused; inpatient = long classical; ER = chief complaint → vital signs → secondary survey.
- Introduce yourself, ask preferred name, chaperon for females, listen but control, use body language.

## 2. Components of the history (1): identification data, complaint, history of the present complaint

### First: Identification data
- **Name, age, sex, marital status, occupation, address, date of interview.**

### 1 — Present (chief) complaint
- **The main complaint that brought the patient to medical attention.**
- **In the patient's own words** (avoid medical terms — e.g. say "watery stool" instead of "diarrhea").
- **With duration** (weeks, days, hours, minutes).
- If there are many complaints, **choose the most bothersome one**.
- Questions: "What are you complaining of?", "What is the problem?", "What is the matter?"

### 2 — History of the present complaint (example: abdominal pain)
- **Site, time and mode of onset, nature, duration, severity, radiation, progression/end, relieving factors, exacerbating factors, cause.**

### Characteristics of pain — **SOCRATES**
| Letter | Component | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | **Site** | **somatic pain well localised** (sprained ankle); **visceral pain diffuse** (angina) |
| **O** | **Onset** | speed of onset and associated circumstances |
| **C** | **Character** | adjectives: sharp/dull, burning, stabbing, crushing — **patient's own description** |
| **R** | **Radiation** | local extension, or **referred** by shared pathway (e.g. **diaphragmatic pain at the shoulder tip via the phrenic nerve C3, C4**) |
| **A** | **Associated symptoms** | e.g. **visual aura with migraine**, leg numbness with back pain → nerve-root irritation |
| **T** | **Timing** | duration, course, pattern (episodic vs continuous) |
| **E** | **Exacerbating/relieving factors** | provoking factors (eating), postures, medications |
| **S** | **Severity** | subjective; compare with common pains (toothache); variation by day/month (menstrual cycle) |

### 3 — Remaining questions of the abnormal system
- Focus on **one "system of blame"** (GIT, CVS, CNS) and ask about **associated symptoms** (e.g. abdominal pain → nausea, vomiting, change in bowel habit).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Identification data: name, age, sex, marital status, occupation, address, date.
- Chief complaint: main reason, in the patient's own words, with duration; pick the most bothersome.
- SOCRATES: Site, Onset, Character, Radiation, Associated symptoms, Timing, Exacerbating/relieving, Severity.
- Referred pain example: diaphragm → shoulder tip (phrenic C3, C4).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the chief complaint and how is it recorded?</summary>

**Answer:** The main complaint that brought the patient to medical attention, recorded in the patient's own words (simple language, e.g. "watery stool" instead of diarrhea) with its duration; if there are many complaints, the most bothersome one is chosen.

**Why:** First step after identification data.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain the SOCRATES mnemonic for pain.</summary>

**Answer:** Site (somatic localised vs visceral diffuse), Onset, Character (patient's own words), Radiation (local extension or referred, e.g. diaphragmatic pain to the shoulder tip via the phrenic nerve C3–C4), Associated symptoms, Timing (duration, course, pattern), Exacerbating/relieving factors, Severity (subjective).

**Why:** A standard framework for pain history.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the identification data and why ask about associated symptoms?</summary>

**Answer:** Name, age, sex, marital status, occupation, address and date of interview. Associated symptoms of the "system of blame" (e.g. nausea, vomiting and bowel habit in abdominal pain) help localize the problem.

**Why:** They narrow the differential diagnosis.
</details>

## 3. Diagnosis and review of systems

### Types of diagnosis
- **Provisional diagnosis** — the primary one based on available information.
- **Differential (working) diagnosis** — **most likely / less likely / least likely — and why**.
- **Final diagnosis** — **confirmed by laboratory tests or natural history**.
- ⭐ **Any diagnosis = anatomical part + pathological part** (e.g. **breast cancer, peptic ulcer, fracture femur, irritable bowel syndrome, ischemic heart disease**).

### 4 — Review of systems (examples of questions)
| System | Typical questions / symptoms |
|---|---|
| **Cardiovascular** | chest pain/tightness, waking at night short of breath (**PND/orthopnea**), palpitations, ankle swelling, dizziness, peripheral vascular symptoms |
| **Respiratory** | cough, sputum (colour), **hemoptysis**, dyspnea, orthopnea, chest pain |
| **Gastrointestinal** | appetite, diet, weight, teeth and taste, swallowing, regurgitation, flatulence, heartburn, vomiting, **haematemesis**, abdominal pain/distension, defecation, skin colour change (jaundice), change in bowel habit, blood/slime in stool |
| **Genitourinary** | pain or difficulty passing urine, **nocturia**, dribbling, urine changes, oedema, thirst, menstruation, pregnancies, breasts, secondary sex characters |
| **Musculoskeletal** | pain, stiffness, swelling, limitation of joint movement, difficulty walking or dressing |
| **Endocrine** | heat/cold intolerance, thirst, drinking more |
| **Neurological** | fits, faints, blackouts, numbness, weakness, clumsiness, **TIAs**, paraesthesia, speech difficulty, unsteadiness, mental state, conscious level |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Provisional → differential (most/less/least likely) → final (lab-confirmed).
- Diagnosis = anatomical + pathological part (breast cancer, peptic ulcer, fracture femur, IBS, IHD).
- Review of systems: CVS, respiratory, GIT, GU, MSK, endocrine, neuro.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Differentiate provisional, differential and final diagnosis.</summary>

**Answer:** Provisional diagnosis is the first one based on available information; differential (working) diagnosis ranks possibilities as most, less and least likely with reasons; final diagnosis is confirmed by laboratory tests or the natural history of the disease.

**Why:** Diagnostic reasoning steps.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What does a diagnosis consist of? Give examples.</summary>

**Answer:** An anatomical part plus a pathological part, e.g. breast cancer, peptic ulcer, fracture femur, irritable bowel syndrome, ischemic heart disease.

**Why:** Lecture rule.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the systems reviewed in the history with two questions for each of two systems.</summary>

**Answer:** GIT, respiratory, cardiovascular, urogenital, endocrine, nervous and musculoskeletal systems. Cardiovascular: chest pain or tightness? waking at night short of breath? Respiratory: breathlessness? cough with sputum or blood?

**Why:** Table 2.3.
</details>

## 4. Other histories

| History | Items |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **5 — Past medical** | **hospitalizations, TB, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, asthma, rheumatic fever, contact with hepatitis or AIDS patients** |
| ⭐ **6 — Past surgical** | **previous operations, blood transfusion, anesthesia complications, bleeding tendencies** |
| ⭐ **7 — Drug history** | **steroids, NSAIDs, insulin/oral hypoglycemics, antihypertensives, hormone replacement therapy** |
| ⭐ **8 — Immunizations** | **DPT (diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus), measles, mumps, rubella, poliomyelitis, smallpox, typhoid** |
| ⭐ **9 — Family history** | **health, age or cause of death of parents and siblings; common illnesses (DM, HT, asthma); consanguinity** |
| ⭐ **10 — Social history & habits** | **marital status, occupational hazards, social status/type of residence, travel abroad (dates), smoking, alcohol, anything unusual** |

### Summary of the case
- **Patient's name, age and sex**; **complaint with the most important positive characteristics**; **most important negative features**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Past medical (TB, DM, HTN, asthma, rheumatic fever, hepatitis/AIDS contact); surgical (operations, transfusion, anesthesia, bleeding).
- Drugs (steroids, NSAIDs, insulin, antihypertensives, HRT); immunizations (DPT, measles, mumps, rubella, polio, typhoid).
- Family history (consanguinity!); social history (occupation hazards, residence, travel, smoking, alcohol).
- Case summary: demographics, complaint + important positives and negatives.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is included in the past medical and surgical history?</summary>

**Answer:** Past medical: hospitalizations, TB, diabetes, hypertension, asthma, rheumatic fever, contact with hepatitis or AIDS patients. Past surgical: previous operations, blood transfusion, complications with anesthesia and bleeding tendencies.

**Why:** Slides 5 and 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What do you ask about in the drug, immunization and family histories?</summary>

**Answer:** Drugs: steroids, NSAIDs, insulin and oral hypoglycemics, antihypertensives, hormone replacement therapy. Immunizations: DPT, measles, mumps, rubella, polio, smallpox, typhoid. Family: parents' and siblings' health and age or cause of death, common illnesses (DM, hypertension, asthma) and consanguinity.

**Why:** Slides 7–9.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What does the social history include and what should the final summary contain?</summary>

**Answer:** Marital status, occupational hazards, social status/type of residence, travel abroad with dates, smoking, alcohol and any unusual habit. The summary: patient's name, age, sex, the complaint with its most important positive characteristics and the most important negative features.

**Why:** Slide 10 and summary slide.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **> 60 % of diagnoses come from the history.**
- **Chief complaint = patient's OWN words + duration; choose the most bothersome.**
- **ER sequence: chief complaint → vital signs → secondary survey.**
- **Inpatient = long classical; outpatient = short focused.**
- **SOCRATES — know all 8 items; "R" radiation includes referred pain (phrenic C3, C4 → shoulder tip).**
- **Diagnosis = anatomical + pathological.**
- **Provisional ≠ differential ≠ final.**
- **Chaperon for female patients.**
- **Consanguinity belongs to family history.**
- **Immunizations: DPT, measles, mumps, rubella, polio, smallpox, typhoid.**
