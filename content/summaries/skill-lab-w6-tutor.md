## 1. Objectives and spine examination

- **Objectives:** **(1) surface anatomy of the spine; (2) focused history in back pain; (3) physical examination of the spine; (4) interpret history and examination (clinical reasoning); (5) neurological examination of the lower limbs.**
- **Exposure:** patient **standing, back fully exposed.**
- **Look:** from **behind, side and front**: **rib hump, abnormal curvature (↑ or ↓ lordosis, scoliosis), hairy patch, lipoma (spina bifida).** **Scoliosis** = lateral curve; **kyphosis** = sagittal curve with **posterior** apex (mild normal thoracic kyphosis); **lordosis** = sagittal curve with **anterior** apex.
- **Feel:** **midline spinous processes, paraspinal tissues, light closed-fist percussion** (warn first).
- **Move:** **flexion** (touch toes), **extension** (lean back), **lateral flexion** (slide hand down the leg), legs straight.
- **Sciatic nerve stretch test (L4-S1):** **supine; raise the leg with knee straight; at the limit lower slightly and dorsiflex the foot.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Kyphosis posterior apex; lordosis anterior apex.
- Sciatic stretch tests L4, L5, S1.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Surface anatomy, focused back pain history, physical examination of the spine, clinical reasoning and neurological examination of the lower limbs.

**Why:** Slide 1.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe look, feel and move.</summary>

**Answer:** Look from all sides for deformity and skin signs; feel spinous processes and soft tissues with light percussion; move through flexion, extension and lateral flexion.

## 2. Pain-sensitive structures and disc mechanics

- **Pain-sensitive:** **outer annulus fibrosus, vertebral end plates, facet joints, ligaments, fascia, blood vessels, spinal nerve roots, dura, surrounding muscles.**
- **Flexion** → **compression anteriorly, tension posteriorly** → **nucleus pulposus squeezed posteriorly** toward the **thinnest annulus**; if degenerated the nucleus **herniates into the canal** and compresses the **cord or cauda equina roots.**
- **Intradiscal pressure:** **lowest supine**, **intermediate standing**, **highest sitting flexed forward holding weights**; weights **closer to the body** = lower pressure.
- **Spinal nerves:** **C1-C8, T1-T12, L1-L5, S1-S5, coccygeal**; cord ends as the **conus medullaris**, roots below form the **cauda equina.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pressure: supine lowest, sitting flexed with weights highest.
- Herniation posteriorly where the annulus is thinnest.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which spinal structures are pain sensitive?</summary>

**Answer:** Outer annulus fibrosus, end plates, facet joints, ligaments, fascia, vessels, nerve roots, dura and surrounding muscles.

**Why:** Slide 3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain disc herniation with flexion.</summary>

**Answer:** Flexion compresses the front and stretches the back, pushing the nucleus pulposus posteriorly to the thinnest annulus; if degenerated it herniates into the canal and compresses cord or cauda equina roots.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How does intradiscal pressure vary?</summary>

**Answer:** Lowest supine, intermediate standing, highest sitting flexed with weights; lower when weights are close to the body.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

## 3. Red flags (serious pathology)

- **History:** **age < 20 or > 55; recent significant trauma; non-mechanical pain (infection, tumour, pathological fracture); fever; difficulty in micturition; faecal incontinence; motor weakness; perineal sensory change (saddle anaesthesia); sexual dysfunction; gait change; bilateral sciatica** (cauda equina syndrome).
- **Past history:** **cancer (metastases), previous glucocorticoid use (osteoporotic collapse).** **System review:** **weight loss or malaise.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cauda equina: bladder, bowel, saddle anaesthesia, bilateral sciatica.

</div>

## 4. Lower limb neurological examination

- **Components:** **tone, power, reflexes, sensory, co-ordination** (the slide says 4 but lists 5). **Inspect** for **wasting and fasciculations.**
- **Tone:** roll the leg, **briskly flip the knee**; **heel moves up the bed.** **Ankle clonus:** knee and ankle at **90°**, **dorsiflex and partly evert**; **repeated beats.**
- **Power:** one side then the other; **gravity first, then resistance across a single joint** (hip flexion: resist at the **thigh**). **Hip** flexion and extension; **knee** at **60°**; **ankle** dorsiflexion (resist above the toes) and plantar flexion; **eversion and inversion** with the ankle stabilized.
- **Reflexes:** hammer at the **far end**; **knee L3-L4, ankle S1, plantar S1-S2.**
- **Sensation:** **light touch (cotton, eyes closed, say yes)**, **pinprick (sharp or dull)** by **dermatomes**; also **vibration and proprioception.**
- **Figures:** **lumbar disc tilt:** medial protrusion → tilt **toward** the painful side; far lateral prolapse → tilt **away.** **Stretch test:** SLR limited by root tension; **dorsiflexion (Bragard) increases it; knee flexion relieves it; popliteal pressure causes bowstring pain.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Reflex roots L3-L4, S1, S1-S2.
- Power: gravity first, then resistance.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is power tested?</summary>

**Answer:** Compare sides, test against gravity first and then resistance across a single joint (e.g., resistance on the thigh for hip flexion).

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give the reflexes and roots.</summary>

**Answer:** Knee L3-L4, ankle S1, plantar S1-S2, with the hammer held at the far end of the handle.

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How are light touch and pinprick tested?</summary>

**Answer:** With the eyes closed, cotton wool for light touch (say yes) and a pin for sharp or dull, testing the dermatomes systematically.

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>
