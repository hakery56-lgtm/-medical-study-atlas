## 1. Classification and receptor map

- ⭐ **First ask: does it stimulate the receptor directly or increase endogenous catecholamines?**

| Class | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | **Bind α or β receptors themselves** | **Phenylephrine, salbutamol** |
| **Indirect** | **↑ release, block reuptake or ↓ metabolism of NE** | **Amphetamine, cocaine** |
| **Mixed** | **Receptor stimulation + transmitter release** | **Ephedrine, pseudoephedrine** |

- Effect depends on **receptor selectivity, route and reflex responses.**

| Receptor | Site | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **α1** | **Vessels, iris radial muscle, bladder neck** | **Contraction** (vasoconstriction, mydriasis) |
| **α2** | **Presynaptic / CNS** | **↓ sympathetic outflow and NE release** |
| **β1** | **Heart, juxtaglomerular cells** | **↑ inotropy, chronotropy, renin** |
| **β2** | **Bronchi, uterus, skeletal muscle vessels** | **Smooth muscle relaxation** |
| **β3** | **Detrusor, adipose tissue** | **Bladder relaxation, metabolic effects** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Direct = receptor; indirect = NE availability; mixed = both.
- α1 constricts, α2 central ↓ outflow, β1 heart, β2 relaxes, β3 bladder.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Classify adrenergic agonists with examples.</summary>

**Answer:** Direct (phenylephrine, salbutamol), indirect (amphetamine, cocaine) and mixed (ephedrine, pseudoephedrine).

**Why:** Slide "Adrenergic agonists".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give the effect of each adrenergic receptor.</summary>

**Answer:** α1 contraction (vessels, iris, bladder neck); α2 reduced sympathetic outflow; β1 increased heart output and renin; β2 relaxation of bronchi, uterus and muscle vessels; β3 detrusor relaxation.

**Why:** Slide "Receptor map".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What determines the clinical effect of an agonist?</summary>

**Answer:** Receptor selectivity, route and reflex responses.

**Why:** Slide "Adrenergic agonists".
</details>

## 2. Epinephrine and norepinephrine

| | **Epinephrine** | **Norepinephrine** |
|---|---|---|
| **Receptors** | **α1, α2, β1, β2** (nonselective) | **Strong α1, α2; β1; minimal β2** |
| **Effects** | **↑ HR and contractility (β1); bronchodilation, vasodilation (β2); vasoconstriction ↑ BP (α1)** | **Marked arterial and venous constriction; ↑ SVR and MAP; reflex bradycardia** |
| **Uses** | **Anaphylaxis (IM first line); cardiac arrest; adjunct to local anesthetics** | **First-line vasopressor in septic shock; severe vasodilatory hypotension (IV infusion)** |
| **Adverse** | **Tachycardia, tremor, arrhythmias, hypertension, ischemia** | **Digital ischemia, arrhythmias, extravasation injury** |

- ⭐ **Pearl:** **IM epinephrine in the mid-outer thigh is first line in anaphylaxis.** Norepinephrine raises BP **mainly through α1 vasoconstriction.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Epinephrine: anaphylaxis, cardiac arrest. Norepinephrine: septic shock.
- NE: reflex bradycardia, extravasation injury.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the effects and uses of epinephrine.</summary>

**Answer:** Nonselective α and β agonist: β1 increases heart rate and contractility, β2 causes bronchodilation and vasodilation, α1 causes vasoconstriction; used in anaphylaxis (IM), cardiac arrest and with local anesthetics.

**Why:** Slide "Epinephrine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is norepinephrine the first-line drug in septic shock?</summary>

**Answer:** It is a potent α1 vasoconstrictor that increases systemic vascular resistance and MAP, given by IV infusion with monitoring of MAP, perfusion and IV site.

**Why:** Slide "Norepinephrine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the precautions of norepinephrine?</summary>

**Answer:** Peripheral/digital ischemia, arrhythmias and tissue injury on extravasation; reflex bradycardia may occur.

**Why:** Slide "Norepinephrine".
</details>

## 3. α agonists: phenylephrine, midodrine, clonidine and guanfacine

- ⭐ **Peripheral α1** = vasoconstriction, ↑ BP (reflex bradycardia). **Central α2** = ↓ sympathetic outflow, ↓ BP.
- **Topical α agonists** = decongestion by local vasoconstriction.

| Drug | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Phenylephrine** | **Selective α1**; parenteral for perioperative hypotension when tachycardia limits β agonists; **ophthalmic mydriasis**; topical nasal decongestant (**limit duration, rebound**). ⚠️ **Oral phenylephrine is considered ineffective as an OTC decongestant (FDA review).** |
| **Midodrine** | **Peripheral α1 prodrug** for **symptomatic orthostatic hypotension**; **daytime dosing (3-4 h apart), not near bedtime (supine hypertension)**; monitor **supine BP, urinary retention, piloerection, pruritus, paresthesia.** |
| **Clonidine, guanfacine** | **Central α2 agonists**; hypertension (selected), withdrawal symptoms, some pain/sedation, **ADHD**; adverse: **sedation, dry mouth, constipation, bradycardia, orthostatic hypotension, sexual dysfunction.** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Phenylephrine = selective α1; oral form ineffective.
- Midodrine = daytime only (supine hypertension).
- Clonidine = central α2, sedation and dry mouth.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe phenylephrine and its update.</summary>

**Answer:** A selective α1 agonist raising BP (reflex bradycardia possible) used parenterally in hypotension, for mydriasis and as a topical nasal decongestant; oral phenylephrine is considered ineffective as an OTC decongestant.

**Why:** Slide "Phenylephrine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How is midodrine used safely?</summary>

**Answer:** For orthostatic hypotension in the daytime at 3-4-hour intervals, avoiding bedtime doses to prevent supine hypertension, with monitoring of supine BP, urinary retention, piloerection, pruritus and paresthesia.

**Why:** Slide "Midodrine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe clonidine and guanfacine.</summary>

**Answer:** Central α2 agonists lowering sympathetic outflow; used in hypertension, withdrawal symptoms, pain/sedation and ADHD; adverse effects: sedation, dry mouth, constipation, bradycardia, orthostatic hypotension, sexual dysfunction.

**Why:** Slide "Clonidine and guanfacine".
</details>

## 4. β agonists: dobutamine, β2 agonists and mirabegron

| Drug | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Dobutamine** | **Mainly β1** (modest β2, α1); **↑ stroke volume and CO with limited vasoconstriction**; **IV in acute heart failure, cardiogenic shock, post-cardiac surgery, stress echo**; may **worsen tachyarrhythmias**, ↑ AV conduction in AF. |
| **SABA** (salbutamol, terbutaline) | **Rapid relief of acute bronchospasm**; **inhaled preferred**; adverse: **tremor, palpitations, tachycardia, hypokalemia**; repeated need = **poor asthma control**. |
| **LABA** (salmeterol, formoterol) | **Maintenance, ~12 h**; **formoterol faster onset**; **asthma: always with ICS**; COPD: bronchodilator maintenance, ICS only for selected phenotypes. |
| **Mirabegron** | **β3 agonist**, relaxes detrusor, ↑ capacity, **overactive bladder**; may **↑ BP**; **inhibits CYP2D6 (↑ metoprolol)**, **↑ digoxin**; less dry mouth and constipation than antimuscarinics. |

- ⭐ **Roles:** **rescue (SABA), maintenance (LABA), anti-inflammatory (ICS).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Dobutamine: β1 inotrope for acute HF.
- LABA never alone in asthma.
- Mirabegron: BP and CYP2D6 interactions.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe dobutamine's uses and cautions.</summary>

**Answer:** A β1-predominant inotrope raising stroke volume and cardiac output with little vasoconstriction, used IV in acute heart failure, cardiogenic shock, after cardiac surgery and in stress echo; may worsen tachyarrhythmias and increases AV conduction in AF.

**Why:** Slide "Dobutamine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare SABAs and LABAs.</summary>

**Answer:** SABAs (salbutamol, terbutaline) relieve acute bronchospasm, preferably inhaled; LABAs (salmeterol, formoterol) give about 12 h maintenance, in asthma only with ICS; both cause tremor, palpitations, tachycardia and hypokalemia.

**Why:** Slides "Beta-2 agonists", "SABAs" and "LABAs".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe mirabegron and its precautions.</summary>

**Answer:** A β3 agonist relaxing the detrusor for overactive bladder; may raise BP, inhibits CYP2D6 (raising metoprolol exposure) and increases digoxin levels; causes less dry mouth and constipation than antimuscarinics.

**Why:** Slide "Mirabegron".
</details>

## 5. Indirect and mixed agonists

- ⭐ **Indirect agents amplify the catecholamine signal:** **release enhancers** (displace NE and DA from storage), **reuptake blockers** (inhibit **NET** → NE accumulates), **metabolism interference** (**MAO** breaks down monoamines).

| Drug | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Amphetamines** | **↑ extracellular DA and NE by non-vesicular release**; **ADHD, narcolepsy**; ↑ BP and pulse (α1, β1); **insomnia, appetite suppression, anxiety, tachycardia, dependence.** |
| **Cocaine** | **Local anesthetic; blocks NET**; potentiates catecholamines; toxicity: **hypertension, tachycardia, coronary vasospasm, ischemia, arrhythmias, agitation**; avoid extra catecholamines. |
| **Ephedrine** | **Mixed (direct + NE release)**; **hypotension during anesthesia.** |
| **Pseudoephedrine** | **Nasal decongestant**, **less CNS stimulation than ephedrine.** Both: insomnia, anxiety, tachycardia, raised BP, urinary retention. |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cocaine = NET blocker; amphetamine = releaser.
- Ephedrine for anesthetic hypotension; pseudoephedrine for congestion.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do indirect agonists work?</summary>

**Answer:** By displacing NE and dopamine from storage, blocking NET so NE accumulates, or interfering with MAO-mediated metabolism.

**Why:** Slide "Indirect-acting adrenergic agonists".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe cocaine as a sympathomimetic.</summary>

**Answer:** It blocks NET, potentiating endogenous and administered catecholamines; toxicity includes hypertension, tachycardia, coronary vasospasm, ischemia, arrhythmias and agitation, so extra catecholamines should be avoided.

**Why:** Slide "Cocaine: NET blockade".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare ephedrine and pseudoephedrine.</summary>

**Answer:** Both mixed sympathomimetics; ephedrine treats hypotension during anesthesia; pseudoephedrine is mainly a nasal decongestant with less CNS stimulation.

**Why:** Slide "Ephedrine and pseudoephedrine".
</details>

## 6. Key clinical selection

| Situation | Drug |
|---|---|
| **Cardiac arrest** | **Epinephrine** |
| **Septic shock** | **Norepinephrine infusion** |
| **Acute HF / cardiogenic shock** | **Dobutamine** |
| **Orthostatic hypotension** | **Midodrine (daytime)** |
| **Acute asthma / before exercise** | **Inhaled SABA** |
| **COPD maintenance** | **LABA** |
| **Overactive bladder** | **Mirabegron** |
