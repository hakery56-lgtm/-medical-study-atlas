## 1. The case and first impressions

- **Mariam, 29 years:** **2 days** of **frequent, large-volume watery diarrhea** and **repeated vomiting**; **no visible blood, no severe abdominal pain**; **several people who ate at the same community gathering have similar illness**; she **drinks very little** fearing fluids will worsen diarrhea.
- **First impressions (slide):** **acute onset, shared exposure, high fluid-loss risk.**
- **Major concern:** **dehydration and electrolyte loss.**
- Standard teaching: shared exposure suggests **common-source gastroenteritis** (food or water); no blood or severe pain suggests **non-inflammatory watery diarrhea**.

## 2. Secretory vs osmotic diarrhea (standard teaching)

| Feature | Secretory | Osmotic |
|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | **Active Cl⁻ and water secretion (↑ cAMP/cGMP, CFTR)** | **Unabsorbed solute holds water** |
| **Volume** | **Large** | **Smaller** |
| **Fasting** | **Persists** | **Improves** |
| **Stool osmotic gap** | **Low (under 50)** | **High (over 100)** |
| **Examples** | **Cholera, ETEC, VIPoma, bile acids, laxatives** | **Lactose intolerance, lactulose, Mg salts, sorbitol** |

- **Osmotic gap = 290 − 2 × (stool Na + K).**
- **Cholera toxin:** **permanently activates Gs → ↑ cAMP → CFTR opens → rice-water diarrhea.**
- **Duration:** **acute under 14 days; persistent 14–30 days; chronic over 30 days.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Secretory: large volume, persists with fasting, low gap.
- Osmotic: stops with fasting, high gap.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Explain the pathophysiology of secretory diarrhea.</summary>

**Answer:** A secretagogue raises cAMP/cGMP in enterocytes, opening CFTR so chloride and water are secreted into the lumen.

**Why:** Objective 1 (standard teaching).
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate secretory from osmotic diarrhea.</summary>

**Answer:** Secretory is large-volume, continues during fasting and has a low osmotic gap; osmotic is smaller, stops with fasting and has a high gap.

**Why:** Objective 2.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is the stool osmotic gap calculated?</summary>

**Answer:** 290 minus twice the sum of stool sodium and potassium.

**Why:** Standard teaching.
</details>

## 3. Causes and diagnostic approach (standard teaching)

- **Acute infectious:** **Vibrio cholerae, ETEC, rotavirus, norovirus; preformed toxins (S. aureus, B. cereus) cause early vomiting.**
- **Chronic secretory:** **VIPoma (WDHA: watery diarrhea, hypokalemia, achlorhydria), carcinoid, gastrinoma, bile acid malabsorption, microscopic colitis, laxative abuse, drugs.**
- **Approach to chronic secretory diarrhea:** **confirm chronicity → stool osmotic gap → review drugs/laxatives → stool tests for infection → hormonal tests (e.g., VIP, chromogranin A, 5-HIAA, gastrin) → imaging → endoscopy with biopsy.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- VIPoma = WDHA syndrome.
- Always exclude drugs and laxatives first.

</div>

## 4. Management algorithm and patient safety (standard teaching)

- **Assess dehydration:** thirst, dry mucosa, ↓ turgor, tachycardia, hypotension, ↓ urine.
- **Mild–moderate:** **ORS and continued feeding** (works because **SGLT1 sodium-glucose cotransport stays intact**).
- **Severe, shock or uncontrolled vomiting:** **IV isotonic crystalloid (Ringer lactate or saline)**, then ORS.
- **Monitor** potassium, sodium, bicarbonate and urine output; **hypokalemia and metabolic acidosis** are common.
- **Do not restrict fluids**; **avoid antimotility drugs** with fever or bloody stool; **antibiotics** only for specific causes (e.g., cholera).
- **Drugs:** racecadotril, bismuth; **octreotide** for hormone-secreting tumors.
- **Health promotion:** **safe water, hand washing, food hygiene, early ORS, recognize danger signs.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- ORS first-line; IV for severe cases.
- Never restrict fluids.

</div>
