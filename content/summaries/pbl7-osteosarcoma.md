## 2. Imaging and diagnosis

- **Chest radiograph:** normal.
- **Plain film of lower thigh:** **mixture of lytic and dense areas, poorly defined margins, cortex breached**, **sunburst effect** (new bone streaks radiating from the cortex), **periosteum elevated with new bone at the angle = Codman's triangle**.
- **Radioisotope:** **↑ uptake lower femur**; **CT** confirmed the bone lesion; **MRI** showed bone and soft-tissue extent.
- **Biopsy under general anesthesia:** **atypical osteoid, cartilage, fibroblasts → osteosarcoma.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sunburst + Codman's triangle = aggressive bone-forming tumor.
- Biopsy confirms the diagnosis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the radiological findings.</summary>

**Answer:** Mixed lytic and dense areas with poorly defined margins, cortical breach, sunburst new-bone streaks and a Codman's triangle.

**Why:** Slide 1.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is Codman's triangle?</summary>

**Answer:** Elevated periosteum with new bone formation at the angle where it lifts off the cortex.

**Why:** Slide 1.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How was the diagnosis made?</summary>

**Answer:** Biopsy under general anesthesia showing atypical osteoid tissue, cartilage and fibroblasts, giving osteosarcoma.

**Why:** Slide 1.

## 3. Osteosarcoma (standard teaching)

- **Age/site:** **adolescents and young adults**, **metaphysis of long bones around the knee** (distal femur, proximal tibia).
- **Histology:** **malignant spindle cells producing osteoid.**
- **Spread:** **hematogenous to the lungs** first → check chest imaging.
- **Treatment:** **chemotherapy + limb-salvage surgery or amputation.**
- **Final diagnosis in the case:** **osteosarcoma of the lower ¼ of the femur, no metastasis, no skip lesions.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Metaphysis near the knee; lungs are the main metastatic site.
- Chemotherapy plus surgery.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Where and in whom does osteosarcoma typically occur?</summary>

**Answer:** In adolescents and young adults, at the metaphysis of long bones around the knee.

**Why:** Standard teaching.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Where does it metastasize?</summary>

**Answer:** Hematogenously, most commonly to the lungs.

**Why:** Standard teaching.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What was the final diagnosis?</summary>

**Answer:** Osteosarcoma localized to the lower quarter of the femur without metastasis or skip lesions.

**Why:** Slide 2.
</details>

## 4. Management and psychosocial aspects

- **Options explained:** **above-knee amputation + chemotherapy** or **limb-saving operation + chemotherapy**; complications and prognosis explained.
- **Reaction:** Ali was **angry and in denial** ("nothing bad could happen"), **refusing surgery**; parents convinced him.
- **Decision:** parents **not in favor of amputation** → **limb-saving procedure**; **written consent from the father**; **total excision + reconstruction, then chemotherapy.**

<div class="key-points">

- Limb salvage + chemotherapy chosen.
- Denial is a typical reaction; parental written consent for a minor.

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Sunburst and Codman's triangle belong to osteosarcoma (not just any infection).**
- **ESR can be raised in tumors and mimic osteomyelitis.**
- **Consent for a minor comes from the parent.**
- The slide writes "HB 11.8 mg/dL"; hemoglobin is normally reported in g/dL.
