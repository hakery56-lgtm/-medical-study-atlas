## 1. Learning architecture and the diagnostic paradigm

### Three tiers
| Tier | Content |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Tier 1 — Foundation** (remember/understand) | recall examination steps; identify the **6 fixed general signs**; describe **pallor, jaundice, cyanosis** |
| ⭐ **Tier 2 — Application and analysis** | execute **patient approach and hygiene**; **measure vitals accurately**; **distinguish localized vs generalized pathologies**; analyze the **patient environment** |
| ⭐ **Tier 3 — Synthesis** (evaluate/create) | **prioritize investigations based on general signs**; **critically assess pathological causes**; **design a case presentation linking signs to a systemic diagnosis** |

### The diagnostic paradigm
- ⭐ **Comprehensive history + physical examination (general = primary; systemic = secondary) + targeted investigations = clinical diagnosis.**
- **Crucial insight:** the **general examination often yields far more immediate diagnostic value than the isolated systemic examination.**

## 2. Engagement and survey protocols

### The first 60 seconds
| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1. Greet and introduce** | rapport with the patient and relatives |
| **2. Explain** | the planned examination process |
| **3. Sterilize** | **hand hygiene (washing, sanitizing, gloves)** |
| **4. Assess pain** | ask about **location and severity BEFORE physical contact** |

### The bedside radar (three rings)
- ⭐ **The patient state:** **age, gender, facies, GCS/level of consciousness, obvious abnormalities (limb defects, injuries), eye contact, audible sounds (wheeze, cough), mobility/position in bed.**
- ⭐ **The apparatus:** **oxygen, continuous life-support systems, IVs.**
- ⭐ **The surroundings:** **medications on the bedside table, monitors, nebulizer machines.**

### Examination pathways
- **Pathway A:** **hand → pulse → BP → face → neck → chest → abdomen → lower limbs.**
- **Pathway B:** **face → descending to the limbs.**
- **Execution:** **narrate findings continuously** or **examine silently and summarize at the end.**

### Baseline vitals (example values)
| Vital sign | Example |
|---|---|
| **Temperature** | **37.1 °C** |
| **Pulse** | **72 bpm** |
| **Blood pressure** | **120/80 mmHg** |
| **Respiratory rate** | **16 breaths/min** |
| **SpO₂** | measured with the pulse oximeter |

- First 60 seconds: greet/introduce → explain → sterilize hands → assess pain.
- Bedside radar: patient state, apparatus (oxygen, life support, IVs), surroundings (drugs, monitors, nebulizer).
- Pathway A (hand → pulse → BP → face…) or B (face → limbs); narrate or summarize.
- Baseline: 37.1 °C, 72 bpm, 120/80 mmHg, 16/min.

## 3. The six diagnostic cards

| Card | Definition / key facts |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **01 Pallor** | **pale colour of the skin, most notably the face**; causes **anemia, acute fear, severe pain**; check **palm creases, mouth mucosa, conjunctiva** |
| ⭐ **02 Jaundice** | **yellowish discolouration of skin and mucosa**; occurs when **total serum bilirubin exceeds the age-normal threshold**; check **sclera (scleral icterus), skin, under the tongue** |
| ⭐ **03 Cyanosis** | **bluish discolouration** from **desaturated Hb > 4–5 g/dL**; **central ("real") = tongue**; **peripheral (acrocyanosis) = fingers, frequently cold-induced** |
| ⭐ **04 Edema** | **↑ fluid in tissues, mainly extracellular**; **localized** (local causes, e.g. peripheral lesion; isolated swelling) vs **generalized** (**hepatic, cardiac, renal, intestinal, nutritional; pitting**) |
| ⭐ **05 Clubbing** | **deformity of nail beds** from **cardiac, pulmonary, hepatic, GIT** causes; **normal angle ≈ 160°, clubbed > 180°**; **Stage 1** normal angle + ↑ fluctuancy, **2** loss of angle, **3** ↑ curvature, **4** drumstick; **Schamroth's window test** |
| ⭐ **06 Lymphadenopathy** | examine **systemic and localized node groups**; **cervical:** preauricular, posterior auricular, occipital, tonsillar, superior/posterior cervical, supraclavicular, submandibular, submental; **systemic:** **axillary, epitrochlear, inguinal (horizontal/vertical groups), popliteal** |

### Pitting-edema scale
| Grade | Indentation and rebound |
|---|---|
| **1** | **0–2 mm; rebounds immediately** |
| **2** | **3–4 mm; < 15 s** |
| **3** | **5–6 mm; up to 30 s** |
| **4** | **8 mm (> 8 mm in the figure); > 20 s** |

### Synthesis: from general sign to systemic verdict
- **General signs rarely exist in isolation** — **their intersections form diagnostic blueprints**: **generalized edema + clubbing + jaundice** point toward **cardiac failure, hepatic failure and pulmonary/GIT distress**.
- **Takeaway:** the standardized general physical examination is the **clinician's most powerful tool for early systemic diagnosis**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Six cards: pallor (anemia/fear/pain), jaundice (TSB), cyanosis (Hb > 4–5 g/dL), edema, clubbing (160° → > 180°), lymphadenopathy.
- Central cyanosis = tongue; peripheral = fingers/cold. Pitting grade scale. Schamroth's window.
- Systemic nodes: axillary, epitrochlear, inguinal, popliteal.
- Signs combine: edema + clubbing + jaundice → cardiac/hepatic/pulmonary-GIT disease.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define pallor, jaundice and cyanosis with their sites and causes.</summary>

**Answer:** Pallor: pale skin, mainly of the face (anemia, fear, severe pain), checked in palm creases, mouth mucosa and conjunctiva. Jaundice: yellow skin/mucosa when total serum bilirubin exceeds the age threshold, seen in sclera, skin and under the tongue. Cyanosis: bluish discoloration from desaturated hemoglobin > 4–5 g/dL; central in the tongue, peripheral (acrocyanosis) in the fingers, often from cold.

**Why:** Diagnostic cards 1–3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe clubbing and Schamroth's window.</summary>

**Answer:** A nail-bed deformity from cardiac, pulmonary, hepatic or GIT causes; the normal nail–nail-bed angle is about 160° and becomes > 180°; stages: 1 increased fluctuancy, 2 loss of angle, 3 increased curvature, 4 drumstick appearance. In Schamroth's test the window between two opposed normal fingernails is lost in clubbing.

**Why:** Diagnostic card 5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do you integrate general signs into a systemic diagnosis?</summary>

**Answer:** General signs rarely occur alone; their combinations form diagnostic blueprints — generalized edema, clubbing and jaundice link to cardiac failure, hepatic failure and pulmonary/GIT disease — so the general examination guides early systemic diagnosis and the choice of investigations.

**Why:** Synthesis slide.
</details>
