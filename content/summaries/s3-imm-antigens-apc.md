## 1. Antigens, epitopes and haptens

- ⭐ **Antigen:** a substance that, when introduced into the body, **stimulates antibody production**; mostly **conjugated proteins** (lipoproteins, glycoproteins, nucleoproteins). **Immunogen:** capable of inducing an immune response. **All immunogens are antigens but not all antigens are immunogens.**
- ⭐ **Epitope (antigenic determinant):** the part of the antigen the antibody or T cell binds; each antigen carries **many epitopes** and needs **≥ 2 epitopes** to be immunogenic. Each Y-shaped antibody has **≥ 2 binding sites**; **lock-and-key** fit; binding identical epitopes on two cells aggregates them.
- **Complete antigen:** induces antibody formation and reacts specifically. **Hapten (incomplete antigen):** cannot induce antibody alone; needs a **carrier** (protein).
- **Hapten characteristics:** foreign, usually **non-protein**; needs a carrier (non-antigenic; e.g. **albumin or globulin**); **MW < 10,000**; reacts specifically with its antibody. **Examples:** pneumococcal capsular polysaccharide, polysaccharide C of β-haemolytic streptococci, cardiolipin.
- **Other antigens:** **self/autoantigens** (thyroglobulin, DNA, corneal tissue); **foreign (non-self)**; **alloantigens** (same species, e.g. **ABO**); **heterophile** (identical in different species: **Forssman antigen**, cross-reacting microbial antigens, **Weil-Felix test**).
- **Weil-Felix:** rapid agglutination test for **rickettsial infections** (typhus, spotted fevers): anti-Rickettsia antibodies agglutinate certain non-motile **Proteus** strains.
- **Adjuvants:** non-immunogenic alone but **enhance immunogenicity**; raise antibody titres and prolong production.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- All immunogens are antigens, not all antigens are immunogens.
- Hapten = incomplete antigen, needs a carrier, MW < 10,000.
- Alloantigens (ABO), heterophile antigens (Forssman, Weil-Felix), adjuvants enhance immunogenicity.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Distinguish antigen from immunogen.</summary>

**Answer:** An antigen stimulates antibody production; an immunogen induces an immune response. All immunogens are antigens, but not all antigens are immunogens.

**Why:** Slide 3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Define a hapten and list its characteristics.</summary>

**Answer:** An incomplete antigen that cannot induce antibodies alone but can when coupled to a protein carrier; it is foreign, usually non-protein, of low molecular weight (< 10,000) and reacts specifically with its antibody (e.g. pneumococcal capsular polysaccharide, cardiolipin).

**Why:** Slides 7–8.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare autoantigens, alloantigens and heterophile antigens.</summary>

**Answer:** Autoantigens are the person's own (thyroglobulin, DNA, corneal tissue); alloantigens differ between members of the same species (ABO); heterophile antigens are identical in different species (Forssman, Weil-Felix).

**Why:** Slides 9–10.
</details>

## 2. Antigenicity, immunogenicity and the H antigen

- **Antigenicity:** ability to **react** with products of a specific immune response (antibody or TCR). **Immunogenicity:** ability to **provoke** a specific immune response.
- **Determinants of immunogenicity:** **(1) Foreignness** (self is not immunogenic); **(2) Size** (potent = large proteins; **< 10,000 Da** weak; amino acids non-immunogenic; haptens need carriers); **(3) Chemical and structural complexity** (proteins and polysaccharides most potent; **heteropolymers > homopolymers**); **(4) Genetic constitution of the host**; **(5) Dose, route and timing.**
- **H antigen:** on red cells of all ABO groups; **precursor of A and B**; not ordinarily important in grouping. **Bombay (Oh) group** (Bhende, Mumbai, 1952): **no A, B or H**; has **anti-A, anti-B and anti-H**; can accept blood **only from its own group.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Antigenicity = react; immunogenicity = provoke.
- Five determinants: foreignness, size, complexity, host genetics, dose/route/timing.
- Bombay group: no H antigen; accepts only Bombay blood.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the determinants of immunogenicity.</summary>

**Answer:** Foreignness, size, chemical and structural complexity, genetic constitution of the host, and dosage, route and timing of administration.

**Why:** Slides 11–14.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How does size affect immunogenicity?</summary>

**Answer:** Large proteins are most potent; molecules below 10,000 Da are weakly immunogenic; very small ones (amino acids) are non-immunogenic; haptens become immunogenic only linked to a carrier.

**Why:** Slide 12.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the Bombay blood group?</summary>

**Answer:** A rare group lacking A, B and H antigens with anti-A, anti-B and anti-H antibodies; these people can accept blood only from their own group.

**Why:** Slide 15.
</details>

## 3. Antigen presenting cells

- ⭐ **APCs** engulf antigen and present fragments to T cells. **Three types:** **macrophages, dendritic cells, B cells.**
- **Macrophages:** usually **resting**, much more phagocytic when **activated**; in most lymphoid tissues (**monocytes** = blood macrophages; **histiocytes** = tissue macrophages).
- **Dendritic cells:** **long cytoplasmic processes**; very effective antigen-trapping and presenting cells; in **lymph nodes, spleen, thymus, skin.** Types: **Langerhans cells** (epidermis, trap organisms at the body surface), **spleen dendritic cells** (antigen in blood), **follicular dendritic cells** (lymph nodes, antigen in lymph). **Steinman: Nobel Prize 2011.** Note: slide 18 calls dendritic cells non-phagocytic while the MHC II slides describe a dendritic cell phagocytosing a bacterium; this inconsistency is not examined.
- **B cells:** membrane **Ig = antigen receptor**; **identical receptors on one B cell, so each binds only one antigen** → more efficient APCs than macrophages, which ingest anything; descendants = **plasma cells** (antibodies).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- APCs: macrophages, dendritic cells, B cells.
- Dendritic cell types: Langerhans (skin), spleen (blood), follicular (lymph).
- B cell = one antigen specificity per cell.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name the APCs and their roles.</summary>

**Answer:** Macrophages, dendritic cells and B cells engulf or capture antigen and present fragments to T cells (and macrophages and dendritic cells also present to B cells) to initiate the immune response.

**Why:** Slides 16–19.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Classify dendritic cells.</summary>

**Answer:** Langerhans cells of the epidermis (trap surface organisms), dendritic cells of the spleen (blood antigen) and follicular dendritic cells of lymph nodes (lymph antigen).

**Why:** Slide 18.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why are B cells efficient APCs?</summary>

**Answer:** Their surface Ig receptors are identical, so each B cell binds a single antigen; macrophages must ingest any foreign material.

**Why:** Slide 20.
</details>

## 4. MHC molecules and antigen presentation

- ⭐ **MHC** = genes on the **short arm of chromosome 6**, coding molecules on **all nucleated cells** (HLA in humans); **mature red cells** do not express MHC.

| | **MHC I** | **MHC II** |
|---|---|---|
| **Found on** | **All nucleated cells** | **Macrophages, dendritic cells, B cells** only |
| **Presents** | Self and non-self antigens to **effector (CD8) T cells** | Non-self antigens for **initial T cell activation (CD4)** |
| **Structure** | Long **α chain + β₂-microglobulin**; only α spans the membrane; domains **α1, α2, α3** | **α and β chains** of similar length, both span the membrane; domains **α1, α2, β1, β2** |
| **Cleft formed by** | **α1 and α2** | **α1 and β1** |

- **MHC II presentation:** phagocytosis → **phagosome** + lysosome = **phagolysosome** → **proteases** process **protein** epitopes → the **most antigenic (immune-dominant)** epitopes load into the MHC II cleft → surface → **T cell activation.**
- **MHC I presentation:** cytoplasmic proteins degraded by **proteasomes** → self epitopes on MHC I = "normal self" (NK cells do not attack). A **virus-infected cell** presents pathogen peptides on MHC I → **marked for destruction.**
- **Cross-presentation:** when the intracellular pathogen does not infect the APC; antigen enters by the **MHC II route (phagocytosis)** but is presented on **MHC I to CD8 T cells**; mainly **dendritic cells.** Two hypothetical models: **special antigen-processing machinery loading exogenous peptides onto MHC I**, or **specialized endocytosis sending antigen directly to an endosome where MHC I is loaded.**
- **Endogenous antigens → CD8 T cells (MHC I); exogenous antigens → CD4 T cells (MHC II).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- MHC I: all nucleated cells, α + β₂m, cleft α1/α2, CD8. MHC II: APCs only, α + β, cleft α1/β1, CD4.
- MHC I: proteasome (endogenous). MHC II: phagolysosome and proteases (exogenous).
- Cross-presentation: dendritic cells, exogenous antigen on MHC I to CD8.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare MHC I and MHC II.</summary>

**Answer:** MHC I is on all nucleated cells (α chain with β₂-microglobulin, cleft α1/α2) and presents to CD8 effector T cells; MHC II is only on macrophages, dendritic cells and B cells (α and β chains, cleft α1/β1) and presents non-self antigen to activate CD4 T cells.

**Why:** Slides 21–24.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe antigen presentation by MHC II on a dendritic cell.</summary>

**Answer:** The bacterium is phagocytosed into a phagosome, fuses with a lysosome to form a phagolysosome, proteases process protein antigens, the most antigenic epitopes load into the MHC II cleft and are presented on the surface to T cells.

**Why:** Slides 26–28.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is cross-presentation?</summary>

**Answer:** Exogenous antigen taken up by phagocytosis (MHC II route) but presented on MHC I to CD8 T cells, mainly by dendritic cells, when an intracellular pathogen does not directly infect the APC.

**Why:** Slides 31–33.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **All immunogens are antigens, NOT all antigens are immunogens.**
- **Hapten = incomplete antigen; it needs a carrier; MW < 10,000.**

- **MHC I cleft = α1 + α2; MHC II cleft = α1 + β1.**
- **Endogenous → MHC I → CD8; exogenous → MHC II → CD4.**
- **Only PROTEIN epitopes are presented by MHC II.**
- **Bombay group lacks H, A and B antigens.**
- **MHC genes lie on the SHORT arm of chromosome 6.**
