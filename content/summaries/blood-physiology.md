## 1. Blood and its components

- ⭐ **Circulatory system** = the **transport system**: supplies **O2 and absorbed GI substances** to tissues, **returns CO2 to the lungs** and other metabolic products to the **kidneys**, helps **regulate body temperature**, and **distributes hormones** and agents that regulate cell function.
- **Hematology** = study of the **blood-forming tissues and circulating blood components**.
- **Blood** = fluid circulating in the cardiovascular system; **adult ≈ 5 L = 5–7% of body weight**; two major elements: **cellular elements** and **fluid elements (plasma)**.

### Cellular elements
| Cell | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Red blood cells (erythrocytes)** | Carry **O2 and CO2** between lungs and tissues via **hemoglobin (Hb)**; **red when O2 is attached to Hb**; **short life span because they lack a nucleus** |
| **White blood cells (leukocytes)** | **Colorless, nucleated**; **protection against invading organisms** |
| **Platelets (thrombocytes)** | **Non-nucleated, disc-shaped**; **prevent leakage/bleeding from vessel walls** |

### Fluid elements — plasma
- **Plasma** = fluid portion of blood in which cellular elements are suspended; made of **water, electrolytes (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, H⁺, Ca⁺⁺) and proteins**.
- **Proteins:** **coagulation protein (fibrinogen)**, **immunoproteins (antibodies)**, **transport proteins (albumin, transferrin)**.
- **Volumes:** plasma **2.6 L** + blood cells **2.4 L** = total **5 L** (plasma is a little more than half). After **centrifugation**: **plasma 55%**, **buffy coat < 1%** (neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, monocytes, lymphocytes, platelets), **erythrocytes 45%**.
- Note: the slide lists plasma "water 70%" and "Mn⁺⁺" — textbooks give water ≈ 90% and Mg⁺⁺; know the slide wording for this lecture but be aware of the difference.

### Plasma vs serum
- **Serum** = clear fluid that separates from blood **upon coagulation** when all cellular elements are removed.
- ⭐ **Serum = Plasma − Fibrinogen.**

### Anticoagulants
| Anticoagulant | Use |
|---|---|
| **EDTA (Na⁺ or K⁺ salts)** | **Hematological work** |
| **Tri-sodium citrate** | **Coagulation studies** |
| **Heparin** | **Fragility test** |
| **Acid citrate dextrose (ACD)** | **Blood transfusion** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Blood ≈ 5 L (5–7% of body weight) = cells (RBC, WBC, platelets) + plasma.
- Plasma 2.6 L + cells 2.4 L; centrifuge: plasma 55%, buffy coat < 1%, RBC 45%.
- Serum = plasma − fibrinogen; EDTA → hematology, citrate → coagulation, heparin → fragility, ACD → transfusion.

</div>

## 2. Blood collection — capillary blood

- ⭐ Blood can be collected from **3 sources: capillary, venous and arterial**. **Tools:** tourniquet, vacutainer or syringe, alcohol swab, bandage/medi-plast.
- Most experiments need **only a drop** from a **fingertip puncture**; more blood → **venipuncture** with needle and syringe.
- **Capillary sites:** **fingertip, heel, earlobe**; in **infants: heel or big toe**. The blood has **no anticoagulant → use quickly before it clots** (special **heparinized capillary tubes** for special investigations).

### Finger (capillary) puncture — procedure
1. **Sterilize** skin around the site with a cotton pad soaked in **70% alcohol**.
2. **Wipe** with dry sterile gauze.
3. **Puncture with a sterile lancet** — **quick, single, sufficiently deep stroke**; **gentle pressure at the proximal part of the finger** (obstructs venous return) enhances flow; **squeezing or milking dilutes the blood with tissue fluid**.
4. **Discard the first drop** by wiping it away with dry gauze.
5. **Collect** the required amount with an appropriate instrument, **pipette or slide**.
6. **Leave the site undisturbed**; if bleeding continues, gauze + gentle pressure.
- **Diagram:** prick **2–3 mm deep, midway between the edge and the midpoint of the fingertip, not near the nails, avoid excessive squeezing**; **avoid the earlobe** (slower blood flow → may give **raised Hb**); **don't take blood from the back of the heel**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 3 sources: capillary, venous, arterial.
- Fingertip/heel/earlobe (infants: heel/big toe); no anticoagulant → work quickly.
- Steps: 70% alcohol → dry gauze → lancet → discard first drop → collect → pressure.
- Don't squeeze (dilutes with tissue fluid); earlobe → raised Hb.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Blood can come from capillary, venous or arterial sources; capillary blood is taken from the fingertip, heel (and big toe in infants) or earlobe.

## 3. Venipuncture and arterial blood

- ⭐ **Venous blood:** from the veins in the **bend of the elbow (antecubital fossa)**; a **tourniquet above the site** obstructs **venous return while permitting arterial flow**. **Other sites** for children and dehydrated/collapsed patients. **Needle: sterile, sharp, not smaller than 20 gauge.**

### Venipuncture — steps
1. **Inspect** the forearm and **identify the vein**.
2. **Sterilize** with 70% alcohol (surgical spirit).
3. **Wipe** with dry sterile gauze.
4. Prepare **syringe and needle aseptically — no air in the syringe**.
5. **Puncture just closer to the vein (not above it)**; push the needle **2–3 mm under the skin**.
6. **Fix the syringe and draw** the needed blood.
7. **Release the tourniquet first**, place dry gauze, **gentle pressure**, withdraw the needle; subject **holds the gauze ~5 minutes**.
- **Alternative sites:** **dorsum (hand) vein, femoral vein, jugular vein, scalp vein.**
- **Precautions:** proper sterilization; **tourniquet not > 1 minute** to avoid **stasis** → **↑ concentration of RBCs, hemoglobin, albumin and calcium**.

### Arterial blood
- Needed for **blood gases (ABG): pH, CO2 and O2**; sites: **radial, brachial, temporal, femoral arteries**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Antecubital vein + tourniquet; needle ≥ 20 gauge; no air; 2–3 mm; release tourniquet FIRST.
- Other sites: dorsum, femoral, jugular, scalp.
- Tourniquet ≤ 1 min (stasis ↑ RBC, Hb, albumin, Ca).
- Arterial blood for ABG (pH, CO2, O2): radial, brachial, temporal, femoral.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the venipuncture procedure.</summary>

**Answer:** Apply a tourniquet above the site, identify the vein in the forearm, sterilize with 70% alcohol and wipe dry, prepare an air-free syringe with a sterile sharp needle (≥ 20 gauge), puncture near (not above) the vein 2–3 mm under the skin, draw the blood, release the tourniquet first, apply gauze with gentle pressure and withdraw the needle; the subject holds the gauze for about five minutes.

**Why:** Slides "Venipuncture procedure".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What precautions apply and what does stasis do?</summary>

**Answer:** Clean the area properly and do not leave the tourniquet on for more than 1 minute; stasis raises the concentration of RBCs, hemoglobin, albumin and calcium.

**Why:** Slides "Precaution" and "Stasis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. When is arterial blood needed and from where is it taken?</summary>

**Answer:** For blood gas estimation (pH, CO2, O2); from superficial arteries such as the radial, brachial, temporal or femoral artery.

**Why:** Slides "Arterial blood".
</details>

## 4. Blood collection tubes

- Tubes are **color-coded plastic tubes with a rubber stopper**; they contain **anticoagulants and/or additives**; **plain tubes have no anticoagulant**; **all tubes must be mixed thoroughly**; **label with patient's name, number, time of collection and date**.

| Tube | Additive / action | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **EDTA (purple)** | **Na or K2 EDTA** (1.5–0.25 mg/ml); **forms Ca salts → removes Ca**; **full draw, invert 8 times** | **Hematology: CBC, PCR, HbA1c** |
| **Light blue** | **Sodium citrate (1:9)**, 32 g/l; **removes calcium** | **Coagulation studies, platelet function** |
| **Dark green** | **Sodium or lithium heparin**; **inactivates thrombin and thromboplastin** | **Lithium level → use Na heparin; ammonia → Na or Li heparin** |
| **Red (plain)** | **No preservative/anticoagulant** | **Blood bank, toxicology, serology** |
| **SST / gold top** | **No additives; clotting accelerator + separation gel** | **Chemistry, immunology, serology** |
| **PST / light green** | **Lithium heparin** (plasma separating tube) | **Chemistries** |
| **Black (ESR)** | **Sodium citrate 1:4 (3.8%)**; **removes calcium** | **Westergren ESR** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- EDTA (full draw, invert 8×) → CBC/PCR/HbA1c; light blue citrate 1:9 → coagulation; black citrate 1:4 → ESR.
- Dark green heparin (inactivates thrombin/thromboplastin); lithium level needs Na heparin.
- Red = plain; SST = accelerator + gel; PST = Li heparin.
- Label every tube: name, number, time, date.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the EDTA, light blue and black tubes.</summary>

**Answer:** All remove calcium: EDTA (purple) for hematology (CBC, PCR, HbA1c; full draw, invert 8 times); light blue sodium citrate 1:9 for coagulation studies and platelet function; black sodium citrate 1:4 (3.8%) for the Westergren ESR.

**Why:** Slides "Anticoagulant tube", "Light blue", "Black tube".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What does the dark green tube contain and which heparin is used for lithium and ammonia levels?</summary>

**Answer:** Sodium or lithium heparin, which inactivates thrombin and thromboplastin; use sodium heparin for a lithium level, and sodium or lithium heparin for ammonia.

**Why:** Slide "Dark green".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare the red, SST (gold) and PST (light green) tubes.</summary>

**Answer:** The red tube is plain (no additive; blood bank, toxicology, serology); SST has a clotting accelerator and separation gel (chemistry, immunology, serology); PST is a plasma separating tube with lithium heparin (chemistries).

**Why:** Slides "Red", "SST/Gold top", "PST/Light green".
</details>
