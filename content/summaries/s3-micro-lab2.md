## 2. Culture media — definition and ingredients

- **Culture medium** = a **mixture of nutrients suitable for the growth of microorganisms**; used for **recognition and identification**; bacteria need **organic and inorganic material**.
- Contained in **sterile test tubes, plates (Petri dishes), flasks or screw-capped bottles**; **sterilized by moist heat**.

### Basic ingredients
| Ingredient | Role |
|---|---|
| **Peptone** | **source of protein** |
| **Meat extract** | **source of nitrogen**; lean meat + water over 24 h (cold meat extract) |
| **NaCl** | **isotonic environment** |
| **H₂O** | **humidity** |
| ⭐ **Agar-agar** | **unbranched polysaccharide from seaweed**, **no nutritional value**, used for **solidifying**; **melting point 92–95 °C**, **solidifying point 42–45 °C**, **concentration 1.5–2 %**; **not digested** by microorganisms |
| **pH** | most pathogenic bacteria need **pH 7.2–7.4**, adjusted with **NaOH or HCl** |

### Uses of media
- **Simple media:** only common ingredients; for **non-fastidious** organisms (e.g. **nutrient broth and agar**).
- **Special-purpose media:** common ingredients **+ an additional material**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Medium = nutrient mixture; sterilized by moist heat; held in tubes, plates, flasks, bottles.
- Ingredients: peptone (protein), meat extract (nitrogen), NaCl (isotonic), water, agar (solidifier), pH 7.2–7.4.
- Agar: seaweed polysaccharide; melts 92–95 °C; solidifies 42–45 °C; 1.5–2 %; not digested.

</div>

## 3. Types of culture media

### A. By chemical composition
- **Synthetic (chemically defined):** exact chemical composition known.
- **Complex:** a formula **cannot** be written for each ingredient.

### B. By laboratory use
| Type | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Supportive (basic/general-purpose/basal)** | support a wide variety of **non-fastidious** organisms, no special advantage to a group | **nutrient agar** |
| ⭐ **Enriched** | simple medium **+ blood 5–10 %, glucose 1–2 %, serum/ascitic fluid 10 %** for **fastidious** bacteria (e.g. **streptococci**) | **blood agar** (also differentiates by **hemolytic properties**), **chocolate agar** |
| ⭐ **Enrichment** | favours growth of a particular organism from a mixed sample | **tetrathionate broth** |
| ⭐ **Selective** | contain **inhibitory substances (bile salts, antibiotics, dyes)** → favour some organisms, inhibit others | **MacConkey agar, SS agar** |
| ⭐ **Differential** | different species give **characteristic growth/effects**; **MacConkey contains lactose** → separates Gram-negative bacteria into **lactose fermenters and non-fermenters** | **MacConkey, blood agar** |
| ⭐ **Transport** | **buffer solutions with carbohydrates, peptones, nutrients (no growth factors)** to **preserve viability during transport** | **Stuart's medium** |
| **Indicator** | **colour change of an indicator** due to metabolism | **sugar media with Durham's tube** |
| **Sensitivity** | to test **antibiotic sensitivity** | **Mueller-Hinton medium** (with antibiotic discs) |

### C. By consistency
| Type | Use and features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Liquid (broth)** | **primary cultivation**; **cannot show colony morphology** | **nutrient broth, peptone water, brain-heart infusion** |
| **Solid** | **colony morphology**; **agar 1.5–2 %** | **nutrient agar, MacConkey agar, blood agar** |
| **Semisolid** | **study motility**; **agar 0.4–0.8 %** | — |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Chemical: synthetic (defined) vs complex.
- Use: supportive (nutrient agar), enriched (blood agar), enrichment (tetrathionate broth), selective (MacConkey, SS), differential (MacConkey: lactose), transport (Stuart's), indicator (Durham tube), sensitivity (Mueller-Hinton).
- Consistency: liquid (no colony morphology), solid (1.5–2 % agar), semisolid (0.4–0.8 %; motility).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Classify culture media according to laboratory use with examples.</summary>

**Answer:** Supportive (nutrient agar), enriched (blood agar, chocolate agar), enrichment (tetrathionate broth), selective (MacConkey, SS agar), differential (MacConkey, blood agar), transport (Stuart's), indicator (sugar media with Durham's tube) and sensitivity (Mueller-Hinton) media.

**Why:** Slide "Based on laboratory use".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate selective from differential media using MacConkey agar.</summary>

**Answer:** Selective media contain inhibitory substances (bile salts, dyes, antibiotics) that favour some organisms and inhibit others; differential media produce recognizable differences between species. MacConkey agar is both: it selects Gram-negative bacteria and, because it contains lactose, differentiates lactose fermenters from non-fermenters.

**Why:** One medium can serve both purposes.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare liquid, solid and semisolid media.</summary>

**Answer:** Liquid broth is used for primary cultivation but cannot show colony morphology (nutrient broth, peptone water, brain-heart infusion); solid media contain 1.5–2 % agar and show colony morphology (nutrient, MacConkey and blood agar); semisolid media contain 0.4–0.8 % agar and are used to study motility.

**Why:** Agar concentration defines consistency.
</details>

- **Broth cannot show colony morphology.**
