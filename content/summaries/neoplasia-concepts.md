## 1. Definitions, genes and nomenclature

- **Neoplasia** = neo + plasia = **new growth**; **neoplasm** = the new growth (benign or malignant); **tumor** = swelling (now = neoplasm); **oncology** = study of neoplasia; **cancer** = **Karkinos (crab)**, infiltrative erosive growth = all malignant neoplasms.
- **Willis (1952):** abnormal tissue mass whose growth **exceeds and is uncoordinated with normal tissue** and **persists after the stimulus ceases.**
- **Tumor growth** depends on the **growth fraction** and **rate of cell death**. Body cells: **labile, stable, permanent**; **G1 and S** take most of the cycle.
- **Genes:** **proto-oncogenes = gas pedal** (mutate to **oncogenes**); **tumor suppressor genes = brakes** (repair, apoptosis). **More than one gene** must change (normal → benign → malignant); factors: **intrinsic (inherited)** and **extrinsic (environment)**.
- **Autonomous** growth but dependent on host for **nutrition and blood supply**; persistence after stimulus separates it from **hyperplasia/inflammation**.
- **Parts:** **stroma** (support, blood supply) and **parenchyma** (neoplastic cells, **determine behavior**).
- **Naming:** by **cell/organ** (hepatocellular carcinoma, osteoma, myoma) or **pattern** (cyst, **adeno** = glandular, **papillo** = finger-like, **polyp** = stalk); **squamous cell carcinoma** vs **adenocarcinoma**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Proto-oncogenes = gas pedal; suppressor genes = brakes.
- Parenchyma = behavior; stroma = blood supply.

</div>

## 2. Types of neoplasms

| Category | Benign | Malignant |
|---|---|---|
| **Epithelial** | **Papilloma, adenoma** | **Carcinoma (squamous, basal cell, transitional, adenocarcinoma)** |
| **Connective tissue** | **Leiomyoma, fibroma, osteoma, chondroma, lipoma, neurofibroma, neurilemmoma** | **Sarcomas: leiomyo-, fibro-, osteo-, chondro-, lipo-sarcoma** |
| **Lymphoid/marrow** | — | **Lymphoma (Hodgkin, non-Hodgkin), leukemia** |
| **Germ cell** | **Mature cystic teratoma (dermoid)** | **Teratoma, seminoma (testis)** |

- **Hamartoma:** **disorganized mass of mature native tissue** (lung hamartoma). **Choristoma:** **normal tissue in an abnormal site** (pancreas in stomach/duodenum/small bowel).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Carcinoma = epithelial; sarcoma = connective tissue.
- Hamartoma = native disorganized; choristoma = heterotopic.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name benign and malignant connective tissue tumors.</summary>

**Answer:** Benign: leiomyoma, fibroma, osteoma, chondroma, lipoma; malignant counterparts are leiomyosarcoma, fibrosarcoma, osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma and liposarcoma.

**Why:** Slides 13–14.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate hamartoma from choristoma.</summary>

**Answer:** A hamartoma is a disorganized overgrowth of mature tissue native to the site; a choristoma is normal tissue from another part located in a foreign site.

**Why:** Slide 17.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give examples of germ cell tumors.</summary>

**Answer:** Mature cystic teratoma (dermoid cyst) of the ovary, and teratoma or seminoma of the testis.

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

## 3. Benign vs malignant; anaplasia; dysplasia

| Feature | Benign | Malignant |
|---|---|---|
| **Differentiation** | **Well differentiated** | **Well → anaplastic** |
| **Growth** | **Slow, expansile, often capsule, compressive atrophy** | **Rapid, infiltrative** |
| **Nuclei** | **Minimal variation, diploid** | **Pleomorphic, variable ploidy** |
| **Mitoses** | **Few, normal** | **Increased, abnormal** |
| **Spread** | **Localized** | **Invasion and metastasis** |

- **Anaplasia** = loss of differentiation; features: **pleomorphism, large hyperchromatic nuclei, ↑ N/C ratio (1:1 vs normal 1:4–1:6), anisonucleosis/poikilonucleosis, abnormal (tripolar/multipolar) mitoses, loss of polarity, tumor giant cells, necrosis.**
- **Mitosis alone ≠ malignancy** (bone marrow, hyperplasia).
- **Dysplasia:** **pre-neoplastic epithelial change** (↑ growth, atypia, altered differentiation); **cervix, bladder, breast**; may or may not progress; **severe dysplasia = carcinoma in situ** (no basement membrane invasion).
- Some well-differentiated tumors can still be invasive or metastatic — evaluate all features.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- N/C ratio 1:1 in malignancy.
- Carcinoma in situ = confined to epithelium.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the features of anaplasia.</summary>

**Answer:** Pleomorphism, large hyperchromatic nuclei, high N/C ratio, abnormal mitoses, loss of polarity, tumor giant cells and necrosis.

**Why:** Slides 22–27.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Define dysplasia and carcinoma in situ.</summary>

**Answer:** Dysplasia is pre-neoplastic epithelial change with increased growth, atypia and altered differentiation; severe dysplasia without basement membrane invasion is carcinoma in situ.

**Why:** Slides 30 and 33.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is mitosis alone not diagnostic?</summary>

**Answer:** Normal or hyperplastic tissues such as bone marrow also have high mitotic rates; abnormal mitoses with other features suggest malignancy.

**Why:** Slide 26.
</details>

## 4. Tumor development, clonality and growth control

- **Multiple mutations** needed (e.g., colonic cancer); **tumor progression** normal → benign → malignant.
- **Clonality:** **monoclonal** (single altered cell); shown with **G6PD** in heterozygous women (mosaicism → tumor has one isoenzyme).
- **Growth control:** ↑ proliferation (more cells enter cycle, faster doubling time), **↓ apoptosis**, altered metabolism, **angiogenesis**; altered **cell–cell and cell–stroma** interactions.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Monoclonal origin (G6PD).
- ↑ proliferation + ↓ apoptosis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is clonality and how is it shown?</summary>

**Answer:** Tumors arise from one altered cell; in heterozygous women a tumor contains only one G6PD isoenzyme.

**Why:** Slide 37.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How do tumor cells differ in growth control?</summary>

**Answer:** More cells enter a faster cycle, apoptosis falls, metabolism is modified and angiogenesis occurs, with altered adhesion and stroma interactions.

**Why:** Slides 38–40.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why are multiple genes involved?</summary>

**Answer:** Sequential alterations are needed to move from normal to benign to malignant cells.

**Why:** Slide 35.
</details>

## 5. Invasion and metastasis

- **Invasion:** breaking through the **basement membrane** and spreading through stroma; **mechanisms:** **↓ adhesion, enzymes digesting basement membrane/stroma, motility factors.**
- **Metastasis:** **secondary remote implants — hallmark of malignancy**; **routes: tumor seeding, lymphatic, hematogenous.**
- **Seed and soil:** failure due to **incorrect receptors, metabolic products, failed angiogenesis (VEGF)**.
- **Lymphatic:** frequent for **carcinomas**. **Hematogenous:** **lung, liver, bone, brain**. **Seeding:** **peritoneal** (also pleural, pericardial, subarachnoid, joint).
- **To lung:** sarcomas (osteosarcoma), breast, stomach, large intestine, kidney, malignant testicular teratoma. **To liver:** large-bowel (portal vein), bronchial, breast. **To bone:** bronchial, breast, thyroid, prostate, renal. **To brain:** bronchial, breast, testicular, melanoma.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Metastasis = hallmark of malignancy.
- Colon → liver via the portal vein.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Explain how tumor cells invade.</summary>

**Answer:** Through reduced adhesion, enzymes that digest the basement membrane and stroma, and motility factors.

**Why:** Slide 42.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Name routes of metastasis with examples.</summary>

**Answer:** Seeding (peritoneal cavity), lymphatic spread (carcinomas) and hematogenous spread to lung, liver, bone and brain.

**Why:** Slides 43–51.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why do some tumors fail to metastasize?</summary>

**Answer:** Seed-and-soil factors: incorrect receptors, unsuitable metabolic products or failure of angiogenesis.

**Why:** Slide 45.
</details>

## 6. Effects, grading and staging

- **Local benign:** compression/pressure atrophy (**pituitary, thyroid adenoma**), obstruction, ulceration/bleeding, space occupying. **Malignant:** + tissue destruction.
- **Systemic:** **hematological** (anemia, ↓ WBCs/platelets, **thrombosis** — pancreatic carcinoma), **endocrine** (**ectopic ACTH, PTH, ADH** — **small cell bronchial carcinoma**), **cutaneous** (pigmentation, pruritus, herpes zoster, **dermatomyositis**), **neuromuscular** (neuropathies, myopathy, PML), **cachexia, malaise, pyrexia**.
- **Grading:** differentiation, **1 (well) → 4 (anaplastic)**; **Gleason** (prostate), **Nottingham** (breast: tubule formation, nuclear pleomorphism, mitotic activity).
- **Staging:** extent of spread, **I–IV**; **most powerful predictor of survival**. **TNM:** **T 0–4, N 0–4, M 0/1**; **c** clinical / **p** pathologic. **Types:** clinical, pathological, post-therapy, recurrence.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Grade = differentiation; stage = spread.
- Small cell lung carcinoma → ectopic ACTH.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List systemic effects of neoplasms.</summary>

**Answer:** Anemia, low WBCs/platelets and thrombosis; endocrine effects (ectopic hormones); cutaneous effects; neuromuscular effects; and cachexia, malaise and pyrexia.

**Why:** Slides 54–56.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate grading from staging.</summary>

**Answer:** Grading describes cell differentiation (1 to 4); staging describes extent of spread (I to IV, TNM) and is the strongest predictor of survival.

**Why:** Slides 57 and 60.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Explain the TNM system.</summary>

**Answer:** T = primary tumor (0–4), N = regional lymph nodes (0–4), M = metastasis (0 absent, 1 present), with c or p for clinical or pathologic.

**Why:** Slide 61.
</details>

- **Mitoses alone do not prove malignancy; abnormal mitoses do.**
- **Grade ≠ stage.**
- **Hamartoma ≠ choristoma.**
