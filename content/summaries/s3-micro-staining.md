## 1. Why we stain, types of stains and dyes

- ⭐ **Why stain?** Bacteria are **microscopic and colorless**, so they must be made **more easily visible** to study their **structure, shape and other characteristics**.
- **Staining lets us observe:** **shape and arrangement, Gram reactions, endospore production, capsule production, pathogenic bacteria.**

### Classification of staining methods
| Stain | Dyes | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple stain** | **One dye** | Reacts with different bacteria in an **identical fashion** |
| **Differential stain** | **Two or more dyes** | Reacts **differently** with different bacteria → **varying results** |
| **Structural stain** | Special stains | Examine **internal or external structures** of the cell (**capsular stain, flagellar stain**) |

### Types of dyes
| | **Basic dye** | **Acidic dye** |
|---|---|---|
| **Charge** | **Positive (cationic)** | **Negative (anionic)** |
| **Behavior** | Works best at **neutral or alkaline pH**; the cell wall has a **slight negative charge at pH 7**, so it attracts the dye | Chromophore is **repelled by the negative cell wall** → **background stained, bacteria colorless** |
| **Examples** | **Crystal violet, methylene blue, safranin** | **Eosin, India ink** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Bacteria are colorless → staining shows shape, arrangement, Gram reaction, spores, capsules.
- Simple = 1 dye; differential = ≥ 2 dyes; structural = capsule/flagella stains.
- Basic dye = cationic, stains the cell; acidic dye = anionic, stains the background.

</div>

## 2. Smear preparation, heat fixation and simple stain

- ⭐ **Smear preparation is ALWAYS the first step** of any microbial stain: **spread microbial cells on a clean glass slide, air dry, then heat fix.**
- **Benefits of heat fixation:** **1) kills the organism; 2) makes the organisms adhere to the slide; 3) alters the organisms so they accept stains more readily.**
- **Simple stain:** **one dye, one step**; a **direct stain using a basic or acidic dye**; used to study **morphology and structure**.
- **Two methods:** **positive staining** and **negative staining** (acidic dye → background stained, bacteria colorless = negative staining).
- A simple stain gives **basic information about cell shape and arrangement** using **only one basic dye**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- First step = smear (spread → air dry → heat fix).
- Heat fixation: kills, adheres, makes cells stain better.
- Simple stain = one dye, one step; positive or negative staining.

</div>

## 3. The Gram stain

- ⭐ **Purpose:** the **first step in bacterial identification**; **distinguishes Gram-positive from Gram-negative cells**; helps identify **morphology and cellular arrangement**. It is the **most common and important differential stain**.

### Principle
| Step | Reagent | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Crystal violet** (primary stain) | **All cells violet** |
| 2 | **Gram's iodine** (mordant) | **Fixes the dye**; all cells stay violet |
| 3 | **Alcohol/acetone** (decolorizer, 95% ethyl alcohol) | **Gram-negative → colorless**; Gram-positive stays violet |
| 4 | **Safranin** (counterstain) | Gram-positive **violet**; Gram-negative **red** |

### Materials
- **24-hour bacterial growth** (agar or broth); **crystal violet; Gram's iodine; 95% ethyl alcohol; safranin;** slides; marking pen/labels.
- ⚠️ **Do not over-decolorize:** many Gram-positive organisms lose the violet stain easily and, after the counterstain, **appear Gram-negative**.

### Procedure
1. Prepare a **fixed smear**.
2. **Crystal violet — 1 minute**; wash with tap water.
3. **Gram's iodine — 1 minute**; wash.
4. **95% alcohol — usually 10–20 seconds**; wash.
5. **Safranin — 1 minute**; wash.
6. Drain and **blot gently with bibulous paper**.
7. **Air dry** and examine under the microscope.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Gram stain = first step of identification; separates G+ (violet) from G− (red).
- Sequence: crystal violet → iodine (mordant) → alcohol (decolorizer) → safranin (counterstain).
- Times: 1 min – 1 min – 10–20 s – 1 min; wash between steps; don't over-decolorize.

</div>

## 4. Gram reaction, shape and arrangement of bacteria

| Organism | Gram reaction | Shape | Size | Arrangement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ***Staphylococcus aureus*** | **Positive** | **Cocci** | Small | **Clusters** |
| ***Escherichia coli*** | **Negative** | **Rods** | Small | **Single** |
| ***Bacillus subtilis*** | **Positive** | **Rods** | **Large** | **Chains** |

- **Streptococci = chains; Staphylococci = clusters.**
- **Gram-positive cocci:** *Streptococcus* spp. (chains) and *Staphylococcus* spp. (clusters); **Gram-negative cocci:** *Neisseria* spp.
- In one field, **Gram-positive rods look violet and Gram-negative rods look red/pink**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- S. aureus: G+ cocci clusters; E. coli: G− rods single; B. subtilis: G+ large rods chains.
- Strep = chains, Staph = clusters; Neisseria = Gram-negative cocci.
- Violet = Gram-positive, red = Gram-negative.

</div>
