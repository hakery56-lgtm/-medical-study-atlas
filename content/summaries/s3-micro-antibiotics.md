## 1. Definition and classification

- **Antibiotics** = **natural, semi-synthetic or synthetic compounds that inhibit or kill microorganisms**; they **reduced mortality from infectious disease**.
- ⭐ **Penicillin** — discovered by **Alexander Fleming in 1928** → start of the antibiotic era.

### Classification
1. **By spectrum of activity:**
   - **Broad-spectrum** — act on **Gram-positive and Gram-negative** bacteria.
   - **Narrow-spectrum** — target **specific** bacteria.
2. **By mechanism of action:** **cell-wall synthesis inhibitors, protein synthesis inhibitors, nucleic acid synthesis inhibitors.**

### Examples by class
| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Beta-lactams** | **penicillins, cephalosporins** |
| **Aminoglycosides** | **gentamicin, amikacin** |
| **Macrolides** | **erythromycin, azithromycin** |
| **Tetracyclines** | **doxycycline** |
| **Fluoroquinolones** | **ciprofloxacin** |
| **Glycopeptides** | **vancomycin** |

**Key points**
- Antibiotics: natural, semi-synthetic or synthetic; Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928.
- Classified by spectrum (broad vs narrow) and mechanism.
- Classes: β-lactams, aminoglycosides, macrolides, tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones, glycopeptides.

## 2. Mechanism of action, spectrum and pharmacokinetics

| Mechanism | How | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **1. Inhibit cell-wall synthesis** | **β-lactams bind PBPs (penicillin-binding proteins)** → no peptidoglycan formation | **penicillin, cephalosporins** |
| ⭐ **2. Inhibit protein synthesis** | bind **30S or 50S ribosomal subunits** | **tetracyclines, aminoglycosides, macrolides** |
| ⭐ **3. Inhibit nucleic acid synthesis** | **fluoroquinolones inhibit DNA gyrase**; **rifampicin blocks RNA polymerase** | **ciprofloxacin, rifampicin** |
| ⭐ **4. Disrupt cell membrane function** | cause **leakage of cell contents** | **polymyxins** |

### Spectrum
- **Broad:** tetracyclines, carbapenems. **Narrow:** penicillin G for *Streptococcus*.
- Choice depends on the **suspected organism, site of infection and resistance patterns**.

### Pharmacokinetics overview
- **Absorption** (oral vs IV bioavailability), **distribution** (tissue penetration, protein binding), **metabolism** (liver vs renal clearance), **excretion** (urine or bile) → optimize dosing and minimize toxicity.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 4 mechanisms: cell wall (PBPs), protein synthesis (30S/50S), nucleic acid (DNA gyrase, RNA polymerase), membrane disruption (polymyxins).
- Choice depends on organism, site and resistance. PK: absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion.

</div>

## 3. Antibiotic resistance

- **Resistance** = bacteria **adapt to survive antibiotic exposure**.
  - **Intrinsic:** **natural**, due to structural traits.
  - **Acquired:** via **mutations or horizontal gene transfer**.
- ⭐ **Mechanisms:**
  1. **Enzymatic degradation** (e.g. **β-lactamases**)
  2. **Altered target sites** (e.g. **MRSA modifies PBP**)
  3. **Efflux pumps** removing the drug
  4. **Reduced permeability** of the cell wall
- **Clinical impact:** **longer hospital stays, increased mortality, higher costs**.
- **Strategies:** **antibiotic stewardship, infection prevention, development of new agents**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Resistance: intrinsic (natural) vs acquired (mutation or HGT).
- Mechanisms: β-lactamases, altered target (PBP in MRSA), efflux pumps, ↓ permeability.
- Impact: longer stay, mortality, cost. Fight with stewardship, infection prevention, new drugs.

</div>

## 4. Clinical uses, adverse effects and rational use

### Clinical uses
- **Common indications:** **upper and lower respiratory infections, UTIs, skin and soft-tissue infections.** Choice depends on likely pathogens, severity and local guidelines; consider **allergies and comorbidities**.
- **Examples:** **penicillins → streptococcal pharyngitis; cephalosporins → pneumonia; aminoglycosides → sepsis; fluoroquinolones → complicated UTIs.**

### Side effects
- **Gastrointestinal** (nausea, diarrhea); **hypersensitivity** (rash, anaphylaxis); **organ toxicity:** **aminoglycosides → nephrotoxicity; tetracyclines → hepatotoxicity.**
- **Special considerations:** **avoid tetracyclines in children (tooth discoloration)**; **fluoroquinolones → tendon rupture**; **macrolides → prolong QT interval.**

### Rational use
- **Confirm infection** before prescribing; use **narrow-spectrum** agents when possible; follow recommended **dose and duration**; **avoid unnecessary broad-spectrum** use. Goal: **optimize outcomes, minimize resistance, reduce side effects.**

### Case
- **35-year-old with fever, cough, lobar consolidation on chest X-ray → community-acquired pneumonia**; empirical treatment: **amoxicillin-clavulanate or a macrolide**; culture results refine therapy.

### Prevention of resistance
- **Hand hygiene/infection control, vaccination (↓ antibiotic demand), education on misuse and self-medication, hospital stewardship programs.**
