## 1. Checklist of the thigh muscles

| Compartment | Structures to identify |
|---|---|
| **Medial** | **Superficial:** gracilis, adductor longus, pectineus. **Intermediate:** adductor brevis. **Deep:** adductor magnus (adductor part and hamstring part), obturator externus |
| **Anterior** | **Hip flexors:** sartorius, pectineus, psoas major, iliacus. **Knee extensors (quadriceps femoris):** rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, vastus medialis, vastus intermedius |
| **Posterior** | **Hamstrings:** biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus, ischial part of adductor magnus |

- **Femoral triangle (labeled image):** **inguinal ligament** (base), **sartorius** (lateral), **adductor longus** (medial); floor = **iliopsoas (psoas major + iliacus)** and **pectineus**.
- **Vastus intermedius** lies **deep to rectus femoris**.

- Medial: gracilis, adductor longus, pectineus (superficial); brevis (intermediate); magnus + obturator externus (deep).
- Anterior: sartorius, pectineus, psoas, iliacus (flexors) + quadriceps.
- Posterior: biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus, ischial part of adductor magnus.

## 2. Review questions: attachments, nerves and the iliotibial band

- **Muscle attachments on the proximal femur:** **gluteus medius → greater trochanter** (internal rotation + **abduction**); **iliopsoas → lesser trochanter** (flexion); **gluteus maximus → gluteal tuberosity** (extension + external rotation).
- ⭐ **PIN around the ischial spine:** **P**udendal nerve, **I**nternal pudendal vessels, **N**erve to obturator internus → supply the **muscles of the perineum**.
- **Superior gluteal nerve injury** → **Trendelenburg sign** (normal vs Trendelenburg shown). Standard teaching, not in the slide text: the pelvis drops on the opposite side because gluteus medius/minimus are weak.
- **Posterior abdominal wall nerves:**

| Group | Nerves | Vulnerable… |
|---|---|---|
| Upper | **Subcostal (T12)**, **iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal (L1)** | **Behind the kidney** |
| Middle | **Genitofemoral (L1–L2)**, **lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh (L2–L3)**, **femoral (L2–L4)** | **Behind the caecum** |
| Lower | **Obturator (L2–L4)** | **Closely related to the ovary** |

- ⭐ **Meralgia paresthetica:** compression of the **lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh (L2–L3) deep to the inguinal ligament** → aching, burning, numbness or stabbing in the lateral thigh.
- **Genitofemoral nerve:** **genital branch → cremasteric muscle**; **femoral branch → skin over the femoral triangle** (anesthetized in **femoral vein cannulation**); carries the **afferent and efferent limbs of the cremasteric reflex**.
- **Iliotibial band (ITB):** **modification of the fascia lata**; **TFL and gluteus maximus insert on it**. **Contracture in polio:** hip **F-AB-ER** (flexion, abduction, external rotation); knee **F-ER** (flexion, external rotation).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Gluteus medius → greater trochanter; iliopsoas → lesser trochanter; gluteus maximus → gluteal tuberosity.
- PIN = pudendal n., internal pudendal vessels, nerve to obturator internus (ischial spine).
- Meralgia paresthetica = lateral cutaneous n. of thigh (L2–L3); ITB takes TFL + gluteus maximus.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give the attachments and actions of gluteus medius, iliopsoas and gluteus maximus on the proximal femur.</summary>

**Answer:** Gluteus medius: greater trochanter, internal rotation and abduction. Iliopsoas: lesser trochanter, flexion. Gluteus maximus: gluteal tuberosity, extension and external rotation.

**Why:** Slide "Review questions" (femur attachments).
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which lumbar plexus nerves are vulnerable behind the kidney and behind the caecum?</summary>

**Answer:** Behind the kidney: subcostal (T12), iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal (L1). Behind the caecum: genitofemoral (L1–L2), lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh (L2–L3), femoral (L2–L4). The obturator nerve (L2–L4) is closely related to the ovary.

**Why:** Slide "Posterior abdominal wall nerves".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the iliotibial band and what happens in polio contracture?</summary>

**Answer:** A modification of the fascia lata into which tensor fasciae latae and gluteus maximus insert. Contracture in polio gives hip flexion, abduction and external rotation, and knee flexion and external rotation.

**Why:** Slide "Iliotibial band".
</details>

## 3. Labeled dissections

- **Proximal / anterior thigh:** inguinal ligament, **tensor fasciae latae, sartorius, rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, vastus medialis, iliopsoas, pectineus, adductor longus, gracilis**; lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh.
- **Medial compartment (layer by layer):** iliopsoas, pectineus, **adductor longus (cut)**, **adductor brevis** with the **anterior division of the obturator nerve**, gracilis; after cutting these: **obturator externus, adductor magnus and the sciatic nerve**.
- **Adductor canal region:** adductor longus, **femoral artery (A), femoral vein (V), saphenous nerve**, vastus medialis, adductor magnus.
- **Posterior thigh (1–7):** **1 adductor magnus; 2 biceps femoris; 3 semitendinosus; 4 and 5 semimembranosus; 6 common peroneal nerve; 7 sural nerve.**
- **Lateral thigh/leg (1–6):** **1 iliotibial tract; 2 biceps femoris; 3 communicating sural nerve; 4 common peroneal nerve; 5 sural nerve; 6 gastrocnemius.**
- **Sartorius:** 1 sartorius; 2 **SGS**.
- **Popliteal fossa:** **popliteal artery and vein, tibial nerve, common peroneal (fibular) nerve, semimembranosus, semitendinosus, biceps femoris, gastrocnemius, plantaris**; at the **adductor hiatus** the femoral artery and vein pass to the popliteal fossa beside the **popliteal surface of the femur**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Posterior thigh key: adductor magnus, biceps femoris, ST, SM, common peroneal, sural n.
- Popliteal fossa: artery, vein, tibial n., common peroneal n.; plantaris and gastrocnemius below.
- Adductor canal dissection: A, V, saphenous nerve between adductor longus/magnus and vastus medialis.

</div>

## 4. Radiological anatomy

- **AP hip radiograph:** **acetabulum, femoral head, femoral neck, greater trochanter, lesser trochanter, intertrochanteric crest**.
- **AP pelvis (with the femur):** **sacrum, sacroiliac joint, ilium, superior pubic ramus, obturator foramen, inferior pubic ramus, femur**.
- **Proximal femur:** femoral head with **fovea**, neck, intertrochanteric crest, greater and lesser trochanter, **compact bone and medullary cavity**.
- **Distal femur/knee:** femur, **popliteal surface, adductor tubercle, lateral condyle, intercondylar fossa**, tibia; AP knee: femur, **patella**, **intercondylar fossa**, **lateral and medial femoral condyles**.
- **Lines on the pelvic radiograph:** **Hilgenreiner's line (horizontal)**, **Perkin's line (vertical)**, **Shenton's line (curved, yellow)**. (Their clinical use, e.g., in hip dysplasia, is standard teaching and is not stated in the slide.)

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- AP hip: acetabulum, head, neck, GT, LT, intertrochanteric crest.
- Pelvis: sacrum, SI joint, ilium, pubic rami, obturator foramen.
- Hilgenreiner (horizontal), Perkin (vertical), Shenton (curved).

</div>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Gluteus medius = greater trochanter (abduction + internal rotation); gluteus maximus = gluteal tuberosity; iliopsoas = lesser trochanter.**
- **Behind the kidney: T12, L1 nerves; behind the caecum: genitofemoral, lateral cutaneous, femoral; the obturator nerve touches the ovary.**
- **Meralgia paresthetica = lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh (L2–L3), NOT the femoral nerve.**
- **Genitofemoral: genital branch = cremaster; femoral branch = skin of the femoral triangle.**
- **ITB: TFL + gluteus maximus; polio contracture = F-AB-ER at the hip, F-ER at the knee.**
- **Vastus intermedius lies deep to rectus femoris.**
