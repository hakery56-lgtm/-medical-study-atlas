## 1. Hemostasis and its stages

- ⭐ **Hemostasis** = the natural mechanism that **prevents blood loss through an injured vessel**. **Bleeding** = loss of blood from damaged small vessels. **Bleeding time** = time from the onset of the wound until bleeding stops.
- **Three stages:** **(1) constriction of the vessel (vascular spasm)** → **(2) temporary platelet plug (primary hemostasis)** → **(3) definitive clot by fibrin from coagulation (secondary hemostasis).**
- **Fibrinolytic phase:** after repair the clot is dissolved by **plasmin** so normal flow resumes (**within about 10 days**, vessel healed).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 3 stages: spasm, platelet plug, coagulation.
- Plasmin dissolves the clot.

</div>

**Answer:** Prevention of blood loss through an injured vessel; stages: vessel constriction, temporary platelet plug, and conversion to a definitive fibrin clot by coagulation.

<details><summary>Q2. What is the difference between primary and secondary hemostasis?</summary>

## 2. The platelet plug and the flowchart

- **Four steps at the site of injury:** **exposed collagen binds and activates platelets → platelets release factors → attract more platelets → aggregate into the plug.**
- **Intact endothelium** releases **prostacyclin and NO**, which **prevent platelet adhesion.**
- **Flowchart:** injury → **exposed collagen** → **von Willebrand factor** → **adherence** → **activation** → three branches: **serotonin → vasoconstriction (stage 1)**; **ADP and thromboxane A2 (+PAF) → aggregation → platelet plug (stage 2)**; **prothrombin activator → blood clotting (stage 3).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- vWF links platelets to collagen.
- Serotonin = stage 1; ADP/TXA2 = stage 2; prothrombin activator = stage 3.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe how the platelet plug forms.</summary>

**Answer:** Exposed collagen binds and activates platelets, which release factors that attract more platelets that aggregate into the plug.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why do platelets not stick to intact endothelium?</summary>

**Answer:** Intact endothelium releases prostacyclin and nitric oxide, which prevent platelet adhesion.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Summarize the three branches of the flowchart.</summary>

**Answer:** Serotonin causes vasoconstriction (stage 1); ADP and thromboxane A2 cause aggregation and plug formation (stage 2); prothrombin activator causes clotting (stage 3).

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>

## 3. Bleeding time

- ⭐ **Definition:** time for a standard skin wound to stop bleeding; measures **platelet number and function.** Methods: **Duke** and **Ivy.**

| Feature | Duke | Ivy |
|---|---|---|
| **Site** | **Ear lobe** (or fingertip) | **Forearm** underside, free of veins |
| **Setup** | Alcohol, lancet puncture | **BP cuff above elbow at 40 mmHg** |
| **Blotting** | Filter paper every **30 s**, never touching the wound | Every **30 s**, a clean area each time |
| **End point** | Bleeding stops | No blood on the paper |
| **Normal** | **< 6 min (1-5 min)** | **< 10 min** |

- **Prolonged in:** **thrombocytopenia (< 60,000/mm3)**, **platelet functional disorders**, **aspirin (2-3 times; stop 1 week before)**, **long-term oral anticoagulants (moderate)**, and **hemophilia** (as listed in the lecture).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Duke < 6 min; Ivy < 10 min.
- Aspirin: stop one week before the test.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the Duke method.</summary>

**Answer:** Clean the ear lobe or fingertip, make a standard lancet puncture, start the timer, blot every 30 seconds without touching the wound until bleeding stops; normal is less than 6 minutes.

**Why:** Slides 10-12.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the Ivy method.</summary>

**Answer:** Place a cuff above the elbow at 40 mmHg, puncture the cleaned forearm, blot every 30 seconds at clean spots, stop when no blood shows; normal is less than 10 minutes.

**Why:** Slides 13-14.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What prolongs the bleeding time?</summary>

**Answer:** Thrombocytopenia, platelet function disorders, aspirin, long-term oral anticoagulants and (as listed) hemophilia.

**Why:** Slides 15-16.
</details>

## 4. Clotting time

- ⭐ **Definition:** time for blood to clot **without any added substance**; a **screening test of the intrinsic system** and used to **monitor heparin.**
- **Capillary tube method:** lancet, stopwatch, **non-heparinized** capillary tube, cotton, alcohol; discard the first drop; after **~1 minute** break small pieces **every 2 minutes** until **fibrin strands** appear; normal **< 10 min (1-8 min).**
- **Tube method:** **water bath 37 °C**, 4 mL fresh whole blood, **3 tubes with 1 mL each**; stopwatch starts when **tube 3** is filled; at **5 min** tilt tube 1 to **45°**, repeat **every 30 s** until fully clotted; tube 2 begins **30 s** after tube 1 clots, then tube 3; normal clot in **< 15 min.**
- **Prolonged in:** **hemophilia, DIC, hypofibrinogenemia.** **DIC** = widespread microthrombosis and lysis that **consumes platelets and coagulation factors.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Capillary < 10 min; tube method < 15 min.
- Prolonged in hemophilia, DIC and hypofibrinogenemia.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the capillary tube method.</summary>

**Answer:** Prick the cleaned finger, discard the first drop, fill a non-heparinized capillary tube, and after one minute break pieces every two minutes until fibrin strands appear; normal under 10 minutes.

**Why:** Slides 17-19.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the tube method.</summary>

**Answer:** Three tubes with 1 mL of blood each in a 37 °C bath; tilt tube 1 to 45° from 5 minutes every 30 seconds until clotted, then tubes 2 and 3; normal clot under 15 minutes.

**Why:** Slides 20-23.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is DIC and how does it affect clotting?</summary>

**Answer:** Disseminated intravascular coagulation: microthrombosis and clot lysis everywhere, consuming platelets and factors, so clotting time is prolonged.

**Why:** Slide 24.
</details>

- **Bleeding time = platelets; clotting time = intrinsic pathway/heparin.**
- **Ivy cuff pressure 40 mmHg; tube method water bath 37 °C.**
- **Aspirin: stop 1 week before; prolongs 2-3 times.**
- **Slide note:** the lecture lists hemophilia among causes of prolonged bleeding time; in standard teaching hemophilia usually has a normal bleeding time. No question is built on this item.
