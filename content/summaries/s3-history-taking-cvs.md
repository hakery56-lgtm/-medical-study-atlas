## 1. Why history taking matters and the 9 components

- ⭐ **Up to 80% of medical diagnoses are made from the history alone** (primary diagnostic tool).
- It **guides the physical exam** (which systems to examine), **directs investigations** (right blood tests, X-rays, ECGs), **saves time and resources** (avoids unnecessary, costly or painful tests) and is **tested in every clinical rotation and OSCE**.

### The 9 components (in order)
1. **Personal data (demographics)**
2. **Chief complaint (CC) and duration**
3. **History of present illness (HPI)**
4. **Past medical history**
5. **Past surgical history**
6. **Drug history and allergies**
7. **Family history**
8. **Social history and lifestyle**
9. **Review of other systems**

### 1) Personal data
- **Full name and age** (identity, age-related disease probability); **sex/gender** (some diseases are more common in males or females); **occupation** (work-related exposures and physical demands); **marital status and address** (social support, living location); **date and time of admission** (disease timeline); **source of history** (patient, relative or transfer notes).

### 2) Chief complaint (CC) and duration
- **The main symptom that made the patient seek help**, in the **patient's own words** ('chest pain', not 'angina'), **with duration** ('shortness of breath for 3 days', 'severe headache for 2 hours'); **keep it short: 1–2 symptoms maximum**.

### 3) History of present illness (HPI)
- **Chronological story** of the main symptom from onset to today: **when it started, sudden or gradual, constant or intermittent; associated symptoms; previous episodes; the patient's thoughts — ICE (Ideas and Concerns)**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Up to 80% of diagnoses come from the history; it guides exam, tests and saves resources.
- 9 components: personal data → CC → HPI → past medical → past surgical → drugs/allergies → family → social → review of systems.
- CC = patient's own words + duration, 1–2 symptoms; HPI = chronological story + associated symptoms + previous episodes + ICE.

## 2. SOCRATES — symptom analysis

| Letter | Meaning | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | **Site** | Where exactly is the pain/symptom? |
| **O** | **Onset** | Sudden or gradual over hours/days? |
| **C** | **Character** | What does it feel like (sharp, dull, crushing, burning)? |
| **R** | **Radiation** | Does it spread (e.g. arm or jaw)? |
| **A** | **Associated symptoms** | Sweating, nausea, vomiting? |
| **T** | **Timing / Duration** | How long; constant or in waves? |
| **E** | **Exacerbating / Relieving** | Worse with exercise? Better with rest? |
| **S** | **Severity** | Scale 1–10? |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- SOCRATES = Site, Onset, Character, Radiation, Associated symptoms, Timing, Exacerbating/Relieving, Severity.
- R = Radiation (not "Rest" or "Risk").

</div>

## 3. Components 4–9

### 4) Past medical history
- **Record all major illnesses.** Ask directly about **hypertension, diabetes mellitus, asthma or chronic lung disease, previous heart attack or stroke, kidney or liver disease**, and **previous hospital admissions**.

### 5) Past surgical history
- **Operations:** name, year and hospital; **complications** (bleeding, wound infection); **anesthesia problems** (patient or family); **blood transfusions**.

### 6) Drug history and allergies
- **Current medications:** name, **dose, frequency** (e.g. Aspirin 100 mg daily); **over-the-counter drugs** (pain relievers, vitamins, herbal remedies); **adherence** (ask gently); **always ask about drug allergies**.
- **Allergy vs side effect:** **true allergy = rash/swelling; side effect = e.g. stomach upset.**
- 🚩 **Document true penicillin or severe drug allergy clearly in red.**

### 7) Family history
- **First-degree relatives** (parents, brothers, sisters, children); **cause and age at death**; **heart disease, high BP, diabetes, stroke**; **premature heart disease = < 55 years in men, < 60 in women**; shows genetic or shared environmental risks.

### 8) Social history and lifestyle
- **Smoking** (cigarettes/day and years), **alcohol** (units/week), **occupation and exposure to dust/chemicals**, **living situation** (with whom; house or apartment with stairs), **diet and exercise**.

### 9) Review of other systems
- **Quick screening checklist** so no major symptom is missed: **CVS** (chest pain, breathlessness, ankle swelling, palpitations); **respiratory** (cough, sputum, shortness of breath, wheeze); **GI** (stomach pain, nausea, heartburn, bowel habit changes); **nervous** (dizziness, faints, headache, weakness); **genitourinary and musculoskeletal** (urination problems, joint or muscle pain).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Past medical: HTN, DM, asthma/lung, MI/stroke, kidney/liver, admissions.
- Surgical: name/year/hospital, complications, anesthesia problems, transfusions.
- Drugs: name-dose-frequency, OTC, adherence, allergy vs side effect (penicillin allergy in red).
- Family: first-degree relatives, premature heart disease (< 55 M, < 60 F). Social: smoking, alcohol, work, living, diet/exercise.

</div>

## 4. CVS history — the 5 key symptoms

- **Core CVS symptoms:** **chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, ankle swelling, dizziness/syncope.**

### Chest pain
- **Common cause: ischemia** (lack of blood flow to the heart muscle).
- **Stable angina:** **dull heaviness/tightness, triggered by exertion, relieved by rest.**
- **Heart attack (MI):** **severe crushing pain, > 20 minutes, with sweating and nausea.** Radiates to **left arm, neck, jaw**.
- 🚩 **Sudden excruciating 'tearing' pain spreading to the back = aortic dissection (emergency).**

| Cardiac causes | Non-cardiac causes |
|---|---|
| **Heavy, crushing, squeezing**; **central, behind the breastbone**; **brought on by exercise or stress**; **eased by rest or nitrate spray**; may spread to **jaw or left arm** | **Muscle/bone:** sharp, worse on touching the chest wall or moving; **Lung/pleuritic:** sharp, worse on deep breath or cough; **Stomach/oesophagus:** burning heartburn after eating |

### Shortness of breath
- **Dyspnoea on exertion:** breathless walking or climbing stairs.
- **Orthopnoea:** breathless **lying flat**; key question: **'How many pillows do you sleep on?'**
- **PND:** **waking suddenly at night gasping for air**.
- **Why:** **fluid backs up into the lungs because the heart muscle is weak (heart failure).**

### Palpitations and dizziness
- **Palpitations:** unpleasant awareness of the heartbeat ('fluttering', 'racing', 'skipped beats'); **ask the patient to tap out the rhythm**.
- **Syncope:** temporary loss of consciousness from reduced brain blood flow; **postural dizziness** on standing quickly (low BP).
- 🚩 **Fainting during exercise needs urgent heart evaluation.**

### Ankle swelling and leg pain
- **Edema:** fluid in both legs/ankles from heart failure; **pitting edema** = thumb leaves a dent.
- **Intermittent claudication:** **cramp-like leg pain on walking a short distance that stops with rest**, due to **narrowed arteries (peripheral arterial disease)**. Question: 'Do your legs hurt when walking, and does rest make the pain go away?'

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 5 CVS symptoms: chest pain, SOB, palpitations, ankle swelling, dizziness/syncope.
- Angina = exertional, relieved by rest; MI = > 20 min, sweating, nausea; tearing pain to the back = aortic dissection.
- Orthopnoea (pillows) and PND = heart failure; claudication = peripheral arterial disease; exercise syncope = urgent.

</div>

## 5. Risk factors, sample script and student mistakes

### Cardiovascular risk factors
- **Smoking** (coronary and peripheral vascular disease); **hypertension** (damages vessel walls); **diabetes mellitus** (damages heart vessels; **'painless' heart attacks**); **high cholesterol/dyslipidaemia** (fatty plaques); **family history** of early heart disease; **unhealthy lifestyle** (inactivity, obesity, high-fat diet).

### Sample CVS script
- 'Heavy pressure in my chest when I walk up stairs' → spreads to **jaw and left shoulder** → **goes away after 3–4 minutes of rest** → slightly **short of breath on hills**, **no ankle swelling** (pattern of **exertional angina**).

### Common student mistakes
1. **Writing a diagnosis** in the chief complaint (write 'chest pain', not 'angina').
2. **Forgetting the duration** of the main symptom.
3. **Skipping past medical history** (diabetes, high BP).
4. **Not asking about drug allergies.**
5. **Asking leading questions** (ask 'What is the pain like?', NOT 'Is the pain sharp?').

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Risk factors: smoking, HTN, DM, high cholesterol, family history, unhealthy lifestyle.
- Exertional chest pain relieved by rest in 3–4 min with jaw radiation = angina pattern.
- Mistakes: diagnosis in CC, no duration, skipped PMH, no allergy question, leading questions.

</div>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Up to 80% of diagnoses come from the history.**
- **Chief complaint = symptom in patient's own words + duration (never a diagnosis).**
- **SOCRATES: R = Radiation (not Rest/Risk).**
- **True allergy = rash/swelling; side effect = stomach upset; penicillin allergy in red.**
- **Premature heart disease: < 55 years men, < 60 years women.**
- **Orthopnoea = breathless lying flat; PND = wakes at night gasping; both = heart failure.**
- **Claudication = leg pain on walking relieved by rest (peripheral arterial disease).**
- **Tearing pain to the back = aortic dissection; exercise syncope = urgent evaluation.**
- **Ask 'What is the pain like?', not 'Is the pain sharp?' (no leading questions).**
