## 1. Malnutrition and the response to starvation

- ⭐ **Malnutrition is common:** **~30%** of surgical patients with GIT disease and **up to 60%** with prolonged hospital stay; often **unrecognised**; patients with signs have **higher complications and mortality.**
- **Starvation response:** **↓ insulin, ↑ glucagon**; **hepatic glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis**; **protein catabolism**; **lipolysis**; **adaptive ketogenesis**; **↓ resting energy expenditure** (from ~25-30 to 15-20 kcal/kg/day).

**Key points**
- 30% (up to 60%) of surgical patients are malnourished.
- Starvation: low insulin, high glucagon, ketogenesis, lower energy expenditure.

## 2. Nutritional assessment

- **Laboratory:** **no single test identifies malnutrition**; **albumin is not a nutritional measure** (but **< 30 g/L = poor prognosis**); **lymphocyte count and delayed hypersensitivity skin tests** show immune defects.
- **Weight and BMI:** **unintentional loss > 10% in 6 months = poor prognosis**; **BMI < 18.5 = nutritional impairment**; **BMI < 15 = significant hospital mortality**; unreliable with **major fluid shifts.**
- **Anthropometry:** **skin fold thickness** and **mid-arm circumference** estimate **fat and muscle mass** (indirect energy/protein stores); **impaired by oedema.**
- **Clinical:** **focused history, physical examination, risk of malabsorption, inadequate dietary intake.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Albumin is not a nutrition marker.
- > 10% weight loss in 6 months; BMI < 18.5 and < 15.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the role of laboratory tests in nutritional assessment?</summary>

**Answer:** No single test identifies malnutrition; albumin is not a nutritional measure though below 30 g/L means poor prognosis; lymphocyte count and skin tests show defective immunity.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

**Why:** Slide 7.

## 3. Parenteral fluid therapy

| Solution | Na+ | K+ | Ca2+ | Cl- | Lactate / colloid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hartmann's** | **131** | **5** | **2** | **111** | **29** |
| **Normal saline (0.9% NaCl)** | **154** | — | — | **154** | — |
| **Dextrose saline** (4% dextrose in 0.18% saline) | **30** | — | — | **30** | — |
| **Gelofusine** | **150** | — | **< 1** | **150** | **Gelatin 4%** |
| **Haemacel** | **145** | **5.1** | **6.26** | **145** | **Polygelin 75 g/L** |
| **Hetastarch** | — | — | — | — | **Hydroxyethyl starch 6%** |

- **Maintenance needs** from **insensible and obligatory losses**; type of fluid depends on the patient.
- **Assessment:** **pulse, BP, CVP**; **skin turgor, urine output, urine specific gravity**; **urine and serum electrolytes, haematocrit**; **estimate current losses** (vomiting, ileus, diarrhoea, sweating, burns) and **future losses** (drains, fistulae, NG tubes, urine or faecal).
- **Most intestinal losses:** **normal saline + supplemental potassium.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Crystalloids: Hartmann's, saline, dextrose saline; colloids: Gelofusine, Haemacel, Hetastarch.
- Intestinal losses: saline plus potassium.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Hartmann's: Na 131, K 5, Ca 2, Cl 111, lactate 29; normal saline: Na 154 and Cl 154 only.

## 4. Nutritional requirements

- **Provide:** **macronutrients (carbohydrate, fat, protein), vitamins, trace elements, electrolytes, water.** **Weigh the patient** and calculate daily needs.
- ⭐ **Overfeeding = the most common complication**; **monitor daily intake** and **regular biochemistry.**
- **Energy:** **20-30 kcal/kg/day.** **Glucose (obligatory):** **~2 g/kg/day** (CNS, haematopoietic cells).
- **Fat:** **linoleic and linolenic acids are essential**; soybean and sunflower emulsions; **1 L per week** avoids deficiency. **Parenteral nutrition: fat + glucose "dual energy"** → fewer metabolic complications, **less fluid retention and CO2 production.**
- **Nitrogen:** **0.10-0.15 g/kg/day**; **hypermetabolic 0.20-0.25 g/kg/day.**
- **Vitamins and minerals:** **B and C = coenzymes in collagen and wound healing**; **vitamin C 60-80 mg/day** postoperatively; **B12** after intestinal/gastric surgery or alcohol dependence; **A, D, E, K** absorption falls in **steatorrhoea and absent bile**; **Na, K, phosphate** lost in diarrhoea (daily monitoring); **Mg, Zn, Fe** fall in inflammation.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Overfeeding is the commonest complication.
- Energy 20-30 kcal/kg/day; glucose 2 g/kg/day; nitrogen 0.10-0.15 g/kg/day.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What must a complete nutrition regimen provide?</summary>

**Answer:** Carbohydrate, fat, protein, vitamins, trace elements, electrolytes and water, calculated from the weighed patient and adjusted to condition.

**Why:** Slides 17-18.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why combine fat and glucose in parenteral nutrition?</summary>

**Answer:** The dual energy supply minimizes metabolic complications, fluid retention and carbon dioxide production.

**Why:** Slide 23.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. State the main macronutrient requirements.</summary>

**Answer:** Energy 20-30 kcal/kg/day; glucose about 2 g/kg/day; nitrogen 0.10-0.15 g/kg/day (0.20-0.25 if hypermetabolic); essential fatty acids from lipid emulsion.

**Why:** Slides 21-24.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Albumin is NOT a nutritional marker** (low albumin only signals poor prognosis).
- **Overfeeding, not underfeeding, is the commonest complication.**
- **Hartmann's Na 131 vs saline 154; Gelofusine contains gelatin, Haemacel polygelin.**
- **Nitrogen 0.10-0.15 (normal) vs 0.20-0.25 (hypermetabolic).**
- **Slide note:** the resting energy expenditure value is cut off at "25-3" on the slide; the standard value (25-30) is used.
