## 1. Introduction, definition and onset

- ⭐ **Hypochondriasis** = a **somatoform disorder** marked by **recurrent preoccupation with fears of having a life-threatening disease** despite **appropriate work-up and medical reassurance.** Simply: **excessive worry about being sick.** Sufferers = **hypochondriacs.**
- The preoccupation may be with a **specific organ or disease** (e.g., cardiac disease), **bodily functioning** (e.g., peristalsis, heartbeat) or **minor physical alterations** (e.g., a small sore, an occasional cough).
- Hypochondriacs have **anxiety that they are constantly ill**, truly believe something is wrong and **visit doctors frequently (doctor's shopping).**
- **Definition 1:** unrealistic or inaccurate interpretation of physical symptoms or sensations → preoccupation and fear of a serious disease **without any organic cause or psychopathology.**
- **Definition 2:** persistent preoccupation with a **fear (or belief)** of having one or more serious diseases based on the person's **own interpretation of normal body function or minor physical abnormalities.**
- **Onset:** from **teenage to older years**; **peak = twenties and thirties.**

**Key points**

- Preoccupation: organ or disease, bodily functioning, minor alterations.

## 2. Epidemiology

- **6-month prevalence** in a general medical clinic population: **4 to 6 percent** (may be as high as **15 percent**).
- **Men and women equally affected.**
- Onset at any age but **most commonly 20 to 30 years.**
- Some evidence: **more common among blacks than whites**; **social position, education level and marital status do not affect** the diagnosis.
- **Medical students:** complaints in about **3 percent**, usually in the **first 2 years**, generally **transient.**

## 3. Features and symptoms

- **Features:** the fear or belief **persists despite assurance** (normal reports); **doctor shopping** is predictably common; **preoccupation with medical terms and syndromes**; the patient changes the physician frequently **to be investigated again**; **easily worried** about health; **lack of physical symptoms** (if present, **mild**).
- **Symptoms:**

| Symptom | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Frequent doctor visits** | If one doctor declares him healthy, he goes to **two more (doctor shopping)** because he thinks the first doctor is missing something |
| **Requests for tests and surgery** | Asked **over and over**, despite risks |
| **Severe anxiety** about health | Constant worry about illness |
| **Excessive self-diagnosis and research** | Reads a news story or online article about a new disease and **thinks he has it** |
| **Thoughts that anything unusual** with the body | Means a serious illness |
| **Lack of attendance** at work or school | Due to anxiety over possible illnesses |
| **Constant attention** to blood pressure and heartbeat | Repeated checking |

## 4. Etiology: explanatory models

| Model | Idea |
|---|---|
| **1. Psychodynamic** | Physical symptoms from **unconscious conflicts** (traced to **Sigmund Freud**); **repression and displacement**; complaints **reconcile unacceptable drives** such as **aggression and hostility** towards others |
| **2. Social learning** | **Social transactional process**: patient assumes the **'sick role'** for a **socially acceptable excuse** or relief from social or occupational obligations; being ill ensures he is **taken care of (sympathy)** |
| **3. Cognitive-behavioral** | Patients **misinterpret bodily symptoms and amplify somatic sensations** into fears of a real life-threatening malady; **cognitive distortions and reassurance-seeking** maintain the disorder |
| **4. Anxiety spectrum** | A **variant of an underlying anxiety disorder**; related disorders: **OCD, specific phobia, GAD**; similar **frontal-striatal and limbic activity** with **increased distractibility** (hypochondriasis, OCD, PD); AD respond to **pharmacotherapy and CBT** |
| **5. Genetics** | **Family history** of hypochondriasis or anxiety issues |

- **Other factors:** major life stress event; abuse or neglect as a child; childhood illness; **disturbance in perception that magnifies normal sensations**; another mental disorder (major depression, OCD, psychotic disorders).
- ⭐ **Health anxiety** = concern about health that is **appropriate, reality-based and responds to evaluation and reassurance.**
- Note: the slide lists "Hypochondriacal psychosis, Body dysmorphic disorder, Hypochondriasis, Somatization disorder" in brackets after health anxiety; its meaning is unclear in the slide, so no exam question is built on it.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Five models: psychodynamic, social learning, cognitive-behavioral, anxiety spectrum, genetics.
- Social learning = sick role; CBT model = misinterpretation plus reassurance-seeking.
- Anxiety spectrum: shares etiology with OCD, specific phobia and GAD.

</div>

## 5. Diagnosis: ICD-10 and DSM-IV

| ICD-10 | DSM-IV |
|---|---|
| **A.** Either: persistent belief of **at least 6 months** in a minimum of **two serious physical diseases** (**at least one named by the patient**); or persistent preoccupation (**body dysmorphic disorder**) | **A.** Preoccupation with fears of having a serious disease based on **misinterpretation of bodily symptoms** |
| **B.** Preoccupation causes **persistent distress or interference** with personal functioning and leads to **seeking medical treatment or investigations** (or help from local healers) | **B.** Preoccupation **persists despite appropriate medical evaluation and reassurance** |
| **C.** **Persistent refusal to accept medical advice** that there is no adequate physical cause | **C.** Causes **clinically significant distress or impairment** in social, occupational or other areas |

- Note: criterion C of ICD-10 and of DSM-IV is partly cut off at the bottom of the slides.

- DSM-IV: misinterpretation, persists despite reassurance, significant distress or impairment.

## 6. Therapy recommendations

- **1. Establishment of therapeutic alliance:** acknowledge the patient's **pain and suffering**; understand symptoms as a **form of emotional communication**; search for **comorbid medical and psychiatric illness**; be aware of **emotional reactions and judgmental stance** towards the patient; **judiciously employ** diagnostic evaluation and referrals.
- **2. Maintenance of therapeutic relationship:** reassure that evaluation will be **ongoing**; focus on **care and not cure**; emphasize **regular scheduled visits**; explain he or she is **not 'crazy'**; introduce that **psychological factors (stress) may amplify** physical symptoms; educate on etiology and treatment if ready; stay current with **routine healthcare maintenance.**
- **3. Psychotherapy:** **counseling** (discussing fears can help); **support groups**; **exposure plus response prevention** (confront fears, learn to relax and release anxiety); **CBT**; **behavioral stress management**; **problem-solving therapy.**
- **4. Pharmacotherapy:** **placebo** (10 or 20 mg pills per schedule); **antidepressants** (SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, others such as **mirtazapine and trazodone**); **antipsychotics** (second-generation: **risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine**; or **pimozide**).
- Note: the "placebo in 10 or 20 mg pills" wording looks like a trial protocol; the slide wording is kept and no exam question tests its logic.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Alliance first, then maintain the relationship: care not cure, regular visits.
- Psychotherapy: counseling, support groups, exposure plus response prevention, CBT, stress management, problem-solving.
- Drugs: antidepressants and antipsychotics (risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, pimozide).

</div>
