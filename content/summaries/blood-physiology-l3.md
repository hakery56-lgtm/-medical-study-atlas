## 1. Hemostasis and vascular spasm

- ⭐ **Hemostasis** = forming **clots in damaged vessel walls** and preventing blood loss while keeping blood **fluid** in the vessels (balance of coagulation and anticoagulation).
- **Four mechanisms:** **1) vascular constriction; 2) platelet plug; 3) clot by coagulation factors; 4) fibrous tissue growth into the clot** (permanent closure).
- **Vascular spasm** is stimulated by **local myogenic spasm, local factors from traumatized tissue and platelets (serotonin), nerve reflex from pain fibers**; extends a few cm and lasts **minutes or hours** until plug and clot are ensured; affects **small and large vessels.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Four steps: spasm, plug, clot, fibrous organization.
- Spasm is myogenic + serotonin + pain reflex.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Forming clots in damaged vessel walls and preventing blood loss while keeping the blood fluid: vascular constriction, platelet plug, clot formation and growth of fibrous tissue into the clot.

**Why:** Slides "Hemostasis" and "Prevention of blood loss".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What stimulates vascular spasm?</summary>

**Why:** Slide "vascular spasm".
</details>

**Why:** Slide "vascular spasm".

## 2. Platelets and the platelet plug

- ⭐ **Platelets** = **non-nucleated discs, 2-4 µm**, from **megakaryocytes**; **half-life 8-12 days**; removed by the macrophage system; **count 150,000-350,000/mm3**; **thrombocytopenia ↓ / thrombocytosis ↑.**
- **Membrane:** **glycoprotein** (no adherence to normal endothelium, adheres to damaged) and **phospholipids** (activate clotting stages).
- **Normal endothelium** releases **nitric oxide and prostacyclin** → prevents aggregation.
- **Damaged endothelium:** platelets adhere to exposed **collagen** via **Von Willebrand factor** → swell, spiked processes, sticky → release **ADP** (potent aggregator) and **serotonin + thromboxane A2** (spasm and aggregation).
- **Positive feedback:** plug built in **~1 minute**; reinforced by coagulation factors; **small holes** (thousands daily) are closed by **plugs, not clots.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- NO and prostacyclin prevent aggregation; collagen + VWF start adhesion.
- ADP, serotonin and thromboxane A2 amplify the plug.
- Plug in about 1 minute.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe platelets.</summary>

**Answer:** Small non-nucleated discs (2-4 µm) from megakaryocytes with a half-life of 8-12 days and a count of 150,000-350,000/mm3, removed by the tissue macrophage system.

**Why:** Slide "Platelets".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe platelet plug formation.</summary>

**Answer:** Platelets adhere to exposed collagen through Von Willebrand factor, swell and release ADP, serotonin and thromboxane A2; this recruits more platelets in a positive feedback cycle and builds the plug within one minute.

**Why:** Slides on platelet adhesion and aggregation.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why do platelets not aggregate in intact vessels?</summary>

**Answer:** Intact endothelium releases nitric oxide and prostacyclin, which prevent platelet aggregation and restrict it to the site of injury.

**Why:** Slide on platelet membrane.
</details>

## 3. Coagulation factors

- ⭐ Most are **plasma proteins from the liver**, numbered **I-XIII by order of discovery** (not reaction sequence), circulating **inactive.**
- **Vitamin K** (fat-soluble; not directly involved) is needed for **factors 10, 9, 7, 2.**
- **Half-lives 10-65 h**, except **FVII 5-7 h.**
- **VWF** = carrier and protective protein of **factor VIII** (endothelial cells and megakaryocytes).
- **~400 substances** affect coagulation; on damage **procoagulants > anticoagulants.** Clot **starts in 15-20 s (severe trauma) or 1-2 min (minor trauma)** and is **complete in 3-6 min.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Vitamin K factors: 10, 9, 7, 2.
- Numbering = discovery order.
- Clot starts in seconds, completes in minutes.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which clotting factors depend on vitamin K?</summary>

**Answer:** Factors 10, 9, 7 and 2.

**Why:** Slide "Coagulation factors".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is Von Willebrand factor?</summary>

**Answer:** A carrier and protective protein of factor VIII made by endothelial cells and megakaryocytes (it also mediates platelet adhesion to collagen).

**Why:** Slides "Coagulation factors" and platelet slide.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. When does a clot form?</summary>

**Answer:** It starts within 15-20 seconds in severe trauma and 1-2 minutes in minor trauma, and is completed within 3-6 minutes.

**Why:** Slide "Blood Coagulation".
</details>

## 4. The coagulation cascade

- ⭐ **Three steps:** **1) prothrombin activators; 2) prothrombin → thrombin; 3) fibrinogen → fibrin.**

| Pathway | Trigger | Key steps |
|---|---|---|
| **Extrinsic** | **Damaged vessel or extravascular tissue** | Tissue thromboplastin + **VII** → VII complex → **X activated** |
| **Intrinsic** | **Blood itself** (collagen, glass; no tissue damage needed) | **XII → XI → IX**, then **VIII complex** → **X activated** |
| **Common** | Activated **X** (with **V complex**) | **Prothrombin → thrombin → fibrinogen → fibrin** → **XIII** → fibrin polymer |

- **Prothrombin:** **α2 globulin, 15 mg/dL**, made in the **liver** (needs **vitamin K**); **liver failure lowers it within 24 h** → bleeding tendency.
- **Thrombin** (proteolytic) **removes 4 peptides** from fibrinogen → **fibrin** that **polymerizes non-covalently.**
- **Fibrinogen:** liver protein, **MW 340,000** (little leaks into tissue, so no clot without damage).
- **Strengthening:** **fibrin stabilizing factor** (plasma and platelets) → **covalent bonds and cross-links**; meshwork traps cells, platelets and plasma.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Extrinsic = tissue damage + VII; intrinsic = blood contact.
- Both converge on activated X.
- Thrombin converts fibrinogen to fibrin; factor XIII stabilizes it.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the extrinsic and intrinsic pathways.</summary>

**Answer:** The extrinsic pathway starts when blood meets damaged vessel or tissue (tissue thromboplastin, VII); the intrinsic pathway starts in the blood itself without tissue damage (XII, XI, IX, VIII); both activate factor X.

**Why:** Slides "Formation of prothrombin activators".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the formation of thrombin and fibrin.</summary>

**Answer:** Prothrombin activators convert the unstable liver-made alpha-2 globulin prothrombin to thrombin; thrombin removes 4 peptides from fibrinogen to form fibrin that polymerizes into threads.

**Why:** Slides 14-15.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is the fibrin clot strengthened?</summary>

**Answer:** Thrombin and fibrin stabilizing factor create covalent bonds between fibrin monomers and cross-links between threads; the meshwork traps blood cells, platelets and plasma.

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

## 5. ABO blood groups

- ⭐ **ABO and Rh** are the most important antigen systems (> 300 minor antigens).

| Group | RBC antigen (agglutinogen) | Plasma antibody (agglutinin) | Genotype |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **A** | **Anti-B** | AA or AO |
| **B** | **B** | **Anti-A** | BB or BO |
| **AB** | **A and B** | **None** | AB |
| **O** | **None** | **Anti-A and anti-B** | OO |

- Plasma antibodies **do not react with the person's own RBC antigens.**
- **Inheritance:** one allele per chromosome (A, B or non-functioning O); **no dominance**; **6 gene combinations.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Antigens on RBCs; antibodies in plasma, opposite to the antigen.
- Group O has both antibodies; AB has none.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give the antigens and antibodies of each ABO group.</summary>

**Answer:** A: A antigen, anti-B; B: B antigen, anti-A; AB: both antigens, no antibodies; O: no antigens, anti-A and anti-B.

**Why:** Slides "ABO system".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give the genotypes of the ABO groups.</summary>

**Answer:** A: AA or AO; B: BB or BO; AB: AB; O: OO.

**Why:** Slide "Genetic determinant of agglutinogen".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How are agglutinogens inherited?</summary>

**Answer:** Each chromosome carries one allele (A, B or non-functioning O), with no dominance, giving six combinations.

**Why:** Slide "Genetic determinant of agglutinogen".
</details>

## 6. Transfusion reaction, typing and donation

- ⭐ **Severe reaction** with a high titer of **IgM (hemolysin)** → **hemolysis** with **complement.**
- **Example:** group A RBCs + group B plasma (anti-A) → **agglutination**, clumps plug small vessels, then **macrophage phagocytosis.**
- **Blood typing and cross matching:** test donor and recipient **before transfusion**; the main reaction is **donor RBCs vs recipient plasma.**
- **Typing with sera:** **AB** agglutinates with both; **B** with anti-B; **A** with anti-A; **O** with neither.

| Group | Can donate to |
|---|---|
| **A** | A, AB |
| **B** | B, AB |
| **AB** | AB only (**universal recipient**: no agglutinins) |
| **O** | O, A, B, AB (**universal donor**) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Donor RBCs vs recipient plasma is the critical reaction.
- O = universal donor; AB = universal recipient.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. When is a transfusion reaction severe?</summary>

**Answer:** With a high titer of IgM hemolysin, causing RBC lysis through complement activation.

**Why:** Slide "Transfusion reaction".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is group O the universal donor and AB the universal recipient?</summary>

**Answer:** O RBCs carry no agglutinogens, and AB plasma has no agglutinins.

**Why:** Slide "Blood donation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which reaction matters most in cross matching?</summary>

**Answer:** The reaction between the donor RBCs and the recipient plasma.

**Why:** Slide "Blood typing and cross matching".
</details>

## 7. Rh system and erythroblastosis fetalis

- ⭐ **Rh** was discovered in **Rhesus monkeys**; **Rh+ve** = agglutinogen present; **Rh-ve** = absent (can form antibodies against Rh+ve RBCs).
- **Rh factors:** **C, D, E, c, d, e**; **D** is strongest and defines **Rh+ve: 85% positive, 15% negative.** Typing uses **4-6 antisera.**
- **First Rh+ve transfusion to an Rh-ve person:** **no immediate reaction** (antibodies take **2-4 weeks**); minor reaction possible; **second transfusion → severe reaction** (sensitization).
- **Erythroblastosis fetalis:** **mother Rh-ve, father Rh+ve, baby Rh+ve**; fetal blood sensitizes the mother during pregnancy/delivery → **anti-Rh agglutinin.**
- **First child usually unaffected** (antibodies after delivery); **second Rh+ve child:** antibodies cross the placenta → **agglutination, hemolysis, macrophage phagocytosis** → Hb to **bilirubin** → **jaundice.** **Rh-ve fetus is not affected.**
- **Note:** the slide says 55% of Rh+ve fathers are heterozygous so 1/4 of the offspring are Rh-ve; no question is built on this statement.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Rh+ve 85%, D antigen.
- First exposure: no immediate reaction; second: severe.
- Second Rh+ve baby of an Rh-ve mother is at risk.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What happens when an Rh-negative person receives Rh-positive blood?</summary>

**Answer:** No immediate reaction the first time (antibodies need 2-4 weeks, a minor reaction may occur), but a severe reaction follows a later transfusion because of sensitization.

**Why:** Slide "Characteristics of Rh reaction".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe erythroblastosis fetalis.</summary>

**Answer:** An Rh-negative mother carrying an Rh-positive baby is sensitized by fetal blood during pregnancy or delivery; in the next Rh-positive pregnancy her antibodies cross the placenta and cause agglutination, hemolysis and phagocytosis of fetal RBCs, with bilirubin rise and jaundice.

**Why:** Slides "Erythroblastosis Fetalis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is the first Rh-positive child usually unaffected?</summary>

**Answer:** The mother's antibodies form only after his delivery.

**Why:** Slide 31.
</details>
