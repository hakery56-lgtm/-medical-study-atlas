## 1. Definition, steps, types, signs and causes

### Definition
- **Inflammation** = a **protective response of vascularized tissues** to **infections and tissue damage** that **brings cells and molecules from the circulation to the site where they are needed** to eliminate the offending agent.
- It eliminates the **initial cause of injury** (microbes, toxins) and the **consequences of injury** (necrotic cells/tissues).
- **Mediators of defense:** **phagocytic leukocytes, antibodies, complement proteins**.

### Steps of the inflammatory reaction
1. The offending agent (in the extravascular tissue) is **recognized** by host cells and molecules.
2. **Leukocytes and plasma proteins are recruited** from the circulation.
3. They are **activated** to destroy and eliminate the agent.
4. The reaction is **controlled and terminated**.
5. The damaged tissue is **repaired**.

### Acute vs chronic inflammation
| Feature | **Acute** | **Chronic** |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | **fast: minutes or hours**; lasts hours to a few days | **slow: days**; longer duration |
| Cellular infiltrate | mainly **neutrophils** | **monocytes/macrophages and lymphocytes** |
| Main characteristics | **edema (fluid + plasma-protein exudation)** and **emigration of leukocytes (neutrophils)** | more **tissue destruction**, **proliferation of blood vessels**, **fibrosis** |
| Tissue injury/fibrosis | usually **mild and self-limited** | may be **severe and progressive** |
| Local and systemic signs | **prominent** | **less** |

- Chronic inflammation **may follow acute inflammation (failure to clear the stimulus) or arise de novo.**

### Cardinal signs
- **Heat (calor), redness (rubor), swelling (tumor), pain (dolor), loss of function.**

### When inflammation becomes a cause of disease
- Reactions that are **misdirected (autoimmune diseases)**, directed against **environmental substances (allergies)**, or **excessively prolonged (microbes resist eradication)**; underlie **rheumatoid arthritis, atherosclerosis, lung fibrosis** and **life-threatening hypersensitivity** (insect bites, drugs, toxins).

### Termination and repair
- Ends when the **offending agent is eliminated**: **mediators are broken down**, **leukocytes are short-lived**, **anti-inflammatory mechanisms** are activated.
- **Repair** = **regeneration of surviving cells** and **filling of residual defects with connective tissue (scarring)**.

### Causes of inflammation
1. **Infections** (bacterial, viral, fungal, parasitic) and **microbial toxins** — the commonest.
2. **Tissue necrosis** (ischemia → MI, trauma, burns/frostbite, irradiation, chemicals).
3. **Foreign bodies** (splinters, dirt, sutures).
4. **Immune (hypersensitivity) reactions** (autoimmune, allergic).

**Key points**
- Inflammation = protective response of vascularized tissue; defense = phagocytes, antibodies, complement.
- 5 steps: recognition → recruitment → activation → termination → repair.
- Acute (min–hours, neutrophils, edema) vs chronic (days, macrophages/lymphocytes, fibrosis, vessel proliferation).
- Cardinal signs: calor, rubor, tumor, dolor, loss of function.
- Causes: infections, necrosis, foreign bodies, immune reactions.

## 2. Acute inflammation — vascular reactions

### Three major components
1. **Dilation of small vessels** → ↑ blood flow.
2. **Increased vascular permeability** → plasma proteins and leukocytes leave the circulation.
3. **Emigration of leukocytes** from the microcirculation → accumulate at the injury and are activated.

### Exudate vs transudate
| | **Exudate** | **Transudate** |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | **high protein content**, cellular debris | **low protein**, few/no cells, **low specific gravity** |
| Cause | **↑ permeability of small vessels** (inflammation) | **ultrafiltrate of plasma** from **osmotic or hydrostatic imbalance** with **normal permeability** |
| Examples | pus | ↑ hydrostatic pressure (**CHF**), ↓ colloid osmotic pressure (**liver disease, kidney disease/protein loss, kwashiorkor**) |

- **Edema** = excess fluid in interstitial tissue or serous cavities (exudate **or** transudate).
- **Pus** = a **purulent exudate** rich in **neutrophils, dead-cell debris and microbes**.
- **Exudation** = escape of fluid, proteins and blood cells into interstitial tissue or body cavities.

### I. Changes in vascular flow and caliber
1. **Vasodilation** — earliest; induced by **histamine** on vascular smooth muscle; may be preceded by **transient vasoconstriction**; involves **arterioles** first → opens new capillary beds → ↑ flow → **heat and redness (erythema)**.
2. **Increased permeability** — outpouring of **protein-rich fluid (exudate)**.
3. **Stasis** — fluid loss + ↑ vessel diameter → **slow flow, concentrated RBCs, ↑ viscosity, engorged small vessels** → **vascular congestion** (micro) / **erythema** (gross). Then **neutrophils line the endothelium**, endothelium **expresses adhesion molecules**, leukocytes adhere and migrate.

### II. Mechanisms of increased vascular permeability
| Mechanism | Features |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **1. Retraction of endothelial cells** | **most common**; **immediate transient response** (**15–30 min**); opens inter-endothelial gaps; by **histamine, bradykinin, leukotrienes** |
| **2. Endothelial injury** | **endothelial necrosis and detachment**; by **severe injury (burns), microbial toxins, neutrophils**; leakage **immediate and sustained for hours** until vessels thrombose or are repaired |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 3 components: vasodilation, ↑ permeability, leukocyte emigration.
- Exudate = high protein (↑ permeability); transudate = low protein (hydrostatic/osmotic imbalance). Pus = purulent exudate.
- Sequence: transient vasoconstriction → arteriolar vasodilation (heat, redness) → permeability → stasis → margination.
- Permeability: endothelial retraction (histamine; 15–30 min; most common) vs endothelial injury (burns, toxins; sustained).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Differentiate exudate from transudate.</summary>

**Answer:** An exudate is protein-rich fluid with cellular debris resulting from increased permeability of small vessels (inflammation). A transudate is a low-protein, low-specific-gravity ultrafiltrate of plasma with few cells produced by hydrostatic or osmotic imbalance (CHF, liver or kidney disease, kwashiorkor) with normal vascular permeability.

**Why:** Permeability is the key difference.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the changes in vascular flow and caliber in acute inflammation.</summary>

**Answer:** Transient vasoconstriction may occur first, then arteriolar vasodilation (histamine) opens new capillary beds and increases blood flow, causing heat and redness; increased permeability yields a protein-rich exudate; the resulting stasis (slow flow, concentrated red cells, higher viscosity) causes congestion and lets neutrophils marginate along the endothelium.

**Why:** Three sequential vascular events.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Explain the mechanisms of increased vascular permeability.</summary>

**Answer:** (1) Endothelial retraction — the commonest, immediate and transient (15–30 min), mediated by histamine, bradykinin and leukotrienes; (2) endothelial injury — necrosis and detachment due to burns, microbial toxins or neutrophils, starting immediately and lasting hours until vessels are thrombosed or repaired.

**Why:** Short-lived vs long-lived leakage.
</details>

## 3. Leukocyte recruitment

- **Neutrophils:** from bone marrow, **rapidly recruited**. **Macrophages:** **slower, more prolonged** responders, produce **growth factors** for repair.
- The journey is a multistep process controlled by **adhesion molecules and cytokines**: **(1) adhesion** → **(2) transmigration** → **(3) movement toward the agent**.

### I. Adhesion to endothelium
1. **Margination** — as flow slows, leukocytes move to the **periphery** along the endothelium.
2. **Rolling** — endothelial cells (activated by cytokines) express adhesion molecules; leukocytes **bind loosely, detach and tumble** (**selectins**).
3. **Firm adhesion** — **integrins** binding **ICAM-1**.

| Molecule | Features |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Selectins** | mediate the **initial weak interactions**; **E-selectin** (endothelium), **P-selectin** (platelets and endothelium), **L-selectin** (most leukocytes); **upregulated by cytokines** at sites of injury; ligand = **sialyl-Lewis X** |
| ⭐ **Integrins** | **transmembrane glycoproteins** on leukocytes (**low-affinity** form); mediate **firm adhesion** (and adhesion to ECM); **activated by chemokines**; **TNF and IL-1 ↑ endothelial ligands (ICAM-1)** |

### II. Transmigration (diapedesis)
- Leukocytes **squeeze between cells at intercellular junctions**, mainly in **post-capillary venules**, aided by **PECAM-1 (CD31)**; then **pierce the basement membrane** into the tissue.

### III. Chemotaxis
- **Locomotion along a chemical gradient.** Chemoattractants: **bacterial products, cytokines (chemokines), complement C5a, leukotriene B4 (LTB4).**
- **Time course:** **neutrophils** dominate the first **6–24 h**, replaced by **monocyte-derived macrophages** over **24–48 h**; neutrophils are short-lived (apoptosis within 24–48 h); macrophages survive longer and dominate prolonged reactions.
- **Exceptions:** **Pseudomonas** → neutrophils for days; **viral infections** → **lymphocytes** first; **hypersensitivity** → lymphocytes, macrophages, plasma cells; **allergic reactions** → **eosinophils**.

### IV. Phagocytosis and clearance
1. **Recognition and attachment** of the particle;
2. **Engulfment** (phagocytic vacuole);
3. **Killing or degradation** of the ingested material.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sequence: margination → rolling (selectins) → firm adhesion (integrins–ICAM-1) → transmigration (PECAM-1, post-capillary venules) → chemotaxis → phagocytosis.
- Selectins: E (endothelium), P (platelets + endothelium), L (leukocytes). Integrins activated by chemokines; TNF/IL-1 upregulate ligands.
- Chemoattractants: bacterial products, chemokines, C5a, LTB4.
- Neutrophils 6–24 h → macrophages 24–48 h. Exceptions: Pseudomonas (neutrophils), viruses (lymphocytes), allergy (eosinophils).
- Phagocytosis: recognition/attachment → engulfment → killing.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the steps of leukocyte adhesion to the endothelium and the molecules involved.</summary>

**Answer:** Margination (leukocytes move to the vessel periphery as flow slows), rolling mediated by selectins (E, P, L) which give weak transient binding, then firm adhesion mediated by integrins (activated by chemokines) binding ICAM-1, whose expression is increased by TNF and IL-1.

**Why:** Selectins = rolling; integrins = firm adhesion.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is chemotaxis and what are the chemoattractants?</summary>

**Answer:** Movement of leukocytes along a chemical gradient toward the injury. Chemoattractants include bacterial products, chemokines (cytokines), complement component C5a and leukotriene B4.

**Why:** Slide "Chemotaxis of leukocytes".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the cellular infiltrate over time and its exceptions.</summary>

**Answer:** Neutrophils predominate in the first 6–24 hours and are replaced by monocyte-derived macrophages over 24–48 hours. Exceptions: Pseudomonas infections (neutrophils for days), viral infections (lymphocytes first), hypersensitivity (lymphocytes, macrophages, plasma cells) and allergy (eosinophils).

**Why:** Neutrophils are short-lived; macrophages persist.
</details>

## 4. Mediators of inflammation

- **Mediators** initiate and regulate the reaction; **short-lived**; **one can stimulate release of others**. Most important in acute inflammation: **vasoactive amines, lipid products (prostaglandins, leukotrienes), cytokines (chemokines), complement products**.
- **Cell-derived:** **preformed** (granules; amines) or **newly synthesized** (PGs, LTs, cytokines). **Plasma-derived:** **inactive precursors** (mainly from the **liver**), activated by **proteolytic cascades** (complement, kinin, clotting).

### A. Vasoactive amines (preformed → released first)
- **Histamine:** richest source = **mast cells** (also basophils and platelets); **dilates arterioles** and **↑ permeability of venules**.
- **Serotonin (5-HT):** from **platelets** (and enterochromaffin cells); **actions similar to histamine**.

### B. Arachidonic acid (AA) metabolites
- **Cyclooxygenase (COX) pathway** → **prostaglandins (PGs) and thromboxane A₂**; **lipoxygenase pathway** → **leukotrienes and lipoxins**.
- **Prostaglandins** (mast cells, macrophages, endothelium) via **COX-1 and COX-2**:
  - **PGD₂ and PGE₂:** **vasodilation + ↑ permeability of postcapillary venules** → edema; **PGD₂ also chemoattracts neutrophils**.
  - **PGE₂:** **pain** (hypersensitivity to painful stimuli) and **fever**.
- **Leukotrienes** (leukocytes and mast cells):
  - **LTB₄** (neutrophils, some macrophages): potent **chemotactic agent and neutrophil activator**.
  - **LTC₄, LTD₄, LTE₄** (mast cells): **intense vasoconstriction, bronchospasm (asthma), ↑ venule permeability**.
- **Lipoxins** (lipoxygenase pathway): **anti-inflammatory** — **inhibit leukocyte recruitment, neutrophil chemotaxis and adhesion**.

### C. Cytokines and chemokines
- **Cytokines:** proteins from **activated lymphocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells** (and endothelial, epithelial, connective cells). **TNF and IL-1** (stimulated by **endotoxin, microbial products, immune complexes, physical injury**): **endothelial activation, leukocyte activation, systemic acute-phase response**; TNF also ↑ lipid/protein catabolism and **suppresses appetite**.
  - Systemic effects: **fever (TNF, IL-1, IL-6)**, **acute-phase proteins from the liver**, **leukocyte production in bone marrow**.
- **Chemokines:** small proteins, **chemoattractants for specific leukocytes** (e.g. **IL-8 → neutrophils**), bind proteoglycans on endothelium/ECM; roles in **acute inflammation and maintaining tissue architecture**.

### Mediators by reaction (Table 3.8)
| Reaction | Principal mediators |
|---|---|
| **Vasodilation** | **histamine; prostaglandins** |
| **↑ vascular permeability** | **histamine; C3a and C5a; leukotrienes C4, D4, E4** |
| **Chemotaxis, leukocyte recruitment/activation** | **TNF, IL-1; chemokines; C3a, C5a; LTB₄** |
| **Fever** | **IL-1, TNF; prostaglandins** |
| **Pain** | **prostaglandins; bradykinin** |
| **Tissue damage** | **lysosomal enzymes of leukocytes; reactive oxygen species** |

### D. Plasma protein systems
- **Complement:** soluble proteins in **inactive form**, activated into an **enzymatic cascade**; the **critical step = proteolysis of C3** (most abundant) by **C3 convertase** (3 pathways) → **C3a + C3b**.
  - **C3a, C5a:** release **histamine** from mast cells → ↑ permeability + vasodilation (**anaphylatoxins**). **C5a:** **chemoattractant**. **C3b:** **opsonin**. **C5b-9 = membrane attack complex.**
- **Kinin system:** activated when plasma contacts **vascular basement-membrane collagen** (via factor XII); **bradykinin** ↑ permeability, contracts smooth muscle, dilates vessels, causes **pain**.
- **Clotting system:** activation → **thrombin** → insoluble **fibrin clot**; thrombin binds receptors on platelets, endothelial and smooth-muscle cells → **recruits leukocytes**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Histamine (mast cells) = vasodilation + venular permeability; serotonin similar (platelets).
- AA: COX → PGs + TxA₂; lipoxygenase → LTs + lipoxins. PGE₂ = pain + fever; LTB₄ = chemotaxis; LTC₄/D₄/E₄ = bronchospasm, vasoconstriction, permeability; lipoxins = anti-inflammatory.
- TNF and IL-1 = endothelial activation, fever, acute phase; IL-8 = neutrophil chemokine.
- Complement: C3 convertase → C3a/C3b; C5a chemotaxis + histamine release; C3b opsonin.
- Bradykinin = permeability + pain; thrombin = fibrin + leukocyte recruitment.

</div>

<details><summary>Q1. Classify the mediators of inflammation and give examples.</summary>

**Answer:** Cell-derived: preformed (histamine, serotonin) and newly synthesized (prostaglandins, leukotrienes, cytokines, PAF, NO, ROS). Plasma-derived: complement (C3a, C5a, C3b, C5b-9), kinin system (bradykinin) and clotting system (thrombin), made mainly in the liver as inactive precursors.

</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the arachidonic acid metabolites and their roles.</summary>

**Answer:** The COX pathway gives prostaglandins and thromboxane (PGD₂/PGE₂: vasodilation and permeability; PGD₂ neutrophil chemoattractant; PGE₂: pain and fever); the lipoxygenase pathway gives leukotrienes (LTB₄ chemotaxis and neutrophil activation; LTC₄, D₄, E₄ vasoconstriction, bronchospasm and permeability) and lipoxins (anti-inflammatory: inhibit leukocyte recruitment, neutrophil chemotaxis and adhesion).

**Why:** Key high-yield table.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the role of the complement system in inflammation?</summary>

**Answer:** Complement proteins are activated in a cascade whose critical step is cleavage of C3 by C3 convertase into C3a and C3b. C3a and C5a release histamine from mast cells (↑ permeability, vasodilation); C5a is a chemoattractant; C3b is an opsonin; C5b-9 forms the membrane attack complex.

**Why:** Vascular, chemotactic and opsonic effects.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Exudate = high protein/↑ permeability; transudate = low protein/normal permeability.**
- **Selectins = rolling (weak); integrins = firm adhesion (need chemokine activation).**
- **E-selectin = endothelium; P-selectin = platelets + endothelium; L-selectin = leukocytes.**
- **Neutrophils first (6–24 h), macrophages later (24–48 h);** exceptions: **viruses → lymphocytes; allergy → eosinophils; Pseudomonas → neutrophils for days.**
- **Transmigration occurs in post-capillary venules.**
- **Histamine = preformed (first); PGs/LTs/cytokines = newly synthesized.**
- **PGE₂ = pain + fever; LTB₄ = chemotaxis; LTC₄/D₄/E₄ = bronchospasm/vasoconstriction.**
- **Lipoxins are anti-inflammatory.**
- **C5a = chemoattractant; C3b = opsonin; C3a + C5a = anaphylatoxins.**
- **Endothelial retraction = fast and transient (15–30 min); endothelial injury = long-lived.**
- **TNF and IL-1 = endothelial activation + fever; IL-8 = neutrophils.**
