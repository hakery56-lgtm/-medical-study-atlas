## 1. Clinical approach and approach to the patient

- **Objectives:** recall the steps of the clinical approach, list the components of the **general and systemic examination**, identify the **six fixed general signs**, differentiate **central vs peripheral cyanosis**, **localized vs generalized oedema**, measure **vital signs and SpO₂**, and build a **case presentation**.

### Clinical approach to diagnosis
- **Full history + full physical examination + investigations if needed.**
- Physical examination = **general + systemic examination**.
- ⭐ **General examination is far more important than systemic examination.**

### Components of the general examination (approach)
1. **Greet** the patient and relative.
2. **Introduce yourself.**
3. **Explain** what you are going to do.
4. **Clean your hands** (washing, sterilizing, gloves).
5. **Ask about any pain** (site, severity).

### Use your eyes and ears
- First take a good look at the **patient and surroundings**: bed-ridden/comfortable, **drugs on the table**, **monitors or machines (nebulizer)**, **position in bed**, **oxygen**, **life support**.
- Quick look at the patient: **age, gender, facies, obvious abnormality (limb defect, injury), eye contact, able to talk (GCS), level of consciousness, obvious sounds (wheeze, cough), able to walk/move.**

### Sequence of examination
- **Usual:** **hand → pulse → BP → face → neck → chest → abdomen → lower limb.**
- **Other:** **face downward to the limbs.**
- Either **talk through the examination** or **examine silently and summarize at the end**.

## 2. Vital signs and the six fixed general signs

### Components
- **Vital signs: temperature, pulse, blood pressure, respiratory rate** + **SpO₂**.
- ⭐ **The 6 fixed general signs:** **Pallor, Jaundice, Cyanosis, Edema, Clubbing, Lymphadenopathy.**

### Pallor
- **Pale colour of the skin, mainly of the face.**
- Look at: **palm creases, mucous membrane of the mouth, conjunctiva.**
- Causes: **anemia, fear, severe pain.**

### Jaundice
- **Yellowish discolouration of skin and mucous membranes**, when **total serum bilirubin (TSB) exceeds the threshold for age.**
- Look at: **sclera (scleral icterus), skin, under the tongue.**

### Cyanosis
- **Bluish discolouration of skin and mucous membranes** from **desaturated Hb > 4–5 g/dL**.
| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Central ("real")** | seen mainly in the **tongue** (and lips) |
| ⭐ **Peripheral (acrocyanosis)** | seen in the **fingers**; mainly due to **cold** |

### Edema
- **Increase in fluid in body tissues, mainly the extracellular compartment.**
- **Localized:** **local cause (peripheral lesion)**. **Generalized:** **hepatic, cardiac, renal, intestinal, nutritional** causes.
- **Pitting grades:**
| Grade | Indentation | Rebound |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **0–2 mm** | **immediately** |
| **2** | **3–4 mm** | **< 15 seconds** |
| **3** | **5–6 mm** | **up to 30 seconds** |
| **4** | **8 mm** | **> 20 seconds** |

### Clubbing
- **Deformity of the nail beds** from **cardiac, pulmonary, hepatic and GIT** causes.
- **Normal angle ≈ 160°; clubbed > 180°.**
- **Stages:** **1** = normal angle, ↑ **fluctuancy** of the nail bed; **2** = **loss of the angle**; **3** = **↑ curvature** of the nail; **4** = **expansion of the terminal phalanx (drumstick)**.
- **Schamroth's window test:** the diamond-shaped window between two opposed normal nails is **lost in clubbing**.

### Lymphadenopathy
- Examine **lymph node groups**: head and neck — **preauricular, parotid, retropharyngeal (tonsillar), submandibular, submental, anterior cervical, supraclavicular (palpable only when there is disease), occipital, postauricular, posterior cervical**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Vital signs: temperature, pulse, BP, RR, + SpO₂. Six signs: pallor, jaundice, cyanosis, edema, clubbing, lymphadenopathy.
- Pallor: conjunctiva, palm creases, mucosa. Jaundice: sclera, skin, under tongue (TSB above threshold). Cyanosis: desaturated Hb > 4–5 g/dL; central (tongue) vs peripheral (fingers, cold).
- Edema: localized vs generalized (hepatic, cardiac, renal, intestinal, nutritional); pitting grades 1–4.
- Clubbing: angle 160° → > 180°, 4 stages, Schamroth's window. Lymph nodes: supraclavicular nodes palpable only in disease.

**Answer:** Vital signs: temperature, pulse, blood pressure and respiratory rate, plus SpO₂. The six fixed general signs: pallor, jaundice, cyanosis, edema, clubbing and lymphadenopathy.

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate central from peripheral cyanosis.</summary>

**Answer:** Both are bluish discoloration from desaturated hemoglobin above 4–5 g/dL. Central (real) cyanosis is seen mainly in the tongue (and mucosa); peripheral cyanosis (acrocyanosis) is seen mainly in the fingers and is often due to cold.

**Why:** Location and cause differ.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Grade pitting edema and describe the stages of clubbing.</summary>

**Answer:** Grade 1: 0–2 mm, rebounds immediately; grade 2: 3–4 mm, < 15 s; grade 3: 5–6 mm, up to 30 s; grade 4: 8 mm, > 20 s. Clubbing: stage 1 increased fluctuancy of the nail bed, stage 2 loss of the nail-bed angle, stage 3 increased nail curvature, stage 4 drumstick expansion of the terminal phalanx (angle > 180° vs normal 160°).

**Why:** Exam favourites.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **General examination > systemic examination in importance.**
- **Six fixed signs: pallor, jaundice, cyanosis, edema, clubbing, lymphadenopathy.**
- **Central cyanosis = tongue; peripheral = fingers (cold).**
- **Cyanosis threshold = desaturated Hb > 4–5 g/dL.**
- **Normal nail angle 160°; clubbing > 180°;** window test lost.
- **Pitting grade 4 = 8 mm and > 20 s rebound** (as in the slide).
- **Pallor sites: palm creases, mouth mucosa, conjunctiva; jaundice sites: sclera, skin, under the tongue.**
- **Supraclavicular nodes are palpable only in disease.**
- **Localized oedema = local cause; generalized = hepatic, cardiac, renal, intestinal, nutritional.**
