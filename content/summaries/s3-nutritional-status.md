## 1. Definition, purposes and influencing factors

- ⭐ **Nutritional assessment** = **systematic collection, analysis and interpretation of dietary, anthropometric, biochemical, clinical and socioeconomic data** to determine the **nutritional status** of an individual or population, find problems and causes, and **guide interventions and monitoring.**
- **Purposes:** **(1) detect malnutrition early** (undernutrition, PEM, micronutrient deficiency, obesity); **(2) guide intervention**; **(3) evaluate treatment**; **(4) public-health planning**; **(5) identify risk factors** (diabetes, hypertension, CVD); **(6) improve clinical outcomes** (wound healing, less morbidity).
- **Factors influencing status:** **disease, cultural patterns, eating habits, psychological stress, economic factors, nutrient absorption.**

- Assessment = data collection, analysis, interpretation and action.

## 2. Direct versus indirect assessment

| Feature | Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|---|
| **Level** | Individual or group | **Community or national** |
| **Focus** | **Actual nutritional condition** | **Determinants and outcomes** of nutrition |
| **Data** | Physical, biochemical, dietary, clinical | **Socioeconomic, environmental, demographic** |
| **Use** | **Diagnosis and treatment** | **Policy, planning, surveillance** |
| **Examples** | BMI, serum albumin, 24-hour recall | **IMR, food balance sheet, poverty rate** |

- **Direct = ABCD:** **A**nthropometric, **B**iochemical, **C**linical, **D**ietary history; **no single technique is enough.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- ABCD = direct methods.
- Indirect assessment targets populations at risk, not individuals.

</div>

## 3. Anthropometric measurements

- ⭐ The **simplest and most quantitative** measure; compare with **national standards**; estimates **fat and lean mass**; used for **infants, children and pregnant women**; **does not separate acute from chronic**; **accurate, simple, safe, cheap, bedside**; detects **only measurable size changes.**
- **Child indicators:** **W/H, H/A, W/A, head circumference, MUAC, BMI.** **Others:** **mid-arm circumference, skin fold, waist circumference, waist/hip ratio, BMI.**
- **Weight:** lever balance; **edema = falsely high.** **Length/height:** **< 2 years supine**; older **standing, no shoes, heels together, head erect.**
- **BMI = kg / m2**; **lowest mortality BMI 19-27** (slide).

| BMI | Class |
|---|---|
| **< 18.5** | Under-weight |
| **18.5-24.9** | Normal |
| **25-29.9** | Over-weight |
| **30-34.9** | Obesity I |
| **35-39.9** | Obesity II |
| **> 40** | Extreme obesity III |

- **Growth chart ("Road to Health"):** weight between the two lines is normal; **below the lower line = undernutrition; above the upper = overweight.**
- **Skin fold** (triceps, biceps, subscapular, suprailiac): **men < 12.5 mm under, > 20 mm over; women < 16.5 mm under, > 25 mm over.**
- **MUAC:** midpoint of **left upper arm**, **fiberglass tape**; **muscle wasting in PEM.**
- **Waist circumference:** **abdominal fat**, risk for **diabetes, CVD, breast cancer**; **best mortality predictor**; **men > 94 (level 1), > 102 (level 2); women > 80, > 88 cm.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- BMI classes: 18.5, 25, 30, 35, 40.
- Skin fold: men 12.5/20 mm; women 16.5/25 mm.
- Waist: men 94/102; women 80/88 cm.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give the BMI classification.</summary>

**Answer:** Under 18.5 underweight; 18.5-24.9 normal; 25-29.9 overweight; 30-34.9 obesity I; 35-39.9 obesity II; over 40 extreme obesity III.

**Why:** Slide 18.
</details>

## 4. Biochemical, clinical and dietary methods

- **Biochemical (B):** **serum proteins, glucose, cholesterol and lipoproteins, alkaline phosphatase**; **hematology** (Hb, hematocrit, iron, vitamins A, D, B12, minerals); **microbiology** (stool for ova); **special tests** (**radiology** for rickets, scurvy, fluorosis, PCM; **dark adaptation** for early vitamin A deficiency).
- **Clinical (C):** **history** (chronic illness, weight change, family history) and **examination:** **pallor, edema, hair changes, glossitis, goiter, skin lesions, muscle wasting**; **emaciation/hair changes = long-term energy deficit**; **xanthoma/corneal arcus = hyperlipidaemia**; **edema = protein depletion or vitamin B1 deficiency**; **breathlessness = anemia**; needs lab and diet confirmation.
- **Dietary (D) methods:**

| Method | Key point |
|---|---|
| **24-hour recall** | Quick, cheap; **may not reflect usual intake** |
| **Food frequency questionnaire** | **Long-term patterns** |
| **Dietary record** | **3-7 days**; accurate; needs **motivation and literacy** |
| **Diet history** | Detailed interview of **usual intake** |
| **Weighed food record** | **Precise, best for research**, time-consuming |

- **Children:** dietitian assesses **breast and complementary feeding, 24-hour recall, protein and calorie content, feeding technique and habits.**
- **Common indicators:** **BMI**; **MUAC < 22 cm adults, < 12.5 cm children = severe malnutrition** (as stated); **W/H Z-score = wasting**; **H/A Z-score = stunting**; **Hb = iron**, **albumin = protein**, **vitamin D = bone.**
- **Interpretation:** compare with **WHO, CDC or local charts**; **use multiple indicators**; **re-assess periodically.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Wasting = W/H; stunting = H/A.
- 24-hour recall quick but not usual; weighed record best for research.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the dietary assessment methods and compare two.</summary>

**Answer:** 24-hour recall, FFQ, dietary record, diet history and weighed food record; recall is quick but may not reflect usual intake, the weighed record is precise but time-consuming.

**Why:** Slides 30-31.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What clinical signs suggest malnutrition?</summary>

**Answer:** Pallor, edema, hair changes, glossitis, goiter, skin lesions, muscle wasting, emaciation; xanthoma or corneal arcus suggest hyperlipidaemia.

**Why:** Slides 28-29.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which indicator detects wasting and which stunting?</summary>

**Answer:** Weight-for-height Z-score detects wasting; height-for-age Z-score detects stunting.

**Why:** Slide 33.
</details>

## 5. Indications and indirect assessment

- **Indications (clinical/hospital):** **unintentional weight loss or gain; chronic disease (diabetes, renal, heart failure); pre-/post-operative; elderly or debilitated; eating disorders or GI disease.**
- **Indirect assessment:** for **public health and national surveillance**; **populations at risk**; guides **policy and planning.**

| Component | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Ecological/environmental** | Food availability, sanitation, climate, agriculture, water supply |
| **Economic indicators** | Income, food prices, employment, family size |
| **Morbidity and mortality** | **IMR, MMR**, PEM and anemia prevalence |
| **Food balance sheet** | National production, imports and consumption per capita (**FAO/WHO**) |
| **Vital statistics** | Birth weight, growth trends, **life expectancy** |

- **Vital statistics:** **mortality at ages 1-4** is particularly related to malnutrition; **IMR, low birth weight, life expectancy** reflect nutrition.
- **Nutritional survey:** **clinical evidence**, **socioeconomic, dietary and cultural background**, **ecology**, and **developing programs** for community needs.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Indirect = community level; examples IMR, food balance sheet, poverty rate.
- Mortality at 1-4 years reflects malnutrition.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**
