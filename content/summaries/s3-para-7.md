## 1. Introduction, hosts and infective stages

- **Disease:** **toxoplasmosis and congenital toxoplasmosis** (**zoonotic**); agent **Toxoplasma gondii**: **obligate intracellular coccidian**, phylum **Apicomplexa**; **Sporozoa** (Toxoplasma, Plasmodium) are **non-motile**, no locomotory organelles, sexual and asexual reproduction.
- **Name:** Greek **toxon** = bow, the **curved trophozoite**; the **most common protozoan parasite globally**.
- ⭐ **Infective stages:** (1) **mature sporulated oocyst** from **cat stool** (oral); (2) **tissue cyst** in **raw meat** (e.g. sheep); (3) **tachyzoite** from **mother to fetus** through the **placenta**. Also **blood transfusion or transplantation**.
- **Hosts:** **intermediate = humans** (and other mammals); **definitive = cats**; **route of entry = oral**; **diagnostic stage = trophozoite (tachyzoite) and cysts**; **sources = cats and sheep**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Three infective stages: sporulated oocyst, tissue cyst, tachyzoite (placenta).
- Cats = definitive host; humans = intermediate host.

</div>

## 2. Morphology

| Stage | Features |
|---|---|
| **Trophozoite (tachyzoite)** | **3–7 µm**, **crescent-shaped** (one end pointed, one rounded), **large nucleus** in rounded end, **intracellular**; **asexual**; **acute** infection; Giemsa: **blue cytoplasm**, dark/red nucleus |
| **Bradyzoite** | **Slowly multiplying** trophozoite within cells |
| **Tissue cyst** | **Resting form**, **oval 10–20 µm**, numerous **bradyzoites**, **chronic** stage: **brain (most common)**, skeletal muscle, organs |
| **Oocyst** | **Only in cat intestine** (not human gut); **10–12 µm**, **oval**, **thick resistant wall**; **fresh = non-infective**, **sporulates** in soil → **2 sporocysts** (infective); cats shed **millions/day** |

- **Pseudocyst:** rounded trophozoites in a **special membrane** in acute infection; **tachyzoites** multiply rapidly (**endogony**), the cell ruptures, spreading through **blood and lymphatics** (**internal autoinfection**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Tachyzoite = crescent 3–7 µm (acute); tissue cyst = bradyzoites 10–20 µm (chronic, brain); oocyst = cat gut only, 10–12 µm.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the trophozoite (tachyzoite).</summary>

**Answer:** An asexually reproducing intracellular crescent-shaped form of about 3-7 µm, with one pointed and one rounded end and a large nucleus at the rounded end; it predominates in acute infection.

**Why:** Slide "Trophozoite (tachyzoite)".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the tissue cyst?</summary>

**Answer:** The resting oval form (10-20 µm) containing numerous bradyzoites, reproducing asexually, found in chronic toxoplasmosis, most commonly in the brain and also in skeletal muscle and other organs.

**Why:** Slide "Tissue cyst".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the oocyst.</summary>

**Answer:** Develops by sexual and asexual reproduction only in the definitive host's (cat) intestine, not in human gut; 10-12 µm, oval with a thick resistant wall; freshly passed oocysts are inactive and sporulate in soil into two sporocysts (the infective stage).

**Why:** Slide "Oocyst stage".
</details>

## 3. Life cycle

| Cycle | Host | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Extra-enteric (exaenteric)** | **Humans, sheep, cattle, pigs, birds** | **Asexual only**. **Ingest sporulated oocysts** (food, water, fingers) or **bradyzoites in undercooked meat** → cysts lysed in gut → **bradyzoites invade small-intestine mucosa → tachyzoites** → engulfed by **macrophages/RES** (lymph glands, CNS, liver, eyes…) → **bradyzoites → tissue cysts** (liver, brain, spleen, eyes) |
| **Enteric** | **Cats (definitive)** | Cats eat **infected rats with tissue cysts** → **schizogony → merozoites**; some form **tissue cysts**, others **gametogony** → **zygote → immature oocyst** in feces (**thousands/day**) → **sporulate in soil** over days → **mature sporulated oocyst (infective)** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Humans: oral intake → tachyzoites → bradyzoite tissue cysts (asexual).
- Cats: schizogony and gametogony → oocysts that sporulate in soil.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the life cycle in humans.</summary>

**Answer:** Humans ingest sporulated oocysts (contaminated food, water, fingers) or tissue cysts in undercooked meat; digestive juices release bradyzoites, which invade the small-intestine mucosa and become tachyzoites, are engulfed by macrophages and spread to organs, where they convert to bradyzoites forming tissue cysts in the liver, brain, spleen and eyes.

**Why:** Slide "Life cycle in human body".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the cycle in cats.</summary>

**Answer:** Cats eat infected rats with tissue cysts; the released organisms multiply by schizogony into merozoites in intestinal cells; some form tissue cysts and others undergo gametogony, producing a zygote that is shed as an immature oocyst, which sporulates in soil into the infective mature oocyst.

**Why:** Slides "Life cycle in animal".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which reproduction occurs in each host?</summary>

**Answer:** In humans and other intermediate hosts only asexual reproduction occurs; in cats (definitive hosts) both sexual and asexual reproduction occur.

**Why:** Slide "Life cycle".
</details>

## 4. Pathogenesis and clinical features

- **Most infections are asymptomatic**; **symptoms with immune deficiency** and **in pregnancy**.
- **Congenital toxoplasmosis:** the fetus acquires **tachyzoites through the placenta**; **abortion/stillbirth** is the main pregnancy complication; may affect **male fertility** (reported).
- **If the fetus survives:** **bradyzoites persist in CNS and retina**; **brain necrosis with cysts, cerebral calcification, convulsions, deafness, microcephaly, hydrocephalus, chorioretinitis, micro-ophthalmos, lymphadenopathy**; a **major cause of blindness in newborns**; **mental retardation, seizures, deafness, blindness**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Congenital: placenta, abortion; survivors: chorioretinitis, hydrocephalus, calcification.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. When does toxoplasmosis cause symptoms?</summary>

**Answer:** Most infections are asymptomatic; symptoms appear with immune deficiency and are most important during pregnancy when the fetus is infected by tachyzoites crossing the placenta.

<details><summary>Q2. Describe congenital toxoplasmosis.</summary>

**Answer:** The fetus infected via the placenta may die (abortion/stillbirth) or survive with persistence in the CNS and retina: brain necrosis and calcification, convulsions, deafness, microcephaly, hydrocephalus, chorioretinitis, micro-ophthalmos and lymphadenopathy, causing blindness and mental retardation.

**Answer:** In the pregnancy complication the fetus dies (abortion/stillbirth); in congenital toxoplasmosis the fetus survives with malformations evident after birth: mental retardation, seizures, deafness and blindness.

## 5. Diagnosis, treatment and prophylaxis

- **Direct:** **trophozoites (tachyzoites) and cysts** in tissues/fluids.
- **Serology (IgG):** **latex agglutination**, **Sabin-Feldman dye test** (first; **alkaline methylene blue**, antibody inhibits trophozoites), **ELISA**, **IFAT**.
- **Biopsy:** **bone marrow (most important)**, lymph node/spleen/liver aspirates (PAS), blood, sputum, CSF, amniotic fluid (**Giemsa/Leishman**: **crescent tachyzoites, blue cytoplasm, dark nucleus**). **Molecular:** **DNA hybridization, PCR**. **Culture:** egg embryo cells.
- **Adult treatment:** **pyrimethamine** (200 mg day 1 then 50 mg daily, 4 weeks) + **sulfadiazine** (2 g load then 1 g four times daily, 4 weeks) + **folic acid** (15 mg twice weekly) + fluids and sodium bicarbonate.
- **Congenital:** pyrimethamine + **sulfadiazine** (50–100 mg/kg/day in 2 doses for 3 weeks) + **folic acid** (3 mg twice weekly) + **corticosteroids** (1 mg/kg/day, tapered) for **sight-threatening lesions**.
- **Prophylaxis:** **trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole daily for 1 year** with monthly antibody titres; **avoid infected cats and feces**; **wash hands**, **wash/disinfect vegetables and fruit**; **treat the female before marriage**; **no human vaccine**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sabin-Feldman = first serologic test (methylene blue); bone marrow = best biopsy specimen.
- Pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine + folic acid; no vaccine.

</div>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Cats = definitive host; humans = intermediate host (asexual only).**
- **Fresh oocysts are not infective; sporulated oocysts are.**
- **Tachyzoite = acute; bradyzoite in tissue cyst = chronic; oocyst only in the cat gut.**
- **Congenital infection needs tachyzoites through the placenta.**
- **Sabin-Feldman = first serologic test, methylene blue.**
