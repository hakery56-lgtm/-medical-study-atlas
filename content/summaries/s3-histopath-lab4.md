## 1. Tissue repair and granulation tissue

- ⭐ **Two reactions of repair:** **regeneration** (replace damaged components and return to normal) and **connective tissue deposition (scar formation)**.
- **Regeneration** by: (1) **proliferation of surviving cells** that keep proliferative capacity; (2) **tissue stem cells**.
- **Scar** forms if the injured tissue **cannot completely restitute** or its **supporting structures are severely damaged**.
- **Granulation tissue** (unique to healing wounds): **proliferating fibroblasts + loose connective tissue + new blood vessels + scattered chronic inflammatory cells**.
- **Gross:** **pink, soft, granular** (beneath a scab). **Microscopic:** **proliferating capillaries (angiogenesis)**, **spindle fibroblasts** making **thin collagen**, myofibroblasts, mixed inflammatory cells (lymphocytes, plasma cells, neutrophils, eosinophils, macrophages), **edematous stroma** from **leaky new capillaries**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Repair = regeneration or scar.
- Granulation tissue = fibroblasts + new vessels + inflammatory cells; pink, soft, granular, edematous.

</div>

## 2. Scars: normal, hypertrophic and keloid

- **Scar:** fibrous replacement tissue of **collagen, fibroblasts and other ECM**. **Gross:** **white, contracted, weaker** than normal skin. **Histology (mature):** **thicker collagen bundles and spindle cells parallel to the epidermis**; **vessels perpendicular** to the epidermis.
- **Epidermis layers (surface → base):** **stratum corneum**, **lucidum**, **granulosum** (purplish granules), **spinosum** (thickest, intercellular bridges), **basal layer** on the **basement membrane**.

| Feature | Hypertrophic scar | Keloid |
|---|---|---|
| **Growth** | **Raised, red, inflexible; confined** to the original wound | **Exceeds** the original wound margins |
| **Course** | **Regresses spontaneously** (12–24 months, maybe incomplete) | **Does not regress** |
| **After excision** | — | **High recurrence** |
| **Histology** | **Epidermal flattening**; scarred papillary dermis; **horizontal wavy collagen** | **Fibroblast aggregates**; **thick, ropey collagen bands** in the dermis |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hypertrophic = within the wound, regresses; keloid = beyond the wound, recurs.
- Keloid: thick ropey collagen; hypertrophic: wavy horizontal collagen.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe a mature skin scar.</summary>

**Answer:** White, contracted and weaker than normal skin; histologically thicker collagen bundles with spindle cells parallel to the epidermis and vessels perpendicular to it.

**Why:** Slides "Scar" and "Skin".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate hypertrophic scars from keloids.</summary>

**Answer:** Hypertrophic scars are raised, red and confined to the original wound and regress spontaneously within 12-24 months; keloids extend beyond the original margins, do not regress and recur frequently after excision.

**Why:** Slides "Excessive scarring".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the histologic features of keloid and hypertrophic scar?</summary>

**Answer:** Hypertrophic: epidermal flattening, scarring of the papillary dermis and horizontal wavy collagen. Keloid: increased fibroblast cellularity and aggregates with thick, ropey collagen bands in the dermis.

**Why:** Slides on hypertrophic scar and keloid.
</details>

## 3. Air pollution and pneumoconioses

- **Environmental diseases:** caused by **chemical or physical agents** (ambient, workplace, personal: diet, drugs, alcohol, tobacco), including **nutritional** disease; exposure **acute** or **chronic low-level**.
- **Air pollution:** the **lung** is the major organ: **inflammation, ↑ airway reactivity, ↓ mucociliary clearance, ↑ infections**.

| Condition | Cause | Pathology |
|---|---|---|
| **Anthracosis / CWP** ("black lung") | **Coal dust**, urban or tobacco smoke particles | **Coal-filled macrophages** around bronchioles, vessels, pleura, hilar nodes; **fibrogenic only in massive amounts** |
| **Silicosis** | **Silica** | **Silicotic nodule:** **concentric pink collagen**, anthracotic pigment, **lymphocyte rim**; silica **colorless on H&E**, **bright white crystals under polarized light**; macrophage fibrogenic response |
| **Asbestos** | **Asbestos fibers** | **Fibrous plaques, effusions, asbestosis, bronchogenic carcinoma, mesothelioma**, laryngeal and possibly colon carcinoma; **asbestos bodies = iron-coated, dumbbell-shaped**, iron stain |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- CWP = coal dust, fibrosis only with massive exposure; silica = nodules, polarized light; asbestos = bodies, mesothelioma.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What are the effects of air pollution on the lung?</summary>

**Answer:** Inflammation, increased airway reactivity, diminished mucociliary clearance and increased infections.

**Why:** Slide "Air pollution".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the silicotic nodule.</summary>

**Answer:** Nodular collagenous fibrosis made of pink collagen in concentric layers, with anthracotic pigment and a peripheral rim of lymphocytes; silica is colorless on H&E but appears as bright white crystals under polarized light and induces macrophage-mediated collagen deposition.

**Why:** Slides "Silicotic nodule" and "Lung pneumoconioses".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the diseases caused by asbestos and what is an asbestos body?</summary>

**Answer:** Fibrous plaques, pleural effusions, asbestosis, bronchogenic carcinoma, mesothelioma, laryngeal and possibly colon carcinoma. An asbestos body is an asbestos fiber coated with iron, dumbbell-shaped and often fragmented, seen in alveolar spaces and macrophages.

**Why:** Slides "Asbestos".
</details>

## 4. Carbon monoxide, lead, tobacco and alcohol

- **Carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning** → **hypoxia**.
- **Lead:** concentrates in **metaphyses of growing bones** (distal femur, both ends of tibia, distal radius); **80–85% of absorbed lead** goes to **bones and teeth**. Case: **blue line at the gum margin** in a **car-battery** worker with **colicky abdominal pain**.
- **Tobacco:** the **most common exogenous cause of human cancer**, **90% of lung cancers**; smoker's lung shows **carbon particle accumulation**.
- **Hepatic steatosis (fatty change):** **large lipid vacuoles in hepatocytes (macrovesicular)**; lipid accumulates when **lipoprotein transport is disrupted** and/or **fatty acids accumulate**; **alcohol** is a hepatotoxin disturbing **mitochondrial and microsomal function**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- CO → hypoxia; lead → metaphyses, gum blue line, colic; tobacco → 90% of lung cancers; alcohol → macrovesicular steatosis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Where does lead accumulate and what did the case show?</summary>

**Answer:** In the metaphyses of growing bones (distal femur, both tibial ends, distal radius); 80-85% of absorbed lead is in bones and teeth. The patient dismantling car batteries had a blue gum line and colicky abdominal pain.

**Why:** Slides "Lead poisoning".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the contribution of tobacco to cancer?</summary>

**Answer:** Tobacco is the most common exogenous cause of human cancers and is responsible for 90% of lung cancers; smokers' lungs accumulate carbon particles.

**Why:** Slides "Effects of tobacco".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe hepatic steatosis and the role of alcohol.</summary>

**Answer:** Large lipid vacuoles within hepatocytes (macrovesicular steatosis) when lipoprotein transport is disrupted or fatty acids accumulate; alcohol is a hepatotoxin that interferes with mitochondrial and microsomal function, causing lipid accumulation.

**Why:** Slide "Hepatic steatosis".
</details>

## 5. Protein-energy malnutrition

- **Causes of PEM:** **inadequate intake, chronic disease, anorexia nervosa, alcoholism**.

| Feature | Marasmus | Kwashiorkor |
|---|---|---|
| **Cause** | **Severe calorie lack** | **High-CHO, low-protein diet** after weaning |
| **Age** | Children | **18–24 months** |
| **Muscle / fat** | **Wasting of fat and muscle** (somatic protein depleted) | Muscle lost, **subcutaneous fat present** |
| **Edema** | None | **Generalized edema, moon face** |
| **Skin / hair** | **Dry loose skin**, dry brittle hair | **Skin exfoliation**; **straight, friable, depigmented** hair |
| **Other** | **Prominent skeleton, large head, old wizened face, sunken fontanelles, weight loss > 40%, anemia** | **Fatty enlarged liver**, anemia, vitamin deficiency, apathy and irritability |

- **Cachexia:** PEM in **chronically ill** patients (cancer, AIDS); mainly **muscle** loss; **TNF and other cytokines → proteolysis**; important cause of death in cancer.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Marasmus: calories, wasting, no edema. Kwashiorkor: protein, edema, fatty liver, preserved fat.
- Cachexia: cytokine (TNF) driven muscle wasting.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare marasmus and kwashiorkor.</summary>

**Answer:** Marasmus: severe calorie deficiency with wasting of fat and muscle, prominent skeleton, old face, no edema and over 40% weight loss. Kwashiorkor: after weaning to a high-carbohydrate, low-protein diet at 18-24 months, with edema and moon face, preserved subcutaneous fat, skin exfoliation, depigmented friable hair and fatty liver.

**Why:** Slides "Marasmus" and "Kwashiorkor".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the causes of PEM.</summary>

**Answer:** Inadequate food intake, chronic diseases, anorexia nervosa and alcoholism.

**Why:** Slide "Causes of PEM".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is cachexia and what causes cancer cachexia?</summary>

**Answer:** Protein-energy malnutrition in chronically ill patients, mainly affecting muscle; in cancer it is caused by TNF and other cytokines leading to proteolysis and progressive muscle wasting.

**Why:** Slide "Cachexia".
</details>

## 6. Vitamins: A and D deficiency

- **Classification:** **fat-soluble A, D, E, K**; **water-soluble B, C**. **Causes of deficiency:** reduced intake, wrong cooking/storage, impaired GI absorption, increased demands, **iatrogenic** (e.g. **isoniazid** and **B6**).
- **Vitamin A:** function of **mucus-secreting epithelium** and **vision**; deficiency = **xerophthalmia**; **Bitot's spots** = **white foamy** desquamated keratinized conjunctival cells with lipid beside the iris, with **dry, inflamed conjunctiva**.
- **Vitamin D:** maintains plasma **Ca⁺⁺ and PO₄** (with **PTH**); from **diet and endogenous synthesis**. Deficiency → **rickets** (children) and **osteomalacia** (adults).
- **Rickets:** **inadequate calcification of epiphyseal cartilage** → **frontal bossing, deformed chest plate, lumbar lordosis, bowing legs**. **Osteomalacia:** **newly formed osteoid inadequately mineralized** → **weak bone, fractures**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Vitamin A: xerophthalmia, Bitot's spots. Vitamin D: rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What are the functions of vitamin A and its deficiency manifestation?</summary>

**Answer:** It affects the function of mucus-secreting epithelium and maintains vision; deficiency causes xerophthalmia, with Bitot's spots (white foamy keratinized conjunctival cells with lipid next to the iris).

**Why:** Slides "Vitamin A deficiency".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What does vitamin D deficiency cause?</summary>

**Answer:** Rickets in growing children and osteomalacia in adults; vitamin D maintains plasma calcium and phosphate with parathyroid hormone.

**Why:** Slide "Vitamin D".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe rickets and osteomalacia.</summary>

**Answer:** Rickets: inadequate calcification of epiphyseal cartilage with deranged bone growth (frontal bossing, deformed chest, lumbar lordosis, bowing legs). Osteomalacia: inadequately mineralized newly formed osteoid, weakening bone and increasing fracture risk.

**Why:** Slides "Rickets" and "Osteomalacia".
</details>
