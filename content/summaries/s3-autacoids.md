## 1. Autacoids: definition and classification

- ⭐ **Autacoid** comes from the Greek **autos (self)** and **akos (medicinal agent, remedy)**.
- **Endogenous compounds formed by the tissues on which they act** → they function as **local hormones**; unlike circulating hormones they are produced by **many tissues**, not specific endocrine glands.

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| **1. Amine autacoids** | **Histamine, 5-hydroxytryptamine (serotonin)** |
| **2. Lipid-derived autacoids** | **Prostaglandins, leukotrienes, platelet activating factor** |
| **3. Peptide autacoids** | **Plasma kinins (bradykinin, kallidin), angiotensin** |
| **4. Others** | **Cytokines** (interleukins, TNFα, GM-CSF, etc.) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Autacoids = local hormones made by the tissues on which they act, not by endocrine glands.
- Four classes: amines, lipid-derived, peptides, others (cytokines).

</div>

## 2. Histamine: distribution, release and receptors

- **Histamine** is involved in **allergic and inflammatory reactions, gastric acid secretion** and **neurotransmission** in parts of the brain.
- **Distribution:** all tissues, especially **lung, skin, GIT**; high in **mast cells and basophils**, in venoms and insect-sting secretions. In mast cells it is stored in **granules as an inactive complex**; if not stored it is inactivated by **amine oxidase**.
- ⭐ **Release:** **A. immunologic** (**IgE**; mediator of **immediate type I allergy**); **B. chemical/mechanical** (certain amines and drugs: **morphine, tubocurarine, vancomycin**).

| Receptor | Postreceptor mechanism |
|---|---|
| **H1** | **Gq: ↑ IP3, DAG** |
| **H2** | **Gs: ↑ cAMP** |
| **H3** | **Gi: ↓ cAMP** |
| **H4** | **Gi: ↓ cAMP** |

- **H1 and H2** are widely expressed and are the targets of clinically useful drugs.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Histamine stored in mast cell granules; released by IgE (type I allergy) or by drugs (morphine, tubocurarine, vancomycin).
- H1 = Gq (IP3, DAG); H2 = Gs (↑ cAMP); H3 and H4 = Gi (↓ cAMP).
- H1 and H2 are the clinically important receptors.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Where is histamine found and how is it stored?</summary>

**Answer:** In all tissues, especially lung, skin and GIT, at high concentration in mast cells and basophils and in venoms/insect stings; in mast cells it is stored in granules as an inactive complex, and unstored histamine is rapidly inactivated by amine oxidase.

**Why:** Slide "Distribution".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the mechanisms of histamine release?</summary>

**Answer:** Immunologic release by IgE antibodies (immediate type I allergic reactions) and chemical/mechanical release by certain amines and drugs such as morphine, tubocurarine and vancomycin.

**Why:** Slide "Release of histamine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give the postreceptor mechanisms of the histamine receptors.</summary>

**Answer:** H1: Gq with increased IP3 and DAG; H2: Gs with increased cAMP; H3 and H4: Gi with decreased cAMP.

**Why:** Slide "Target of histamine" (receptor table).
</details>

## 3. Effects and uses of histamine

| System | Effect |
|---|---|
| **H1: exocrine** | ↑ **nasal and bronchial mucus** (respiratory symptoms) |
| **H1: bronchial smooth muscle** | **Constriction** → asthma symptoms, ↓ lung capacity |
| **H1: intestinal smooth muscle** | **Constriction** → cramps, diarrhea |
| **H1: sensory nerve endings** | **Itching and pain** |
| **H1 + H2: cardiovascular** | **↓ BP** (↓ peripheral resistance); **positive chronotropism (H2)**; **positive inotropism (H1 and H2)** |
| **H1 + H2: skin** | **Capillary dilation and ↑ permeability** → **triple response** |
| **H2: stomach** | **↑ gastric HCl secretion** |

- ⭐ **Triple response:** intradermal histamine causes **redness** (small vessel dilation), **edema** (extravasation of fluid) and **flare** (axon reflex).
- **Metabolic effect:** absence of the **H3 receptor** in animals causes **obesity**.
- **Clinical uses:** **histamine aerosol** = provocative test of **bronchial hyper-reactivity**; **betahistine** for **Meniere's disease** (↑ blood supply to the inner ear).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- H1: mucus, bronchoconstriction, gut cramps, itch; H2: gastric acid; both: ↓ BP, skin triple response.
- Triple response = redness, edema, flare.
- Uses: bronchial challenge test; betahistine in Meniere's disease.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the H1 receptor effects.</summary>

**Answer:** Increased nasal and bronchial mucus, bronchial smooth muscle constriction (asthma symptoms), intestinal smooth muscle constriction (cramps and diarrhea) and sensory nerve stimulation (itching and pain).

**Why:** Slide "Tissue and organ system effects of histamine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the triple response.</summary>

**Answer:** Intradermal histamine causes redness (dilation of small vessels), edema (extravasation of fluid) and flare (axon reflex).

**Why:** Slide "Other effects of histamine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the clinical uses of histamine?</summary>

**Answer:** Histamine aerosol is used in pulmonary function laboratories as a provocative test of bronchial hyper-reactivity; betahistine is used for Meniere's disease (increases blood supply to the inner ear).

**Why:** Slide "Clinical uses of histamine".

## 4. H1 antagonists

- **First generation:** **diphenhydramine, hydroxyzine, cyclizine, meclizine, chlorpheniramine, promethazine, cyproheptadine**. **Second generation:** **fexofenadine, loratadine, cetirizine, levocetirizine, desloratadine**.

| **First generation** (vs second) |
|---|
| **Strong sedative effects** |
| **More likely to block autonomic receptors** |
| **High distribution into the CNS** |
| **Less metabolized by CYP3A4**, fewer interactions (e.g. ketoconazole) |
| **Shorter duration of action** (except **meclizine**) |

### Actions not caused by H1 blockade
| Action | Drug |
|---|---|
| **Sedation** | First-generation agents |
| **Anti-nausea / anti-emetic** | **Doxylamine** |
| **Anti-parkinsonism** | **Diphenhydramine** |
| **Anticholinoceptor** | Especially 1st generation |
| **Adrenoceptor blocking** | **Promethazine** |
| **Serotonin blocking** | **Cyproheptadine** |
| **Local anesthesia** (block Na⁺ channels; more potent than procaine) | **Diphenhydramine, promethazine** |
| **Mast cell stabilizing** | **Cetirizine** |

- **Uses:** **allergic rhinitis, urticaria, angioedema, atopic dermatitis**; **motion sickness and vestibular disturbances** (diphenhydramine, promethazine); **nausea and vomiting of pregnancy** (meclizine).
- **Interaction:** additive effects with other **CNS depressants**; contraindicated while **driving or operating machinery**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- First generation: sedating, anticholinergic effects, crosses the BBB, shorter action (meclizine excepted).
- Doxylamine anti-emetic; diphenhydramine anti-parkinson and local anesthetic; promethazine adrenoceptor block; cyproheptadine anti-serotonin; cetirizine mast cell stabilizer.
- Avoid driving with CNS depressant combinations.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Classify the H1 antagonists and compare first with second generation.</summary>

**Answer:** First generation: diphenhydramine, hydroxyzine, cyclizine, meclizine, chlorpheniramine, promethazine, cyproheptadine; second generation: fexofenadine, loratadine, cetirizine, levocetirizine, desloratadine. First generation is strongly sedative, more likely to block autonomic receptors, widely distributed in the CNS, less metabolized by CYP3A4 (fewer interactions) and shorter-acting (except meclizine).

**Why:** Slides "Histamine antagonists" and "First-generation, in contrast to the second generation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the actions of H1 antagonists not caused by histamine receptor blockade.</summary>

**Answer:** Sedation, anti-nausea/anti-emetic (doxylamine), anti-parkinsonism (diphenhydramine), anticholinoceptor actions, adrenoceptor blockade (promethazine), serotonin blockade (cyproheptadine), local anesthesia (diphenhydramine and promethazine) and mast cell stabilization (cetirizine).

**Why:** Slide "Actions not caused by histamine receptor blockade".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the clinical uses and the main drug interaction of H1 antagonists?</summary>

**Answer:** Allergic rhinitis, urticaria, angioedema and atopic dermatitis; motion sickness (diphenhydramine, promethazine); nausea and vomiting of pregnancy (meclizine). Concurrent CNS depressants give additive effects and are contraindicated while driving or operating machinery.

**Why:** Slides "Clinical uses" and "Drug interactions".
</details>

## 5. Serotonin (5-HT): roles and receptors

- **Roles:** **neurotransmitter**; **local GIT hormone** regulating motility (**about 90%** of the body's 5-HT is in the **intestines**); role in **clotting**; involved in **migraine headache**.
- **Synthesis:** from the amino acid **L-tryptophan**; the free amine is stored or rapidly inactivated by **monoamine oxidase**.

| Receptor | Distribution | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **5-HT1A** | Raphe nuclei, hippocampus | **Gi ↓ cAMP** |
| **5-HT1B** | Substantia nigra, globus pallidus, basal ganglia | Gi ↓ cAMP |
| **5-HT1D / 1E / 1F** | Brain / cortex, putamen / cortex, hippocampus | Gi ↓ cAMP |
| **5-HT1P** | Enteric nervous system | Go, **slow EPSP** |
| **5-HT2A** | Platelets, smooth muscle, cerebral cortex | **Gq ↑ IP3** |
| **5-HT2B** | Stomach fundus | Gq ↑ IP3 |
| **5-HT2C** | Choroid, hippocampus, substantia nigra | Gq ↑ IP3 |
| **5-HT3** | Area postrema, sensory and enteric nerves | **Na⁺/K⁺ ion channel** |
| **5-HT4** | CNS, myenteric neurons, smooth muscle | **Gs ↑ cAMP** |
| **5-HT5A,B** | Brain | ↓ cAMP |
| **5-HT6,7** | Brain | Gs ↑ cAMP |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 5-HT: neurotransmitter, GI hormone (90% in the gut), clotting, migraine; made from L-tryptophan, inactivated by MAO.
- 5-HT1 = Gi; 5-HT2 = Gq; 5-HT3 = ion channel; 5-HT4 and 6,7 = Gs.

</div>

## 6. Serotonin: effects, agonists and antagonists

- **Respiratory:** a small direct stimulant effect. **Nervous system:** neurotransmitter, **precursor of melatonin** (sleep-wake), role in the **vomiting center**, a **potent stimulant of pain and itching**. **Cardiovascular:** **vascular smooth muscle contraction** and **enhanced platelet aggregation**. **GIT:** **↑ tone, facilitates peristalsis**. **Skeletal muscle — serotonin syndrome:** **MAO inhibitors + serotonin agonists** → **hyperthermia** from excessive muscle contraction.

| Drug | Type and use |
|---|---|
| **Buspirone** | **5-HT1A agonist**; non-benzodiazepine **anxiolytic** |
| **Cisapride** | **5-HT4 agonist**; GIT motility disorders |
| **Tegaserod** | **5-HT4 partial agonist**; **IBS with constipation** |
| **Sumatriptan** (and congeners) | **Acute migraine and cluster headache** |
| **Phenoxybenzamine** | Long-lasting block of **5-HT2 and α-receptors** |
| **Cyproheptadine** | **H1 + 5-HT2 block**, antimuscarinic, sedation; **carcinoid tumor**, **cold-induced urticaria** |
| **Ondansetron** | Prototypical **5-HT3 antagonist**; **nausea and vomiting after surgery and chemotherapy** |

- On the serotonergic neuron diagram: **LSD** (psychedelic), **fluoxetine** (antidepressant), **buspirone** (anxiolytic), **ondansetron** (antiemetic), **sumatriptan** (antimigraine), **cisapride** (prokinetic).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Serotonin: constricts vessels, aggregates platelets, increases GI motility, precursor of melatonin, stimulates pain/itch.
- Serotonin syndrome = MAO inhibitors + serotonin agonists → hyperthermia.
- Buspirone (5-HT1A), sumatriptan (migraine), cisapride/tegaserod (5-HT4), ondansetron (5-HT3 antagonist).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the tissue and organ system effects of serotonin.</summary>

**Answer:** Small direct respiratory stimulation; CNS neurotransmitter, melatonin precursor, vomiting center role and potent stimulation of pain and itching; vascular smooth muscle contraction and enhanced platelet aggregation; increased GIT tone and peristalsis; and serotonin syndrome (hyperthermia) when MAO inhibitors are given with serotonin agonists.

**Why:** Slide "Tissue and organ system effects" (serotonin).
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the serotonin agonists and their uses.</summary>

**Answer:** Buspirone (5-HT1A agonist, anxiolytic), cisapride (5-HT4 agonist, GIT motility disorders), tegaserod (5-HT4 partial agonist, IBS with constipation) and sumatriptan and its congeners (acute migraine and cluster headache).

**Why:** Slide "Serotonin agonists".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the serotonin antagonists and their uses.</summary>

**Answer:** Phenoxybenzamine (long-lasting 5-HT2 and α blocker); cyproheptadine (H1 and 5-HT2 block, antimuscarinic, sedative; carcinoid tumor and cold-induced urticaria); ondansetron (5-HT3 antagonist; nausea and vomiting after surgery and chemotherapy).

**Why:** Slide "Serotonin antagonists".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **H1 = Gq (↑ IP3, DAG); H2 = Gs (↑ cAMP); H3/H4 = Gi (↓ cAMP).**
- **Gastric acid secretion is H2**, not H1; **positive chronotropism is H2.**
- **Meclizine is the long-acting exception among first-generation H1 antagonists.**
- **Doxylamine = anti-emetic; diphenhydramine = anti-parkinsonism; cetirizine = mast cell stabilizer.**
- **5-HT3 is an ion channel;** 5-HT1 is Gi, 5-HT2 is Gq, 5-HT4 is Gs.
- **Ondansetron is a 5-HT3 antagonist;** buspirone is a 5-HT1A agonist; cisapride and tegaserod are 5-HT4 agonists.
- **Serotonin syndrome = MAO inhibitors + serotonin agonists.**
- **Morphine, tubocurarine and vancomycin release histamine chemically** (no IgE needed).
