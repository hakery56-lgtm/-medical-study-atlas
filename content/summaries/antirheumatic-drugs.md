## 1. Traditional DMARDs and methotrexate

- ⭐ **Traditional DMARDs:** **methotrexate, hydroxychloroquine, leflunomide, sulfasalazine**. They **slow the disease, induce remission and prevent further joint destruction**; start **as soon as possible** after diagnosis.
- **Monotherapy** may start with any of them, but **methotrexate is generally preferred**. If the response is inadequate: **combine traditional DMARDs**, or add a **TNF inhibitor or non-TNF biologic**. **NSAIDs or glucocorticoids** can add anti-inflammatory action.
- **Methotrexate:** **folic acid antagonist** → inhibits **cytokine production and purine nucleotide biosynthesis** (immunosuppressive and anti-inflammatory); a **mainstay** of RA treatment; response in **3–6 weeks**.
- **Dose:** **much lower than in cancer chemotherapy**, **once weekly**.
- **Adverse effects:** common: **mucosal ulceration and nausea**; chronic use: **cytopenias (leukopenia), cirrhosis, acute liver dysfunction, pneumonia-like syndrome**.
- **Folic acid** improves tolerability (↓ GI and hepatic effects); monitor **LFTs, CBC, signs of infection**; **contraindicated in pregnancy**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Traditional DMARDs: methotrexate (preferred), hydroxychloroquine, leflunomide, sulfasalazine.
- Methotrexate: folate antagonist, weekly low dose, onset 3–6 weeks, hepatotoxic and myelosuppressive, give folic acid, contraindicated in pregnancy.

</div>

## 2. Hydroxychloroquine, leflunomide, sulfasalazine and glucocorticoids

| Drug | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Hydroxychloroquine** | **Early, mild RA**; may combine with methotrexate; mechanism **unknown**; onset **6 weeks–6 months**; **fewer liver/immune adverse effects**; **ocular toxicity (irreversible retinal damage, corneal deposits)**, CNS disturbances, GI upset, skin discoloration/eruptions |
| **Leflunomide** | After biotransformation a **reversible DHODH inhibitor** (pyrimidine synthesis) → **cell arrest of autoimmune lymphocytes**; used when **methotrexate is not tolerated/contraindicated** or **added to methotrexate**; adverse: headache, diarrhea, nausea, weight loss, allergic/flu-like reactions, rash, **alopecia, hypokalemia**; **hepatotoxic** (not in liver disease); **contraindicated in pregnancy**; monitor infection, CBC, electrolytes, liver enzymes |
| **Sulfasalazine** | Indications like leflunomide; mechanism **unclear**; onset **1–3 months**; **GI effects (nausea, vomiting, anorexia) and leukopenia** |
| **Glucocorticoids** | **Symptomatic relief and bridge** until DMARDs work; **lowest dose, shortest duration** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hydroxychloroquine: mild early RA, retinal toxicity.
- Leflunomide: DHODH inhibitor (pyrimidines), hepatotoxic, teratogenic.
- Sulfasalazine: slow onset, GI effects and leukopenia.
- Glucocorticoids: bridge therapy at the lowest dose for the shortest time.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe hydroxychloroquine in RA.</summary>

**Answer:** Used for early, mild RA and may be combined with methotrexate; its mechanism is unknown and onset takes 6 weeks to 6 months; it has fewer liver and immune adverse effects but can cause ocular toxicity (irreversible retinal damage, corneal deposits), CNS disturbances, GI upset and skin discoloration and eruptions.

**Why:** Slide "Hydroxychloroquine".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the mechanism, uses and precautions of leflunomide.</summary>

**Answer:** After biotransformation it reversibly inhibits DHODH (needed for pyrimidine synthesis), arresting autoimmune lymphocytes; used as monotherapy when methotrexate is not tolerated or contraindicated, or combined with methotrexate. It is hepatotoxic (not for liver disease), contraindicated in pregnancy, and requires monitoring of infection signs, CBC, electrolytes and liver enzymes.

**Why:** Slides "Leflunomide".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the role of glucocorticoids in RA?</summary>

**Answer:** Potent anti-inflammatory drugs giving symptomatic relief and bridging the time until DMARDs become effective, used at the lowest dose and for the shortest duration possible.

**Why:** Slide "Glucocorticoids".
</details>

## 3. Biologic DMARDs: TNF-α inhibitors

- **Biologic DMARDs** = **TNF-α inhibitors** + **non-TNF biologics**. **IL-1 and TNF-α** (from **synovial macrophages**) stimulate synovial cells to proliferate and make **collagenase** → **cartilage degradation, bone resorption, ↓ proteoglycan synthesis**.
- ⭐ **TNF-α inhibitors:** **adalimumab, certolizumab, etanercept, golimumab, infliximab** — ↓ signs and symptoms, ↓ structural damage, ↑ function; response within **2 weeks**; used **after inadequate response to traditional DMARDs**, alone or combined.
- **Cautions:** **heart failure** (cause/worsen), possible ↑ **lymphoma and other cancers**; also used in **ulcerative colitis, Crohn disease, psoriasis, ankylosing spondylitis**.

| Drug | Type | Dosing | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adalimumab** | **Recombinant monoclonal antibody** | **SC weekly or every other week** | Headache, nausea, agranulocytosis, rash, injection-site reaction, infections |
| **Certolizumab** | **Humanized, pegylated antibody** | **SC every 2 weeks** | Similar adverse effects |
| **Etanercept** | **Fusion protein** | **SC once weekly** | Well tolerated; **+ methotrexate** more effective than either alone |
| **Golimumab** | Binds and neutralizes TNF-α | **SC once a month with methotrexate** | May ↑ hepatic enzymes |
| **Infliximab** | **Chimeric (human + murine) antibody** | **IV every 8 weeks** | **Not monotherapy** (anti-infliximab antibodies) → with methotrexate; infusion reactions (fever, chills, urticaria) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- TNF-α inhibitors: adalimumab, certolizumab (pegylated), etanercept (fusion protein), golimumab, infliximab (chimeric, IV).
- Use after DMARD failure; caution in heart failure; ↑ cancer risk.
- Infliximab needs methotrexate to prevent anti-drug antibodies.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the role of IL-1 and TNF-α in RA and the benefits of TNF-α inhibitors.</summary>

**Answer:** Secreted by synovial macrophages, they stimulate synovial cells to proliferate and make collagenase, degrading cartilage, stimulating bone resorption and inhibiting proteoglycan synthesis. TNF-α inhibitors decrease signs and symptoms, reduce progression of structural damage and improve physical function, with response within 2 weeks.

**Why:** Slides "Biologic DMARDs" and "TNF-α inhibitors".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the TNF-α inhibitors with their route and frequency.</summary>

**Answer:** Adalimumab (SC weekly/every other week), certolizumab (pegylated, SC every 2 weeks), etanercept (fusion protein, SC weekly), golimumab (SC monthly with methotrexate) and infliximab (chimeric, IV every 8 weeks with methotrexate).

**Why:** Slides "Adalimumab" to "Infliximab".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the cautions of TNF-α inhibitors?</summary>

**Answer:** Use cautiously in heart failure (can cause or worsen it); they may increase the risk of lymphoma and other cancers; infliximab should not be used as monotherapy because of anti-infliximab antibodies.

**Why:** Slides "TNF-α inhibitors" and "Infliximab".
</details>

## 4. Non-TNF biologic agents

- **Non-TNF biologics:** **abatacept, rituximab, tocilizumab** (and sarilumab); used **after inadequate response to traditional DMARDs**, alone or combined; if one fails, **try another non-TNF biologic ± methotrexate**.
- ⭐ **Class warnings:** ↑ **infections (TB, fungal opportunistic, sepsis)**; **do not combine biologics**; **hepatitis B reactivation**; **no live vaccines**.

| Drug | Mechanism | Dosing / adverse effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Abatacept** | **Recombinant fusion protein, co-stimulation modulator**: competes with **CD28** for **CD80/CD86** on the APC → **blocks full T-cell activation** | **IV every 4 weeks**; headache, URTI, infusion reactions, nausea |
| **Rituximab** | **Chimeric anti-CD20 antibody** → **B-cell depletion** (B cells activate T cells, make autoantibodies/RF and TNF-α, IL-1) | **IV every 16–24 weeks**; **infusion reactions** (urticaria, hypotension, angioedema) mostly first dose → premedicate with **methylprednisolone, acetaminophen, antihistamine** |
| **Tocilizumab / sarilumab** | **Anti-IL-6 receptor** monoclonal antibodies | **SC every 2 weeks** (tocilizumab also **IV every 4 weeks**); ↑ LFTs, **hyperlipidemia, neutropenia, hypertension**, infusion/injection-site reactions |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Abatacept: CD80/CD86–CD28 co-stimulation blocker; rituximab: anti-CD20 (B-cell depletion); tocilizumab/sarilumab: anti-IL-6 receptor.
- Biologics: infection risk, hepatitis B reactivation, no live vaccines, do not combine.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Explain the mechanism of abatacept.</summary>

**Answer:** T cells need two interactions: the APC must engage the T-cell receptor, and CD80/CD86 on the APC must engage CD28 on the T cell. Abatacept, a recombinant fusion protein and co-stimulation modulator, competes with CD28 for CD80/CD86, preventing full T-cell activation and reducing inflammation (IV every 4 weeks).

**Why:** Slide "Abatacept".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain the mechanism of rituximab and how its reactions are reduced.</summary>

**Answer:** B cells perpetuate synovial inflammation by activating T cells, producing autoantibodies and rheumatoid factor and making TNF-α and IL-1; rituximab, a chimeric anti-CD20 antibody, depletes B cells (IV every 16–24 weeks). Infusion reactions (urticaria, hypotension, angioedema) are reduced by premedication with methylprednisolone, acetaminophen and an antihistamine.

**Why:** Slides "Rituximab".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the general warnings for biologic DMARDs?</summary>

**Answer:** Increased risk of infections (tuberculosis, fungal opportunistic infections, sepsis), biologic DMARDs should not be used together, hepatitis B may reactivate, and live vaccines must not be given.

**Why:** Slide "Non-TNF biologic agents".
</details>

## 5. Tofacitinib and other drugs

- **Tofacitinib:** **synthetic small-molecule oral Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor** (JAKs modulate immune cell activity); for **moderate to severe RA** with **inadequate response or intolerance to methotrexate**.
- **Precautions:** metabolized mainly by **CYP3A4** (adjust dose with potent inhibitors/inducers); **hemoglobin > 9 g/dL to start** and monitor (**anemia**); check **lymphocyte and neutrophil counts**; may ↑ **new primary malignancy and opportunistic infections**; **reserved** because of long-term safety concerns.
- **Infrequently used:** **anakinra, azathioprine, cyclosporine, gold, minocycline** (adverse effects or better alternatives).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Tofacitinib: oral JAK inhibitor, after methotrexate failure; check Hb > 9 g/dL, lymphocytes, neutrophils; CYP3A4.
- Anakinra, azathioprine, cyclosporine, gold and minocycline are rarely used.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is tofacitinib and when is it used?</summary>

**Answer:** A synthetic small-molecule oral inhibitor of Janus kinases, indicated for moderate to severe established RA in patients with inadequate response or intolerance to methotrexate.

**Why:** Slides "Other drugs for rheumatoid arthritis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the precautions and monitoring for tofacitinib.</summary>

**Answer:** CYP3A4 metabolism (dose adjustment with potent inhibitors or inducers); hemoglobin above 9 g/dL to start and monitoring for anemia; lymphocyte and neutrophil counts before and during therapy; increased risk of new primary malignancy and opportunistic infections.

**Why:** Slide on tofacitinib monitoring.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which drugs are used infrequently in RA?</summary>

**Answer:** Anakinra, azathioprine, cyclosporine, gold and minocycline, because of their adverse effect profile or the availability of agents with more proven efficacy.

**Why:** Last slide.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Methotrexate = folate antagonist (purines);** leflunomide = DHODH inhibitor (pyrimidines).
- **Hydroxychloroquine = retinal toxicity;** leflunomide = hepatotoxic, hypokalemia, alopecia.
- **Infliximab is chimeric, IV every 8 weeks, never monotherapy;** certolizumab is pegylated, etanercept is a fusion protein.
- **Abatacept blocks CD80/CD86–CD28;** rituximab is anti-CD20; tocilizumab/sarilumab are anti-IL-6 receptor.
- **No live vaccines and no combination of biologics; hepatitis B can reactivate.**
- **TNF-α inhibitors: caution in heart failure.**
- **Tofacitinib: start only if Hb > 9 g/dL.**
