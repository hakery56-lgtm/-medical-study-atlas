## 1. The case and hypokalemia

- **Case:** asthma patient on **oral theophylline, prednisolone and on-demand inhaled salbutamol**; severe attack not responding to inhaled salbutamol → **s.c. salbutamol 0.5 mg (repeated after 4 h)** → **weak and lethargic; ECG: flat T wave + prominent U wave.**
- **ECG = hypokalemia.**
- **Cause:** **β₂ stimulation ↑ Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase in skeletal muscle → K⁺ shifts into cells** (transient hypokalemia), worsened by **corticosteroids and theophylline**.
- **Receptors:** **β₂ mainly (K⁺ uptake)**; **α₁ stimulation → hyperkalemia.**
- **Asthma:** chronic inflammatory disease with hyperactive bronchi (allergens, viruses, ozone); mediators from mast cells: **histamine, prostaglandins, leukotrienes, PAF.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Flat T + U wave = hypokalemia.
- β₂ agonist → K⁺ into cells.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What do the ECG changes indicate and why did they occur?</summary>

**Answer:** Hypokalemia, because salbutamol (β₂ agonist) increases Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase activity and drives K⁺ into skeletal muscle cells; corticosteroids and theophylline worsen it.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which receptors regulate potassium?</summary>

**Answer:** β₂ receptors mainly (K⁺ uptake); α₁ stimulation causes hyperkalemia.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What advice about potassium should the patient get?</summary>

**Answer:** If weak and lethargic, blood potassium should be measured because several anti-asthmatic drugs cause hypokalemia.

**Why:** Slide 1.
</details>

## 2. β receptors, agonists and blockers

| Receptor | Location | Effect | Agonists | Blockers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **β₁** | **Heart (SA, AV, myocardium), kidney JG cells** | **↑ rate, contractility, conduction, renin** | **Dobutamine, isoprenaline, adrenaline** | **Metoprolol, atenolol, bisoprolol (selective); propranolol (non-selective)** |
| **β₂** | **Bronchi, vessels (skeletal muscle), uterus, liver** | **Bronchodilation, vasodilation, uterine relaxation, ↑ glycogenolysis, hypokalemia** | **Salbutamol, terbutaline, formoterol** | **Propranolol, nadolol (non-selective)** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- β₁ = heart; β₂ = bronchi, vessels, uterus, liver.
- Non-selective blockers block β₂.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give effects of β₁ and β₂ stimulation.</summary>

**Answer:** β₁: ↑ heart rate, contractility, conduction and renin; β₂: bronchodilation, vasodilation, uterine relaxation, glycogenolysis and hypokalemia.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Name β₁-selective and non-selective blockers.</summary>

**Answer:** Selective: metoprolol, atenolol, bisoprolol; non-selective: propranolol, nadolol.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Name β₂ agonists.</summary>

**Answer:** Salbutamol, terbutaline and formoterol.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

## 3. Inhaler sequence, β-blockers in asthma and α₂ agonists

- **Acute attack:** **β₂ agonist first** (rapid bronchodilation → better steroid deposition); **corticosteroids (beclomethasone) take hours → control, not relief.**
- **β-blockers in asthma + hypertension:** **non-selective (propranolol) contraindicated** (β₂ block → bronchoconstriction); **β₁-selective only with great caution** (**selectivity is dose-dependent**); **prefer CCBs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs.**
- **Drugs that stimulate receptors but reduce sympathetic activity:** **central α₂ agonists** — **clonidine** (↓ NE release → ↓ HR, BP) and **methyldopa** (→ α-methylnorepinephrine; useful in **pregnancy hypertension**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- β₂ agonist first, steroid later.
- Clonidine and methyldopa = central α₂ agonists.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which inhaler is used first and why?</summary>

**Answer:** The β₂ agonist, because it opens the airways and improves steroid deposition; steroids act slowly and are for control.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Can β-blockers treat hypertension in an asthmatic?</summary>

**Answer:** Non-selective ones are contraindicated; β₁-selective drugs only with great caution at the lowest dose; CCBs, ACE inhibitors or ARBs are preferred.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give two sympathetic-inhibiting adrenergic agonists.</summary>

**Answer:** Clonidine and methyldopa, which stimulate central α₂ receptors and reduce sympathetic outflow.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

## 4. β-blockers in diabetes

- **Risks:** **mask hypoglycemia symptoms (tachycardia, tremor)** and **prolong hypoglycemia** (↓ β₂ hepatic glycogenolysis).
- **Safer:** **β₁-selective (atenolol, metoprolol, bisoprolol)**; avoid **propranolol**.
- **Precautions:** use cardioselective drugs only if necessary; **monitor glucose**; **educate** about masked warning signs.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- β-blockers mask hypoglycemia.
- Use β₁-selective if needed.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Why are β-blockers risky in diabetics?</summary>

**Answer:** They mask hypoglycemia symptoms and prolong hypoglycemia by blocking β₂ hepatic glycogenolysis.

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which are safer?</summary>

**Answer:** β₁-selective blockers (atenolol, metoprolol, bisoprolol).

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List precautions.</summary>

**Answer:** Cardioselective drugs only if needed, regular glucose monitoring and patient education.

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>

## 5. Patient guide and theophylline

- **Salbutamol:** **1–2 puffs as needed, up to every 4–6 h; max 8 puffs/24 h**; needing it more often = poor control.
- **Inhaler technique:** shake, breathe out, inhale slowly while pressing, **hold 10 s**, **wait ~30 s between puffs**, **spacer** if coordination is poor.
- **Theophylline:** do not switch brands/doses; keep caffeine steady (**xanthine saturates metabolism**); tell the doctor about drugs and smoking changes.
- **Theophylline PK:** **liver metabolism**; **↑ in heart failure, hepatic impairment, viral infection**; **↓ in smokers and with alcohol (enzyme induction)**; **narrow margin, zero-order (saturation) kinetics**; **toxicity: arrhythmias, convulsions.**
- **Aminophylline** = theophylline + ethylenediamine (more water soluble).
- **Drug names to spell:** **salbutamol, theophylline, prednisolone.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Max 8 puffs/24 h.
- Theophylline: narrow margin; toxic = arrhythmia, convulsions.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is the inhaler used correctly?</summary>

**Answer:** Shake, breathe out fully, inhale slowly while pressing, hold the breath 10 seconds, wait about 30 seconds between puffs, and use a spacer if needed.

**Why:** Slide 2.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What changes theophylline levels?</summary>

**Answer:** Raised by heart failure, hepatic impairment and viral infections; lowered by smoking and alcohol (enzyme induction).

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is theophylline dangerous?</summary>

**Answer:** The toxic dose is close to the therapeutic dose with saturation kinetics; toxicity causes arrhythmias and convulsions.

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>
