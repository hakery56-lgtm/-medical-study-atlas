## 1. The 12-lead ECG and limb leads

- ⭐ **12-lead ECG** = **non-invasive** record of the heart's electrical activity; **10 electrodes → 12 leads** (**12 electrical views, not 12 electrodes**). Used for **arrhythmias, ischemia and MI.**
- **4 limb electrodes (RA, LA, RL, LL)** → **6 limb leads: I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF**; **RL = ground.** **6 chest electrodes (V1-V6)** → **6 chest leads.**
- **Equipment:** ECG machine, 10 electrodes, ECG paper, alcohol wipes/gel, razor if needed. **Preparation:** explain, **supine**, expose **chest, wrists, ankles**, clean skin, shave if needed.
- **Einthoven's triangle:** limb leads between **RA, LA, LL**: **Lead I = RA → LA; Lead II = RA → LL; Lead III = LA → LL.** Place limb electrodes **the same way every time.**

| System | RA / R | LA / L | RL / N | LL / F |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AHA** | **White** | **Black** | **Green** | **Red** |
| **IEC** | **Red** | **Yellow** | **Black** | **Green** |

(AHA mnemonic: **smoke over fire, snow over grass.** Check which system your machine uses.)

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 10 electrodes = 4 limb + 6 chest → 12 leads; RL is the ground.
- Lead I RA→LA; II RA→LL; III LA→LL.
- AHA colors: RA white, LA black, RL green, LL red.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Why do 10 electrodes give 12 leads?</summary>

**Answer:** Four limb electrodes give six limb leads (I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF) and six chest electrodes give V1-V6: a 12-lead ECG is 12 electrical views, not 12 electrodes.

**Why:** Slides "What is a 12-lead ECG?" and "10 electrodes, 12 leads".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Define leads I, II and III.</summary>

**Answer:** Lead I: RA to LA; lead II: RA to LL; lead III: LA to LL (Einthoven's triangle).

**Why:** Slide "Einthoven's triangle".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give the AHA and IEC lead wire colors.</summary>

**Answer:** AHA: RA white, LA black, RL green, LL red; IEC: R red, L yellow, N black, F green.

**Why:** Slide "Lead wire colors".
</details>

## 2. Chest lead placement

- **Find the 4th intercostal space first:** **1 feel the sternal angle (angle of Louis) → 2 find the 2nd rib → 3 count down to the 4th ICS → 4 place V1 and V2.**

| Lead | Position |
|---|---|
| **V1** | **4th ICS, right sternal border** |
| **V2** | **4th ICS, left sternal border** |
| **V3** | **Midway between V2 and V4** (not a landmark) |
| **V4** | **5th ICS, midclavicular line** |
| **V5** | **Horizontal to V4, anterior axillary line** |
| **V6** | **Horizontal to V4, mid axillary line** |

- **Tips:** **place V4 first** (reference level); **V3 halfway**; **V5/V6 level with V4** on a **straight horizontal line** (not following the rib curve); **do not use the nipple** (under the breast with breast tissue).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sternal angle → 2nd rib → 4th ICS → V1 right, V2 left.
- V4: 5th ICS midclavicular; V5, V6 level with V4 at anterior and mid axillary lines.
- V4 first; V3 midway; not the nipple.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do you locate the 4th intercostal space?</summary>

**Answer:** Feel the sternal angle (angle of Louis), find the 2nd rib at that level, count down to the 4th intercostal space and place V1 and V2.

**Why:** Slide "Find the 4th intercostal space first".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Where are V1 to V6 placed?</summary>

**Answer:** V1: 4th ICS right sternal border; V2: 4th ICS left sternal border; V3: between V2 and V4; V4: 5th ICS midclavicular line; V5: level with V4 at the anterior axillary line; V6: level with V4 at the mid axillary line.

**Why:** Slide "Chest lead placement".
</details>

**Answer:** Place V4 first; V3 goes halfway between V2 and V4; V5 and V6 stay level with V4 on a horizontal line; do not use the nipple (place under the breast).

**Why:** Slide "Tips for the chest leads".
</details>

## 3. Checking, recording, errors and safety

- **Check:** good skin contact, no loose cables, minimal movement/tremor, **calibration 25 mm/s and 10 mm/mV.**
- **Record:** **patient still → press Record → ensure clear tracings → print and label.**
- **Common errors:** **wrong lead placement, poor skin contact, reversed limb leads, patient movement.** If quality is poor, **fix the cause and repeat** (do not just print).

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| **V1/V2 too high** | Find the sternal angle, count to the 4th ICS, reposition |
| **V4-V6 not horizontal** | Align V5 and V6 with V4 |
| **Loose electrodes / oily skin** | Clean and dry the skin, replace electrodes |
| **Patient talking, shivering, moving** | Reassure, keep warm, ask for silence |
| **Reversed limb leads** | Check RA, LA, RL, LL connections |

- **Safety:** **clean cables, dispose of electrodes, hand hygiene, maintain privacy.** **Summary:** **Prepare → Place → Check → Record → Label.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Calibration 25 mm/s, 10 mm/mV.
- Poor tracing: fix the cause and repeat.
- Safety: clean cables, dispose electrodes, hand hygiene, privacy.

</div>

**Answer:** Good skin contact, no loose cables, minimal movement or tremor and calibration of 25 mm/s and 10 mm/mV; then keep the patient still, press Record, ensure clear tracings, print and label.

**Why:** Slides "Checking lead connections" and "Recording the ECG".

<details><summary>Q2. List the common errors and their fixes.</summary>

**Answer:** V1/V2 too high (recount from the sternal angle), V4-V6 not horizontal (align with V4), loose electrodes or oily skin (clean, dry, replace), movement or shivering (reassure, warm, silence) and reversed limb leads (check connections).

**Why:** Slide "Error → Fix".
</details>

**Answer:** Clean cables, dispose of electrodes, hand hygiene and maintaining privacy.

**Why:** Slide "Safety and infection control".

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **12 leads come from 10 electrodes, not 12.**
- **RL is the ground; Lead II = RA → LL.**
- **AHA: RA white, LA black; IEC: R red, L yellow.**
- **V4 is at the 5th ICS (not the 4th); V1/V2 are at the 4th ICS.**
- **Do NOT use the nipple as a landmark.**
- **Poor tracing: fix and repeat, do not just print.**
