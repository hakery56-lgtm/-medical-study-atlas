## 1. Objectives, culture and media

- ⭐ **Objectives:** **aseptic transfer, pure cultures, streak plate isolation, colony morphology.**
- **Culture** = grow, isolate and identify bacteria; microscopy shows **morphology**, culture shows **growth characteristics.**
- **Culture medium** = nutrient preparation, **liquid (nutrient broth)** or **solid (nutrient agar).**

| Medium | Agar | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Solid** | **1.5-2%** | **Isolated colonies, pure cultures**, colony traits, short-term plates/slants |
| **Liquid** | **None** | **Bulk growth**, rapid biochemical tests; growth = **turbidity** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Solid media (1.5-2% agar) give isolated colonies; liquid media show turbidity.

</div>

## 2. Aseptic technique and methods of transfer

- ⭐ **Aseptic precautions:** **sterilize the loop before and after use; cool it before touching the culture; open tubes carefully; avoid touching sterile instruments to non-sterile surfaces; label cultures; follow biosafety.**
- **Slant → broth:** sterilize and cool loop → collect growth → open broth aseptically → release inoculum → close and label.
- **Slant → slant:** collect growth → open sterile slant → **zigzag** on the agar surface → close and label.
- **Isolated colony:** pick a well-isolated colony with a **sterile cooled loop** → transfer to broth or slant → label.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sterilize and cool the loop; label everything.
- Slant transfer = zigzag streak.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the aseptic precautions.</summary>

**Answer:** Sterilize the loop before and after use, let it cool, open tubes carefully, avoid touching sterile instruments to non-sterile surfaces, label cultures and follow biosafety procedures.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe transfer from a slant to broth.</summary>

**Why:** Slide 7.

## 3. Streak plate technique

- ⭐ **Materials:** **mixed broth culture, sterile agar plate.**
- **Steps:** **flame and cool the loop and take a loopful; hold the agar half near the flame at an angle.**
  1. **First quadrant:** **tight zigzags** over 1/4 of the plate, not touching the edges.
  2. **Second:** **flame, cool, cross the first streak 2-3 times, zigzag at a right angle.**
  3. **Third:** flame, cool, cross the second streak, third right-angle zigzags.
  4. **Fourth and center:** flame, cool, cross the third streak, then a loose final zigzag **toward the center** without touching older streaks.
- **Incubate upside down for 24 h** (condensation must not drip on growth); isolated colonies appear in the **final quadrants.**
- **Other methods:** **streak plating** (pure colonies), **pour and spread plating** (count viable colonies), **broth and slants/stabs** (bulk growth, short-term maintenance, oxygen requirement).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Flame and cool between quadrants; right-angle streaks.
- Incubate bottom-up for 24 h.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the four quadrants of streaking.</summary>

**Answer:** Tight zigzags in the first quarter; flame and cool and cross the first streak 2-3 times for the second set at a right angle; cross the second streak for the third; cross the third for the fourth and finish with a loose zigzag toward the center.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How is the plate incubated and why?</summary>

**Answer:** Upside down for 24 hours so condensation does not drip onto the growth and isolated colonies form in the last quadrants.

**Why:** Slides 10 and 12.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare streak plating with pour and spread plating.</summary>

**Answer:** Streak plating isolates pure colonies from a mixed sample; pour and spread plating dilute liquid samples to enumerate viable colonies.

**Why:** Slide 12.
</details>

## 4. Colony morphology

- ⭐ **Colony** = visible mass of bacteria on solid medium; morphology allows **preliminary recognition and differentiation.**

| Character | Options / notes |
|---|---|
| **1. Color** | Pigments, **diffusible or non-diffusible**: **green = Pseudomonas aeruginosa; red = Serratia marcescens; buff = Mycobacterium tuberculosis** |
| **2. Surface texture** | Smooth, rough, granular, radiated |
| **3. Shape** | Circular, irregular, lobulated, radiated |
| **4. Elevation** | Flat, slightly elevated, dome-shaped (convex), umbonate |
| **5. Margin** | Smooth (entire), crenated, undulated, irregular |
| **6. Size** | **Diameter in mm** |
| **7. Consistency** | Watery, slightly viscous, **mucoid** (capsule or slime) |
| **8. Odor** | **Never smell cultures directly** (infectious aerosols): **Proteus mirabilis = rotten cooked fishy; Pseudomonas aeruginosa = sweet grape-like; Streptococcus milleri = caramel** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Eight characters; pigment examples and odor examples are testable.
- Never smell plates directly.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Color, surface texture, shape, elevation, margin, size, consistency and odor.

## 5. Preservation, storage and disposal

- **Short-term:** slants or plates at **4 °C** (a few weeks).
- **Mineral oil overlay:** sterile **liquid paraffin** slows drying and prolongs viability.
- **Cryopreservation:** broth with **15% glycerol** at **-70 °C** or liquid nitrogen **-196 °C.**
- **Lyophilization:** freeze-drying under vacuum; viable for **decades.**
- **Disposal:** **autoclave** used plates, tubes and instruments in autoclavable bags; non-autoclaved **BSL-1** items in **1:9 household bleach or 2% sodium hypochlorite.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 4 °C short term; glycerol -70 °C or -196 °C; freeze-drying for decades.
- Autoclave or bleach for disposal.

</div>
