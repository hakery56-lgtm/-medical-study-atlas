## 1. The temporal fossa

- **Temporal region** = **temporal + infratemporal fossae** (interconnected spaces on the lateral head; bone and soft-tissue boundaries).
- **Boundaries of the temporal fossa:** **anterior** — **zygomatic process of frontal + zygomatic bone**; **superior and posterior** — **temporal lines**; **inferior** — **zygomatic arch**. **Floor:** **greater wing of sphenoid, squamous temporal, frontal, parietal**.
- **Contents:** **temporalis and its fascia**, **deep and middle temporal nerves and vessels**, **zygomatico-temporal nerve**, **superficial temporal artery**.
- **Temporalis:** **fan-shaped**, from the **bony floor** and **deep surface of the temporal fascia**; tendon passes **deep to the zygomatic arch** → **coronoid process**; **deep temporal nerve** (anterior division of mandibular); **elevation and retraction** of the mandible.
- **Temporal fascia:** covers temporalis above the zygomatic arch; attaches to the **superior temporal line** above and the **zygomatic arch** below; deep surface gives origin to temporalis; used as a graft in **myringoplasty**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Temporal fossa floor: greater wing of sphenoid, squamous temporal, frontal, parietal.
- Temporalis → coronoid process; elevates and retracts the mandible; deep temporal nerve.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the boundaries and floor of the temporal fossa.</summary>

**Answer:** Anterior: zygomatic process of the frontal bone and zygomatic bone; superior and posterior: temporal lines; inferior: zygomatic arch. The floor is formed by the greater wing of the sphenoid, squamous temporal, frontal and parietal bones.

**Why:** Slides "Boundaries" and "Floor of temporal fossa".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe temporalis.</summary>

**Answer:** A fan-shaped muscle arising from the bony floor of the temporal fossa and the deep surface of the temporal fascia, whose tendon passes deep to the zygomatic arch to the coronoid process; supplied by the deep temporal nerve (anterior division of the mandibular nerve); it elevates and retracts the mandible.

**Why:** Slide "Temporalis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the clinical use of the temporal fascia?</summary>

**Answer:** It is taken as a graft in tympanic membrane perforation (myringoplasty).

**Why:** Slide "Temporal fascia".
</details>

## 2. The infratemporal fossa

- **Wedge-shaped** fossa **inferior** to the temporal fossa, between the **mandibular ramus (lateral)** and the **pharyngeal wall (medial)**; has a roof, lateral and medial walls; **open to the neck posteroinferiorly**.

| Boundary | Structure |
|---|---|
| **Lateral (superficial)** | **Ramus of mandible** |
| **Medial (deep)** | **Lateral pterygoid plate** |
| **Superior** | **Infratemporal surface of greater wing of sphenoid** |
| **Anterior** | **Tuberosity of maxilla** |

- **Communications:** **temporal fossa** (gap deep to the zygomatic arch); **cranial cavity** (**foramen ovale, foramen spinosum**); **pterygopalatine fossa** (**pterygomaxillary fissure**); **orbit** (**inferior orbital fissure**).
- **Contents:** **lateral and medial pterygoid muscles**, **mandibular nerve**, **maxillary nerve**, **otic ganglion**, **chorda tympani**, **maxillary artery**, **pterygoid venous plexus**, **sphenomandibular ligament**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- ITF: lateral = ramus; medial = lateral pterygoid plate; roof = greater wing; anterior = maxillary tuberosity.
- Communications: foramen ovale/spinosum, pterygomaxillary fissure, inferior orbital fissure.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the infratemporal fossa and its boundaries.</summary>

**Answer:** A wedge-shaped fossa below the temporal fossa between the mandibular ramus laterally and the pharyngeal wall medially; lateral boundary: ramus of mandible; medial: lateral pterygoid plate; superior: infratemporal surface of the greater wing of sphenoid; anterior: maxillary tuberosity.

**Why:** Slides "Infratemporal fossa".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the communications of the infratemporal fossa.</summary>

**Answer:** With the temporal fossa through a gap deep to the zygomatic arch; with the cranial cavity through the foramen ovale and foramen spinosum; with the pterygopalatine fossa through the pterygomaxillary fissure; and with the orbit through the inferior orbital fissure.

**Why:** Slide "Communications".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the contents of the infratemporal fossa.</summary>

**Answer:** Lateral and medial pterygoid muscles, mandibular nerve, maxillary nerve, otic ganglion, chorda tympani, maxillary artery, pterygoid plexus of veins and the sphenomandibular ligament.

**Why:** Slide "Contents".
</details>

## 3. Pterygoid muscles and masseter

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Nerve | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lateral pterygoid** | **Upper head:** infratemporal surface of greater wing; **lower head:** lateral surface of lateral pterygoid plate | **Neck of mandible** (front) + **articular disc of TMJ** | **Anterior division of mandibular nerve** | **Pulls condyle forward (depresses mandible)**; **protrusion** (both); **side-to-side** (alternating) |
| **Medial pterygoid** | **Superficial head:** maxillary tuberosity; **deep head:** medial surface of lateral pterygoid plate | **Medial surface of ramus and angle** | **Mandibular nerve** | **Elevation** of mandible |
| **Masseter** | **Zygomatic arch** | **Mandibular angle** | — | **Elevation and protrusion**; supports TMJ capsule |

- **Masticatory muscles:** **temporalis, masseter, lateral pterygoid, medial pterygoid**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Lateral pterygoid depresses and protrudes; medial pterygoid elevates.
- Lateral pterygoid inserts into the TMJ disc.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the lateral pterygoid.</summary>

**Answer:** Upper head from the infratemporal surface of the greater wing of sphenoid and lower head from the lateral surface of the lateral pterygoid plate; inserted into the front of the neck of the mandible and the articular disc of the TMJ; supplied by the anterior division of the mandibular nerve; it pulls the condyle forward to depress the mandible, protrudes the mandible (both sides) and produces side-to-side movement (alternating).

**Why:** Slides "Lateral pterygoid".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the medial pterygoid.</summary>

**Answer:** Superficial head from the maxillary tuberosity and deep head from the medial surface of the lateral pterygoid plate; inserted into the medial surface of the ramus and angle of the mandible; supplied by the mandibular nerve; it elevates the mandible.

**Why:** Slide "Medial pterygoid".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which muscles are the muscles of mastication?</summary>

**Answer:** Temporalis, masseter, lateral pterygoid and medial pterygoid; masseter is a thick rectangular muscle from the zygomatic arch to the mandibular angle that elevates and protrudes the mandible.

**Why:** Slide "Masseter".
</details>

## 4. Mandibular nerve, otic ganglion and chorda tympani

- **Mandibular nerve:** **motor + sensory roots** emerge separately through the **foramen ovale**, unite **below** it → **main trunk** → **small anterior + large posterior division**.
- **From the trunk:** **motor** — **nerve to medial pterygoid** (also two branches via the **otic ganglion without relay** to **tensor palati and tensor tympani**); **sensory** — **meningeal branch (nervus spinosus)** through **foramen spinosum**.

| Division | Branches |
|---|---|
| **Anterior (3 motor + 1 sensory)** | **Masseteric** (masseter), **deep temporal** (temporalis; emerge through the upper border of lateral pterygoid), **nerve to lateral pterygoid**; **buccal nerve** (sensory; between the two heads of lateral pterygoid; skin/mucosa over buccinator) |
| **Posterior (3 sensory + 1 motor)** | **Auriculotemporal, lingual, inferior alveolar**; **mylohyoid nerve** (mylohyoid + anterior belly of digastric) |

- **Otic ganglion:** **parasympathetic**, **medial to the mandibular nerve** just below the foramen ovale; **preganglionic from glossopharyngeal**; **postganglionic via auriculotemporal to the parotid**.
- **Chorda tympani:** branch of the **facial nerve** in the temporal bone → enters the ITF via the **petrotympanic fissure** → **joins the lingual nerve**; **preganglionic to submandibular and sublingual glands**; **taste from anterior 2/3 of the tongue**.
- **Clinical:** **trigeminal neuralgia** — severe pain in the **mandibular or maxillary** distribution; the **ophthalmic** division usually **escapes**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Anterior division: mostly motor (3) + buccal (sensory); posterior: mostly sensory (3) + mylohyoid (motor).
- Otic ganglion = glossopharyngeal → auriculotemporal → parotid; chorda tympani = taste + submandibular/sublingual secretomotor.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the formation and branches of the mandibular nerve.</summary>

**Answer:** Motor and sensory roots pass separately through the foramen ovale and unite below it into a trunk that divides into a small anterior and a large posterior division. The trunk gives the nerve to medial pterygoid (with branches to tensor palati and tensor tympani) and the meningeal branch; the anterior division gives masseteric, deep temporal, lateral pterygoid and buccal nerves; the posterior gives auriculotemporal, lingual, inferior alveolar and mylohyoid nerves.

**Why:** Slides "Mandibular nerve" and its branches.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the otic ganglion.</summary>

**Answer:** A parasympathetic ganglion medial to the mandibular nerve just below the foramen ovale; preganglionic fibres come from the glossopharyngeal nerve and postganglionic fibres reach the parotid gland through the auriculotemporal nerve.

**Why:** Slide "Otic ganglion".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the chorda tympani.</summary>

**Answer:** A branch of the facial nerve in the temporal bone that enters the infratemporal fossa via the petrotympanic fissure and joins the lingual nerve, carrying preganglionic fibres to the submandibular and sublingual glands and taste from the anterior two thirds of the tongue.

**Why:** Slide "Chorda tympani".
</details>

## 5. Maxillary artery and pterygoid venous plexus

- **Maxillary artery:** the **larger terminal branch of the external carotid** (in the **parotid**); supplies the **nasal cavity, lateral wall and roof of the mouth, all teeth, dura mater**; runs up and forward into the ITF → **pterygopalatine fossa** via the **pterygomaxillary fissure**.

| Part | Relation | Branches |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | Between the **neck of mandible and the sphenomandibular ligament** | **Middle meningeal** (foramen spinosum), **inferior alveolar** (mandibular foramen); **deep auricular**, **anterior tympanic**, **accessory meningeal** (foramen ovale) |
| **2nd** | Related to **lateral pterygoid** | **4 muscular/soft tissue:** **deep temporal, pterygoid, masseteric, buccal** |
| **3rd** | In the **pterygopalatine fossa** | **6:** **posterior superior alveolar, infra-orbital, greater palatine, pharyngeal, sphenopalatine, artery of the pterygoid canal** |

- **Pterygoid plexus:** veins corresponding to maxillary artery branches; **posterior end → maxillary vein**; **anterior communication → facial vein (deep facial vein)**; **posterior → retromandibular vein** (via the short maxillary vein).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Maxillary artery parts: 1st (mandibular neck–sphenomandibular ligament), 2nd (lateral pterygoid), 3rd (pterygopalatine fossa).
- Branch counts: 1st = 2 major + 3 minor, 2nd = 4, 3rd = 6.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the maxillary artery and its parts.</summary>

**Answer:** The larger terminal branch of the external carotid in the parotid gland, supplying the nasal cavity, oral cavity wall and roof, all teeth and the dura; its first part lies between the neck of the mandible and the sphenomandibular ligament, the second is related to the lateral pterygoid and the third is in the pterygopalatine fossa.

**Why:** Slides "Maxillary artery".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the branches of the first and third parts.</summary>

**Answer:** First part: middle meningeal and inferior alveolar (major), deep auricular, anterior tympanic and accessory meningeal. Third part: posterior superior alveolar, infra-orbital, greater palatine, pharyngeal, sphenopalatine and the artery of the pterygoid canal.

**Why:** Slides "First part" and "Third part".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the pterygoid venous plexus.</summary>

**Answer:** A venous network around the pterygoid muscles receiving veins corresponding to maxillary artery branches; it drains posteriorly by the maxillary vein, communicates anteriorly with the facial vein through the deep facial vein and posteriorly with the retromandibular vein.

**Why:** Slide "Pterygoid plexus".
</details>

## 6. Temporomandibular joint

- **TMJ:** between the **mandibular fossa of the temporal bone** and the **condylar process**; **synovial joint**; **articular disc** divides it into **upper and lower** compartments; has a **capsule**.
- **Ligaments:** **lateral temporomandibular** (zygomatic root → neck of mandible; **limits posterior movement**); **sphenomandibular** (medial); **stylomandibular** (behind, styloid process → mandibular angle).
- **Relations:** **anterior** — mandibular notch, masseteric nerve and artery; **posterior** — tympanic plate of the external auditory meatus, glenoid process of the parotid; **lateral** — parotid fascia and skin; **medial** — maxillary artery, vein, auriculotemporal nerve.

| Movement | Muscles |
|---|---|
| **Depression** (open) | **Digastric, geniohyoid, mylohyoid** |
| **Elevation** (close) | **Temporalis, masseter, medial pterygoid** |
| **Protrusion** | **Lateral pterygoid** |
| **Retraction** | **Posterior fibres of temporalis** |
| **Lateral shift** | **One-sided lateral pterygoid** + contralateral masseter; mandible moves **away from the contracting side** |

- **Dislocation:** **extreme lateral pterygoid contraction (yawning)** — disc and head lie **in front of the mandibular fossa**; **bilateral → mouth fixed open**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Synovial, disc-divided; lateral ligament limits backward movement.
- Open = digastric/geniohyoid/mylohyoid; close = temporalis/masseter/medial pterygoid; protrusion = lateral pterygoid.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the type and ligaments of the TMJ.</summary>

**Answer:** A synovial joint between the mandibular fossa of the temporal bone and the condylar process, with an articular disc dividing it into superior and inferior parts and a capsule; ligaments are the lateral temporomandibular (limits posterior movement), the sphenomandibular (medial) and the stylomandibular (behind the joint).

**Why:** Slides "Temporomandibular joint" and "Ligaments".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the movements of the TMJ and their muscles.</summary>

**Answer:** Depression by digastric, geniohyoid and mylohyoid; elevation by temporalis, masseter and medial pterygoid; protrusion by lateral pterygoid; retraction by posterior fibres of temporalis; lateral shift by one-sided lateral pterygoid with the contralateral masseter.

**Why:** Slide "Movements".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is TMJ dislocation?</summary>

**Answer:** Extreme contraction of the lateral pterygoid (e.g. yawning) moves the disc and mandibular head in front of the mandibular fossa; if bilateral, the mouth is fixed open.

**Why:** Slide "Dislocation".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Temporalis → coronoid process; masseter → mandibular angle; medial pterygoid → medial ramus/angle; lateral pterygoid → neck + disc.**
- **Mandibular anterior division is mostly motor, with the buccal nerve as its only sensory branch.**
- **Posterior division is mostly sensory, with the mylohyoid nerve as its motor branch.**
- **Chorda tympani joins the lingual nerve; the otic ganglion is supplied by the glossopharyngeal nerve.**
- **Maxillary artery: 2 + 3 branches (1st), 4 (2nd), 6 (3rd).**
- **Lateral temporomandibular ligament limits posterior movement.**
