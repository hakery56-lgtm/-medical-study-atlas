## 1. Femoral triangle

- ⭐ **Boundaries (labeled on the slide):** **base = inguinal ligament**; **lateral = sartorius**; **medial = adductor longus**; **floor = iliopsoas (psoas major + iliacus) and pectineus**.
- **Clinical importance:** a **lump in the groin** is a very common clinical condition; the **femoral artery** is commonly used for **percutaneous arterial catheterization** and **withdrawing blood**.
- **Femoral hernia:** **protrusion of abdominal contents through the femoral canal**.
- The slide does not list the contents or the roof of the triangle.

- Floor: iliopsoas + pectineus.
- Groin lump, arterial catheterization and femoral hernia (through the femoral canal).

## 2. Adductor (subsartorial) canal

- **Also called** the **subsartorial or Hunter's canal**; an **intermuscular canal** in the **middle third of the medial aspect of the thigh**; extends **from the apex of the femoral triangle to the adductor hiatus**.
- ⭐ **Boundaries:** **anteromedial = sartorius**; **lateral = vastus medialis**; **posterior = adductor longus and adductor magnus**.
- **Transmits:** **femoral artery**, **femoral vein (posterior to the artery)**, **nerve to vastus medialis** and the **saphenous nerve**.
- **Adductor canal compression syndrome:** **entrapment of the neurovascular bundle** in the canal; caused by **hypertrophy of adjacent muscles such as vastus medialis**; presents with **claudication (femoral artery occlusion)** and **neurological symptoms (saphenous nerve entrapment)**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hunter's canal: apex of the femoral triangle → adductor hiatus; middle third of the medial thigh.
- Sartorius (anteromedial), vastus medialis (lateral), adductor longus + magnus (posterior).
- Contents: femoral artery and vein, nerve to vastus medialis, saphenous nerve.

</div>

**Why:** Slide "Adductor canal".
</details>

## 3. Popliteal fossa

- **Definition:** **diamond-shaped hollow behind the knee joint**, prominent when the knee is **extended**; a **transitional area between thigh and leg** for the main vessels and nerves.
- ⭐ **Boundaries:** **superomedial = semitendinosus and semimembranosus**; **superolateral = biceps femoris**; **inferomedial = medial head of gastrocnemius**; **inferolateral = lateral head of gastrocnemius and plantaris**. (Floor and roof are blank on the slide; standard teaching, not in the slides: floor = popliteal surface of the femur, posterior knee capsule and popliteus fascia; roof = popliteal fascia and skin.)
- **Contents:** **popliteal artery and branches; popliteal vein and tributaries; tibial nerve and branches; common peroneal nerve and branches; popliteal lymph nodes; popliteal pad of fat**; plus the **termination of the posterior cutaneous nerve of the thigh** and the **genicular branch of the obturator nerve**.
- **Popliteus muscle:** origin **lateral surface of the lateral femoral condyle** (rounded tendon); insertion **posterior surface of the tibia above the soleal line**; nerve **tibial**; action **medial rotation of the tibia on the femur (unlocking the knee)**.
- **Clinical:** knowing the structures explains lumps in the fossa; **popliteal aneurysm** = dilatation of the popliteal artery presenting as a **pulsatile midline swelling**. The artery is a common site because of **(1) constant pulsation against the adductor magnus tendon at the adductor hiatus (wall weakness)** and **(2) fixation to the knee capsule by a fibrous band just above the femoral condyles (traction)**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Diamond-shaped; ST + SM (superomedial), biceps femoris (superolateral), 2 heads of gastrocnemius (+ plantaris) below.
- Contents: popliteal artery/vein, tibial and common peroneal nerves, lymph nodes, fat.
- Popliteus unlocks the knee (medial rotation of tibia); popliteal aneurysm = pulsatile midline swelling.

</div>

**Answer:** Superomedial: semitendinosus and semimembranosus; superolateral: biceps femoris; inferomedial: medial head of gastrocnemius; inferolateral: lateral head of gastrocnemius and plantaris.

**Why:** Slide "Popliteal fossa – boundaries".

**Why:** Slide "Popliteal fossa – contents".

<details><summary>Q3. Describe popliteus and explain why the popliteal artery is prone to aneurysm.</summary>

**Answer:** Popliteus arises from the lateral surface of the lateral femoral condyle, inserts above the soleal line on the posterior tibia, is supplied by the tibial nerve and medially rotates the tibia to unlock the knee. The popliteal artery pulses against the adductor magnus tendon at the hiatus (weakening its wall) and is fixed to the knee capsule by a fibrous band above the condyles (continuous traction).

**Why:** Slides "Popliteus muscle", "Clinical importance of popliteal fossa".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Femoral triangle: sartorius = LATERAL; adductor longus = MEDIAL; inguinal ligament = BASE.**
- **Adductor canal: sartorius anteromedial, vastus medialis LATERAL, adductor longus + magnus posterior.**
- **In the adductor canal the vein is POSTERIOR to the artery.**
- **Popliteal fossa upper boundaries: ST + SM medially, biceps femoris laterally; lower: gastrocnemius heads (+ plantaris laterally).**
- **Popliteus = medial rotation of the tibia (unlocking), tibial nerve.**
- **Popliteal aneurysm = pulsatile MIDLINE swelling.**
