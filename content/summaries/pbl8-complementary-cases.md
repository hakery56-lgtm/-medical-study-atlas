## 1. Case 1 — footballer with a knee injury (ACL tear)

- **Case:** 20-year-old footballer; after **high jumping and forced friction** with another player he **landed with the right leg driven anteriorly while the knee was flexed at a right angle**; **popping sound**, **could not stand**; **ice applied immediately.**
- **Exam:** **swollen, tender knee; severe pain on bending; positive anterior drawer sign; several centimeters of excess anterior mobility of the lower leg.** Imaging confirmed the diagnosis; **arthroscopic reconstruction** recommended.
- ⭐ **Injured structure: anterior cruciate ligament (ACL).**
- **Ice:** causes **vasoconstriction → less bleeding, swelling, inflammation and pain** (RICE).
- **ACL attachments:** **anterior intercondylar area of tibia → posteromedial surface of the lateral femoral condyle.**
- **PCL attachments:** **posterior intercondylar area of tibia → anterolateral part of the medial femoral condyle.**
- **Function:** **ACL prevents anterior displacement of the tibia on the femur; PCL prevents posterior displacement of the tibia** (important in a flexed, weight-bearing knee).
- **Anterior drawer test:** patient supine, **hip flexed 45°, knee flexed 90°, foot flat**; examiner sits on the foot and **pulls the upper tibia forward**; **excess forward movement = ACL rupture.**
- **Associated injuries:** **"unhappy triad" = ACL + MCL + medial meniscus** (the medial meniscus is attached to the deep fibers of the MCL).
- **Imaging:** **MRI** — shows **ligaments, menisci and cartilage in detail without radiation.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pop + swelling + positive anterior drawer = ACL tear.
- ACL: anterior tibia → lateral femoral condyle; prevents anterior tibial translation.
- Unhappy triad: ACL, MCL, medial meniscus. MRI confirms.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which structure is injured, and what is the significance of the anterior drawer test?</summary>

**Answer:** The anterior cruciate ligament; a positive anterior drawer test shows excess forward movement of the tibia on the femur, indicating ACL rupture.

**Why:** Case 1, questions 1 and 5 (standard teaching).
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the attachments and functions of the cruciate ligaments.</summary>

**Answer:** ACL: anterior intercondylar area of tibia to the posteromedial surface of the lateral femoral condyle; prevents anterior tibial displacement. PCL: posterior intercondylar area of tibia to the anterolateral part of the medial femoral condyle; prevents posterior tibial displacement.

**Why:** Case 1, questions 3 and 4 (standard teaching).
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which other injuries accompany an ACL tear and how is the diagnosis confirmed?</summary>

**Answer:** The unhappy triad (ACL, MCL and medial meniscus); MRI confirms it and shows soft tissues without radiation.

**Why:** Case 1, questions 6 and 7 (standard teaching).
</details>

## 2. Case 2 — postpartum foot drop (common peroneal nerve palsy)

- **Case:** 23-year-old woman, day after a **difficult vaginal delivery** with a **prolonged (3-hour) pushing stage in stirrups**; **numb and weak right foot, foot drop, toes drag**; **no back pain**; **decreased sensation in the right foot and leg**; **cannot dorsiflex the right foot.**
- ⭐ **Diagnosis: common peroneal (fibular) nerve palsy.**
- **Cause:** **prolonged lithotomy position/stirrups compressing the lateral knee** at the fibular head.
- **Why vulnerable:** the nerve **winds around the neck of the fibula, superficial and against bone.**
- **Muscles lost:** **anterior compartment dorsiflexors (deep peroneal nerve): tibialis anterior, extensor digitorum longus, extensor hallucis longus, peroneus tertius**; also **peroneus longus and brevis (eversion)**.
- **Sensory loss:** **lateral lower leg and dorsum of the foot** (superficial peroneal); **first web space** (deep peroneal).
- **Foot drop:** **unopposed plantar flexors (tibial nerve)** hold the foot plantar flexed and inverted.
- **Gait:** **high-stepping (steppage)** to clear the toes.
- **Prognosis/treatment:** usually **neurapraxia**; remove the cause, **ankle-foot orthosis** and physiotherapy.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Stirrups + foot drop + lateral leg numbness = common peroneal nerve.
- Fibular neck = superficial course; anterior compartment paralyzed.
- Steppage gait; inversion is preserved (differs from L5 radiculopathy).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the diagnosis and what caused it?</summary>

**Answer:** Common peroneal nerve palsy caused by prolonged pressure on the lateral knee (fibular head) from stirrups during a long labour.

**Why:** Case 2, questions 1 and 2 (standard teaching).
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is the nerve vulnerable and which muscles are paralyzed?</summary>

**Answer:** It winds superficially around the fibular neck against bone; the anterior compartment dorsiflexors (tibialis anterior, EDL, EHL, peroneus tertius) and the peroneal evertors are paralyzed.

**Why:** Case 2, questions 3 and 4 (standard teaching).
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the sensory loss, the cause of foot drop and the gait.</summary>

**Answer:** Sensory loss over the lateral lower leg and dorsum of the foot (and first web space); foot drop from paralysis of the dorsiflexors with unopposed plantar flexors; a high-stepping gait.

**Why:** Case 2, questions 5 to 7 (standard teaching).
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **ACL prevents anterior tibial translation; PCL prevents posterior translation; cruciates are named by tibial attachment.**
- **ACL arises from the ANTERIOR intercondylar area and goes to the LATERAL femoral condyle; PCL goes to the MEDIAL femoral condyle.**
- **Unhappy triad = ACL + MCL + MEDIAL meniscus.**
- **Peroneal palsy spares inversion (tibial nerve); L5 radiculopathy weakens inversion and gives back pain.**
- **Deep peroneal = anterior compartment + first web space; superficial peroneal = lateral compartment.**
- **The questions have no answers in the file: all answers are standard teaching.**

## 🔥 Final review — memorize before the exam

1. Pop + swelling + positive anterior drawer = ACL tear; ice reduces bleeding, swelling and pain.
2. ACL: anterior tibial intercondylar area → posteromedial lateral femoral condyle.
3. PCL: posterior tibial intercondylar area → anterolateral medial femoral condyle.
4. ACL stops anterior tibial slide; PCL stops posterior slide.
5. Drawer tests: knee flexed 90°, pull (anterior) or push (posterior) the tibia.
6. Unhappy triad: ACL, MCL, medial meniscus; MRI is the best confirmatory test.
7. Stirrups + foot drop = common peroneal nerve at the fibular neck.
8. Paralyzed: tibialis anterior, EDL, EHL, peroneus tertius, peroneals; steppage gait.
