## 2. Histopathology

- ⭐ **The most significant branch of pathology.**
- It focuses on:
  - **Gross examination** — examining tissues **with the naked eye** (e.g. kidney with an **abnormal white mass**; **hysterectomy for placenta accreta**; **normal stomach opened along the greater curvature**: oesophagus, antrum, pylorus, duodenum).
  - **Microscopic examination (light microscopy)** — **definitive diagnosis** (e.g. **H&E stomach**: surface mucosa, gastric pits, gastric glands, muscularis mucosae; **skin**: epidermis, dermis, adipose tissue).

### Tissue-processing equipment
- **Grossing hood, tissue cassettes, automated tissue processor, paraffin-embedded tissue block, microtome, H&E staining, H&E-stained slides.**

### Tissue-processing steps
| Step | Details |
|---|---|
| **Tissue processing** | divided into **hard tissue** and **soft tissue** |
| **Decalcification** (hard tissue only) | **5 % HNO₃** (nitric acid) or "10 % HCO₃" as written on the slide |
| ⭐ **Fixation** | **10 % formalin** (soft tissue goes directly; hard tissue after decalcification) |
| ⭐ **Dehydration** | **alcohol in increasing grades** |
| ⭐ **Clearing** | **xylene** |
| ⭐ **Infiltration** | **molten paraffin** |
| ⭐ **Embedding** | **paraffin blocks** |
| ⭐ **Sectioning** | **microtome** → **H&E staining** |

### Frozen section
- **Freeze the tissue → cut thin frozen slices (cryostat) → mount on glass slides.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Histopathology = most significant branch; gross (naked eye) + light microscopy (definitive diagnosis).
- Steps: (decalcification for hard tissue) → fixation (10 % formalin) → dehydration (alcohol) → clearing (xylene) → infiltration (molten paraffin) → embedding → sectioning (microtome) → H&E.
- Frozen section: freeze, thin slices with a cryostat, mount on slides.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the steps of tissue processing in histopathology.</summary>

**Answer:** Hard tissue is decalcified first; then fixation in 10 % formalin, dehydration in increasing grades of alcohol, clearing in xylene, infiltration with molten paraffin, embedding in paraffin blocks, sectioning with a microtome and H&E staining.

**Why:** Flowchart in the lab.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate gross from microscopic examination.</summary>

**Answer:** Gross examination is examination of tissues by the naked eye (e.g. a white kidney mass, placenta accreta hysterectomy specimen); microscopic examination uses the light microscope on H&E-stained sections for definitive diagnosis.

**Why:** Two approaches in histopathology.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is a frozen section?</summary>

**Answer:** A rapid technique in which the tissue is frozen, thin frozen slices are cut (with a cryostat) and mounted on glass slides.

**Why:** Alternative to paraffin processing.
</details>

## 3. Cytopathology

- ⭐ **Use of specialist techniques to examine individual cells extracted from tissues to determine the cause and nature of disease.**
- **Exfoliative cytopathology:** **spontaneous cell shedding** — cells from **urine and sputum** (and **cervical smear**: endocervical and squamous cells).
- **Fine needle aspiration (FNA):** cells extracted with **negative-pressure aspiration** — e.g. **thyroid and breast**.

### FNA cytology (FNAC)
- Uses a **narrow-gauge needle (22G–25G)** to collect a sample from a lesion.
- **Minimally invasive, rapid diagnosis.**
- ⚠️ **Does NOT preserve the histological architecture.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cytopathology = individual cells: exfoliative (urine, sputum, cervical smear) vs FNA (thyroid, breast).
- FNAC: 22–25G needle, minimally invasive, rapid; no tissue architecture.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define cytopathology and name its two main types.</summary>

**Answer:** The use of specialist diagnostic techniques to examine individual cells extracted from tissues to determine the cause and nature of disease; types: exfoliative cytology (spontaneously shed cells in urine and sputum) and fine needle aspiration (cells aspirated with negative pressure from thyroid or breast).

**Why:** Lab slide.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the advantages and limitations of FNAC?</summary>

**Answer:** It uses a narrow-gauge (22–25G) needle, is minimally invasive and gives a rapid diagnosis, but it does not preserve the histological architecture of the tissue.

**Why:** Compare with histopathology.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare histopathology and cytopathology.</summary>

**Answer:** Histopathology examines whole tissue sections (preserved architecture, definitive diagnosis, but needs processing); cytopathology examines individual cells (FNA or exfoliated), is faster and less invasive but lacks tissue architecture.

**Why:** Lab objective 3.
</details>
