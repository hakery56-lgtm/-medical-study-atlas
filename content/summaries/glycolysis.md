# Glycolysis — Exam Night Review

Dr. Weaam Awad's biochemistry lecture: **the definition of glycolysis, the 10 reactions with their enzymes and coenzymes, hexokinase vs glucokinase, regulation by substrate, products and hormones, the energetics (ATP yield aerobic and anaerobic), substrate-level phosphorylation, and the fate of pyruvate (acetyl-CoA vs lactate).** After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer).

---

## 1. Definition & significance

- **Glycolysis** = the sequence of reactions that converts **glucose into pyruvate (in the presence of oxygen — aerobic)** or **into lactate (in the absence of oxygen — anaerobic)** with the production of **ATP**.
- Major pathway of glucose utilization; occurs in the ⭐ **cytosol of all cells**.

### Significance
1. The **only pathway that takes place in all the cells** (cytoplasm).
2. ⭐ The **only source of energy in erythrocytes**.
3. In **strenuous exercise**, **anaerobic glycolysis** is the major energy source of muscle.
4. Provides **carbon skeletons for non-essential amino acids** and the **glycerol part of fat** (glycerol from glucose via **DHAP**).
5. **Most reactions are reversible** and are also used for **gluconeogenesis**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Glycolysis: glucose → pyruvate (aerobic) or lactate (anaerobic) + ATP; cytosolic, in all cells.
- Only energy source of RBCs; main energy source of muscle in strenuous exercise; supplies carbon skeletons (non-essential amino acids, glycerol of fat).
- Most steps are reversible and shared with gluconeogenesis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define glycolysis and distinguish aerobic from anaerobic glycolysis.</summary>

**Answer:** Glycolysis is the sequence of cytosolic reactions converting glucose into pyruvate in the presence of oxygen (aerobic) or into lactate in its absence (anaerobic), with the production of ATP.

**Why:** Definition + the end product depends on O₂.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Mention the significance of glycolysis.</summary>

**Answer:** It is the only pathway occurring in all cells; the only energy source of erythrocytes; the major source of energy for muscle in strenuous exercise (anaerobic); it provides carbon skeletons for non-essential amino acids and the glycerol of fat (via DHAP); and most of its reactions are reversible and used for gluconeogenesis.

**Why:** Five points.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Where does glycolysis occur and why is it the only energy source in erythrocytes?</summary>

**Answer:** In the cytosol; erythrocytes have no mitochondria, so they cannot oxidize pyruvate and depend completely on glycolysis (ending in lactate even under aerobic conditions) for ATP.

**Why:** No mitochondria = no oxidative phosphorylation.
</details>

## 2. Phase 1 — the energy-requiring (preparative) phase

| # | Reaction | Enzyme | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Glucose → glucose-6-phosphate** | ⭐ **hexokinase or glucokinase** | uses **ATP**; **irreversible** (first priming reaction) |
| 2 | **Glucose-6-P → fructose-6-P** | **phosphohexose isomerase** | isomerization; **reversible** |
| 3 | **Fructose-6-P → fructose-1,6-bisphosphate** | ⭐ **phosphofructokinase-I (PFK-I)** | **second ATP use**; **irreversible**; **the key regulatory enzyme** |
| 4 | **Fructose-1,6-bisP → glyceraldehyde-3-P + DHAP** | **aldolase** | cleavage of the 6-carbon sugar into two 3-carbon compounds |
| 5 | **DHAP → glyceraldehyde-3-P** | **phosphotriose (triose phosphate) isomerase** | gives **2 glyceraldehyde-3-P per glucose** |

- **ATP consumed in this phase = 2.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Steps 1–5 (preparative phase) use 2 ATP: hexokinase/glucokinase (irreversible), phosphohexose isomerase, PFK-I (irreversible), aldolase, triose phosphate isomerase.
- One glucose → 2 glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the first phase of glycolysis.</summary>

**Answer:** Glucose is phosphorylated to glucose-6-phosphate by hexokinase or glucokinase using ATP (irreversible); phosphohexose isomerase converts it to fructose-6-phosphate (reversible); PFK-I phosphorylates it to fructose-1,6-bisphosphate using a second ATP (irreversible); aldolase cleaves this into glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate and DHAP; triose phosphate isomerase converts DHAP into glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate, so two molecules are formed per glucose.

**Why:** Five reactions, two ATP.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the first step of glycolysis and why is it important?</summary>

**Answer:** Phosphorylation of glucose to glucose-6-phosphate by hexokinase/glucokinase using ATP; it traps glucose inside the cell (the phosphorylated sugar cannot cross the membrane) and commits it to metabolism, and the reaction is irreversible.

**Why:** Lecture review question 1.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which steps consume ATP and which enzymes are irreversible?</summary>

**Answer:** The first ATP-consuming step is the hexokinase/glucokinase reaction and the second is the PFK-I reaction; these two plus pyruvate kinase are the irreversible, regulated steps.

**Why:** Review question 2 and the regulation topic.
</details>

## 3. Phase 2 — the energy-generating (payoff) phase

| # | Reaction | Enzyme | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | **Glyceraldehyde-3-P → 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate** | ⭐ **glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase** | **NAD⁺-dependent oxidation** (+ Pi); **NADH** formed; reversible |
| 7 | **1,3-BPG → 3-phosphoglycerate** | ⭐ **phosphoglycerate kinase** | **first ATP-forming step — substrate-level phosphorylation**: **2 ATP per glucose** |
| 8 | **3-PG → 2-phosphoglycerate** | **phosphoglycerate mutase** | reversible |
| 9 | **2-PG → phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP)** | **enolase** | dehydration (−H₂O) |
| 10 | **PEP → pyruvate** | ⭐ **pyruvate kinase** | **irreversible**; **second substrate-level phosphorylation**: **2 ATP per glucose** |

### Aerobic vs anaerobic fate of NADH and pyruvate
- **Aerobic:** pyruvate enters the **mitochondria** → **acetyl-CoA** → **citric acid cycle → CO₂ + H₂O**.
- **Anaerobic:** NADH cannot be reoxidized by the respiratory chain → it is **reoxidized by converting pyruvate to lactate (lactate dehydrogenase)**.
- Tissues working under **hypoxia produce lactate**: **skeletal muscle, smooth muscle, erythrocytes**.
- In **erythrocytes glycolysis ends in lactate even under aerobic conditions** (**no mitochondria**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- GAPDH (NAD⁺ → NADH) → phosphoglycerate kinase (ATP) → mutase → enolase → pyruvate kinase (ATP, irreversible).
- Two substrate-level phosphorylations (steps 7 and 10), 2 ATP each per glucose.
- Anaerobic: pyruvate + NADH → lactate (LDH) regenerates NAD⁺; erythrocytes always form lactate.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the second phase of glycolysis.</summary>

**Answer:** Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate is oxidized to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate by glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase using NAD⁺; phosphoglycerate kinase converts 1,3-BPG to 3-phosphoglycerate generating ATP (substrate-level phosphorylation); phosphoglycerate mutase gives 2-phosphoglycerate; enolase forms phosphoenolpyruvate; and pyruvate kinase converts PEP to pyruvate (irreversible), generating a second ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation.

**Why:** Five reactions, two ATP-generating steps.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is substrate-level phosphorylation? Give examples from glycolysis.</summary>

**Answer:** Formation of ATP by direct transfer of a phosphate group from a high-energy substrate to ADP, without the respiratory chain. In glycolysis: the phosphoglycerate kinase reaction (1,3-BPG → 3-PG) and the pyruvate kinase reaction (PEP → pyruvate), each yielding 2 ATP per glucose.

**Why:** Definition + two examples (lecture objective 6).
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is lactate formed in anaerobic glycolysis and in erythrocytes?</summary>

**Answer:** Under anaerobic conditions the respiratory chain cannot reoxidize NADH, so pyruvate is reduced to lactate by lactate dehydrogenase, regenerating NAD⁺ so that glycolysis can continue. Erythrocytes have no mitochondria, so lactate is the end product even in the presence of oxygen.

**Why:** NAD⁺ regeneration.
</details>

## 4. Hexokinase vs glucokinase

| | **Hexokinase** | **Glucokinase** |
|---|---|---|
| **Site** | **extrahepatic tissues** | **liver** |
| **Affinity for glucose** | **high (low Km)** | **low (high Km)** |
| **Product inhibition** | **inhibited by glucose-6-phosphate** | **not inhibited** |
| **Function** | ensures **glucose supply to the tissues regardless of blood glucose** | **removes glucose from the blood after a meal** (↑ blood glucose) |
| **Substrates** | also other hexoses (**fructose, galactose**…) | **specific for glucose** |
| **Insulin** | **not affected by insulin** | **inducible — synthesis increased by insulin** (decreased by glucagon) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hexokinase: extrahepatic, low Km, inhibited by G-6-P, other hexoses, insulin-independent.
- Glucokinase: liver, high Km, not inhibited by G-6-P, glucose only, induced by insulin; clears glucose after meals.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Differentiate between hexokinase and glucokinase.</summary>

**Answer:** Hexokinase is found in extrahepatic tissues, has a high affinity (low Km) for glucose, is inhibited by glucose-6-phosphate, ensures glucose supply regardless of blood glucose, phosphorylates other hexoses and is not affected by insulin. Glucokinase is found in the liver, has a low affinity (high Km), is not inhibited by glucose-6-phosphate, removes glucose from the blood after a meal, is specific for glucose and is an inducible enzyme increased by insulin.

**Why:** Six-row table.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why does the liver rely on glucokinase after a meal?</summary>

**Answer:** Because glucokinase has a high Km and is not inhibited by its product, it acts only when blood glucose is high and removes the excess glucose from the portal blood for storage as glycogen or metabolism.

**Why:** Km explains function.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do insulin and glucagon affect glucokinase?</summary>

**Answer:** Insulin induces (increases the synthesis of) liver glucokinase, while glucagon decreases it.

**Why:** Hormonal regulation.
</details>

## 5. Regulation of glycolysis

Regulated at the **3 irreversible steps**: **(1) hexokinase/glucokinase, (2) PFK-I, (3) pyruvate kinase**.

| Enzyme | Activated by | Inhibited by |
|---|---|---|
| **Hexokinase** | — | **glucose-6-phosphate** (allosteric, product inhibition) |
| **Glucokinase** | **insulin** (induces synthesis) | **glucagon** |
| ⭐ **PFK-I** (key enzyme) | **insulin, AMP** (low energy), **fructose-6-P**, **fructose-2,6-bisphosphate** (via PFK-2) | **glucagon, ATP, citrate, low pH, cyclic AMP** |
| **Pyruvate kinase** | **insulin, fructose-1,6-bisphosphate** | **glucagon, ATP, cyclic AMP** |
| **Pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH)** | **CoA, NAD⁺** | **acetyl-CoA, NADH** |

- **PFK-I** is an **inducible enzyme** (↑ with insulin, ↓ with glucagon) and an **allosteric** enzyme; **pyruvate kinase** also ↑ with insulin and ↓ with glucagon.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Three irreversible regulated steps: hexokinase/glucokinase, PFK-I, pyruvate kinase.
- Insulin induces glucokinase, PFK-I and pyruvate kinase; glucagon decreases them.
- PFK-I: activated by AMP, F-6-P, F-2,6-BP; inhibited by ATP, citrate, low pH, cAMP.
- Pyruvate kinase: activated by F-1,6-BP; inhibited by ATP and cAMP.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the regulation of glycolysis.</summary>

**Answer:** Glycolysis is regulated at its three irreversible steps. Hexokinase is allosterically inhibited by glucose-6-phosphate, while liver glucokinase is induced by insulin and decreased by glucagon. PFK-I is induced by insulin and decreased by glucagon; it is activated by fructose-6-phosphate, AMP and fructose-2,6-bisphosphate and inhibited by ATP, citrate, low pH and cAMP. Pyruvate kinase is increased by insulin and decreased by glucagon, activated by fructose-1,6-bisphosphate and inhibited by ATP and cAMP.

**Why:** Three enzymes × activators × inhibitors.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is AMP an activator and ATP an inhibitor of PFK-I?</summary>

**Answer:** AMP signals a low energy state, so glycolysis should be stimulated, whereas a high ATP level signals sufficient energy and inhibits PFK-I.

**Why:** Energy charge control.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the effect of insulin and glucagon on glycolysis?</summary>

**Answer:** Insulin increases the synthesis of glucokinase, PFK-I and pyruvate kinase (stimulating glycolysis); glucagon decreases them (and raises cAMP, which inhibits PFK-I and pyruvate kinase).

**Why:** Fed vs fasting state.
</details>

## 6. Energetics — ATP yield

### Anaerobic glycolysis (net 2 ATP)
| Step | Enzyme | ATP |
|---|---|---|
| Hexokinase | uses ATP | **−1** |
| PFK-I | uses ATP | **−1** |
| 1,3-BPG kinase (phosphoglycerate kinase) | substrate-level | **+2** (1 × 2) |
| Pyruvate kinase | substrate-level | **+2** (1 × 2) |
| **Total** | | **4 − 2 = 2 ATP** |

### Aerobic glycolysis (net 8 ATP in the lecture's count)
| Step | Source | ATP |
|---|---|---|
| Hexokinase | | **−1** |
| PFK-I | | **−1** |
| GAPDH | **2 NADH → respiratory chain (3 ATP each in the lecture)** | **+6** |
| Phosphoglycerate kinase | | **+2** |
| Pyruvate kinase | | **+2** |
| **Total** | | **10 − 2 = 8 ATP** |

(Lecture note: 3 ATP per NADH is the older convention; current texts use about 2.5.)

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Anaerobic: net 2 ATP (4 produced − 2 used); aerobic: net 8 ATP in the lecture (adds 6 ATP from 2 NADH).
- ATP used: hexokinase and PFK-I; ATP made by substrate-level phosphorylation: phosphoglycerate kinase and pyruvate kinase.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Calculate the ATP yield of anaerobic and aerobic glycolysis.</summary>

**Answer:** Anaerobic: 2 ATP are used (hexokinase, PFK-I) and 4 are produced by substrate-level phosphorylation (2 at phosphoglycerate kinase, 2 at pyruvate kinase) → net 2 ATP. Aerobic: the same net 2 ATP plus 6 ATP from the 2 NADH formed at GAPDH (3 ATP each) that are reoxidized by the respiratory chain → 8 ATP.

**Why:** Two tables, same logic.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why does anaerobic glycolysis yield less ATP?</summary>

**Answer:** Because NADH is reoxidized by converting pyruvate to lactate instead of by the respiratory chain, so no ATP is obtained from oxidative phosphorylation.

**Why:** NADH is not used for ATP.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. In which steps is ATP consumed and in which is it produced?</summary>

**Answer:** Consumed at the hexokinase/glucokinase and PFK-I steps; produced at the phosphoglycerate kinase and pyruvate kinase steps (substrate-level phosphorylation).

**Why:** 2 used, 4 made.
</details>

## 7. Fate of pyruvate

### Aerobic — oxidative decarboxylation to acetyl-CoA
- Pyruvate is made in the **cytoplasm**, transported into **mitochondria** by a **pyruvate transporter** and converted by **pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH)** to **acetyl-CoA** (the "active acetate") in tissues with **high oxidative capacity**.
- **Irreversible**; acetyl-CoA is the main fuel of the **TCA cycle**.
- PDH is a **multi-enzyme complex with 5 coenzymes**: ⭐ **TPP (thiamine pyrophosphate), lipoic acid, CoA-SH, FAD, NAD⁺**.
- Reaction: **pyruvate + NAD⁺ + CoA-SH → acetyl-CoA + CO₂ + NADH + H⁺**.
- Overall aerobic: **glucose → 2 pyruvate → 2 acetyl-CoA (−2 CO₂) → citric acid cycle → 4 CO₂ + 4 H₂O**.

### Anaerobic — reduction to lactate
- **2 pyruvate → 2 lactate** by **lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)** (NADH + H⁺ → NAD⁺).
- Occurs in **vigorously contracting muscle, erythrocytes, some other cells and some microorganisms**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Aerobic: pyruvate → mitochondria → PDH (TPP, lipoate, CoA, FAD, NAD⁺) → acetyl-CoA + CO₂ + NADH (irreversible).
- Anaerobic: pyruvate → lactate by LDH, regenerating NAD⁺.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the fate of pyruvate under aerobic and anaerobic conditions.</summary>

**Answer:** With oxygen, pyruvate enters the mitochondria and is oxidatively decarboxylated by the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex to acetyl-CoA, which enters the citric acid cycle (glucose finally gives CO₂ and H₂O). Without oxygen, pyruvate is reduced to lactate by lactate dehydrogenase, regenerating the NAD⁺ needed for glycolysis.

**Why:** Two fates.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is pyruvate dehydrogenase and what are its coenzymes?</summary>

**Answer:** A multi-enzyme complex that irreversibly converts pyruvate into acetyl-CoA, CO₂ and NADH inside mitochondria; it needs five coenzymes: thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP), lipoic acid, coenzyme A, FAD and NAD⁺.

**Why:** Five coenzymes (the vitamin-derived ones).
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. In which tissues is lactate produced?</summary>

**Answer:** In hypoxic or anaerobic tissues such as vigorously contracting skeletal muscle, smooth muscle and erythrocytes (in which glycolysis always ends in lactate).

**Why:** Link to exercise and RBCs.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Irreversible/regulated steps:** hexokinase/glucokinase, **PFK-I**, **pyruvate kinase**.
- **ATP is used at hexokinase and PFK-I** (2 ATP); **made at phosphoglycerate kinase and pyruvate kinase** (4 ATP) → **net 2 (anaerobic)**.
- **GAPDH** is the **only NAD⁺-dependent step** (not an ATP-producing step).
- **Hexokinase = low Km, inhibited by G-6-P; glucokinase = high Km, induced by insulin.**
- **Erythrocytes:** glycolysis = only energy source, always ends in **lactate**.
- **Glucagon inhibits** PFK-I and pyruvate kinase (cAMP); **insulin induces** them.
- **PFK-I activators:** AMP, F-6-P, **F-2,6-BP**; **inhibitors:** ATP, citrate, low pH, cAMP.
- **PDH coenzymes:** TPP, lipoate, CoA, FAD, NAD⁺; reaction is **irreversible**.

## 🔥 Final review — memorize before the exam

1. **Glycolysis** = glucose → pyruvate (aerobic) / lactate (anaerobic) + ATP; **cytosol of all cells**.
2. **Phase 1 (2 ATP used):** hexokinase/glucokinase → isomerase → **PFK-I** → aldolase → triose phosphate isomerase.
3. **Phase 2:** **GAPDH (NAD⁺ → NADH)** → **phosphoglycerate kinase (ATP)** → mutase → enolase → **pyruvate kinase (ATP)**.
4. **Substrate-level phosphorylation** = phosphoglycerate kinase + pyruvate kinase.
5. **Hexokinase vs glucokinase** (site, Km, G-6-P inhibition, substrates, insulin).
6. **Regulation:** insulin ↑, glucagon ↓; PFK-I key (AMP, F-2,6-BP ↑; ATP, citrate ↓).
7. **ATP:** anaerobic **net 2**; aerobic **8** (lecture count).
8. **Pyruvate:** aerobic → acetyl-CoA by **PDH (5 coenzymes)**; anaerobic → **lactate (LDH)**.
