## 1. Mini PBL 1 — Rana, 12 years (osteoid osteoma)

- **Case:** sudden **severe left knee pain** that **awakens her from sleep** over **6 weeks**; **aspirin relieves** each episode; **normal examination**.
- **Diagnosis:** **osteoid osteoma** — benign bone-forming tumor of children and young adults (male > female).
- **Pain:** **severe, worse at night, relieved by aspirin/NSAIDs** because the nidus produces **prostaglandins (PGE₂)**.
- **Next step:** **plain radiograph then CT** (best for the nidus).
- **Radiology:** **small radiolucent nidus (< ~1.5–2 cm) surrounded by dense reactive sclerosis.**
- **Gross:** small **round-oval red-brown gritty nidus**. **Micro:** **interlacing woven-bone trabeculae rimmed by osteoblasts in a vascular fibrous stroma.**
- **Size matters:** **< 2 cm = osteoid osteoma; larger = osteoblastoma.**
- **Treatment:** **complete removal of the nidus** (excision or CT-guided radiofrequency ablation); NSAIDs for symptoms. **Prognosis excellent, no malignant change.**

**Key points**
- Night pain relieved by aspirin = osteoid osteoma.
- Nidus < 2 cm with sclerosis.

</div>

## 2. Mini PBL 2 — Samer, 13 years (Ewing sarcoma)

- **Case:** previously healthy boy, **right leg pain for 1 month**, **less active**, **no trauma or recent illness**; **tenderness over the anterior lower right thigh**; **right thigh slightly larger**.
- **Two commonest malignant bone tumors of children:** **osteosarcoma and Ewing sarcoma.**
- **Most likely diagnosis:** **Ewing sarcoma.**
- **Radiology:** **lytic destructive diaphyseal/metadiaphyseal lesion, layered "onion-skin" periosteal reaction, soft-tissue mass.**
- **Next step:** **plain radiograph → MRI → biopsy.**
- **Diagnostic lab:** **biopsy histology (small round blue cells, glycogen-rich/PAS+) + CD99 immunostain + EWSR1 translocation (FISH/RT-PCR).**
- **Karyotype:** **t(11;22)(q24;q12) → EWS-FLI1.**
- **Mimics osteomyelitis:** **pain, swelling, fever, ↑ ESR, leukocytosis, onion-skin reaction.**
- **Treatment:** **multi-agent chemotherapy + surgery and/or radiotherapy.** **Prognosis:** ~70% 5-year survival if localized; **worse with metastases.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Child + painful thigh mass + onion-skin = Ewing sarcoma.
- t(11;22), CD99, small round blue cells.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name the two commonest malignant bone tumors in children.</summary>

**Answer:** Osteosarcoma and Ewing sarcoma.

**Why:** Mini PBL 2, question 2.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the characteristic genetic change and histology of Ewing sarcoma?</summary>

**Answer:** t(11;22) producing the EWS-FLI1 fusion; sheets of small round blue cells that are CD99 positive.

**Why:** Standard teaching.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why can Ewing sarcoma mimic osteomyelitis and how is it treated?</summary>

**Answer:** It causes pain, swelling, fever, raised ESR and leukocytosis with periosteal reaction; treatment is multi-agent chemotherapy with surgery or radiotherapy.

**Why:** Standard teaching.
</details>

## 3. Comparison

| Feature | **Osteoid osteoma** | **Osteosarcoma** | **Ewing sarcoma** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | **Benign** | **Malignant** | **Malignant** |
| **Pain** | **Night, relieved by aspirin** | **Progressive, night** | **Painful swelling, fever possible** |
| **Radiology** | **Radiolucent nidus + sclerosis** | **Sunburst, Codman's triangle** | **Onion-skin, lytic diaphysis** |
| **Histology** | **Woven bone + osteoblasts** | **Malignant osteoid** | **Small round blue cells** |
| **Genetics** | **—** | **Complex** | **t(11;22) EWS-FLI1** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sunburst = osteosarcoma; onion-skin = Ewing.
- Aspirin-responsive night pain = osteoid osteoma.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Differentiate osteosarcoma from Ewing sarcoma radiologically.</summary>

**Answer:** Osteosarcoma shows sunburst and Codman's triangle; Ewing shows onion-skin periosteal reaction in a lytic diaphyseal lesion.

**Why:** Standard teaching.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which tumor produces osteoid?</summary>

**Answer:** Osteosarcoma (malignant) and osteoid osteoma (benign).

**Why:** Standard teaching.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which of the three is benign?</summary>

**Answer:** Osteoid osteoma.

**Why:** Standard teaching.
</details>
