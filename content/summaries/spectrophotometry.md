## 1. Spectroscopy, light and color

- ⭐ **Spectroscopy** = the study of the **interaction of light and matter**.
- **Spectrophotometer** = an instrument using **UV, visible or IR** radiation to analyze the **absorption or transmission** of a sample. The lab uses **visible** light.
- **Colorimetry:** many compounds have **characteristic colors**, and the **intensity of the color is proportional to the concentration**.
- Light can be **transmitted or absorbed** by dissolved substances; the **presence and concentration** of a substance is found by passing light through the sample.
- **Spectroscopes** measure electromagnetic **emission**; **spectrophotometers** measure electromagnetic **absorption**.

## 2. The instrument and the absorption of light

- **Two most common instruments:** the **visible spectrophotometer** (e.g. Spect 20, Spect 88; **Xe or W lamps**; **glass cuvettes**) and the **atomic-absorption spectrophotometer**.
- The visible spectrophotometer measures the **amount of light absorbed** by the dissolved substance: **qualitative** (color gives information on composition) and **quantitative** (numerical data for concentration).
- **White light** = all colors (**polychromatic**); **monochromatic light** = one color. A **green solution absorbs red light**.
- **Monochromator** = spreads light into its component **wavelengths**.
- **Parts:** **source → entrance slit → dispersion device → exit slit (monochromator) → sample → detector.**
- The **wavelength absorbed** relates to the **electronic structure** of the substance; the **intensity absorbed** depends on the **concentration**; when light is absorbed the **radiant power (P)** of the beam **decreases**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Visible spectrophotometer: Xe or W lamp, glass cuvette; gives qualitative and quantitative data.
- Monochromator splits white (polychromatic) light into single wavelengths.
- More concentrated solution = more intense color = more light absorbed.

</div>

## 3. Transmittance, absorbance and Beer's law

- ⭐ **Transmittance (T)** = fraction of incident light that passes through the sample: **T = P/Po** (or **I/Io**); **Po** = intensity of the **blank** (a solution identical to the sample but **without solute**). **%T = T × 100.**
- **%T and % absorption are not linear with concentration**, so a straight-line graph uses **absorbance**: **A = log(1/T) = −log T.**
- Light absorbed depends on **concentration (c)** and **path length (b)** → **Beer's law.**
- **Beer's law:** the intensity of monochromatic light decreases **exponentially** as the concentration of the absorbing medium increases; **more dissolved substance = more absorption and less transmittance.**
- **Equation:** **A = ε b c**, where **ε = molar absorptivity coefficient** (constant for a substance).
- **Standardization graph:** **standards of known concentration** are prepared and their **absorbances and concentrations** plotted (example: absorbance vs concentration % of **CoCl₂**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- T = P/Po; %T = T × 100; A = −log T.
- Beer's law: A = ε b c (ε constant for a substance, b = path length, c = concentration).
- Standard curve: absorbance of known standards vs concentration gives a straight line.

</div>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Spectroscope = emission; spectrophotometer = absorption.**
- **Use absorbance, not % transmission, for the straight-line graph** (A = −log T).
- **Po is the intensity of the BLANK**, not of the sample.
- **Absorbance rises with concentration, transmittance falls.**
- **Visible range is about 380–780 nm**; UV < 380 nm, near IR > 780 nm.
- **ε is constant for a substance**; b is the path length.
