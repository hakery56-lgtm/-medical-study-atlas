## 1. Introduction and the ABO system

- ⭐ **Blood group must be known** for **blood transfusion, paternity dispute, medico-legal problems and organ transplantation.**
- The current system was discovered by **Landsteiner in 1900** (**Landsteiner's ABO system**).
- **RBC antigens = agglutinogens; plasma antibodies = agglutinins.** To group a person, RBCs are reacted with **sera of known agglutinins** and the slide is read for **agglutination and hemolysis** (naked eye or, ideally, under the **microscope**).

| Group | RBC antigens | Plasma antibodies |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **A** | **Anti-B** |
| **B** | **B** | **Anti-A** |
| **AB** | **A and B** | **None** |
| **O** | **None** | **Anti-A and anti-B** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Needed for transfusion, paternity, medico-legal cases and transplantation.
- Antigens (agglutinogens) on RBCs; antibodies (agglutinins) in plasma.
- A: A + anti-B; B: B + anti-A; AB: A and B + none; O: none + anti-A and anti-B.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. When is it essential to know a person's blood group?</summary>

**Answer:** In blood transfusion, paternity disputes, medico-legal problems and organ transplantation.

**Why:** Slide "Introduction".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. State the principle of blood grouping.</summary>

**Answer:** RBC agglutinogens are reacted with sera containing known agglutinins, and the presence or absence of agglutination and hemolysis shows the group.

**Why:** Slide "Principle".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give the antigens and antibodies of groups A, B, AB and O.</summary>

**Why:** Figure "ABO blood group system".

## 2. The Rh factor and Rh incompatibility

- ⭐ **Rh factor (D antigen)** named after the **Rhesus monkey**; present in **> 85%** (**Rh positive**); **Rh negative ≈ 15%** lack it but their plasma **normally has no anti-Rh antibody.** In **Iraq: 92% positive, 8% negative.**
- ⭐ **Rh incompatibility:** an **Rh-negative mother** with an **Rh-positive fetus** (factor from the **father**). **Fetal red cells** entering her circulation during pregnancy and childbirth make her form **anti-Rh antibodies.** In a **later Rh-positive pregnancy** the antibodies cross to the fetus and **destroy its RBCs** = **hemolytic disease of the newborn (erythroblastosis fetalis).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Rh = D antigen; > 85% positive; Iraq 92%.
- Rh-negative mother + Rh-positive fetus → sensitization.
- Later Rh-positive pregnancy → hemolytic disease of the newborn.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the Rh factor and how common is it?</summary>

**Answer:** The D red cell antigen, named after the Rhesus monkey; present in more than 85% of people (about 92% in Iraq); Rh-negative people (about 15%) lack it.

**Why:** Slide "The Rh factor".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain how Rh incompatibility harms a later fetus.</summary>

**Answer:** Fetal Rh-positive red cells sensitize an Rh-negative mother who forms anti-Rh antibodies; in a later Rh-positive pregnancy these cross the placenta and destroy fetal red cells.

**Why:** Slides "The Rh factor" and "Rh incompatibility in pregnancy".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Name the disease that results.</summary>

**Answer:** Hemolytic disease of the newborn (erythroblastosis fetalis).

**Why:** Slide "The Rh factor".
</details>

## 3. Procedure and results

- **Equipment:** **sterile lancet, cotton swab, alcohol, glass dropper, toothpicks, microscope, porcelain tile, ABO kit (anti-A, anti-B, anti-D sera).**
- **Steps:** **1.** divide a slide into **A, B and D**; **2.** prick a finger and put **one drop of blood in each**; **3.** add **anti-A, anti-B, anti-D** respectively; **4.** **mix** each with a glass rod or match stick; **5.** read after **2-3 minutes** for **agglutination** (**dark red clumps**).

| Group | Anti-A | Anti-B | Anti-D |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A+** | + | – | + |
| **A–** | + | – | – |
| **B+** | – | + | + |
| **B–** | – | + | – |
| **AB+** | + | + | + |
| **AB–** | + | + | – |
| **O+** | – | – | + |
| **O–** | – | – | – |

(+) = agglutination; (–) = no agglutination.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Three drops of blood: anti-A, anti-B, anti-D; read after 2-3 minutes.
- Agglutination with anti-D = Rh positive.
- AB+ reacts with all three; O– with none.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Outline the procedure for ABO and Rh grouping.</summary>

**Answer:** Divide a slide into A, B and D, place a drop of blood from a finger prick in each, add anti-A, anti-B and anti-D, mix and read for agglutination after 2-3 minutes.

**Why:** Slide "Procedure".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Interpret a sample that agglutinates with anti-B and anti-D only.</summary>

**Answer:** Group B Rh positive (B+).

**Why:** Results table.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which group shows no agglutination with any serum?</summary>

**Answer:** O negative.

**Why:** Results table.
</details>

## 4. Safe transfusions and mismatch

| Group | Can donate to | Can receive from |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **A, AB** | **A, O** |
| **B** | **B, AB** | **B, O** |
| **AB** | **AB** | **A, B, AB, O** |
| **O** | **A, B, AB, O** | **O** |

- ⭐ **Mismatched transfusion:** **agglutination → hemolysis → jaundice, cardiac shock and renal shutdown.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- O gives to all; AB receives from all.
- Mismatch → agglutination → hemolysis → jaundice, shock, renal shutdown.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. From whom can group B receive and to whom can it give?</summary>

**Answer:** It receives from B and O and gives to B and AB.

**Why:** Safe transfusion table.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which groups are the universal donor and recipient?</summary>

**Answer:** O donates to all groups; AB receives from all groups.

**Why:** Safe transfusion table.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the complications of mismatched transfusion?</summary>

**Answer:** Agglutination and hemolysis, causing jaundice, cardiac shock and renal shutdown.

**Why:** Slide "Complications of mismatched blood transfusion".
</details>
