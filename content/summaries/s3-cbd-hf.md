## 3. The mechanism map

| Step | Event |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | **Stopped diuretic + salt load** |
| **Pump problem** | **HFrEF → ↓ cardiac output** |
| **Compensation** | **RAAS + sympathetic (SNS) activation** |
| **Retention** | **Na⁺ + water retention** |
| **Pressure** | **↑ circulating volume + ↑ filling pressures** |
| **Congestion** | **Pulmonary edema + peripheral edema** |

- ⭐ **Key idea:** **compensation that initially supports perfusion becomes part of the congestion problem**.

## 4. Acute management algorithm

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| **Always** | **Assess ABCs; oxygen if hypoxic** |
| **Adequate BP** | **IV loop diuretic ± IV vasodilator** |
| **Severe respiratory distress** | **Consider non-invasive ventilation (NIV)** |
| **Hypotension / shock** | **Inotropic support; consider mechanical support** |
| **Always** | **Treat the precipitating cause**; **monitor weight, urine output and renal function** |

- **Think:** the patient is **hypoxic with pulmonary edema** → immediate priority? **BP 100/62** → which treatments fit? **When does NIV enter?** **What changes if hypotensive with cardiogenic shock?**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- ABCs + oxygen → diuretic ± vasodilator (BP adequate) → NIV if severe distress → inotropes if shock.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Outline the acute management algorithm.</summary>

**Answer:** Assess ABCs and give oxygen if hypoxic; with adequate BP give an IV loop diuretic with or without an IV vasodilator; consider NIV for severe respiratory distress; use inotropic (or mechanical) support in hypotension/shock; treat the precipitating cause; monitor weight, urine output and renal function.

**Why:** Slide "Reveal: acute management algorithm".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. When is NIV considered?</summary>

**Answer:** In severe respiratory distress.

**Why:** Slide "Reveal: acute management algorithm".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What changes in cardiogenic shock?</summary>

**Answer:** With hypotension or shock inotropic support is used (consider mechanical support) rather than vasodilators.

**Why:** Slide "Reveal: acute management algorithm".
</details>

## 5. Choosing the drug by the problem

| Drug | Pharmacodynamics | PK point |
|---|---|---|
| **Furosemide** | **Loop diuretic; blocks Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter** (natriuresis/diuresis, ↓ congestion) | IV effect; renal handling |
| **Metolazone** | **Thiazide-like; distal NaCl blockade** | **Oral; longer effect than loop diuresis** |
| **Nitroglycerin** | **Venodilation; ↓ preload/congestion** (use when BP adequate) | **Rapid onset; short acting** |
| **Nitroprusside** | **Balanced vasodilation; ↓ preload + afterload** | **Very rapid, titratable** |
| **Dobutamine** | **β₁ inotropy → ↑ contractility** (low output/shock) | **Short acting; continuous IV** |
| **Milrinone** | **PDE-3 inhibition → inotropy + vasodilation** | **Renal elimination; longer than dobutamine** |
| **Norepinephrine** | **α₁ vasoconstriction with some β₁ effect** | **Short acting; IV titration** |
| **Spironolactone** | **Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist** | **Longer-term therapy; monitor K⁺/renal function** |

- **Three key groups:** **loop diuretic (furosemide)** → promotes natriuresis; **nitroglycerin** → lowers vascular pressure/preload when BP permits; **dobutamine** → supports contractility when low output/shock. **Two drugs can relieve congestion at different physiological points.** **Use the reference after the clinical decision — not before it.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Furosemide = decongestion; nitroglycerin = preload reduction with adequate BP; dobutamine = low output/shock.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Match the drug to the problem.</summary>

**Answer:** Congestion with volume overload: furosemide (loop diuretic); raised vascular pressure and preload with adequate BP: nitroglycerin; low output or cardiogenic shock: dobutamine (milrinone or norepinephrine for selected profiles).

**Why:** Slides "Reveal: three key drug groups" and "Pharmacology: the essentials".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe furosemide and nitroglycerin.</summary>

**Answer:** Furosemide blocks the Na+/K+/2Cl- cotransporter in the loop of Henle and is the main decongestive drug in the acute pathway; nitroglycerin is a vasodilator that lowers vascular pressure and venous return, used when BP is adequate while watching for hypotension.

**Why:** Slide "Pharmacology: the essentials".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare dobutamine, milrinone and norepinephrine.</summary>

**Answer:** Dobutamine: beta-1 inotropy, short acting, continuous IV; milrinone: PDE-3 inhibition giving inotropy and vasodilation with renal elimination and longer action; norepinephrine: alpha-1 vasoconstriction with some beta effect, short acting, IV titration.

**Why:** Slide "Pharmacology reference".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Breathlessness is a symptom: look for congestion features (orthopnea, PND, JVP, S3, edema, BNP).**
- **Use nitroglycerin only when BP is adequate; shock → inotropic support.**
- **Furosemide blocks the Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter in the loop of Henle.**
- **Dobutamine = β₁ inotrope; milrinone = PDE-3 inhibitor; norepinephrine = α₁ vasoconstrictor.**
- **Compensation (RAAS + SNS) becomes part of the congestion problem.**
- **Use the drug reference after the clinical decision.**
