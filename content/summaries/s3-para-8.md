## 1. Species and general facts

| Species | Disease |
|---|---|
| **P. vivax** | **Benign tertian malaria** |
| **P. ovale** | **Benign tertian malaria** |
| **P. falciparum** | **Malignant tertian malaria** |
| **P. malariae** | **Benign quartan malaria** |

- ⭐ **Infective stage: sporozoite**; **entry: bite of the female Anopheles**; **intermediate host: human** (liver, blood, brain); **definitive host: female Anopheles**; **diagnostic stage: ring stage in peripheral blood**; **source: infected person.**
- Malaria is **Anopheles-borne**, **only the female bites**, **no direct person-to-person spread**, mostly **tropical.** **Male Anopheles feeds on fruit juices.**
- **Schizogony** = **asexual** multiplication in humans; **sporogony** = **sexual** reproduction in the mosquito.

**Key points**
- Four species; P. falciparum = malignant.
- Human = intermediate host; female Anopheles = definitive host.

## 2. Life cycle in humans (asexual, schizogony)

- ⭐ **Exo-erythrocytic (liver) cycle:** infected mosquito injects **sporozoites** (non-motile, spiral, flagellate-like, **protein-coated**) → reach skin vessels in minutes → blood **~30 min** → **hepatic parenchymal cells.** Some stay dormant as **hypnozoites** (**P. vivax and P. ovale only**).
- Sporozoites become **hepatic merozoites**; most are **engulfed by macrophages**, some infect new liver cells. The liver cell **ruptures in 6-15 days** releasing **~1000** spherical multinucleated merozoites (**45-60 µm**).
- **Erythrocytic cycle:** merozoites attach to **glycophorin** and enter the RBC by **invagination** → **ring stage** (one large ring with one dot; **P. falciparum: 2 or more small rings, double chromatin dots**) → **amoeboid stage** (peripheral blood in all species **except P. falciparum**, which develops in **deep capillaries, mainly brain**) → **schizont** (**8-32 merozoites + hemozoin**) → rupture → **erythrocytic merozoites** reinvade RBCs (**re-erythrocytic**) or liver cells (**re-exoerythrocytic**, **not in P. falciparum**).
- **Stippling:** **Schuffner's dots** (P. vivax, P. ovale; **crenated stage** in P. ovale), **Ziemann's dots and band forms** (P. malariae), **Maurer's dots** (P. falciparum, deep capillaries).
- **Gametocytes:** **male (micro) and female (macro)**, **~12%** of the blood forms; **cause no symptoms** but **transmit** (carrier); **crescent** in P. falciparum, rounded/oval in the others.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Hypnozoites: vivax and ovale only.
- Ring → amoeboid → schizont; P. falciparum sequesters in deep capillaries.
- Gametocytes are asymptomatic but infectious.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the exo-erythrocytic cycle.</summary>

**Answer:** Sporozoites reach the blood within minutes, circulate about 30 minutes and enter hepatic parenchymal cells, forming hepatic merozoites; the liver cell ruptures in 6-15 days releasing about 1000 merozoites; some sporozoites stay dormant as hypnozoites (vivax and ovale).

**Why:** Slides 10-12.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the erythrocytic cycle.</summary>

**Answer:** Merozoites enter RBCs via glycophorin, form rings, amoeboid stages and schizonts (8-32 merozoites with hemozoin) and burst out as erythrocytic merozoites that reinvade RBCs or liver cells (not in P. falciparum) or form gametocytes.

**Why:** Slides 13-18.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are hypnozoites?</summary>

**Answer:** Dormant liver forms of sporozoites that reactivate into schizonts; they occur in P. vivax and P. ovale only.

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

## 3. Life cycle in the mosquito (sexual, sporogony)

- Mosquito ingests **gametocytes** → in the **midgut** the **male gametocyte exflagellates in 5-10 min into 6-8 flagellate bodies** → **fertilize the female gametocyte** → **zygote (30-60 min)** → **ookinete (18-24 h)** enters the stomach wall → **oocyst** → many **sporozoites** → burst and migrate to the **salivary glands**; cycle **~1-4 weeks.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Gametocyte → zygote → ookinete → oocyst → sporozoite → salivary gland.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Outline the sporogonic cycle.</summary>

**Answer:** Gametocytes ingested; exflagellation of the male in 5-10 minutes; fertilization gives a zygote (30-60 min), the ookinete (18-24 h) penetrates the stomach wall and forms an oocyst whose sporozoites migrate to the salivary glands.

**Why:** Slides 19-20.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How long does the mosquito cycle take?</summary>

**Answer:** About 1-4 weeks.

**Why:** Slide 20.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Where do the sporozoites go?</summary>

**Answer:** To the body cavity and the salivary glands of the female Anopheles.

**Why:** Slide 20.
</details>

## 4. Comparison of the four species

| Feature | P. vivax | P. ovale | P. falciparum | P. malariae |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ring** | One delicate ring, prominent chromatin, **1/3 RBC** | One ring 1/3 RBC, **more compact** | **Multiple small rings, double chromatin, 1/6 RBC** | One compact ring up to 1/3 RBC, thicker |
| **RBCs infected** | **Young** (enlarged) | **Young** (enlarged, oval, irregular edge) | **All stages** (normal size) | **Old** (normal or small) |
| **Hypnozoites** | **Yes** | **Yes** | No | No |
| **Hepatic stage** | 8 days, 10,000, 45 µm | 9 days, 15,000, 60 µm | **6 days, 40,000-50,000**, 60 µm | 14-15 days, 7,500, 55 µm |
| **Fever** | Every **48 h** | Every **48 h** | Every **36-48 h** | Every **72 h** |
| **Dots** | **Schuffner's** | Fine **Schuffner's** | **Maurer's** | **Ziemann's** |
| **Special stage** | — | **Crenated** | — | **Band** |
| **Gametocyte** | Oval filling the cell | Like vivax | **Crescent** | Like vivax |
| **Incubation (days)** | 12-14 | 14 | 12 | 28-30 |
| **Sporogony (days)** | 9-10 | 14-16 | 10-12 | 25-28 |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- P. falciparum: multiple rings, all RBC ages, 40-50 thousand merozoites, crescent gametocyte, Maurer's dots.
- P. malariae: 72 h fever, old RBCs, longest incubation.

</div>

## 5. Pathogenesis and the malaria paroxysm

- ⭐ Clinical features arise from **erythrocytic schizogony products, host reaction and hypoxia** (obstructed blood flow).
- **Organs:** **liver** hepatomegaly with **hemozoin**; **spleen** splenomegaly with **thick black capsule**; **brain** encephalopathy (P. falciparum, soft gray cortex); **heart** congestive failure; **kidney** hemoglobinuric nephrosis (black water fever); **anemia** (complement-mediated and autoimmune hemolysis).
- **Paroxysm** (mass rupture of parasitized RBCs, release of merozoites, pigment and toxin):

| Phase | Features | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Cold** | Sudden cold, shivering, headache, slight fever | **< 1 hour** |
| **2. Fever** | **40.5-41 °C**, dry skin, severe headache, joint and muscle pain, ± vomiting and mild diarrhea | **2-4 hours** |
| **3. Sweating** | Temperature falls, sweating, **↓ WBCs**, pain disappears, deep sleep | **1-2 hours** (longer in P. falciparum) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Paroxysm = cold, fever, sweating.
- Hemozoin darkens the liver, spleen and brain.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What causes the clinical features?</summary>

**Answer:** Products of erythrocytic schizogony, the host reaction to antigens and tissues, and hypoxia from obstructed blood flow by parasites in erythrocytes.

**Why:** Slide 21.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the three phases of the paroxysm.</summary>

**Answer:** Cold phase (under 1 hour), fever phase up to 40.5-41 °C (2-4 hours) and sweating phase with fewer WBCs and sleep (1-2 hours or more).

**Why:** Slides 22-23.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the organ changes.</summary>

**Answer:** Hepatomegaly with hemozoin, splenomegaly with a thick black capsule, encephalopathy in P. falciparum, heart failure, hemoglobinuric nephrosis and hemolytic anemia.

**Why:** Slide 21.
</details>

## 6. Complications

- **Cerebral malaria** (P. falciparum): infected RBCs **aggregate and stick** to capillary endothelium and macrophages in deep brain capillaries → ↑ plasma viscosity and **capillary obstruction**; **headache, hyperpyrexia, convulsions, paralysis, coma**; **fatal** if untreated; resembles meningitis. Late schizonts form **knob-like** surface projections.
- **Black water fever** (malarial hemoglobinuria): **intravascular hemolysis** of infected and uninfected RBCs in chronic falciparum malaria, with **irregular anti-malarial treatment or G6PD deficiency**; **bilious vomiting, prostration, dark red/black urine**, renal failure, death.
- **General complications:** **severe anemia; organ damage (brain, kidney, lungs, intestine); black water fever; hypoglycemia (esp. with quinine); septicemia; hypotensive shock; metabolic acidosis; splenic rupture; pregnancy (maternal death, abortion, stillbirth, low birth weight).**
- **Falciparum complications:** **cerebral, algid, septicemic malaria, black water fever, acute renal failure, hypoglycemia (< 40 mg/dL), severe anemia (Hb < 5 g/dL, PCV < 15%), hyperpyrexia, metabolic acidosis and shock.**
- **Why falciparum is more dangerous:** **40,000-50,000 merozoites quickly**, **all RBC ages**, **deep-capillary development** (brain), **adhesion of infected RBCs**, **re-erythrocytic cycle only.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Cerebral malaria = leading cause of death in falciparum malaria.
- Black water fever = hemolysis + dark urine (G6PD, irregular treatment).

</div>

**Answer:** Aggregation of infected RBCs in deep brain capillaries obstructs flow, causing headache, hyperpyrexia, convulsions, paralysis and coma; it resembles meningitis and is usually fatal if untreated.

**Why:** Slides 24-25.

</details>

## 7. Diagnosis, treatment and prophylaxis

- **Direct:** **thin and thick blood films** from the fingertip, **Giemsa or Leishman** → **ring stage.**
- **Indirect (serology):** **IHA, IFA, ELISA, rapid ICT** (kits ~**15 minutes**). **Molecular:** **DNA probe, PCR** (species identification, drug resistance).
- **Uncomplicated:** **chloroquine** 1000 mg then 500 mg after 6 h then 500 mg daily × 2 days; **if resistant: Fansidar (pyrimethamine + sulfadoxine) or quinine 600 mg every 8 h for a week + doxycycline 100 mg daily.**
- **Severe:** **immediate parenteral IV quinine or quinidine.**
- **Prophylaxis:** **vector control** (chemical, physical, biological): **oil standing water to kill larvae**, reduce breeding sites (**drainage, filling, deepening, flushing, water-level management**); **intermittent irrigation.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Giemsa thin and thick films show rings.
- Chloroquine first; quinine for resistance and IV quinine/quinidine for severe disease.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is malaria diagnosed?</summary>

**Answer:** By Giemsa or Leishman stained thin and thick blood films showing ring stages; also IHA, IFA, ELISA, rapid ICT and molecular tests (DNA probe, PCR).

**Why:** Slides 29-30.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How is malaria treated?</summary>

**Answer:** Chloroquine for uncomplicated cases; Fansidar or quinine plus doxycycline if resistant; immediate IV quinine or quinidine for severe malaria.

**Why:** Slide 32.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is malaria prevented?</summary>

**Answer:** Vector control: oiling standing water, reducing breeding sites by drainage, filling, deepening or flushing, water-level management and intermittent irrigation.

</details>
