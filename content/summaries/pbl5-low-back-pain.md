## 1. The case and the history

- ⭐ **Patient:** **Mr. Qasim, 43, taxi driver in Karbala**, formerly a **veterinarian** in Tal-Aaffar (Al-Mosul); migrated two years ago; **wife and 3 children; no paid sick leave.**
- **Background:** **ten-year** moderate localized **nagging low back pain**, started when **handling heavy uncooperative animals**; **aggravated by prolonged sitting and heavy lifting** (suitcases); otherwise healthy.
- **Acute event:** lifting a **large suitcase** from the taxi boot → **sudden sharper twinge in the lower back shooting down the right leg.** Hot water bottle + **Voltaren** did not help; within **48 hours** pain radiated **down the back of the right leg into the foot**, **severe and persistent.** After 3 days he could not work and rested **lying on a firm surface.**
- **At the GP:** pain in the **lower back and right leg** (**buttock, back of thigh, outer leg, little toe, sole**); **severe, throbbing deep**; painkillers every 4 hours; **VAS 70 mm (7.0 cm)**; **worse with sitting (driving), lifting, coughing, sneezing**; **better lying down; standing more comfortable**; **burning/tingling on the outer leg and foot, numbness of the little toe.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Chronic mechanical back pain + sudden lifting injury → radicular leg pain.
- Aggravated by sitting, lifting, coughing and sneezing; relieved by lying down.
- VAS 70 mm at first visit.

</div>

|---|---|
| **Gait** | **Limp** (called claudication in the case) |
| **Posture** | **Forward tilt + lateral tilt (scoliosis) to the right** |
| **Palpation** | **Hard lower back muscles = spasm of the erector spinae** |
| **Spinal movement** | **Flexion, extension and lateral flexion painful and restricted** |
| **Straight leg raising** | **Diminished** |
| **Sensation** | **Diminished over the little toe** |
| **Reflexes** | **Right ankle reflex diminished; plantar reflex normal** |

- **Note:** hind foot **eversion occurs at the subtalar joint**; what is measured is **hind foot eversion + forefoot pronation.**

## 3. Diagnosis: sciatica and anatomy reasoning

- ⭐ **Diagnosis in the case: sciatica** = **root (radicular) pain** caused by **compression over a nerve root**; **many conditions** could cause the compression.
- **Standard teaching (not stated in the case):** the distribution (back of thigh and outer leg to the **little toe and sole**), the **reduced ankle jerk (S1)**, **weak eversion** and **difficulty on tiptoes (S1 plantar flexion)** point to the **L5 and S1 roots** on the right; the **commonest cause is a lumbar disc prolapse** compressing a root (usually **L4/L5 or L5/S1**); **reduced straight leg raise** reflects tension on these roots.
- **Standard teaching, red flags:** **urinary retention or incontinence, saddle anesthesia, bilateral leg weakness (cauda equina)**, progressive deficit, fever, cancer history or severe trauma need urgent assessment.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sciatica = radicular pain from nerve root compression.
- Case findings fit right L5/S1 root involvement (textbook reasoning).
- Cauda equina red flags need urgent action.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Root (radicular) pain caused by compression over a nerve root; many conditions can cause the compression.

**Why:** Case page 2.
</details>

**Answer:** L5 and S1: pain to the back of the thigh, outer leg, little toe and sole, diminished ankle jerk (S1), weak eversion and weak plantar flexion (tiptoe walking), usually from a prolapsed disc.

**Why:** Anatomical reasoning applied to the case findings.

## 4. Management, psychosocial issues and alternative therapies

- ⭐ **Initial plan:** **2 weeks off work** (medical certificate), **normal activity at home with rest when severe**, **physiotherapy**, **review in 5 days.**
- **Psychosocial:** he is **anxious, tense, sweaty, sleeping badly** and worried about **income** (no paid sick leave, rent and groceries); **expense** stopped him attending physiotherapy.
- **Review at 5 days:** **VAS 70 → 35**; still uncomfortable sitting; **slightly more spinal movement and slightly better neurological signs.**
- **Questions raised:** **supportive brace**, **injection**, **chiropractic/osteopathic manipulation** and **acupuncture**. Alternative practitioners claim more than orthodox doctors, but others say they **relieve pain chiefly through psychic (placebo) effects.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Two weeks off, activity as tolerated, physiotherapy, review in 5 days.
- Pain halved (VAS 70 → 35) at review.
- Financial pressure and cost are barriers; alternative therapies may act mainly via placebo effects.

</div>

<details><summary>Q1. What was the initial management plan?</summary>

**Answer:** Two weeks off work with a medical certificate, a mix of normal activity at home and rest when the pain is severe, physiotherapy, and review after five days.

**Why:** Case page 2.
</details>
