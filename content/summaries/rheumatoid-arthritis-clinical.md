## 1. Definition, epidemiology and etiology

- ⭐ **RA:** a **chronic systemic autoimmune inflammatory disease**: **persistent synovitis**, **symmetrical peripheral polyarthritis**, **cartilage destruction**, **bone erosion**, **deformity** and **disability**; may involve **extra-articular organs**: **a systemic disease, not simply a joint disease**.
- **Rheumatology** (Greek rheuma = "flow"): **over 200 disorders**; **osteoarthritis** is the **most common** adult condition; recent advances = **biologic DMARDs**.
- **Epidemiology:** **0.5–1%** of adults; **women > men**; onset most common in **middle adulthood**; **family history** ↑ risk; **smoking** (esp. **anti-CCP-positive** RA).
- **Etiology:** **genetic** — **HLA-DRB1 shared epitope**; **environmental** — **smoking**, **periodontal disease (Porphyromonas gingivalis)**, pulmonary/mucosal exposures, **obesity**, infections, **hormonal** factors.

**Key points**
- RA = systemic, symmetric, erosive; 0.5–1%; F > M; HLA-DRB1 and smoking.

## 2. Pathogenesis

- **Sequence:** **genetic susceptibility + environmental trigger** → **loss of tolerance** → **citrullination** → **ACPA (anti-CCP)** → **T- and B-cell activation** → **cytokines** → **synovitis** → **pannus** → **cartilage destruction + bone erosion** → **deformity and disability**.
- **Cells:** **CD4+ T cells, B cells, macrophages, fibroblast-like synoviocytes, osteoclasts**. **Cytokines:** **TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1, IL-17** → synovitis, **angiogenesis**, osteoclast activation, cartilage destruction, systemic inflammation.
- **Citrullination:** **arginine → citrulline** by **peptidyl-arginine deiminase**; **anti-CCP** is **highly specific** and may **precede** arthritis.
- **Synovium:** thickened, hyperplastic, vascular, inflamed → **pannus** invades **cartilage and subchondral bone** → **marginal erosions**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Citrullination → ACPA → T/B cells → TNF, IL-6, IL-1, IL-17 → pannus → erosions.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Outline the pathogenesis of RA.</summary>

**Answer:** Genetic susceptibility plus an environmental trigger leads to loss of tolerance, citrullination and ACPA formation, T- and B-cell activation, cytokine production, synovitis and pannus, then cartilage destruction, bone erosion, deformity and disability.

**Why:** Slides "Pathogenesis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is citrullination?</summary>

**Answer:** Conversion of arginine to citrulline by peptidyl-arginine deiminase; citrullinated proteins can become immune targets, generating ACPA (measured as anti-CCP), which is highly specific for RA and may appear before arthritis.

**Why:** Slide "What is citrullination?".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which cytokines and cells are important?</summary>

**Answer:** Cells: CD4+ T cells, B cells, macrophages, fibroblast-like synoviocytes and osteoclasts. Cytokines: TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1 and IL-17, promoting synovitis, angiogenesis, osteoclast activation, cartilage destruction and systemic inflammation.

**Why:** Slides "Important immunological mechanisms".
</details>

## 3. Clinical presentation and hand deformities

- **Classical:** **chronic symmetrical inflammatory polyarthritis** of **small joints of the hands and feet**; **pain, swelling, morning stiffness, reduced movement, fatigue, malaise, weakness**.
- **Screening questions:** pain/stiffness? dress yourself? stairs? — all "yes" → serious disease **unlikely**.
- **Morning stiffness:** **RA > 30–60 min** (improves with movement); **OA < 30 min** (activity-related).
- **Joints:** **MCP, PIP, wrist, MTP**; **DIP usually spared**; **symmetrical**, **persistent**.

| Deformity | Description |
|---|---|
| **Ulnar deviation** | **MCP** joints deviate toward the **ulna** |
| **Swan-neck** | **PIP hyperextension + DIP flexion** |
| **Boutonnière** | **PIP flexion + DIP hyperextension** |
| **Z-thumb** | **MCP flexion + IP hyperextension** |

- **Nodules:** firm subcutaneous over the **olecranon/extensor surfaces**; **seropositive and severe** disease.
- **Cervical spine:** **atlantoaxial subluxation** → **cord compression**; warning signs: **neck pain, occipital headache, neurological symptoms, weakness, gait disturbance, myelopathy**; special precautions before **anesthesia/neck manipulation**.
- **Extra-articular clues in history:** rashes/photosensitivity (SLE); Raynaud's; dry eyes/mouth (Sjögren's); iritis (AS); diarrhoea/urethritis (reactive); nodules (RA, TB, gout); ulcers (Behçet's, SLE).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Symmetric MCP/PIP/wrist/MTP; DIP spared; stiffness > 30–60 min.
- Swan-neck = PIP hyperextension; boutonnière = PIP flexion.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Chronic symmetrical inflammatory polyarthritis of small hand and foot joints with pain, swelling, morning stiffness longer than 30-60 minutes, reduced range of movement, fatigue, malaise and weakness; MCP, PIP, wrist and MTP joints are commonly affected while DIP joints are usually spared.

**Why:** Slides "Clinical presentation" and "Joints commonly affected".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Define the hand deformities of chronic RA.</summary>

**Answer:** Ulnar deviation (MCP toward the ulna); swan-neck (PIP hyperextension, DIP flexion); boutonnière (PIP flexion, DIP hyperextension); Z-thumb (MCP flexion, IP hyperextension).

**Why:** Slide "Cervical spine involvement".

## 4. Extra-articular manifestations

| System | Manifestations |
|---|---|
| **Skin** | **Rheumatoid nodules, vasculitis** |
| **Eye** | **Episcleritis, scleritis** (severe RA), **keratoconjunctivitis sicca** |
| **Lung** | **Pleural effusion, ILD, nodules, bronchiectasis** |
| **Heart** | **Pericarditis, myocarditis, endocarditis, valve destruction**, ↑ **CV risk** |
| **Nervous** | **Peripheral and entrapment neuropathy, cervical myelopathy** |
| **Hematological** | **Anemia of chronic disease, thrombocytosis** |

- **Felty syndrome:** **RA + splenomegaly + neutropenia**; long-standing **severe seropositive** RA; **recurrent infections**.
- **RA-ILD:** **UIP, NSIP**; **progressive dyspnea, dry cough, fine crackles, ↓ oxygenation**; **HRCT**.
- **Cardiovascular:** chronic inflammation → **accelerated atherosclerosis**; manage **smoking, BP, lipids, diabetes, activity**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Felty = RA + splenomegaly + neutropenia.
- ILD (UIP/NSIP) and cardiovascular disease are major complications.

</div>

## 5. Investigations

| Test | Key points |
|---|---|
| **ESR / CRP** | Usually **elevated in active disease**; monitor activity/response; **normal does not exclude RA** |
| **RF** | Antibody against the **Fc of IgG**; **not specific** (Sjögren, SLE, chronic infections, hepatitis C, elderly) |
| **Anti-CCP (ACPA)** | **High specificity**, useful **early**, may **precede** arthritis, ↑ risk of **erosions**; RF + anti-CCP both positive = ↑ probability |
| **ANA** | May be positive, **not diagnostic**; for SLE, Sjögren, systemic sclerosis |
| **Synovial fluid** | **Inflammatory**: ↑ WBC (neutrophils), ↓ viscosity; exclude **septic/crystal** arthritis |
| **X-ray** | Early **soft tissue swelling, periarticular osteopenia**; later **joint-space narrowing, marginal erosions, deformity, subluxation** |
| **Ultrasound** | **Synovitis, effusion, tenosynovitis, early erosions, power Doppler** |
| **MRI** | Sensitive for **synovitis, bone marrow edema, early erosions, tenosynovitis** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Anti-CCP is more specific than RF; RF positive ≠ RA.
- Marginal erosions are typical of RA.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare RF and anti-CCP.</summary>

**Answer:** RF is directed against the Fc of IgG and is not specific (also in Sjögren, SLE, chronic infection, hepatitis C, older people); anti-CCP (ACPA) is highly specific, useful in early disease, can precede arthritis and predicts erosive disease; both positive greatly raise the probability of RA.

**Why:** Slides "Rheumatoid factor" and "Anti-CCP antibody".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the X-ray changes of RA?</summary>

**Answer:** Early soft tissue swelling and periarticular osteopenia; later joint-space narrowing, marginal erosions, deformity and subluxation.

**Why:** Slide "Imaging".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What does synovial fluid show in RA and why is it examined?</summary>

**Answer:** Inflammatory fluid with increased WBC (predominantly neutrophils) and reduced viscosity; it is examined mainly to exclude septic or crystal arthritis in an acutely swollen joint.

**Why:** Slide on ANA and synovial fluid.
</details>

## 6. 2010 ACR/EULAR criteria and differential diagnosis

- **For classification** of patients with **definite synovitis** and **no better alternative diagnosis**; **total ≥ 6/10 = definite RA**.

| Domain | Score |
|---|---|
| **Joints** | 1 large = **0**; 2–10 large = **1**; 1–3 small = **2**; 4–10 small = **3**; > 10 incl. ≥ 1 small = **5** |
| **Serology** (RF/anti-CCP) | both negative **0**; low-positive (> ULN to 3× ULN) **2**; high-positive (> 3× ULN) **3** |
| **Acute-phase reactants** | normal **0**; abnormal ESR or CRP **1** |
| **Duration** | < 6 weeks **0**; ≥ 6 weeks **1** |

| Feature | RA | OA |
|---|---|---|
| **Pain** | Inflammatory | Mechanical |
| **Morning stiffness** | > 30–60 min | < 30 min |
| **MCP / DIP** | MCP common; DIP spared | MCP spared; DIP common |
| **ESR/CRP, anti-CCP** | May ↑ / may be positive | Normal / negative |
| **Erosions, osteophytes** | **Marginal** erosions; osteophytes not typical | Erosions absent; **osteophytes** typical |

- **Psoriatic arthritis:** **DIP, dactylitis, enthesitis, nail pitting, psoriasis, asymmetric**. **Gout:** acute severe, red, **first MTP**; **needle-shaped, negatively birefringent urate crystals**. **SLE arthritis:** **non-erosive**, systemic features, **ANA+**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- ≥ 6/10 = RA; joints up to 5, serology up to 3, APR 1, duration 1.
- RA: marginal erosions, DIP spared; OA: osteophytes, DIP involved.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the 2010 ACR/EULAR scoring.</summary>

**Answer:** Four domains: joints (0 to 5), serology (0, 2 or 3), acute-phase reactants (0 or 1) and duration (0 if under 6 weeks, 1 if 6 weeks or more); a total of 6 or more out of 10 classifies definite RA when there is no better alternative diagnosis.

**Why:** Slides "2010 ACR/EULAR classification criteria".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate RA from osteoarthritis.</summary>

**Answer:** RA has inflammatory pain, morning stiffness over 30-60 minutes, common MCP and PIP involvement with DIP sparing, symmetry, raised ESR/CRP, possible anti-CCP and marginal erosions; OA has mechanical pain, stiffness under 30 minutes, DIP involvement, normal ESR/CRP, negative anti-CCP and osteophytes.

**Why:** Slide comparing RA and OA.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do gout, psoriatic arthritis and SLE arthritis differ from RA?</summary>

**Answer:** Gout: acute severe red joint (often first MTP) with needle-shaped negatively birefringent crystals. Psoriatic arthritis: DIP, dactylitis, enthesitis, nail pitting, psoriasis, asymmetric. SLE arthritis: non-erosive, with systemic features and ANA positive; RA gives persistent erosive synovitis.

**Why:** Slides on differential diagnosis.
</details>

## 7. Treatment and prognosis

- **Treat to target:** **early**; goals: **remission** (or **low disease activity**), prevent **structural damage**, preserve **function**, prevent **disability**, reduce **systemic complications**.

| Drug | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Methotrexate** | **Anchor** csDMARD; **once weekly** (oral/SC); **folic acid**; **hepatotoxicity, marrow suppression, mucositis, GI, pulmonary toxicity, infection**; **contraindicated in pregnancy** |
| **Leflunomide** | **DHODH inhibitor** (↓ pyrimidine); **hepatotoxicity, diarrhea, alopecia, hypertension, teratogenic**; **long half-life** → **cholestyramine washout** |
| **Sulfasalazine** | Mild/moderate, combination, **pregnancy**; **GI upset, rash, hepatotoxicity, leukopenia** |
| **Hydroxychloroquine** | Mild disease, combination; **retinal toxicity** → eye screening |
| **Glucocorticoids** | **Bridging**, severe flares, intra-articular |
| **Biologics** | **TNF** (adalimumab, infliximab); **IL-6** (tocilizumab, sarilumab); **T-cell costimulation** (abatacept); **B-cell depletion** (rituximab) |
| **JAK inhibitors (tsDMARD)** | **Tofacitinib**; **serious infections, herpes zoster, malignancy** |

- **Vaccines:** influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19, recombinant zoster, hepatitis B; **avoid live vaccines** during significant immunosuppression.
- **Non-drug:** education, exercise, physio/OT, joint protection, weight, **stop smoking**, CV risk, osteoporosis prevention. **Surgery:** severe destruction, pain, disability, instability: **joint replacement, synovectomy, cervical spine surgery**.
- **Poor prognosis:** **high activity, anti-CCP+, high RF, early erosions, smoking, extra-articular disease, poor initial response**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Methotrexate weekly + folic acid; not in pregnancy. Leflunomide: DHODH, washout. Hydroxychloroquine: retina.
- No live vaccines on immunosuppression; poor prognosis = anti-CCP+, high RF, early erosions, smoking.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. State the principles and goals of RA treatment.</summary>

**Answer:** Treat to target, started early: remission (or low disease activity), prevention of structural damage, preservation of function, prevention of disability and reduction of systemic complications.

**Why:** Slides "Principles of treatment".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe methotrexate in RA.</summary>

**Answer:** The anchor conventional DMARD, given once weekly (oral or subcutaneous) with folic acid; adverse effects include hepatotoxicity, bone marrow suppression, mucositis, GI symptoms, pulmonary toxicity and infection; it is teratogenic and contraindicated in pregnancy.

**Why:** Slides "Methotrexate".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the poor prognostic factors.</summary>

**Answer:** High disease activity, anti-CCP positivity, high RF, early erosions, smoking, extra-articular disease and poor response to initial therapy.

**Why:** Slide "Prognostic factors for severe disease".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **RA spares the DIP; OA affects the DIP.**
- **Swan-neck = PIP hyperextension; boutonnière = PIP flexion.**
- **Positive RF does not equal RA; anti-CCP is more specific.**
- **Normal ESR/CRP does not exclude RA.**
- **Methotrexate is weekly, not daily, and teratogenic.**
- **Hydroxychloroquine = retina; leflunomide = long half-life/washout.**
- **Felty = RA + splenomegaly + neutropenia.**
- **No live vaccines during significant immunosuppression.**
