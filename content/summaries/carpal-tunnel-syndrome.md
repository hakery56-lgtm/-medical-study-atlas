## 1. Epidemiology, anatomy and definition

- **CTS** = the **most common entrapment neuropathy**; **pooled prevalence about 14.4%** (2024 meta-analysis); older strict studies ≈ **105 per 100 000 person-years**.
- **Age:** any adult age; peak **40–60 years** (**women 40–49**, **men 50–59**); second rise in older women. **Women > men** (**pregnancy, menopause**, anatomy, metabolic disease).
- ⭐ **Definition:** **median nerve entrapment** at the wrist from **↑ pressure in the carpal tunnel** → **sensory symptoms, pain**, and in advanced cases **motor weakness and thenar wasting**.
- **Tunnel contents:** **median nerve + 9 flexor tendons** (**4 FDS, 4 FDP, 1 FPL**); **roof = flexor retinaculum (transverse carpal ligament)**.
- **Median nerve supply:** sensation to **thumb, index, middle, radial half of ring finger**; **recurrent motor branch → most thenar muscles**; **little finger spared**.

- Tunnel: median nerve + 9 flexor tendons; roof = flexor retinaculum.

## 2. Pathophysiology and etiology

- **Early:** **↑ tunnel pressure → impaired venous return → nerve edema → ischemia → demyelination** (compression/demyelinating neuropathy).
- **Prolonged/severe:** **persistent ischemia → axonal degeneration → denervation → thenar wasting**.

| Cause | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Local** | **Small tunnel, distal radius fracture, carpal arthritis, ganglion, tumor, tenosynovitis, post-traumatic deformity** |
| **Systemic** | **Diabetes, hypothyroidism, RA, acromegaly, CKD, amyloidosis, obesity, pregnancy, menopause** |
| **Fluid retention** | **Pregnancy, renal disease, heart failure, hypothyroidism** |
| **Occupational** | **Repetitive wrist flexion/extension, forceful grip, vibrating tools, prolonged wrist flexion, pressure on the palm** (not the sole cause) |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Early = demyelination; late = axonal loss and thenar wasting.
- Multifactorial: local, systemic, fluid retention, occupational.

</div>

## 3. Clinical presentation

- **Sensory:** **nocturnal numbness and paresthesia** in the **median distribution** (tingling, burning, electric shock, pins and needles); patients often describe the **whole hand** as numb.
- **Night waking** relieved by **shaking/hanging/rubbing the hand** = **flick sign** (strongly suggestive, not a formal test).
- **Motor (advanced):** **weak thumb opposition and abduction**, trouble with jars, buttons, small objects, dropping things; **thenar wasting** = significant compression with **axonal loss**.
- **Pain:** wrist, hand, forearm (± toward elbow/shoulder); **pain from neck down the arm → cervical radiculopathy**.
- **Central palm sensation preserved:** the **palmar cutaneous branch** arises **proximal** to the tunnel and passes **superficial** to the flexor retinaculum; loss suggests **more proximal median neuropathy**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Nocturnal paresthesia + flick sign; thenar wasting = late/axonal.
- Palm sensation preserved (palmar cutaneous branch).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the sensory presentation of CTS.</summary>

**Answer:** Gradual nocturnal numbness and paresthesia (tingling, burning, electric shocks, pins and needles) in the thumb, index, middle and radial half of the ring finger; patients often feel the whole hand is numb and wake at night, relieved by shaking the hand (flick sign).

**Why:** Slides "Clinical presentation" and "Nocturnal symptoms".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the motor features and what does thenar wasting mean?</summary>

**Answer:** Weak thumb opposition and abduction with difficulty opening jars, buttoning and holding small objects; thenar wasting indicates significant prolonged median nerve compression with axonal loss.

**Why:** Slide "Motor symptoms".
</details>

**Why:** Slide "Palmar cutaneous branch".
</details>

## 4. Provocative tests and differential diagnosis

| Test | Technique | Positive |
|---|---|---|
| **Phalen** | **Maximal wrist flexion** (dorsal surfaces together) **≈ 60 s** | Median numbness/tingling (↑ tunnel pressure) |
| **Reverse Phalen** | **Palms together, wrists extended** | Median symptoms (less used) |
| **Tinel** | **Tap** over the median nerve at the tunnel | Tingling/electric sensation; supports, not diagnostic alone |
| **Durkan** | **Direct compression ≈ 30 s** | Typical paresthesia; **often more sensitive than Tinel/Phalen** |
| **Hand elevation / flick sign** | Hands above head; ask about night behaviour | Symptoms reproduced / shaking relieves |

| Condition | Clue |
|---|---|
| **CTS** | Median distribution; nocturnal |
| **C6 / C7 / C8 radiculopathy** | **Neck pain + thumb/index** / **middle finger** / **ring-little finger** |
| **Ulnar neuropathy** | **Little + ulnar half of ring finger** |
| **Brachial plexopathy** | Broader distribution |
| **Polyneuropathy** | **Bilateral stocking-glove** |
| **Pronator syndrome** | **Median symptoms + forearm pain**, less nocturnal |
| **Cervical myelopathy** | **Hand symptoms + UMN signs** |
| **Thumb CMC OA** | **Thumb-base pain**, not paresthesia |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Phalen 60 s flexion; Durkan 30 s compression; Tinel tap.
- C6 thumb/index, C7 middle, C8 ring/little.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the Phalen, Tinel and Durkan tests.</summary>

**Answer:** Phalen: wrists in maximal flexion for about 60 seconds reproduces median symptoms (flexion raises tunnel pressure). Tinel: tapping over the median nerve at the tunnel gives tingling. Durkan: direct compression of the tunnel for about 30 seconds reproduces paresthesia and is often more sensitive than Tinel or Phalen.

**Why:** Slides "Clinical provocative tests".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How do radiculopathies and ulnar neuropathy differ from CTS?</summary>

**Answer:** C6: neck pain with thumb/index symptoms; C7: middle finger; C8: ring/little finger; ulnar neuropathy: little finger and ulnar half of the ring finger; CTS: median distribution with nocturnal symptoms.

**Why:** Slide "Condition / important clue".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What other conditions mimic CTS?</summary>

**Answer:** Brachial plexopathy (broader distribution), polyneuropathy (bilateral stocking-glove), pronator syndrome (forearm pain, less nocturnal), cervical myelopathy (UMN signs) and thumb CMC osteoarthritis (thumb-base pain).

**Why:** Slide "Condition / important clue".
</details>

## 5. Investigations and severity

- **CTS is primarily a clinical diagnosis**; investigate when **uncertain, atypical, severe, before surgery, or alternative pathology/nerve damage suspected**.
- **NCS (principal confirmation):** **↑ median sensory distal latency**, **↓ sensory velocity across the wrist**, **↑ distal motor latency**, **↓ CMAP amplitude** (advanced); severe: **reduced sensory and motor responses, axonal loss**.
- **EMG:** **denervation, reduced recruitment, chronic neurogenic change** (**abductor pollicis brevis**); **severity, axonal loss, exclude radiculopathy**.
- **Ultrasound:** **↑ median nerve CSA** (**≥ 10 mm²** suspicious; **normal < 9**), flattening, tenosynovitis, ganglion, mass; useful when **NCS equivocal** or **mass** suspected.
- **Labs:** no specific test; **glucose/HbA1c, TSH ± fT4, renal function, ESR/CRP, RF, anti-CCP**.

| Severity | Features |
|---|---|
| **Mild** | Sensory symptoms, abnormal sensory conduction, **no significant motor deficit** |
| **Moderate** | Sensory abnormalities + **prolonged motor distal latency** |
| **Severe** | **Absent/reduced sensory response, marked motor abnormalities, thenar weakness/wasting, axonal loss** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- NCS confirms; EMG for axonal loss; ultrasound CSA ≥ 10 mm².
- No specific blood test; look for causes.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What are the NCS findings in CTS?</summary>

**Answer:** Prolonged median sensory distal latency, reduced sensory conduction velocity across the wrist, prolonged median distal motor latency, and reduced CMAP amplitude in advanced disease; severe disease shows reduced sensory and motor responses with axonal loss.

**Why:** Slide "Nerve conduction studies".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the roles of EMG and ultrasound?</summary>

**Answer:** EMG detects denervation and chronic neurogenic change in abductor pollicis brevis, assesses severity and axonal loss and excludes cervical radiculopathy; ultrasound shows increased nerve cross-sectional area (about 10 mm² or more, normal below 9), flattening, tenosynovitis, ganglion or masses, and helps when NCS is equivocal.

**Why:** Slides "EMG" and "Ultrasound".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Classify CTS severity.</summary>

**Answer:** Mild: sensory symptoms with abnormal sensory conduction and no motor deficit. Moderate: sensory abnormalities plus prolonged motor distal latency. Severe: absent or reduced sensory response, marked motor abnormalities, thenar weakness and wasting and axonal loss.

**Why:** Slide "Severity classification".
</details>

## 6. Management, pregnancy and prevention

- **Guided by** severity, duration, function, thenar weakness, NCS/EMG and cause.
- **Conservative** (mild, recent, pregnancy, no motor deficit): **neutral-position wrist splint, especially at night**; **activity modification**; **NSAIDs** relieve pain but **do not treat compression**.
- **Steroids:** **local injection** → **rapid short- to intermediate-term relief** (symptomatic, failed conservative care, surgery delayed) but **recurrence common** and risk of **nerve/tendon injury** with repeats; **oral steroids** short courses only, **not preferred long term**.
- **Surgery:** **division of the transverse carpal ligament** (**open** or **endoscopic**). **Indications:** **severe CTS** (motor deficit, thenar wasting, severe NCS, axonal loss); **persistent symptoms** despite treatment (sleep, work, daily activities); **progressive deficit** (thumb abduction/opposition); **structural lesion** (ganglion, tumor, severe tenosynovitis, post-traumatic deformity).
- **Pregnancy:** **fluid retention → ↑ pressure → median compression**; **night splinting, activity modification, elevation**; usually **improves after delivery**; **local steroid injection** for severe persistent symptoms.
- **Prevention:** neutral wrist, less repetitive forceful movement, avoid prolonged flexion, breaks, alternate tasks; **treat diabetes, hypothyroidism, RA, obesity, renal disease**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Night splint first; steroid injection for temporary relief; surgery = divide transverse carpal ligament.
- Surgery for severe/axonal/progressive or refractory disease.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Outline the conservative treatment of CTS.</summary>

**Answer:** A neutral-position wrist splint especially at night, activity modification (less repetitive flexion, forceful gripping, vibrating tools and pressure), NSAIDs only for pain (not definitive), and local corticosteroid injection for temporary relief; oral steroids are not preferred long term.

**Why:** Slides "Conservative treatment" to "Oral corticosteroids".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. State the surgical principle and indications.</summary>

**Answer:** Division of the transverse carpal ligament (open or endoscopic) to decompress the median nerve. Indications: severe CTS with motor deficit, thenar wasting or axonal loss; persistent symptoms despite conservative treatment; progressive neurological deficit; or a structural lesion.

**Why:** Slides "Surgical treatment" and "Indications for surgery".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is CTS in pregnancy managed?</summary>

**Answer:** Fluid retention increases carpal tunnel pressure; management is night splinting, activity modification and elevation; symptoms often improve after delivery, and a local corticosteroid injection may be considered for severe persistent symptoms.

**Why:** Slide "CTS in pregnancy".

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Little finger is spared; palm sensation is preserved (palmar cutaneous branch).**
- **Phalen = 60 s flexion; Durkan = 30 s compression; Tinel = tapping.**
- **Pain from the neck into the arm = cervical radiculopathy, not CTS.**
- **NSAIDs do not treat the compression; steroid injection recurs.**
- **Thenar wasting = axonal loss = surgical indication.**
- **No specific blood test; NCS is the confirmatory investigation.**
- **Ultrasound CSA ≥ 10 mm² suspicious (normal < 9).**
