## 1. Introduction to nutrition

- ⭐ **Nutrition** = **the science of food, nutrients and other substances, their action, interaction and balance in relation to health and disease, and the process by which they are used by the body.**
- Essential for **life, health and development** at **all life stages: fetal growth, birth and breastfeeding, childhood and adolescence, adulthood and old age.**
- A balanced diet is key to **survival and physical growth, mental development, improved performance/productivity, overall health and well-being.**

### Why is nutrition important?
1. **Supports wellness** — wellness is more than the absence of disease (physical, emotional, mental, psychological, spiritual); critical components: **nutrition and physical activity**.
2. **Prevents disease** — **deficiency diseases: scurvy, goiter, rickets**; influenced by nutrition: **chronic diseases (heart disease)**; role in **osteoarthritis and osteoporosis**.
3. **Obesity is a growing problem** — **eating more calories than expended**; risk factor for **heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, some cancers**.
4. **International goal** — to promote optimal health and prevent disease.

### Healthy People 2030 overarching goals
- Healthy, thriving lives **free from preventable disease, disability, injury, premature death**; **eliminate health disparities** and improve **health literacy**; create **supportive social, physical and economic environments**; promote **healthy development and behaviors across all life stages**.

### Role of nutrition in tissues
- **Building tissues** (proteins, fats), **repairing and renewing tissues**, **maintaining body functions** (vitamins, minerals, energy).
- Health is influenced by **nutrient intake**, **nutrient utilization (absorption and use)** and **preventing deficiencies** (weakened immunity, slow growth, chronic diseases).

**Key points**
- Nutrition = science of food, nutrients and their action/balance in health and disease.
- Importance: wellness, disease prevention (scurvy, goiter, rickets), obesity control, international goal.
- Roles: building, repairing, maintaining; intake, utilization, preventing deficiency.

## 3. Terms, essential nutrients and classifications

- **Nutrients** = the nourishing chemical substances in food that provide **energy and promote growth and maintenance**.
- ⭐ **Essential nutrients** = **cannot be made by the body in sufficient quantity**, so must come from food.

### Criteria for an essential nutrient
1. The body **cannot synthesize it** (or not enough).
2. It must be **obtained from the diet**.
3. **Deficiency causes specific signs/impaired function.**
4. **Providing it prevents or corrects the deficiency.**
- Examples: **essential amino acids, essential fatty acids, vitamins, minerals, water.**

### The six types of nutrients (> 45 nutrients)
- **Carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water.**

### Organic vs inorganic
| | **Organic** (contain carbon, complex) | **Inorganic** (no carbon, simple) |
|---|---|---|
| Members | **carbohydrates** (energy), **fats** (long-term energy, help absorb vitamins), **proteins** (build and repair), **vitamins** (support body functions) | **minerals**, **water** (hydration) |

### By function
- ⭐ **Fuel nutrients (energy):** **carbohydrates, fats, proteins.**
- ⭐ **Regulatory nutrients (no calories):** **vitamins, minerals, water.**

### Food classification by function (Go, Grow, Glow)
| Foods | Role | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Protective (Glow)** | **prevent disease; rich in vitamins and minerals** | **vegetables, fruits** |
| ⭐ **Building (Grow)** | **growth and cell repair** | **meat, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes (beans, lentils, chickpeas, kidney beans)** |
| ⭐ **Energy (Go)** | **energy for activity, movement, warmth, functions** | **grains, tubers (potatoes, sweet potatoes), sugars, animal fats, vegetable oils** |

### By amount
- ⭐ **Macronutrients** (large amounts daily): **carbohydrates, fats, proteins** (water is also needed in large amounts but gives **no energy**).
- ⭐ **Micronutrients** (small amounts daily): **vitamins and minerals**.
- Also in the figure: **safe (boiled/treated) water and iodized salt**.

### Water
- **WHO: no single universal value**; generally **2–3 liters/day for adults**, adjusted for **hot environments, exercise, pregnancy/lactation, illness (fever, diarrhea)**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Six nutrients: carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water. Essential = body can't make enough.
- Organic (CHO, fat, protein, vitamins) vs inorganic (minerals, water). Fuel (CHO, fat, protein) vs regulatory (vitamins, minerals, water).
- Protective (fruit/vegetables), building (meat, dairy, legumes), energy (grains, tubers, fats) foods.
- Macronutrients: CHO, fat, protein; micronutrients: vitamins and minerals. Water 2–3 L/day.

</div>

## 4. Dietary reference values

- **Characteristics of a nutritious food/diet:** **adequate, balanced, moderate, varied.**

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **EAR (Estimated Average Requirement)** | average daily intake estimated to meet the requirements of **50 % of healthy individuals** in a life-stage/gender group |
| ⭐ **RDA (Recommended Dietary Allowance)** | daily amount adequate for **almost 98 % of healthy people**; **calculated from the EAR**; **17 categories by age and sex**; does **not** cover needs altered by **disease, chronic drug use**; **intake below 70 % of RDA** = risk of clinical deficiency |
| ⭐ **AI (Adequate Intake)** | **observational standard used when data are insufficient to set an RDA (no EAR)**; set using healthy groups; expected to meet or exceed requirements of **all healthy members** |

- **Logic:** **enough evidence → EAR → RDA; not enough evidence → AI.**
- RDA table (Food and Nutrition Board, USA) lists **Ca, Fe, Mg, Zn** by group: infants 0–½ y (6 kg): **Ca 360, Fe 10, Mg 60, Zn 3 mg**; infants ½–1 y (9 kg): **Ca 540, Fe 15, Mg 70, Zn 5 mg**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Nutritious food: adequate, balanced, moderate, varied.
- EAR = 50 %; RDA = ~98 % (from EAR); AI = when no EAR; RDA < 70 % = risk of deficiency.
- RDA doesn't account for disease or chronic drug use; 17 age/sex groups.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define EAR, RDA and AI.</summary>

**Answer:** EAR: average daily intake meeting the requirements of 50 % of healthy individuals of a life-stage and gender. RDA: daily amount adequate for almost 98 % of healthy people, derived from the EAR. AI: an observational standard used when evidence is insufficient to set an EAR/RDA, expected to meet or exceed the needs of all healthy members.

**Why:** Dietary reference values.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. When is AI used instead of RDA?</summary>

**Answer:** When there is not enough scientific evidence to establish an EAR (and hence an RDA); with enough evidence, EAR is established and RDA calculated from it.

**Why:** Decision logic.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the limitations of the RDA?</summary>

**Answer:** It does not cover needs altered by disease states, chronic use of certain drugs or other factors requiring individual attention; intake below 70 % of the RDA is often considered the minimum level below which clinical deficiency is a risk.

**Why:** RDA is for healthy people.
</details>

## 5. Food pyramid and serving sizes

### Food pyramid
- A **triangular guide** categorizing foods by health benefit; **higher = less healthy, eat in moderation**; guides **portion sizes, balance, avoiding fatty foods, preventing obesity**.
- **First pyramid published in Sweden in 1974.**
- **Evolution:** **USDA Food Guide Pyramid (1992)** — base **6–11 grain servings**, then fruits/vegetables, dairy and meat, top **fats/sweets (use sparingly)**; **MyPyramid (2005)** — vertical colored bands, a figure on steps (activity); **MyPlate (2011)** — a plate (fruits, vegetables, grains, protein + dairy "cup"); **2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines: "Eat More Real Food"** — skip ultra-processed foods, prefer protein, dairy, healthy fats, vegetables/fruits, whole grains.
- **Pyramid from top to bottom:** **fats → dairy → meat → fruits and vegetables → grains.**

### Serving size
- The suggested amount of food/beverage per meal or snack; regulates calories and encourages variety.

| Group | Servings/day | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Milk** | **2–3** | **calcium, protein, riboflavin**; 1 cup milk/yogurt; cheese **1 oz = 28 g** (≈ four dice) |
| ⭐ **Meat** | **2–3** | **protein, fat, iron, minerals**; **3 oz ≈ palm/deck of cards**; **6–9 oz/day** protein equivalents |
| ⭐ **Bread and cereal** | **6–11** | **carbohydrate, B vitamins, iron**; ½ cup cooked rice ≈ tennis ball; slice of bread; 1 cup pasta/cereal ≈ fist |
| ⭐ **Vegetables** | **3–5** | **vitamin C, precursors of vitamin A**; ½ cup cooked, 1 cup raw leafy |
| ⭐ **Fruit** | **2–4** | one medium fruit ≈ tennis ball |

- **Hand guide:** **hand = breads; palm = meats; fist = vegetables, rice, pasta, fruits; fingertip = fats (butter).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pyramid: first in Sweden 1974; USDA 1992 (6–11 grains), MyPyramid 2005, MyPlate 2011, 2025–2030 "Eat More Real Food".
- Servings/day: milk 2–3, meat 2–3, bread/cereal 6–11, vegetables 3–5, fruit 2–4.
- Portion tricks: palm = meat, fist = veg/rice/pasta/fruit, fingertip = fat; cheese 1 oz = 28 g.

</div>

## 6. Calculating energy from food

- ⭐ **Carbohydrate = 4 kcal/g; Protein = 4 kcal/g; Fat = 9 kcal/g** → multiply grams × kcal/g and **add**.

### Worked example (1 slice of bread + 1 tbsp peanut butter)
| Nutrient | Calculation | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | 16 g × 4 | **64 kcal** |
| Protein | 7 g × 4 | **28 kcal** |
| Fat | 9 g × 9 | **81 kcal** |
| **Total** | | **173 kcal** |

- **% calories from fat = 81 ÷ 173 = 0.468 ≈ 0.47 → 47 %.**

### Homework
- **Bean burrito with cheese: 55 g carbohydrate, 15 g protein, 12 g fat** → **220 + 60 + 108 = 388 kcal**; carbohydrate ≈ **57 %**, protein ≈ **15 %**, fat ≈ **28 %**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- CHO 4, protein 4, fat 9 kcal/g; add them up.
- Example: 16 g CHO + 7 g protein + 9 g fat = 173 kcal; fat = 47 %.
- Burrito (55/15/12 g) = 388 kcal (CHO 57 %, protein 15 %, fat 28 %).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do you calculate the energy available from a food?</summary>

**Answer:** Multiply the grams of carbohydrate by 4 kcal/g, protein by 4 kcal/g and fat by 9 kcal/g, then add the results.

**Why:** Atwater values.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Calculate the energy and % fat of a slice of bread with peanut butter (16 g CHO, 7 g protein, 9 g fat).</summary>

**Answer:** 16 × 4 = 64 kcal; 7 × 4 = 28 kcal; 9 × 9 = 81 kcal; total 173 kcal. Fat percentage = 81/173 = 0.47 → 47 %.

**Why:** Worked example.
</details>
