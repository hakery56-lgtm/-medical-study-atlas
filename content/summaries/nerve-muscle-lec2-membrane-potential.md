## 1. Resting membrane potential

- **All living cells** have a **potential difference across the plasma membrane**; **inside negative** relative to outside = **RMP**, from **different distribution of ions**.
- **RMP values:** **nerve −70 mV**; **skeletal muscle −90 mV**; **cardiac muscle −85 mV**; **smooth muscle ≈ −50 mV (variable)**.
- **Causes:** **(1) Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump** (3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in); **(2) fast Na⁺ channels closed at rest**; **(3) leaky channels — continuous passive K⁺ diffusion outward = MAJOR factor**; **(4) Cl⁻ stays inside**; **(5) large anionic proteins and phosphates cannot leave.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Nerve −70; skeletal −90; cardiac −85; smooth ≈ −50 mV.
- K⁺ leak is the major cause.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the resting membrane potential and its values?</summary>

**Answer:** The negative potential inside the membrane relative to the outside: nerve −70 mV, skeletal muscle −90 mV, cardiac muscle −85 mV and smooth muscle about −50 mV.

**Why:** Slide 1.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the causes of RMP.</summary>

**Answer:** Na⁺/K⁺ pump, closed fast Na⁺ channels, passive K⁺ outward diffusion (major), chloride staying inside, and large anionic proteins and phosphates trapped inside.

**Why:** Slide 2.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the Na⁺/K⁺ pump.</summary>

**Answer:** It extrudes three sodium ions and brings in two potassium ions, leaving more positive ions outside.

**Why:** Slide 2.
</details>

## 2. Excitable cells and stimuli

- **Nerve and muscle** = **excitable cells**; a **sufficient stimulus** (electrical, chemical, physical) **reverses the negativity** → **action potential (AP)**.
- **Response:** **nerve → impulse transmission; muscle → contraction.**
- **Subthreshold stimuli:** **local responses only** — graded with stimulus, subside, **stay local, no propagation**.
- **Threshold stimulus:** raises potential **15 mV above RMP (−70 → −55 mV)**; AP then runs to completion = **all-or-none rule**; **threshold (firing) potential**.
- **Supramaximal stimuli:** same effect, **no change in AP shape**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Threshold = −55 mV (15 mV above RMP).
- All-or-none; supramaximal changes nothing.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the action potential.</summary>

**Answer:** The reversal of membrane polarity after a sufficient stimulus so that the inside becomes positive; in a nerve it is transmitted, in a muscle it causes contraction.

**Why:** Slides 2–3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate local responses from action potentials.</summary>

**Answer:** Local responses follow subthreshold stimuli, vary with strength, subside and do not propagate; action potentials follow threshold stimuli, are all-or-none and propagate.

**Why:** Slide 3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. State the all-or-none rule.</summary>

**Answer:** Once threshold (15 mV above RMP) is reached the action potential phases occur completely and do not stop until the cycle ends.

**Why:** Slide 3.
</details>

## 3. Phases of the neuronal action potential

| Phase | Event | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Depolarization** | **Rises toward 0 and overshoots to ≈ +35 mV** | **All fast Na⁺ channels open → Na⁺ inrush** |
| **2. Repolarization** | **Returns toward negativity** | **Fast Na⁺ channel inactivation + Na⁺ pumping + K⁺ diffusion out** |
| **3. Hyperpolarization** | **More negative than rest (after-hyperpolarization)** | **K⁺ remains elevated 5–15 ms; approaches K⁺ equilibrium potential (−72 mV)** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Depolarization: Na⁺ in; repolarization: K⁺ out.
- Overshoot ≈ +35 mV.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the three phases.</summary>

**Answer:** Depolarization to about +35 mV by Na⁺ inrush, repolarization by Na⁺ channel inactivation and K⁺ efflux, and hyperpolarization toward −72 mV while K⁺ permeability stays raised.

**Why:** Slides 3–4.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What causes depolarization?</summary>

**Answer:** Opening of all fast Na⁺ channels with a large inrush of sodium.

**Why:** Slide 3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why does hyperpolarization occur?</summary>

**Answer:** Potassium permeability stays elevated for 5–15 ms after RMP is reached, driving the potential toward the K⁺ equilibrium potential (−72 mV).

**Why:** Slides 3–4.
</details>

## 4. Refractory periods

- **Absolute:** from depolarization until **≈ ⅔ of repolarization**; **no new AP however large the stimulus**, because **inactivated Na⁺ channels cannot be reactivated**.
- **Relative:** from **⅔ of repolarization to the end of hyperpolarization**; **a stronger stimulus** can give a **new but weaker AP** because **some Na⁺ channels can be activated**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Absolute: no AP at all.
- Relative: stronger stimulus → weaker AP.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the absolute refractory period.</summary>

**Answer:** The time from depolarization to about two-thirds of repolarization when no stimulus can start a new AP because inactivated Na⁺ channels cannot yet be reactivated.

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Define the relative refractory period.</summary>

**Answer:** From two-thirds of repolarization to the end of hyperpolarization; a stronger stimulus can induce a new, weaker AP.

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is the second AP weaker?</summary>

**Answer:** Only some inactivated Na⁺ channels have recovered and can be activated.

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>
