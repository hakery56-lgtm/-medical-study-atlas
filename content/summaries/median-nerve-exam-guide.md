## 1. Clinical relevance and origin

- ⭐ **Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS):** **median nerve compressed between the flexor retinaculum and carpal bones; the most common entrapment neuropathy;** **sensory symptoms and pain in the hands, occasionally radiating up the arm, typically at night; common in pregnancy.**
- **Origin:** **brachial plexus; roots C5-T1;** formed by **two roots anterior or lateral to the axillary artery: lateral root (lateral cord, C5-C7) + medial root (medial cord, C8-T1).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- CTS = commonest entrapment neuropathy, worse at night.
- Median nerve: C5-T1, lateral + medial cord roots.

</div>

## 2. Course and branches

| Region | Course |
|---|---|
| **Axilla** | **Formed anterior or lateral to the axillary artery** |
| **Arm** | **With the brachial artery, crossing it lateral → medial; NO muscular branches** |
| **Cubital fossa** | **Medial to the brachial artery, deep to the bicipital aponeurosis** |
| **Forearm** | **Between the two heads of pronator teres, deep to the fibrous arch of FDS** |
| **Wrist** | **Through the carpal tunnel, deep to the flexor retinaculum** |
| **Hand** | **Recurrent motor branch + palmar digital branches** |

- **Branches:** **forearm: muscular branches, anterior interosseous nerve (AIN), palmar cutaneous branch; hand: recurrent motor branch, common and proper palmar digital nerves.**
- ⭐ **Palmar cutaneous branch** arises in the distal forearm and passes **superficial to the retinaculum → spared in CTS.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- No muscular branches in the arm.
- Palmar cutaneous branch is spared in CTS.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the course of the median nerve.</summary>

**Answer:** Axilla (anterior or lateral to the axillary artery), arm (with the brachial artery, crossing lateral to medial), cubital fossa (medial to the artery, deep to the bicipital aponeurosis), forearm (between pronator teres heads, deep to the FDS arch), carpal tunnel (deep to the retinaculum) and hand.

**Why:** Slide "Course and relations".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the branches of the median nerve.</summary>

**Answer:** None in the arm; in the forearm muscular branches, the AIN and the palmar cutaneous branch; in the hand the recurrent motor branch and palmar digital nerves.

**Why:** Slide "Branches and distribution".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is the palmar cutaneous branch spared in CTS?</summary>

**Answer:** It arises in the distal forearm and passes superficial to the flexor retinaculum, outside the carpal tunnel.

**Why:** Slide "Branches and distribution".
</details>

## 3. Motor and sensory supply

| Forearm muscle | Action |
|---|---|
| **Pronator teres** | **Pronation** |
| **Flexor carpi radialis** | **Wrist flexion, radial deviation** |
| **Palmaris longus** | **Tenses palmar aponeurosis** |
| **Flexor digitorum superficialis** | **PIP flexion, digits 2-5** |
| **Flexor pollicis longus (AIN)** | **Thumb IP flexion** |
| **Pronator quadratus (AIN)** | **Pronation** |
| **Lateral half of FDP (AIN)** | **DIP flexion, index and middle** |

- ⭐ **Hand: LOAF** = **L**ateral two **l**umbricals, **O**pponens pollicis, **A**bductor pollicis brevis, **F**lexor pollicis brevis (**superficial head only**) → **opposition and precision grip.**
- **Exceptions (ulnar):** **FCU, medial half of FDP, deep head of flexor pollicis brevis.**
- ⭐ **Sensory:** **palmar surface of the lateral 3½ digits** (thumb, index, middle, lateral half of ring); **distal dorsal surfaces and nail beds** of the same; **lateral palm and thenar eminence via the palmar cutaneous branch.** **Autonomous zone: palmar tip of the index finger.** **Considerable variation** exists between median and ulnar territories.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- LOAF muscles; FCU and medial FDP are ulnar.
- Autonomous zone = index fingertip.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What are the LOAF muscles?</summary>

**Answer:** Lateral two lumbricals, opponens pollicis, abductor pollicis brevis and flexor pollicis brevis (superficial head only); they are essential for thumb opposition and precision grip.

**Why:** Slide "Motor supply".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which forearm muscles are supplied by the AIN and which are ulnar exceptions?</summary>

**Answer:** AIN: flexor pollicis longus, pronator quadratus and the lateral half of FDP; ulnar exceptions: FCU and the medial half of FDP.

**Why:** Slide "Motor supply".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the sensory supply and autonomous zone.</summary>

**Answer:** Palmar lateral 3½ digits, distal dorsal surfaces and nail beds of the same digits, and the lateral palm and thenar eminence (palmar cutaneous branch); the autonomous zone is the palmar tip of the index finger.

**Why:** Slide "Sensory supply".
</details>

## 4. Look, feel and move

- **Before starting:** **wash hands, introduce, explain, permission; seat patient; expose both arms shoulder to fingertips; ask about pain, tingling, weakness, hand dominance; equipment: neurotip or pin, cotton wool, reflex hammer; always compare sides.**
- ⭐ **Look:** **thenar wasting (flat thenar eminence, chronic injury); ape thumb deformity (thumb in plane of palm: loss of opposition and abduction); hand of benediction (attempting a fist, proximal lesion); scars; skin changes from sensory loss.**
- **Feel:** **tenderness (forearm, wrist, thenar eminence); mass or swelling (carpal tunnel, wrist, palm); temperature (compare).**
- ⭐ **Move:** **thumb abduction (abductor pollicis brevis):** palm up on a flat surface, thumb moved vertically against resistance; **opposition (opponens pollicis):** touch thumb and ring finger together against pull; **weak distal flexion of thumb and index = proximal lesion (anterior interosseous branch);** **abnormal OK sign** (pinch with straight thumb and index) = **AIN injury.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Ape thumb = loss of opposition and abduction; benediction hand = proximal lesion.
- APB = abduction; opponens = opposition; abnormal OK sign = AIN.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What do you look for on inspection?</summary>

**Answer:** Thenar wasting, ape thumb deformity, hand of benediction on making a fist in proximal lesions, scars or previous surgery and skin changes from sensory loss.

**Why:** Slide "Look".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How do you test thumb abduction and opposition?</summary>

**Answer:** Abduction: hand palm up on a flat surface, thumb moved vertically against resistance (abductor pollicis brevis); opposition: patient touches thumb and ring finger together while you try to pull them apart (opponens pollicis).

**Why:** Slides "Move".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What does the OK sign test?</summary>

**Answer:** The anterior interosseous nerve; an abnormal OK sign (pinch with straight thumb and index) suggests AIN injury, as does weak distal flexion of the thumb and index.

**Why:** Slide "Opposition and anterior interosseous nerve".
</details>

## 5. Sensation and special tests

- **Sensation:** **demonstrate on the forehead (eyes open); eyes closed: light touch (cotton wool) then pinprick; compare sides; test index palmar tip (autonomous zone), thumb, palm, middle finger, lateral half of ring finger.**

| Test | Method | Positive |
|---|---|---|
| **Phalen's** | **Arms at shoulder level, dorsa of hands together in midline, forced wrist flexion 30-60 s** | **Tingling/numbness in median distribution** |
| **Reverse Phalen's (prayer sign)** | **Palms pressed together, wrists extended** | **Same** |
| **Tinel's** | **Tap distal wrist crease with tendon hammer** | **Tingling in median territory** |
| **Durkan's (carpal compression)** | **Direct pressure over the carpal tunnel up to 30 s** | **Numbness/tingling in median distribution** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Phalen = flexion; reverse Phalen = extension (prayer); Tinel = tap; Durkan = pressure.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe how to test median nerve sensation.</summary>

**Answer:** Demonstrate the stimulus on the forehead, then with eyes closed test light touch and pinprick on the index palmar tip (autonomous zone), thumb, palm, middle finger and lateral half of the ring finger, comparing sides.

**Why:** Slide "Sensation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare Phalen's and reverse Phalen's tests.</summary>

**Answer:** Phalen's: dorsa of hands together with forced wrist flexion for 30-60 seconds; reverse Phalen's (prayer sign): palms pressed together with wrists extended; both positive with tingling and numbness in the median distribution.

**Why:** Slides "Phalen's test" and "Reverse Phalen's test".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe Tinel's sign and Durkan's test.</summary>

**Answer:** Tinel: tapping the distal wrist crease with a tendon hammer produces tingling in the median territory; Durkan: direct pressure over the carpal tunnel for up to 30 seconds produces numbness or tingling in the median distribution.

**Why:** Slide "Tinel and Durkan".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **No muscular branches in the arm.**
- **Palmar cutaneous branch is spared in CTS (superficial to the retinaculum).**
- **LOAF: flexor pollicis brevis median only by its superficial head.**
- **FCU and medial FDP are ulnar.**
- **Weak distal thumb and index flexion = proximal lesion (AIN).**
- **Phalen = flexion 30-60 s; reverse Phalen = prayer, extension; Durkan = pressure up to 30 s.**
