## 1. Overview of the cycle

- ⭐ **Citric acid cycle** = a series of **mitochondrial reactions** that **catabolize acetyl-CoA to CO2 and H2O** with generation of **ATP.**
- **Eight reactions:** citrate → isocitrate → α-ketoglutarate → succinyl-CoA → succinate → fumarate → malate → oxaloacetate (regenerated).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Mitochondrial pathway oxidizing acetyl-CoA.
- Eight reactions; oxaloacetate is regenerated.

</div>

## 2. Reactions 1-4: from acetyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA

| # | Reaction | Enzyme | Products |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Acetyl-CoA + **oxaloacetate** → **citrate** | **Citrate synthase** | — |
| **2** | Citrate → **isocitrate** | **Aconitase** (via cis-aconitate, Fe2+) | — |
| **3** | Isocitrate → oxalosuccinate → **α-ketoglutarate** | **Isocitrate dehydrogenase** | **NADH + CO2** |
| **4** | α-Ketoglutarate → **succinyl-CoA** | **α-Ketoglutarate dehydrogenase complex** | **2nd NADH + 2nd CO2** |

- Succinyl-CoA is a **high-energy compound**; reaction 4 is **physiologically irreversible.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Two decarboxylations: steps 3 and 4.
- Step 4 is irreversible and forms succinyl-CoA.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe reactions 1 and 2.</summary>

**Answer:** Citrate synthase condenses acetyl-CoA with oxaloacetate to give citrate, and aconitase converts citrate to isocitrate.

**Why:** Slide 5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What happens at isocitrate dehydrogenase?</summary>

**Answer:** Isocitrate is dehydrogenated to oxalosuccinate and decarboxylated to α-ketoglutarate, forming the first NADH and CO2.

**Why:** Slides 5-6.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is special about the α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase step?</summary>

**Answer:** A multi-enzyme complex oxidatively decarboxylates α-ketoglutarate to the high-energy succinyl-CoA, producing the second NADH and CO2; the reaction is physiologically irreversible.

**Why:** Slide 7.
</details>

## 3. Reactions 5-8: back to oxaloacetate

| # | Reaction | Enzyme | Products |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5** | Succinyl-CoA → **succinate** | **Succinate thiokinase** | **GDP → GTP** (substrate-level phosphorylation), GTP → ATP |
| **6** | Succinate → **fumarate** | **Succinate dehydrogenase** | **FADH2** |
| **7** | Fumarate + H2O → **malate** | **Fumarase** | — |
| **8** | Malate → **oxaloacetate** | **Malate dehydrogenase** (NAD+) | **3rd NADH** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Substrate-level phosphorylation at succinate thiokinase.
- FADH2 at succinate dehydrogenase; third NADH at malate dehydrogenase.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe reaction 5.</summary>

**Answer:** Succinate thiokinase converts succinyl-CoA to succinate coupled with phosphorylation of GDP to GTP (substrate-level phosphorylation); the GTP is converted to ATP.

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Where are FADH2 and the third NADH produced?</summary>

**Answer:** FADH2 at succinate dehydrogenase (succinate to fumarate) and the third NADH at malate dehydrogenase (malate to oxaloacetate).

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What does fumarase do?</summary>

**Answer:** It adds water to fumarate to give malate.

**Why:** Slide 10.
</details>

## 4. Significance and amphibolic role

- ⭐ **Primary function:** provide **energy as ATP.** **Final common pathway** for oxidation of **carbohydrates, lipids and proteins.** **Amphibolic.**
- **Catabolic role:** oxidizes **acetyl-CoA** (from carbohydrates, fats, amino acids) to **CO2**, generating **NADH, FADH2, GTP/ATP** for the **electron transport chain.**
- **Anabolic role:** **citrate → cytosol → fatty acids and sterols**; **α-ketoglutarate → glutamate, glutamine, proline, arginine.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Amphibolic = catabolic and anabolic.
- Citrate feeds lipid synthesis; α-ketoglutarate feeds amino acids.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Why is the cycle called amphibolic?</summary>

**Answer:** It has a catabolic role (oxidizing acetyl-CoA to CO2) and an anabolic role (supplying precursors for biosynthesis).

**Why:** Slides 12-13.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give the anabolic roles.</summary>

**Answer:** Citrate is exported to the cytosol for fatty acid and sterol synthesis; α-ketoglutarate gives glutamate, glutamine, proline and arginine.

**Why:** Slide 13.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is the cycle the final common pathway?</summary>

**Answer:** Carbohydrates, lipids and proteins are all oxidized through acetyl-CoA in this cycle.

**Why:** Slides 12-13.
</details>

## 5. Energetics

- Each turn gives **3 NADH + 1 FADH2 + 1 GTP/ATP.**
- **3 NADH × 2.5 = 7.5 ATP; FADH2 = 1.5 ATP; substrate-level ATP = 1** → **10 ATP per acetyl-CoA.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 7.5 + 1.5 + 1 = 10 ATP per acetyl-CoA.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Calculate the ATP yield per acetyl-CoA.</summary>

**Answer:** 3 NADH give 7.5 ATP, FADH2 gives 1.5 ATP and substrate-level phosphorylation gives 1 ATP: a total of 10 ATP.

**Why:** Slide 14.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What does one turn produce?</summary>

**Answer:** Three NADH, one FADH2 and one GTP/ATP (and two CO2).

**Why:** Slides 2 and 14.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Where is ATP generated at substrate level?</summary>

**Answer:** In the conversion of succinyl-CoA to succinate.

**Why:** Slide 14.
</details>

## 6. Regulation

- ⭐ Regulated at **three steps:** **citrate synthase, isocitrate dehydrogenase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase.**
- Activity depends on the **energy status:** **excess ATP, NADH and succinyl-CoA (high energy) inhibit**; **high ADP (low energy) stimulates.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Three regulated enzymes; fumarase is NOT one of them.
- ATP/NADH inhibit; ADP stimulates.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which enzymes regulate the cycle?</summary>

**Answer:** Citrate synthase, isocitrate dehydrogenase and α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase.

**Why:** Slide 15.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What inhibits and what stimulates the cycle?</summary>

**Answer:** Excess ATP, NADH and succinyl-CoA (high energy status) inhibit it; high ADP (low energy status) stimulates it.

**Why:** Slide 16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which is not a rate-controlling step?</summary>

**Answer:** Fumarase.

**Why:** Slide 9 (self-assessment).
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **CO2 is released at the isocitrate dehydrogenase and α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase steps only.**
- **GTP, not ATP, is formed directly at succinate thiokinase.**
- **FADH2 (1.5 ATP) comes from succinate dehydrogenase; NADH (2.5 ATP) elsewhere.**
- **Fumarase is not a regulated step.**
- **RBC glycolysis ends in lactate.**
