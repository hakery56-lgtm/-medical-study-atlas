## 1. What is first aid? Kit and the safe approach

- ⭐ **First aid** = help given to a sick or injured person **until full medical treatment is available** (initial care for an illness or injury).
- **Three aims (3 P's):** **Preserve life**, **Prevent worsening** of the condition, **Promote recovery**.
- **First step in any emergency: call for help (911).**
- **First aid kit** (at home, in the car, at work): bandages, roller bandages and tape; sterile gauze; antiseptic wipes and swabs; absorbent compresses; antibiotic cream and burn ointment; **mask for breathing (rescue breathing / CPR)**; chemical cold pack, eye shield and eyewash; first aid guide with local phone numbers.

### The safe approach (7 steps)
| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Remove the danger** - make the scene safe |
| 2 | **Call for help** - 911 |
| 3 | **Wash hands, wear gloves** - use PPE if available |
| 4 | **Check the response** - gently tap the shoulder; look at signs and symptoms |
| 5 | **Airway** - open? any object inside? |
| 6 | **Breathing** - chest rising and falling? |
| 7 | **Circulation** - pulse, any sign of life |

Then: **identify the problem, give first aid, reassess, arrange transfer.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- First aid = initial care until medical treatment; aims: preserve life, prevent worsening, promote recovery.
- Scene safe → call 911 → gloves/PPE → response → A, B, C.
- Keep a kit at home, in the car and at work; it includes a mask for rescue breathing/CPR.

</div>

## 2. Nose bleeding (epistaxis)

- **Epistaxis** is common: the **tender blood vessels of the nose break**. **Causes:** change of season, dryness, scratching, some medicines and injuries. **People on blood thinners may bleed more.** Usually only annoying, not a true medical problem.
- ⭐ **Steps:** (1) scene safe, gloves on; (2) **sit the patient FORWARD, not back**; (3) **pinch both nostrils just below the bony part**; (4) **press 10-15 minutes**, then check; (5) still bleeding → **press another 10 minutes**; (6) catch the blood with a clean tissue or damp cloth. Then **reassess**; if not well, arrange medical care.
- **Call 911 if:** more blood than expected; **lasts > 30 minutes**; faint or lightheaded; after a fall or injury (broken nose?); trouble breathing or severe distress.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sit forward, pinch below the bony part for 10-15 min (+10 if needed).
- Do not tilt the head back; catch the blood.
- Call 911 if > 30 min, faintness, after injury or breathing trouble.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the first aid of a nosebleed.</summary>

**Answer:** Make the scene safe and put on gloves; sit the patient forward; pinch both nostrils below the bony part for 10-15 minutes; press another 10 minutes if still bleeding; catch the blood with a tissue; reassess and arrange care if it continues.

**Why:** Slide "Nose Bleeding: First Aid Steps".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. When is a nosebleed an emergency?</summary>

**Answer:** More blood than expected, lasting longer than 30 minutes, with faintness or lightheadedness, after a fall or injury, or with trouble breathing or severe distress.

**Why:** Slide "When to Call 911".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which position is used for a nosebleed, and what is done with the blood?</summary>

**Answer:** The patient sits forward, not back, and the blood is caught with a clean tissue or damp cloth while the nostrils are pinched.

**Why:** Slide "Nose Bleeding: First Aid Steps".
</details>

## 3. Burns

- **Burn** = traumatic injury to the skin (sometimes deeper) caused by **extreme heat, chemicals, radiation or electricity**.

| Degree | Name | Layers |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | Superficial | **Epidermis only** |
| **Second** | Partial-thickness | **Epidermis + dermis** (vessels, nerves, glands) |
| **Third** | Full-thickness | Both layers; may reach **fat, muscle or bone** |

- ⭐ **Steps:** stop the burning (remove clothing; roll on the ground or use a blanket); **cool running water (cool, not cold)**; **chemical burn → large volumes of water**; cover with a **sterile non-adhesive bandage** or clean cloth; transfer wrapped in a clean sheet.
- **Don't:** ice; butter, oil, lotions; break blisters.
- **By degree:** **1st** - cool until pain eases, cover, petroleum-based ointment 2-3×/day; **2nd** - cool 10-15 min, no ice/butter/ointment, do not break blisters; **3rd** - **call 911 immediately**, cover loosely with a clean sheet, transfer to hospital.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Degrees: 1st = epidermis; 2nd = epidermis + dermis; 3rd = both layers ± fat, muscle, bone.
- Cool running water (not ice); chemical burn = copious water.
- Never ice, butter, oil, lotions; never break blisters.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Classify burns by depth.</summary>

**Answer:** First degree (superficial) involves the epidermis only; second degree (partial-thickness) involves the epidermis and dermis; third degree (full-thickness) involves both layers and may reach fat, muscle or bone.

**Why:** Slide "Burns".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the first aid steps for burns and what to avoid.</summary>

**Answer:** Stop the burning, cool with running water (cool, not cold), wash chemicals off with large volumes of water, cover with a sterile non-adhesive bandage or clean cloth, and transfer. Do not use ice, butter, oil or lotions and do not break blisters.

**Why:** Slide "Burns: First Aid Steps".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How does the management differ between the three degrees?</summary>

**Answer:** First degree: cool until pain eases, cover, petroleum ointment 2-3 times a day. Second degree: cool 10-15 minutes, no ice, butter or ointments, keep blisters intact. Third degree: call 911, cover loosely with a clean sheet and transfer to hospital.

**Why:** Slide "Burns by Degree".
</details>

## 4. Convulsion

- **Convulsion** = rapid, involuntary muscle contractions with uncontrollable shaking. Common in **epilepsy**; also **fever, infection, brain trauma, low blood sugar, head injury, heart disease, toxins, heat illness**.
- The person may **fall, bite the tongue, lose bowel or bladder control**; often followed by a short period of unresponsiveness.
- ⭐ **Steps:** place on the **floor**; clear hard or sharp objects; **soft flat item under the head**, remove glasses; **loosen the neck** (tie, necklace); **turn on the side** (easier breathing, no choking on vomit); **call 911 if > 5 minutes** and stay until help arrives.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Protect from injury: floor, clear the area, soft item under the head.
- Turn on the side to protect the airway.
- Call 911 if > 5 minutes; stay with the person.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define convulsion and list its causes.</summary>

**Answer:** Rapid involuntary muscle contractions with uncontrollable shaking. Causes: epilepsy, fever, infection, brain trauma, low blood sugar, head injury, heart disease, toxins and heat illness.

**Why:** Slide "3) Convulsion".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give the first aid steps for a convulsion.</summary>

**Answer:** Place on the floor, clear the area, put a soft flat item under the head and remove glasses, loosen the neck, turn on the side, and call 911 if it lasts more than 5 minutes while staying until help arrives.

**Why:** Slide "Convulsion: First Aid Steps".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is the person turned onto the side?</summary>

**Answer:** It makes breathing easier and prevents choking on vomit.

**Why:** Slide "Convulsion: First Aid Steps".
</details>

## 5. Sprains, fractures and amputation

- **Sprain** = joint stretched beyond normal: pain, swelling, bruising. Common in sport, the elderly and the infirm. ⭐ **A fracture cannot be ruled out without an X-ray.**
- **Steps:** scene safe/PPE; **gauze on any open wound**; **ice pack up to 20 minutes**; rest it, see a doctor. **Call 911 if:** open wound over a joint, bent limb, or obvious dislocation.
- **Open fracture** (bone through the skin): **don't push it back or straighten the limb**; **splint as found** (magazine, wood, rolled towel); pad, then secure with tape or gauze on both sides; **not too tight - fingers/toes warm and pink**; seek immediate care.
- **Amputation - the person:** scene safe/kit/PPE; call 911; **direct pressure with gauze**; stay with the person. **The part:** rinse gently with clean water; gauze, then **sealed bag**; place the bag in **ice**; write the name on it; send with the patient (**surgeons may reattach it**).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Fracture can't be excluded without an X-ray; ice up to 20 min.
- Open fracture: do not push back or straighten; splint as found, pad, not too tight.
- Amputated part: rinse, gauze, sealed bag, on ice, label, send with patient.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do you manage an open fracture?</summary>

**Answer:** Do not push the bone back or straighten the limb; splint it as found with a magazine, wood or rolled towel; pad, then secure with tape or gauze on both sides without making it too tight (fingers or toes warm and pink); then seek immediate medical care.

**Why:** Slide "Open Fractures and Splinting".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. When do you call 911 for a sprain or fracture?</summary>

**Answer:** If there is an open wound over a joint, a bent limb or an obvious dislocation.

**Why:** Slide "Sprains and Fractures".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do you care for an amputated part?</summary>

**Answer:** Rinse gently with clean water, wrap in gauze and place in a sealed bag, put the bag in ice, write the name on it and send it with the patient, because surgeons may be able to reattach it.

**Why:** Slide "Amputation".
</details>

## 6. Choking

- **Choking** = food or an object stuck in the airway; stops oxygen reaching the lungs and brain. **> 4 minutes** without oxygen to the brain can cause **brain damage or death**.
- **Signs:** clutching the chest or neck; can't speak, breathe or cough.
- ⭐ **Steps:** call 911 and get consent; **stand behind and lean the person forward**; **5 back blows** (heel of the hand, between the shoulder blades); arms around the waist, **fist just above the navel**; **5 upward abdominal thrusts** (grasp the fist); **repeat until the object comes out**.
- **Infant:** face-down on your forearm, **5 back blows** with the heel of the hand.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Signs: clutching chest/neck, cannot speak, breathe or cough.
- Adult: 5 back blows + 5 upward abdominal thrusts, repeat.
- Infant: face-down on the forearm, 5 back blows.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Why is choking an emergency?</summary>

**Answer:** The airway is blocked so oxygen cannot reach the lungs and brain; more than 4 minutes without oxygen to the brain can cause brain damage or death.

**Why:** Slide "5) Choking".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the relief of choking in an adult.</summary>

**Answer:** Call 911 and get consent, stand behind and lean the person forward, give 5 back blows with the heel of the hand between the shoulder blades, then put arms around the waist with the fist just above the navel and give 5 upward abdominal thrusts, repeating until the object comes out.

**Why:** Slide "Choking: Back Blows and Abdominal Thrusts".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is choking managed in an infant?</summary>

**Answer:** Place the infant face-down on your forearm and give 5 back blows with the heel of the hand.

**Why:** Slide "Choking: Back Blows and Abdominal Thrusts".
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Nosebleed: sit FORWARD, not back; pinch BELOW the bony part.**
- **Burns: cool, not cold; never ice, butter, oil or lotions; never break blisters.**
- **Open fracture: do not push the bone back or straighten the limb.**
- **Convulsion: call 911 after 5 minutes (not 30); turn on the side.**
- **Nosebleed: call 911 after 30 minutes; choking brain damage after > 4 minutes.**
