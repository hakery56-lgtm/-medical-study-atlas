## 1. Objectives, definitions and modern specimen stewardship

- ⭐ **Rule of thumb: RIGHT TEST → RIGHT SITE → RIGHT TRANSPORT.**
- **Learning objectives:** (1) **confirm the requested assay and specimen source** before collection; (2) use **standard/transmission-based precautions**; (3) collect **nasal, throat and skin/wound** specimens with an **assay-appropriate technique**; (4) **avoid non-target contamination** and protect specimen integrity; (5) **label, package and transport** per the receiving laboratory's validated requirements.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Collection device** | The tool used to collect the specimen, e.g. a specific type of **swab** |
| **Transport medium** | A special medium that **preserves the specimen during transport** to the lab |
| **Specimen type** | The type of sample needed for the test: swab, blood, urine or tissue |

### Modern specimen stewardship
- **Definition:** using current microbiology guidance (**IDSA/ASM 2024 Guide to Utilization of the Microbiology Laboratory** and **CDC respiratory specimen collection guidance**) that emphasizes **test-specific specimen type, device, transport medium, timing and temperature**, **completely replacing one universal swab technique for every assay.**
- **Historical lecture** = broad bacterial/viral/fungal indications and general procedures; **updated teaching** = test-specific details. ⚠️ **The ultimate determinant of the procedure is the requested test and the local wound/specimen SOP. If uncertain, check the laboratory test directory before collecting.**
- The slide "Disease paradigms" notes that **etiology, risk factors, pathophysiology and clinical features are not covered in the provided source.**

## 2. Classification by specimen source and the universal swab workflow

| Source | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Nasal** | **Anterior, mid-turbinate and nasopharyngeal are NOT interchangeable** — follow the assay |
| **Throat** | Target the **posterior pharynx and tonsillar areas**; **avoid tongue, teeth and gums** |
| **Skin / wound** | Use the **clinically relevant site** and the **test-specific wound/specimen SOP** |
| **Transport** | Use the **laboratory-approved swab, medium and conditions** for the requested test |

### The universal 7-step workflow
1. **Verify test:** organism/assay + specimen source + collection device + transport.
2. **Prepare:** identity, explanation, **PPE**, equipment, privacy.
3. **Collect:** target the **correct anatomical site** with a validated technique.
4. **Protect:** avoid non-target contact; **place immediately into transport.**
5. **Label:** identifiers + source + **collection time.**
6. **Transport:** package/send under **required conditions.**
7. **Document:** source, difficulty, deviation and recollection need.
- **Decision point:** *"Did the swab contact non-target anatomy?"* **YES → deviation/contamination: assess whether recollection is needed per local policy and document the deviation.**

**Key points**
- Nasal sites are not interchangeable; throat = posterior pharynx/tonsils, avoid tongue/teeth/gums.
- 7 steps: verify, prepare, collect, protect, label, transport, document.
- Non-target contact → assess recollection + document the deviation.

## 3. Procedures: nasal, throat and skin/wound swabs

### A. Nasal swab — *key update: use the assay-approved site, device and depth*
- **Equipment:** sterile swab, **transport medium** (e.g. **viral transport medium** for viruses), **gloves and mask.**
1. Ask the patient to **tilt their head back slightly** (figure: about 15–30°).
2. **Insert the swab gently into the nostril (about 2–4 cm) until resistance is felt.**
3. **Rotate the swab for 5–10 seconds** to absorb the specimen.
4. **Remove carefully** and place **immediately** into the correct transport tube/system.
5. **Label** the specimen and send it for analysis.

### B. Throat swab
- ⚠️ **Warning: avoid the tongue, teeth, cheeks and gums.**
1. Ask the patient to **open their mouth wide and say "Ahh."**
2. **Depress the tongue** with a tongue depressor (if required).
3. **Swab the posterior pharynx and tonsillar area.**
4. Place the swab in the **transport tube** and send **for culture.**

### C. Skin / wound swab
- 💡 **Clinical pearl:** do **not** assume every wound swab uses the same technique; follow the **requested test and local wound/specimen SOP.**
1. **Clean the area** (if a non-infectious site) with **saline or alcohol.**
2. **Use the swab to collect material from the infected site.**
3. **For wounds: swab the BASE of the lesion, not just the surface.**
4. Place the swab in the **transport medium**, **label properly** and **record the anatomical source.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Nasal: tilt head back, insert 2–4 cm to resistance, rotate 5–10 s, place in transport, label.
- Throat: open wide "Ahh", depress tongue if needed, swab posterior pharynx/tonsils, avoid tongue/teeth/cheeks/gums.
- Wound: clean non-infectious site, swab the base of the lesion, transport medium, record source.

</div>

## 4. Transport, complications and the OSCE critical items

### Transport and storage logistics
- Handling after collection is **as critical as swab technique.**

| Target | Transport | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| **Virus** | **Viral transport media (VTM)** | Apply **specific viral temperature guidelines** |
| **Bacteria** | **Amies/Stuart's transport** | Apply **specific bacterial temperature guidelines** |
- Then **immediate, prompt dispatch to the lab.** **Store at the correct temperature** (varies bacterial vs viral cultures), use a **validated transport medium**, **label at collection**, and **send promptly under laboratory-specified conditions.**

### Clinical scenario: the swab touches the tongue before the posterior pharynx
- **Think → Act → Reassess → Document.** Swab touches tongue → **contamination with oral flora** → **quality degraded** → **consult local policy for recollection + mandatory source/deviation documentation.**

| Question | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| What is the specimen-quality problem? | **Non-target contamination** introduced oral flora that may invalidate pharyngeal results |
| Should you recollect per local policy? | **Check local SOPs; recollection is generally required** to protect specimen integrity |
| Why does source documentation matter? | Documenting **source, exact difficulty, deviation and recollection need** ensures accurate lab interpretation and transparent patient records |

### OSCE critical items (each graded PASS or PROMPT)
1. **Test/source verified** 2. **Identity/explanation/PPE** 3. **Assay-appropriate device** 4. **Correct target site** 5. **Non-target contamination avoided** 6. **Correct transport system** 7. **Label: identifiers + source + time** 8. **Transport conditions followed** 9. **Deviation documented.**

### Take-home answers
- **One technique for every respiratory assay? No — site, depth and device depend on the validated assay.** **Throat swab avoids** the **tongue, teeth and gums.** **Transport medium is determined by** the **requested test and the receiving laboratory's instructions.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Virus → VTM + viral temperature rules; bacteria → Amies/Stuart's + bacterial rules; dispatch promptly, label at collection.
- Tongue contact = contamination with oral flora → recollect per policy + document.
- OSCE: 9 critical items (verify, identity/PPE, device, site, avoid contamination, transport system, labeling, conditions, deviation documented).

</div>
