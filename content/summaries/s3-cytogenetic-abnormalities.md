## 1. Disorders of sex differentiation

- ⭐ **True hermaphrodite:** a **testis on one side and an ovary on the other**, or **ovotestes.** Karyotype **46,XX in 50%**; the rest **mosaics 46,XX/46,XY.**
- **Pseudohermaphrodite:** **phenotype and genotype do not match.**

| Type | Genotype and gonads | External genitalia | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Male pseudohermaphroditism** | **XY, testes** | **Ambiguous or completely female** | **Androgen insensitivity syndrome, 5α-reductase deficiency** |
| **Female pseudohermaphroditism** | **XX, ovaries** | **Ambiguous or virilised** | **Adrenogenital syndrome (21- or 11-hydroxylase deficiency)** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- True hermaphrodite = both testicular and ovarian tissue; 46,XX in 50%.
- Pseudohermaphrodite = genotype and phenotype mismatch.
- Male: XY with testes (AIS, 5α-reductase deficiency); female: XX with ovaries (adrenogenital syndrome).

</div>

## 2. Karyotyping, ploidy and aneuploidy

- **Karyotyping** = study of chromosomes (skin fibroblasts, peripheral blood lymphocytes, amniotic cells). **Somatic = diploid 46,XX or 46,XY**; **gametes = haploid (23,X) or (23,Y).**
- **Euploidy** = exact multiple of the haploid number (2n, 3n, 4n); **aneuploidy** = not an exact multiple. **Genome mutations** = loss or gain of whole chromosomes → **monosomy or trisomy.**

| Chromosome type | Centromere | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Metacentric** | Centre, two equal arms | |
| **Submetacentric** | Off-centre, unequal arms | **X** (large) |
| **Acrocentric** | Near the tip | **Y** (small); 13, 14, 15, 21, 22 |
| **Telocentric** | At the extreme end | **Not seen in humans** |

- ⭐ **Aneuploidy** (number not a multiple of 23) arises from **nondisjunction** or **anaphase lag.**
- **Nondisjunction** = failure of chromosomes to separate in meiosis or mitosis → gametes with **22 or 24** chromosomes; **meiotic nondisjunction is the most common cause of aneuploidy.** Examples: **Turner 45** (22 + 23), **Down 47** (24 + 23).
- **Anaphase lag** = loss of a chromosome during division; early in embryonic life it gives **mosaicism** (a normal line plus a monosomic line).
- **Polyploidy** = multiple greater than two of the haploid number (**triploidy 3n, tetraploidy 4n**); **rarely compatible with life, spontaneous abortion.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Diploid 46, haploid 23; euploid = exact multiples, aneuploid = not.
- Nondisjunction (most common cause, meiotic) vs anaphase lag (mosaicism).
- Polyploidy usually ends in spontaneous abortion.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Distinguish euploidy, aneuploidy and polyploidy.</summary>

**Answer:** Euploidy is an exact multiple of the haploid number; aneuploidy is not a multiple (loss or gain of one or two chromosomes); polyploidy is a multiple greater than two (3n, 4n), rarely compatible with life.

**Why:** Slides 7, 11, 18.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain nondisjunction and give examples.</summary>

**Answer:** Failure of chromosomes to separate in meiosis or mitosis, giving gametes with 22 or 24 chromosomes; it is the most common cause of aneuploidy, e.g. Turner (45) and Down (47).

**Why:** Slide 14.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How does anaphase lag lead to mosaicism?</summary>

**Answer:** Loss of a chromosome during division in early embryonic life produces two cell lines, one normal and one monosomic for that chromosome pair.

**Why:** Slide 17.
</details>

## 3. Structural chromosomal abnormalities

- ⭐ **Deletion:** loss of a chromosome portion (minus sign; **p = short arm, q = long arm**). **Cri du chat = 5p-** (46,XY,5p- or 46,XX,5p-). **Terminal** = one break, distal part lost; **interstitial** = piece between two breaks lost.
- **Tumour suppressor gene loss:** **Rb** gene (interstitial deletion, **long arm of chromosome 13**) → **retinoblastoma** (hereditary or sporadic); **short arm of chromosome 11** → **Wilms tumour** (large well-defined renal mass).
- **Inversion:** reunion after **two breaks** with the fragment reinserted inverted. **Paracentric** = breaks on one side of the centromere; **pericentric** = both arms, centromere involved, **more severe.** Result: unbalanced homologues (one duplicated, one deficient).
- **Translocation:** exchange between **non-homologous** chromosomes, written **t(...)**; translocation Down syndrome = **t(14q;21q).**
  - **Reciprocal (balanced):** exchange with **no loss** of material, often **clinically silent**; may move **oncogenes** to uncontrolled positions: **CML t(9;22) Philadelphia chromosome**; Burkitt lymphoma written as t(8;22) on the slide.
  - **Robertsonian:** **long arms of two acrocentric chromosomes fuse** at a common centromere, **short arms lost**; acrocentric = **13, 14, 15, 21, 22.**
- **Isochromosome:** **transverse** division giving two long arms or two short arms (short-arm isochromosome often lost).
- **Ring chromosome:** **both ends deleted**, sticky ends join into a ring.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Deletion: cri du chat 5p-; Rb on 13q (retinoblastoma); 11p (Wilms).
- Inversion: pericentric (centromere included) is worse than paracentric.
- Translocation: balanced is silent but oncogenic (Philadelphia t(9;22)); Robertsonian involves acrocentric chromosomes 13, 14, 15, 21, 22.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Differentiate terminal from interstitial deletion with an example of each syndrome or tumour.</summary>

**Answer:** Terminal: one break, distal portion lost. Interstitial: piece between two breaks lost (e.g. Rb loss on 13q causing retinoblastoma). Cri du chat is partial loss of 5p.

**Why:** Slides 21-24.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare paracentric and pericentric inversion.</summary>

**Answer:** Paracentric: breaks on one side of the centromere. Pericentric: breaks on both arms involving the centromere, more severe; both create unbalanced homologues.

**Why:** Slide 27.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Compare reciprocal and Robertsonian translocation.</summary>

**Answer:** Reciprocal: exchange between two chromosomes without loss of material, often silent but can activate oncogenes (Philadelphia t(9;22)). Robertsonian: long arms of two acrocentric chromosomes fuse at a common centromere and the short arms are lost (e.g. translocation Down syndrome).

**Why:** Slides 30-33.
</details>

## 4. X inactivation (Lyon's hypothesis) and autosomal syndromes

- ⭐ **Lyon's hypothesis:** only **one X is active**; the other (maternal or paternal) is inactivated (pyknosis) **at random** by about **day 16** of embryonic life; the **same X stays inactive** in all daughter cells.
- **Xist** gene causes silencing by **DNA methylation.** The inactive X = **Barr body (X chromatin)** at the nuclear membrane. **Barr bodies = number of X − 1** → **absent in normal males and Turner syndrome; one in a normal female.**

| Syndrome | Cause | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Down (trisomy 21)**, about 1/700 | **Nondisjunction 95%; Robertsonian translocation 4% (46 chromosomes); mosaicism 1%** | Mental retardation, epicanthic folds and flat facial profile, simian crease, congenital heart defects, intestinal stenosis, umbilical hernia, hypotonia, gap between first and second toe, predisposition to leukaemia |
| **Edwards (trisomy 18)**, 1/8000 | Nondisjunction; **second most common trisomy** | Prominent occiput, micrognathia, low-set ears, overlapping fingers, heart and renal malformations, limited hip abduction, rocker-bottom feet |
| **Patau (trisomy 13)**, 1/15,000 | Trisomy 13 | Microphthalmia, polydactyly, microcephaly, cleft lip and palate, cardiac and renal defects, umbilical hernia, rocker-bottom feet |
| **Cri du chat (5p-)** | Deletion of the short arm of chromosome 5 | "Cry of the cat" |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Lyon: one X active, random inactivation by day 16, Xist, Barr bodies = X − 1.
- Down: 95% nondisjunction, 4% Robertsonian translocation, 1% mosaic.
- Trisomy 18 = Edwards (1/8000); trisomy 13 = Patau (1/15,000).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. State Lyon's hypothesis and the formula for Barr bodies.</summary>

**Answer:** Only one X is active; the other is randomly inactivated by about day 16 (Xist, DNA methylation) and seen as a Barr body; Barr bodies = number of X chromosomes minus 1 (absent in normal males and Turner).

**Why:** Slide 38.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the causes of Down syndrome with percentages.</summary>

**Answer:** Nondisjunction (95%, trisomy 21), Robertsonian translocation (4%, 46 chromosomes) and mosaicism (1%).

**Why:** Slide 39.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Differentiate Edwards from Patau syndrome.</summary>

**Answer:** Edwards is trisomy 18 (1/8000, second most common trisomy; prominent occiput, overlapping fingers, rocker-bottom feet). Patau is trisomy 13 (1/15,000; cleft lip and palate, polydactyly, microphthalmia).

**Why:** Slides 39-41.
</details>

## 5. Sex chromosome abnormalities

- ⭐ **Klinefelter (47,XXY):** at least two X and one or more Y; **male phenotype with testes** (**one Y is enough**: XY, XXY, XXXY are all male). **Meiotic nondisjunction: 40% spermatogenesis, 60% oogenesis** (maternal most often); **incidence rises with maternal age.** Features: **atrophic testes, tall stature, eunuchoid appearance, gynecomastia, ↓ testosterone**; frequent cause of **male infertility.**
- **XYY:** tallness, severe acne, rarely mild mental retardation (slide links it to violent behaviour).
- **XXX (47,XXX):** usually no abnormality, maybe menstrual irregularities; **extra X beyond XXX** → progressive mental deficiency.
- ⭐ **Turner (45,X):** complete or partial **X monosomy**; **female hypogonadism**; **autoantibody-mediated hypothyroidism**; **streak ovaries**, **↓ estrogen with ↑ gonadotropins** (loss of feedback), infantile genitalia, poor breasts, **short stature, webbed neck, shield chest with widely spaced nipples, wide carrying angle (cubitus valgus)**, lymphedema, **coarctation of the aorta**; **most common cause of primary amenorrhea** (incidence about 1/3000 female births).

| Feature | **Klinefelter 47,XXY** | **Turner 45,X** |
|---|---|---|
| **Phenotype** | Male | Female |
| **Gonads** | Atrophic testes | Streak ovaries |
| **Hormones** | ↓ testosterone | ↓ estrogen, ↑ gonadotropins |
| **Barr body** | One | None |
| **Stature** | Tall | Short |
| **Presentation** | Male infertility, gynecomastia | Primary amenorrhea |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Klinefelter 47,XXY: tall, atrophic testes, gynecomastia, infertility; usually maternal nondisjunction.
- Turner 45,X: streak ovaries, short stature, webbed neck, coarctation, primary amenorrhea.
- 47,XXX is usually normal; XYY gives tallness and acne.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe Klinefelter syndrome.</summary>

**Answer:** 47,XXY male with atrophic testes, tall eunuchoid habitus, gynecomastia and low testosterone; from meiotic nondisjunction (60% oogenesis, 40% spermatogenesis), rising with maternal age; a frequent cause of male infertility.

**Why:** Slide 42.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe Turner syndrome.</summary>

**Answer:** 45,X monosomy with streak ovaries, low estrogen and high gonadotropins, infantile genitalia, short stature, webbed neck, shield chest, cubitus valgus, lymphedema and coarctation of the aorta; most common cause of primary amenorrhea.

**Why:** Slide 44.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why does a single Y chromosome make XXY individuals male?</summary>

**Answer:** The presence of a single Y chromosome is enough for the male phenotype, so XY, XXY and XXXY are all males with testes.

**Why:** Slide 42.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Barr bodies = X chromosomes − 1: none in normal males and in Turner syndrome.**
- **Down syndrome: 95% nondisjunction, 4% Robertsonian translocation (46 chromosomes), 1% mosaic.**
- **Edwards = trisomy 18 (second most common); Patau = trisomy 13.**
- **Pericentric inversion (centromere involved) is more severe than paracentric.**
- **Balanced translocation is clinically silent; the Philadelphia chromosome is t(9;22).**
- **Acrocentric chromosomes: 13, 14, 15, 21, 22 (Robertsonian translocations).**
- **Klinefelter is mostly maternal nondisjunction (60%); Turner is the commonest cause of primary amenorrhea.**
- **Note:** the slide gives Burkitt lymphoma as t(8;22); and its statement linking XYY to criminal behaviour is kept as written but is not a basis for exam questions.
