## 1. Embryonic period and gastrulation

- ⭐ **Embryonic period = weeks 3 to 8** (**period of organogenesis**): **greatest change**, **all major structures and systems form**, **most perilous** for the developing child.
- Week 3 = **"the week of threes"**: **three cavities** (amniotic, chorionic, yolk sac; chorionic cavity established by the end of week 2) and **three germ layers** (**ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm**).
- **Bilaminar disc** (epiblast + hypoblast) → **trilaminar disc**.
- ⭐ **Gastrulation** = the **most characteristic event of week 3**: establishes all **three germ layers**.

### Steps
| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Primitive streak** forms on the surface of the **epiblast** (caudal part); at **15–16 days** it is a **narrow groove** with bulging regions on either side |
| **2** | **Primitive node** = cephalic end of the streak: elevated area around the small **primitive pit** |
| **3** | Epiblast cells migrate to the streak, become **flask-shaped**, detach and slip beneath the epiblast = **invagination** |
| **4** | Some displace the **hypoblast → embryonic endoderm**; others lie **between epiblast and endoderm → mesoderm**; cells remaining in the epiblast → **ectoderm** |

### Gastrulation summary
| | |
|---|---|
| **When** | Third week, start of the embryonic period |
| **What** | Bilaminar → trilaminar disc; three germ layers |
| **How** | Primitive streak in the **caudal epiblast** → migration and **invagination** of epiblast cells |
| **Why** | **Correct placement of precursor tissues** so morphogenesis can follow |

### Examples of derivatives (figure)
| Germ layer | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Endoderm** | Alveolar lung cells, thyroid cells, pancreatic (digestive) cells |
| **Mesoderm** | Cardiac, skeletal and gut smooth muscle, kidney tubule cells, red blood cells |
| **Ectoderm** | Epidermal skin cells, brain neurons, pigment cells |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Embryonic period = weeks 3–8 (organogenesis), the most sensitive period.
- Gastrulation = primitive streak → invagination → endoderm, mesoderm, ectoderm.
- Node at the cephalic end of the streak, with the pit.
- Hypoblast displaced → endoderm; in between → mesoderm; remaining epiblast → ectoderm.

</div>

## 2. Growth of the embryonic disc, mesoderm and the two membranes

- Disc **initially flat and almost round** → **elongated** with a **broad cephalic** and **narrow caudal** end.
- ⭐ **Cephalocaudal development:** **gastrulation continues in caudal segments** while **cranial structures are differentiating**.
- Disc figure (cranial → caudal): **cardiogenic area, prechordal plate (future mouth), notochord, primitive pit, node, groove, cloacal membrane (future anus)**.
- **Mesoderm** spreads **laterally and cephalad**, between ectoderm and endoderm **like the filling of a sandwich**, **with two exceptions:**

| Membrane | Location | Structure | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Oropharyngeal membrane** | **Cranial** end of the disc | **Ectoderm + endoderm tightly adherent, no mesoderm** | **Opening of the oral cavity** |
| **Cloacal membrane** | **Caudal** end of the disc | **Similar structure** (no mesoderm) | **Future anus** |

- Mesoderm regions on the figure: **paraxial, intermediate, lateral plate** and **extraembryonic mesoderm**, with the **notochord** in the midline.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Disc: round → elongated; broad cephalic, narrow caudal.
- Cephalocaudal development: caudal gastrulation continues while the head region differentiates.
- Mesoderm spreads laterally and cephalad like a sandwich filling.
- Exceptions: oropharyngeal (cranial) and cloacal (caudal) membranes have no mesoderm.

</div>

## 3. Formation of the notochord

1. **Prenotochordal cells** invaginating in the **primitive node** move **cranially** in the midline to the **prechordal plate** (forms between the **tip of the notochord and the oropharyngeal membrane**).
2. They intercalate in the **hypoblast**: for a short time the midline has **two cell layers** = **notochordal plate**.
3. As hypoblast is replaced by **endoderm**, the plate cells **proliferate and detach** from the endoderm.
4. They form a **solid cord** = **definitive notochord**.
5. Elongation is dynamic: the **cranial end forms first**, **caudal regions are added** as the streak assumes a more caudal position.
6. The notochord extends **cranially to the prechordal plate** and **caudally to the primitive pit**; at the pit, an indentation in the epiblast, the **neurenteric canal** temporarily connects the **amniotic and yolk sac cavities**.
7. Around **day 16** the posterior yolk sac wall forms a diverticulum into the connecting stalk: the **allantois**.
8. ⭐ **Significance:** **defines the midline**; **axial skeleton forms around it**; **drives neurulation**.
9. At the **end of week 4** the primitive streak regresses, shrinks and disappears.
- Note: the slide says the vestigial remnant in the adult is the nucleus pulposus of the intervertebral discs; in standard embryology the nucleus pulposus is a remnant of the notochord, so treat this slide statement with care.

### Fate map of the streak
| Cells migrating through | Become |
|---|---|
| **Cranial region of the node** | **Prechordal plate and notochord** |
| **Lateral edges of the node and cranial streak** | **Paraxial mesoderm** |
| **Mid-streak** | **Intermediate mesoderm** |
| **More caudal streak** | **Lateral plate mesoderm** |
| **Caudal-most streak** | **Extraembryonic mesoderm** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Prenotochordal cells → notochordal plate → solid definitive notochord.
- Notochord: prechordal plate to primitive pit; cranial end first.
- Neurenteric canal (amniotic ↔ yolk sac) and allantois (day 16).
- Notochord: midline, axial skeleton, neurulation.
- Fate map: cranial → caudal gives notochord, paraxial, intermediate, lateral plate, extraembryonic.

</div>

## 4. Clinical correlates

- ⭐ **Teratogenesis:** the **beginning of week 3** (gastrulation starts) is **highly sensitive to teratogens**; it is **2 weeks after fertilization ≈ 4 weeks from the last menses**, so the woman **may not know she is pregnant**.
- **Holoprosencephaly:** **small forebrain**, the two **lateral ventricles often merge into one**, **eyes close together (hypotelorism)**.
- **Situs inversus:** **transposition of thoracic and abdominal viscera**; other defects only slightly more frequent. About **20%** of complete cases also have **immotile cilia (Kartagener) syndrome** → **bronchiectasis and chronic sinusitis**.
- **Sacrococcygeal teratoma:** the **most common tumor in newborns**; **1 in 37,000**; contains tissues from **all three germ layers**; arises from **persisting primitive streak remnants** in the sacrococcygeal region or from **primordial germ cells that fail to migrate** to the gonadal ridge.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Early week 3 is highly teratogen-sensitive, before pregnancy is recognized.
- Holoprosencephaly: single ventricle, hypotelorism.
- Situs inversus: 20% have Kartagener syndrome.
- Sacrococcygeal teratoma: commonest newborn tumor, three germ layers, 1 in 37,000.

</div>
