## 1. Definition and history of surgery

- ⭐ **Surgery** = the branch of medicine dealing with **physical manipulation of a bodily structure to diagnose, prevent or cure a disease**, performed by a **surgeon**.

### The three major obstacles in surgery
1. **Pain**
2. **Bleeding**
3. **Infection**
- Until the **industrial revolution**, surgeons couldn't overcome them; advances turned surgery from a **risky "art" into a scientific plan of treatment**.

### History
- First techniques treated **injuries and trauma**: **suturing lacerations, amputating unsalvageable limbs, draining pus**.
- **Oldest surgery: burr holes (craniotomy).**
- **Intramedullary bone fixation with wooden splints** (early Spanish texts).
- **Anaesthesia with alcohol or opium** — one of the oldest techniques.
- **Hippocrates (c. 460–377 BCE):** "He who wishes to be a surgeon must first go to war."

## 3. Courses of surgical care

### Three courses: **pre-operative, per-operative (intra-operative), post-operative.**

### 1 — Pre-operative care
**For the patient:**
- Medical examination, **pre-operative tests**, **signed consent**, **blood prepared** if significant loss is expected.
- ⭐ **NPO order 6–8 hours before the procedure** to reduce the effect of stomach contents and the **risk of aspiration** if the patient vomits.
- Change out of street clothes; **vital signs recorded**; **peripheral IV line**; **pre-operative medications (antibiotics, sedatives)**.

**For the surgical team:**
- **Surgical masks** (prevent contamination from droplets).
- **Hands, wrists and forearms washed for at least 4 minutes**, then **sterile gloves**.

### Anesthesia and operating field
- Anesthesia **prevents pain** from incision, manipulation and suturing: **general, spinal, regional or local**. With **local and spinal** the site is anesthetized and the patient can stay **conscious/minimally sedated**; **general anesthesia = unconscious**.
- **Operating field** = the skin surface to be operated on: **hair clipped**, cleaned with an **antiseptic such as povidone-iodine**, covered with **sterile drapes**.

### 2 — Per-operative care
- **Incision** to access the site; vessels **clamped or cauterized**; **retractors** expose the site.
- ⭐ **Haemostasis** = preventing and stopping bleeding (pressure, cautery, clips, sutures).
- ⭐ **Ligation** = tying off vessels, ducts or tubes.
- ⭐ **Excision** = removal of tissue (knife, laser, other tool).
- ⭐ **Debridement** = removal of **dead or damaged tissue and foreign material** from a wound.

### 3 — Post-operative care
- Transfer to the **post-anesthesia care unit (PACU)** with close monitoring → **surgical ward** after recovery.
- Assess **general function, the procedure outcome**, and the **surgical site for infection or complications**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pre-op: exam, tests, consent, blood, NPO 6–8 h (aspiration), IV line, antibiotics/sedatives; team: mask, scrub ≥ 4 min, sterile gloves.
- Anesthesia: general (unconscious), spinal, regional, local (conscious). Field: clip hair, povidone-iodine, sterile drapes.
- Per-op: incision, haemostasis, ligation, excision, debridement. Post-op: PACU then ward; monitor for infection.

</div>

## 4. Examples of surgical procedures

- **Removal of diseased organs:** **appendectomy** (vermiform appendix), **cholecystectomy** (gallbladder).
- **Amputation** (trauma/disease): finger, lower limb.
- **Other:** **skin graft** (skin removed from one area and transplanted to another); **draining** pus/blood; **removal of urinary or biliary stones**; **repair of fistula, hernia, prolapse**; **transplant surgery** (donor organ → recipient); **creation of a stoma** (temporary or permanent).

### Stomas and joint fusion
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Colostomy** | opening (stoma) in the **large intestine** |
| ⭐ **Nephrostomy** | opening between the **kidney and the skin** |
| ⭐ **Tracheostomy** | opening into the **trachea** |
| ⭐ **Arthrodesis** | **uniting two bones at a joint (joint fusion)** |

### Endoscopic procedures
- **Laparoscopy:** laparoscope inserted **through the abdominal wall** to examine the abdomen and pelvis (diagnosis + treatment).
- **OGD:** oesophago-gastro-duodenoscopy.
- **Cystoscopy:** looks inside the **bladder and urethra**.
- **Arthroscopy:** diagnoses/treats **joint problems**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Appendectomy, cholecystectomy, amputation, skin graft, drainage, stone removal, repair of fistula/hernia/prolapse, transplant, stoma.
- Colostomy (large intestine), nephrostomy (kidney–skin), tracheostomy (trachea), arthrodesis (joint fusion).
- Laparoscopy (abdomen/pelvis), OGD, cystoscopy (bladder/urethra), arthroscopy (joint).

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Three obstacles: pain, bleeding, infection** (not hypotension or trauma).
- **Urgent = within 1–2 days;** emergency = immediate; planned = elective.
- **NPO 6–8 h** to reduce aspiration risk.
- **Hand scrub ≥ 4 minutes.**
- **Local/spinal = conscious; general = unconscious.**
- **Haemostasis ≠ ligation ≠ debridement.**
- **Colostomy = large intestine; nephrostomy = kidney–skin; tracheostomy = trachea.**
- **Oldest surgery = burr holes; oldest anaesthesia = alcohol/opium.**
- **Antiseptic for the field = povidone-iodine.**
