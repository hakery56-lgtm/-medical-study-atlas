# Orthopedics — PBL 8: Painful Knee (Osteoarthritis) — Exam Night Review

Unit III, Week 8 PBL (Musculoskeletal and Neuroscience I, Year 2): **a 55-year-old nurse with years of mechanical knee pain → osteoarthritis of the knee → knee replacement**. After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer). Items beyond the case text are marked **standard teaching**.

## 1. Presentation and first assessment

- **Fatima, 55 years, nurse** from Kerbala (10 years in a private hospital): **intermittent pain in the left knee for some years.**
- **Pain pattern:** **worse with walking and standing, especially on stairs, worse at the end of the day, least first thing in the morning**; **worsening despite paracetamol.**
- **History (GP Dr. Hammed):** **no trauma** to back, hip or knee; **family history of osteoarthritis (mother).**
- **Anthropometry:** **158 cm, 66 kg** (BMI about 26.4, overweight — standard teaching).
- **Examination of the left knee:** **moderate swelling, joint-line tenderness, no free fluid (massage test and patellar tap negative), reduced range of motion, crepitus** (a grating sound better felt than heard).
- **Plain X-ray:** **marked loss of joint space and moderate osteophytes.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Mechanical pain: worse with use and later in the day, best in the morning.
- No effusion, but joint-line tenderness, limited motion and crepitus.
- X-ray: loss of joint space + osteophytes.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the pain pattern in this case.</summary>

**Answer:** Intermittent pain made worse by walking, standing and stairs, worse at the end of the day and least first thing in the morning, not controlled by paracetamol.

**Why:** Case text, paragraph 1–2.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What did the first knee examination and X-ray show?</summary>

**Answer:** Moderate swelling, joint-line tenderness, no free fluid, reduced range of motion and crepitus; X-ray showed marked loss of joint space and moderate osteophytes.

**Why:** Case text, examination paragraph.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which risk factors for osteoarthritis are present?</summary>

**Answer:** Age 55, family history (mother), weight (later gain and a sedentary lifestyle) and probably her occupation as a nurse (standing and walking, standard teaching).

**Why:** Case text and standard teaching.
</details>

## 2. Diagnosis and initial management

- **Referral:** the GP (Dr. Hammed) referred Fatima to the **rheumatologist (Dr. Zaid)**, who diagnosed **osteoarthritis of the knee**.
- **Plan:** **explanation and treatment options, physiotherapy, advice to lose weight, regular paracetamol, glucosamine.**
- **Why not other analgesics?** **Constipation with codeine, heartburn with diclofenac (NSAID).**
- **Outcome:** **only limited benefit**; Fatima found it **too difficult to continue as a nurse and resigned** from the hospital in Kerbala; **more sedentary lifestyle and further weight gain.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Primary knee osteoarthritis: education, physiotherapy, weight loss, paracetamol.
- Codeine → constipation; diclofenac → heartburn.
- Limited benefit, loss of work, weight gain.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the initial management of Fatima's osteoarthritis.</summary>

**Answer:** Discussion of treatment options, physiotherapy, weight loss, regular paracetamol and glucosamine.

**Why:** Case text, rheumatologist visit.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why were codeine and diclofenac not used?</summary>

**Answer:** Codeine had caused constipation and diclofenac had caused heartburn.

**Why:** Case text.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How did the disease affect Fatima's life after the first treatment?</summary>

**Answer:** Limited benefit; she resigned as a nurse, became more sedentary and gained weight.

**Why:** Case text.
</details>

## 3. Progression and surgical referral

- **Five years later:** Fatima returns to Dr. Zaid; **pain now wakes her at night; trouble getting into her car and on and off the toilet; struggling to cope.**
- **Examination:** **flexion limited to 80 degrees with crepitus.**
- **Repeat X-ray:** **almost no joint space, irregularity of the joint margins, sclerosis and osteophytes.**
- **Management:** Dr. Zaid suggests **knee joint replacement**, answers her questions and explains the **risk of complications**, then **refers her to an orthopedic surgeon.** Fatima is **eager for the operation but worried about the cost.**
- **Radiographic features of osteoarthritis (LOSS, standard teaching):** **L**oss of joint space, **O**steophytes, **S**ubchondral **S**clerosis (and subchondral cysts).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- End-stage knee OA: night pain, flexion 80°, almost no joint space.
- Treatment: knee replacement after counseling about risks.
- LOSS: loss of joint space, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis, subchondral cysts.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Why:** Case text, second visit.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **No free fluid on massage test and patellar tap: not an effusion case.**
- **Pain is worse at the end of the day and best in the morning (mechanical, not inflammatory).**
- **Codeine = constipation; diclofenac = heartburn.**
- **First X-ray: loss of joint space + moderate osteophytes; second X-ray: almost no joint space + sclerosis + irregular margins.**
- **Night pain and flexion limited to 80° mark advanced disease.**
