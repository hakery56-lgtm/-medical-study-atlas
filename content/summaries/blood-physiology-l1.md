## 1. Blood, its functions and hematopoiesis

- ⭐ **Blood** = a **liquid connective tissue** in a closed vessel system: **cells (erythrocytes, leukocytes, platelets) in plasma**; unique from one person to another.
- **Functions:** **transport** (O2, CO2, nutrients, heat, wastes, hormones, enzymes, vitamins); **regulation** (pH, temperature, arterial BP); **prevention of blood loss** (clotting factors); **protection** (WBCs, antibodies, interferons, complement).
- **Hematopoiesis** = **erythropoiesis, leucopoiesis, thrombopoiesis.**

| Age | Site |
|---|---|
| **Fetus 0-2 months** | Yolk sac |
| **Fetus 2-7 months** | Liver and spleen |
| **Fetus 5-9 months** | Bone marrow |
| **Infant** | Bone marrow of all bones |
| **Adult** | Axial skeleton and proximal ends of long bones |

- **Red marrow** = active, highly vascularized; **yellow marrow** = inactive, fat cells, can **revert to red** when needed.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Blood = cells in plasma; four functions: transport, regulation, hemostasis, protection.
- Fetal sites: yolk sac → liver/spleen → bone marrow.
- Yellow marrow can become red marrow in disease.

</div>

## 2. Bone marrow cells

- ⭐ **Bone marrow** = **fat tissue + hematopoietic tissue** (stem, progenitor, precursor and mature cells).
- **Stem cells:** give rise to **all cell lines**; proliferate and differentiate; **few and constant in number**; need growth factors and a suitable microenvironment.
- **Progenitor cells:** early and late; **multi-lineage or uni-lineage**; differentiation increases over proliferation.
- **Precursor cells:** **single line**, morphologically recognizable.
- **Mature cells:** released to the peripheral blood.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Stem → progenitor → precursor → mature.
- Precursors are recognizable and single-line.

</div>

## 3. Blood volume and physical properties

- ⭐ **Adult male: 5-6 L (≈ 8% of body weight) = 80 mL/kg**; 70 kg × 80 mL = **5.6 L**; 1 kg blood = 1 L.
- **Newborn ≈ 300 mL**; reaches adult range at **puberty.**
- **Properties:** **3-4 × more viscous than water; temperature 38 °C; pH 7.35-7.45.**
- **Females 7% less** (fewer RBCs, more fat); volume **increases in pregnancy**; varies with electrolytes and adipose tissue.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 80 mL/kg; 5.6 L in a 70 kg man.
- pH 7.35-7.45; 3-4 × water viscosity.
- Lower in females; higher in pregnancy.

</div>

## 4. Plasma, plasma proteins and serum

- ⭐ **Plasma** = straw-colored fluid, **3,500 mL = 5% of body weight**; **91.5% water, 8.5% solutes** (70% proteins, 20% small molecules, 10% electrolytes).
- **Centrifuged blood:** plasma **55%**, buffy coat (WBCs and platelets) **< 1%**, erythrocytes **45%.**

| Protein | Share | Concentration | Site and function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Albumin** | **54%** | 4.5 g/dL | Liver; **osmotic pressure, transport** |
| **Globulins (α, β, γ)** | **38%** | 2.7 g/dL | Liver and lymphoid tissue; **clotting factors, transport, immunoglobulins** |
| **Fibrinogen** | **7%** | 0.3 g/dL | Liver; **clotting** |

- **Total proteins 7-7.5 g/dL.** Other solutes: electrolytes, nutrients, gases, enzymes, hormones, wastes.
- ⭐ **Serum = plasma minus fibrinogen and clotting factors II, V, VII, with more serotonin.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Plasma: 91.5% water; proteins 7-7.5 g/dL.
- Albumin 54%, globulins 38%, fibrinogen 7%.
- Serum lacks fibrinogen and factors II, V, VII.

</div>

## 5. The red blood cell

- ⭐ **RBC** = **biconcave non-nucleated disc, 7.5 µ diameter, 2.0 µ thick**; **500-1000 ×** more numerous than other blood cells.
- **Glycolysis → ATP** for: **membrane flexibility, iron in the ferrous state, active ion transport, preventing protein oxidation.**
- **Functions:** **keeps Hb from being lost** (free Hb: 3% lost per circulation); **O2 transport** (≈ **280 million Hb molecules**, **29 pg** per cell); **CO2 transport** (**carbonic anhydrase**); **Hb = 50% of blood buffering.**
- **Specialization:** **no nucleus** (more room), **no mitochondria** (anaerobic ATP; does not consume O2), **biconcave shape** (large surface area), **thin membrane.**
- **O2 is poorly soluble in plasma: 95-98% travels on Hb.** Hb: **14 g/dL females, 15 g/dL males.**
- **Marrow:** **75% myeloid (WBC) series, 25% maturing red cells**, because red cells live longer.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Biconcave, no nucleus, no mitochondria.
- Carbonic anhydrase speeds CO2 + H2O.
- Hb gives 50% of blood buffering and carries 95-98% of O2.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Why is the RBC specialized for O2 transport?</summary>

**Answer:** It has no nucleus (all space for O2), no mitochondria (makes ATP anaerobically so it does not use the O2 it carries), a biconcave shape with large surface area and a thin membrane for fast diffusion.

**Why:** Slide "RBC Physiology".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the role of RBC glycolysis?</summary>

**Answer:** It supplies ATP to maintain membrane flexibility, keep iron ferrous, maintain ion transport and prevent protein oxidation.

**Why:** Slide on RBC enzymes.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the functions of the RBC.</summary>

**Answer:** Keeps Hb from being lost through the kidney and tissues, transports O2, transports CO2 using carbonic anhydrase and provides 50% of blood buffering.

**Why:** Slides "RBC Function".
</details>

## 6. RBC count and its variations

- ⭐ **Males 5.4 × 10^6/mm3; females 4.8 × 10^6/mm3.**
- **Increased** in **old age** (hypoxia of impaired lung function, as stated), **pregnancy** and **high altitude** (hypoxia).
- **Pregnancy:** RBCs rise but **plasma rises more → relative anemia (dilution).**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Male 5.4, female 4.8 million per mm3.
- High altitude raises the count (hypoxia).
- Pregnancy anemia = dilution.

</div>

## 7. Regulation of RBC production and erythropoietin

- ⭐ **Erythropoiesis is regulated by tissue oxygen needs**; the RBC number must stay constant (**too few → poor oxygenation; too many → high viscosity**).
- **Reticulocytes** rise from **1% to 30-50%** on stimulation; with greater output **erythroblasts** may appear (erythroblastosis fetalis).
- **Erythropoietin:** the **kidneys sense low O2** and release it; **85-90% from the kidneys**, the rest mainly from the **liver**; **inactivated in the liver**; **half-life ≈ 5 h**; red-cell rise after **2-3 days.**
- **Increased by:** **tissue hypoxia (anemia, lung disease), reduced renal blood flow, some tumors, androgens.** **Decreased by:** diseases or tumors **destroying the kidneys.**
- **Clinical use:** **anemia of renal failure** (90% of dialysis patients are anemic from EPO deficiency).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Kidneys make 85-90% of EPO; the liver inactivates it.
- EPO half-life 5 h; red cell rise after 2-3 days.
- Used in renal-failure anemia.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How does hypoxia stimulate RBC production?</summary>

**Answer:** Low O2 content is sensed by the kidneys (and liver), which secrete erythropoietin; it stimulates the red bone marrow to convert committed stem cells into RBCs, raising the RBC count and O2 transport.

**Why:** Slides "Erythropoietin" and "Regulation of RBC production".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. When is erythropoietin secretion increased or decreased?</summary>

**Answer:** Increased in tissue hypoxia, reduced renal blood flow, some tumors and with androgens; decreased in diseases or tumors that destroy the kidneys.

**Why:** Slide "Erythropoietin secretion".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is erythropoietin used in renal failure?</summary>

**Answer:** Most dialysis patients with end-stage renal failure (90%) are anemic because of erythropoietin deficiency.

**Why:** Last slide.
</details>
