# Anatomy of the Arm II — Exam Night Review

A short lecture with three big topics: the **elbow joint**, the **proximal (superior) radio-ulnar joint** with pronation and supination, and the **cubital fossa**. It closes with two rules about how nerves and arteries pass around the elbow. After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer).

---

## 1. The elbow joint

### Type and articular surfaces
- **Synovial joint, hinge variety (uniaxial)**.
- **Compound joint**: it involves **three bones** — humerus, ulna and radius.
- **Articular surfaces**:
  - Humerus: **trochlea** and **capitulum** (lower end).
  - Ulna: **trochlear notch**; radius: **head of the radius**.

### Capsule
- **Thin in front and behind** (to allow flexion and extension).
- **Thick at the sides** (reinforced by the collateral ligaments).

### Ligaments — like every hinge joint, two strong collateral ligaments
1. **Radial collateral ligament** — strong, triangular, on the **lateral** side.
   - **Apex** → **lateral epicondyle**; **base** → **annular ligament**.
2. **Ulnar collateral ligament** — strong, triangular, on the **medial** side; **apex** → **medial epicondyle**. It has three borders (bands):
   - **Anterior**: medial epicondyle → **medial border of the coronoid process**.
   - **Posterior**: medial epicondyle → **medial margin of the olecranon**.
   - **Lower (transverse)**: **coronoid process → olecranon**.
   - ⭐ N.B. the **ulnar collateral ligament is crossed by the ulnar nerve**.

### Movements
**Flexion and extension** only (it is uniaxial).

### Carrying angle
- **Definition**: the angle between the **long axes of the arm and forearm** when the **elbow is fully extended**.
- About **15°**, and **wider in females**.
- **Importance**: lets the forearm **clear the hip** when the arm swings during walking, and is important in **carrying objects**.
- **Abnormalities**: **decreased = cubitus varus**; **increased = cubitus valgus**.

> ⚠️ **Exam trap:** The carrying angle is measured **with the elbow fully extended** (and the forearm supinated). Varus = **less** angle; valgus = **more** angle.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Synovial hinge (uniaxial), compound (3 bones): trochlea + capitulum ↔ trochlear notch + radial head.
- Capsule thin in front and behind, thick at the sides.
- Radial collateral: lateral epicondyle → annular ligament. Ulnar collateral: medial epicondyle → coronoid (anterior), olecranon (posterior), coronoid ↔ olecranon (lower); crossed by the ulnar nerve.
- Movements: flexion & extension. Carrying angle ≈ 15°, wider in females; ↓ varus, ↑ valgus.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Give the type and articular surfaces of the elbow joint. Why is it a compound joint?</summary>

**Answer:** It is a synovial joint of the hinge variety (uniaxial). Articular surfaces: the trochlea and capitulum of the lower end of the humerus, with the trochlear notch of the ulna and the head of the radius. It is compound because three bones (humerus, radius and ulna) take part.

**Why:** Type + surfaces + "3 bones" gives the full mark.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the ulnar collateral ligament of the elbow.</summary>

**Answer:** A strong triangular ligament on the medial side with its apex attached to the medial epicondyle. Its anterior border runs to the medial border of the coronoid process, its posterior border to the medial margin of the olecranon, and its lower border runs from the coronoid process to the olecranon. It is crossed by the ulnar nerve.

**Why:** Three borders + the ulnar nerve relation.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Define the carrying angle and give its importance and abnormalities.</summary>

**Answer:** The angle between the long axes of the arm and forearm when the elbow is fully extended — about 15°, wider in females. It allows the forearm to clear the hip during arm swing in walking and is important in carrying objects. A decreased angle is cubitus varus; an increased angle is cubitus valgus.

**Why:** Definition + value + importance + abnormalities.
</details>

## 2. Proximal (superior) radio-ulnar joint, pronation & supination

### Type and surfaces
- **Synovial, uniaxial pivot joint**.
- **Articular surfaces**: **head of the radius** rotating within a ring formed by the **radial notch of the ulna + annular ligament**.

### Capsule
- **Covers the annular ligament**.
- **Above**: continuous with the capsule of the elbow joint — **their joint cavities are continuous**.
- **Below**: loosely attached to the **neck of the radius** (so the radius can rotate).
- The **synovial membrane is a downward prolongation** of the elbow's synovial membrane.

### Ligaments
1. **Annular ligament** — strong and curved; forms **¾ of the ring** (the radial notch forms the rest).
2. **Quadrate ligament** — a thin quadrangular fibrous band from the **lower border of the radial notch** (ulna, medially) to the **neck of the radius** (laterally); **closes the joint inferomedially**.

### Movements: pronation and supination — the **axis of movement is the ulna**
| | Initiated by | Completed by |
|---|---|---|
| **Supination** | **brachioradialis** (from full pronation → midprone) | **supinator** (elbow extended) and **biceps** (elbow flexed) |
| **Pronation** | **brachioradialis** (from full supination → midprone) | **pronator teres** and **pronator quadratus** |

> ⚠️ **Exam trap:** **Brachioradialis starts both movements** — it brings the forearm to the **midprone** position from either extreme. Biceps completes supination **only when the elbow is flexed**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pivot (uniaxial) joint: radial head ↔ radial notch + annular ligament.
- Cavity and synovium continuous with the elbow joint; capsule loosely attached below to the radial neck.
- Annular ligament = ¾ of the ring; quadrate ligament closes the joint inferomedially.
- Supination: brachioradialis → supinator (extended) / biceps (flexed). Pronation: brachioradialis → pronator teres + quadratus. Axis = ulna.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the ligaments of the proximal radio-ulnar joint.</summary>

**Answer:** The annular ligament is strong and curved and forms three-quarters of the ring around the radial head, the radial notch of the ulna forming the rest. The quadrate ligament is a thin quadrangular fibrous band extending from the lower border of the radial notch of the ulna medially to the neck of the radius laterally, closing the joint inferomedially.

**Why:** Two ligaments with their shape and attachments.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the relation between the proximal radio-ulnar joint and the elbow joint?</summary>

**Answer:** The capsule of the radio-ulnar joint is continuous above with that of the elbow joint, their joint cavities are continuous, and its synovial membrane is a downward prolongation of the synovial membrane of the elbow joint.

**Why:** "Continuous cavities" is the key phrase.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which muscles produce pronation and supination?</summary>

**Answer:** Both are initiated by brachioradialis, which rotates the radius from full pronation or full supination to the midprone position. Supination is completed by supinator (elbow extended) and biceps (elbow flexed); pronation is completed by pronator teres and pronator quadratus. The axis of movement is the ulna.

**Why:** The initiate/complete table in one answer.
</details>

## 3. The cubital fossa

### Definition, shape and boundaries
- **Intermuscular space in front of the elbow**, **triangular**, with the **base above** and **apex below**.

| Boundary | Formed by |
|---|---|
| **Base** | **intercondylar line** between the two humeral **epicondyles** |
| **Medial border** | **pronator teres** |
| **Lateral border** | **brachioradialis** |
| **Apex** | meeting of the medial and lateral borders |
| **Floor** | **brachialis** (above & medially) + **supinator** (below & laterally) |
| **Roof** | skin + superficial fascia + **deep fascia reinforced by the bicipital aponeurosis** |

**Superficial fascia of the roof contains:**
- **Basilic vein** with the **medial cutaneous nerve of the forearm** (medially).
- **Cephalic vein** with the **lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm** (laterally).
- **Median cubital vein** connecting the basilic and cephalic veins.

### Contents — from medial to lateral (the lecture's "T.A.N." figure, read lateral→medial: **T**endon, **A**rtery, **N**erve)
1. **Median nerve** → leaves between the **two heads of pronator teres**.
2. **End of the brachial artery** and the start of the **radial** and **ulnar** arteries:
   - **Radial artery** leaves **superficial to pronator teres**.
   - **Ulnar artery** leaves **deep to pronator teres**.
3. **Biceps tendon** → passes backwards to the **radial tuberosity**.
4. **Radial nerve** and its **posterior interosseous** branch:
   - Radial nerve leaves **deep to brachioradialis**.
   - **Posterior interosseous nerve** leaves by **piercing the supinator**.
- ⭐ N.B. the fossa contains **one lymph node** — the **epitrochlear (supratrochlear) node**, in front of the **medial epicondyle**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Triangle in front of the elbow: base = line between epicondyles; medial = pronator teres; lateral = brachioradialis.
- Floor: brachialis + supinator. Roof: skin, superficial fascia (basilic, cephalic, median cubital veins + cutaneous nerves), deep fascia + bicipital aponeurosis.
- Contents medial→lateral: median nerve, brachial artery (→ radial & ulnar), biceps tendon, radial nerve + posterior interosseous nerve; epitrochlear lymph node.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the boundaries of the cubital fossa.</summary>

**Answer:** Base: the intercondylar line between the two humeral epicondyles. Medial border: pronator teres. Lateral border: brachioradialis. Apex: meeting of the two borders. Floor: brachialis (above and medially) and supinator (below and laterally). Roof: skin, superficial fascia containing the basilic, cephalic and median cubital veins with the medial and lateral cutaneous nerves of the forearm, and deep fascia reinforced by the bicipital aponeurosis.

**Why:** A classic "boundaries" question — list all six.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Enumerate the contents of the cubital fossa from medial to lateral.</summary>

**Answer:** 1) Median nerve (leaves between the two heads of pronator teres); 2) the terminal brachial artery and the beginning of the radial artery (leaves superficial to pronator teres) and ulnar artery (leaves deep to pronator teres); 3) biceps tendon (to the radial tuberosity); 4) radial nerve (leaves deep to brachioradialis) and its posterior interosseous branch (pierces supinator). It also contains the epitrochlear lymph node.

**Why:** Order + how each structure leaves the fossa.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What lies in the roof of the cubital fossa?</summary>

**Answer:** Skin; superficial fascia containing the basilic vein with the medial cutaneous nerve of the forearm (medially), the cephalic vein with the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm (laterally), and the median cubital vein connecting them; and deep fascia reinforced by the bicipital aponeurosis.

**Why:** The veins of the roof are where blood is drawn — a common question.
</details>

## 4. Nerves and arteries around the elbow — two rules

**Rule 1 — nerves never pass between bones; they pass between the two heads of muscles:**
- **Radial** (posterior interosseous) nerve → between the two heads of **supinator**.
- **Median** nerve → between the two heads of **pronator teres**.
- **Ulnar** nerve → through the fibrous arch between the two heads of **flexor carpi ulnaris**.

**Relations of the two heads of pronator teres (humeral and ulnar):**
- **Median nerve**: **between** the two heads.
- **Radial artery**: **superficial** to both heads.
- **Ulnar artery**: **deep** to both heads.

**Rule 2 — arteries may pass between bones:** the **posterior interosseous artery** passes between the **ulna and radius** (on either side of the interosseous membrane with the anterior interosseous artery, both from the **common interosseous** branch of the ulnar artery).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Nerves pass between two heads of a muscle: radial–supinator, median–pronator teres, ulnar–flexor carpi ulnaris.
- Pronator teres: median nerve between its heads, radial artery superficial, ulnar artery deep.
- Arteries can pass between bones (posterior interosseous artery between radius and ulna).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. State Rule 1 of the relations of nerves around the elbow with examples.</summary>

**Answer:** Nerves never pass between bones but pass between the two heads of muscles: the radial (posterior interosseous) nerve between the two heads of supinator, the median nerve between the two heads of pronator teres, and the ulnar nerve through the fibrous arch between the two heads of flexor carpi ulnaris.

**Why:** Rule + three examples.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the relations of the two heads of pronator teres.</summary>

**Answer:** The median nerve passes between the humeral and ulnar heads; the radial artery passes superficial to both heads; the ulnar artery passes deep to both heads.

**Why:** Nerve between, radial artery superficial, ulnar artery deep.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do arteries differ from nerves in their relation to bones at the elbow?</summary>

**Answer:** Nerves never pass between bones, but arteries may — for example, the posterior interosseous artery passes between the ulna and radius.

**Why:** Rule 2 contrasts with Rule 1.
</details>

## 🔥 Final review — memorize before the exam

1. Elbow = **synovial hinge, uniaxial, compound (3 bones)**: trochlea + capitulum ↔ trochlear notch + radial head.
2. Capsule **thin front & back, thick at the sides**.
3. **Radial collateral**: lateral epicondyle → **annular ligament**. **Ulnar collateral**: medial epicondyle → coronoid / olecranon / coronoid↔olecranon; **crossed by the ulnar nerve**.
4. Movements: **flexion & extension**.
5. **Carrying angle ≈ 15°**, wider in females; **↓ varus, ↑ valgus**.
6. Proximal radio-ulnar joint = **pivot**; **annular ligament ¾ of the ring**; **quadrate ligament** closes it inferomedially; cavity **continuous with the elbow**.
7. **Brachioradialis initiates** both movements; supination completed by **supinator (extended) / biceps (flexed)**; pronation by **pronator teres + quadratus**; axis = **ulna**.
8. Cubital fossa: base = **line between epicondyles**, medial = **pronator teres**, lateral = **brachioradialis**, floor = **brachialis + supinator**, roof reinforced by **bicipital aponeurosis**.
9. Contents medial→lateral: **median nerve, brachial artery, biceps tendon, radial nerve** (+ epitrochlear node).
10. Pronator teres: **median nerve between**, **radial artery superficial**, **ulnar artery deep**.
11. Nerves pass between **muscle heads**, never bones; arteries may pass between bones (**posterior interosseous**).
