## 1. Definition, bone strength and remodeling

- ⭐ **Osteoporosis** = **systemic skeletal disease: ↓ bone mass + deteriorated microarchitecture → ↑ fragility and fracture risk**; the key consequence is **fragility fracture** (**hip, vertebrae, distal radius, proximal humerus**).
- **Bone strength = quantity (BMD) + quality** (microarchitecture, turnover, mineralization, collagen quality, microdamage, cortical thickness); **BMD alone does not determine fracture risk.**
- **Remodeling:** **osteoclasts resorb, osteoblasts form**, normally balanced. **Osteocytes** sense **mechanical loading**, regulate remodeling and make **sclerostin** (inhibits **Wnt/β-catenin** → ↓ osteoblasts; blocked by **romosozumab**).
- **RANK/RANKL/OPG:** **RANKL** (from osteoblast-lineage cells) binds **RANK** on osteoclast precursors → **differentiation and activation (↑ resorption)**; **OPG** is a **decoy receptor** → **inhibits osteoclastogenesis.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Osteoclasts resorb; osteoblasts form; osteocytes sense load.
- RANKL ↑ resorption; OPG blocks it; sclerostin blocks osteoblasts.

</div>

## 2. Pathophysiology, types and causes

- **Pathophysiology:** **resorption > formation** → **trabecular loss, cortical thinning, ↑ cortical porosity.**
- **Peak bone mass** in **early adulthood** (genetics, nutrition, activity, vitamin D/calcium, sex hormones, disease); lower peak = higher lifetime risk.
- **Postmenopause:** **↓ estrogen → ↑ RANKL → ↑ osteoclasts → ↑ resorption**, mainly **trabecular (vertebral)** bone. **Aging:** **↓ osteoblasts, ↓ calcium absorption, vitamin D changes, muscle loss, falls, ↑ cortical porosity.**
- **Primary:** **Type 1 = postmenopausal (estrogen deficiency); Type 2 = age-related (senile), both sexes.**
- **Secondary causes:** **endocrine** (hyperthyroidism, hyperparathyroidism, Cushing, hypogonadism, diabetes, premature menopause); **GI** (celiac, malabsorption, bariatric surgery, IBD); **renal** (CKD); **hematological** (myeloma, leukemia); **RA**; **drugs** (**glucocorticoids**, aromatase inhibitors, androgen deprivation, some antiseizure drugs, excess thyroid hormone, cancer therapies).
- **Glucocorticoid-induced:** **↓ osteoblasts, ↑ osteoblast/osteocyte apoptosis, ↑ early osteoclasts, ↓ gut calcium absorption, ↑ renal calcium loss, gonadal suppression**; **rapid bone loss.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Type 1 postmenopausal; type 2 senile.
- Glucocorticoids are the commonest drug cause.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Falling estrogen raises RANKL and osteoclast activity, increasing resorption and reducing mainly trabecular bone.

## 3. Risk factors, presentation and examination

- **Risk factors:** **age, female sex, previous fragility fracture, parental hip fracture, low BMI, early menopause, hypogonadism, long-term glucocorticoids, smoking, excess alcohol, RA, frequent falls, low activity.**
- **Fragility fracture:** from a fall at **standing height or less**; a prior one **substantially increases** later risk.
- **Presentation:** **silent disease** until fracture; **vertebral compression, hip or wrist fracture, loss of height, kyphosis, back pain.** **Vertebral fractures:** acute or chronic back pain, kyphosis, height loss, **dowager's hump** (multiple fractures); may be **silent.**
- **Hip fracture:** **loss of independence, prolonged stay, immobility, VTE, pneumonia, pressure injuries, ↑ mortality** → triggers **osteoporosis assessment and secondary prevention.**
- **Examination:** **height, kyphosis, vertebral tenderness, gait, weakness, balance, previous fracture, endocrine signs** + **fall risk** (vision, gait, balance, neurology, drugs, home hazards).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Silent until fracture.
- Hip fracture should trigger osteoporosis work-up.

</div>

## 4. Investigations: DXA, FRAX and laboratory tests

| T-score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **≥ −1.0** | **Normal** |
| **−1.0 to −2.5** | **Osteopenia** |
| **≤ −2.5** | **Osteoporosis** |

- **DXA** (lumbar spine, total hip, femoral neck, ± forearm); **T-score** vs **young healthy adult of the same sex**; **T ≤ −2.5 + fragility fracture = severe/established osteoporosis.** **Z-score** vs **same age, sex, ethnicity**; **very low Z → suspect secondary causes.**
- **DXA for:** older women and men, previous fragility fracture, long-term glucocorticoids, secondary causes, high FRAX.
- **FRAX:** **10-year probability of major osteoporotic fracture (hip, vertebral, forearm, proximal humerus) and hip fracture**, clinical factors ± femoral-neck BMD.
- **Laboratory aims:** **exclude secondary causes, correct abnormalities, baseline.** **Common:** CBC, calcium, albumin, ALP, creatinine/eGFR, LFTs, **25-OH vitamin D**, TSH. **If indicated:** PTH, protein electrophoresis, testosterone (men), celiac serology, 24-h urine calcium, cortisol, immunofixation, free light chains.
- **Vitamin D** (25-OH): **calcium absorption, mineralization, muscle**; severe deficiency → **osteomalacia, falls, secondary hyperparathyroidism.**
- **Osteoporosis vs osteomalacia:** **osteoporosis = ↓ quantity with normal mineralization**; **osteomalacia = defective osteoid mineralization: low vitamin D, low/normal calcium, low phosphate, ↑ ALP, secondary hyperparathyroidism.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- T-score: ≥ −1 normal; −1 to −2.5 osteopenia; ≤ −2.5 osteoporosis.
- Osteomalacia: low phosphate, high ALP.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Interpret the T-score.</summary>

**Answer:** At least −1.0 normal, between −1.0 and −2.5 osteopenia, −2.5 or below osteoporosis; with a fragility fracture it is severe osteoporosis.

**Why:** Slides 25-26.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is FRAX?</summary>

**Answer:** A tool giving the 10-year probability of major osteoporotic fracture and hip fracture from clinical risk factors with or without femoral-neck BMD.

**Why:** Slide 28.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Distinguish osteoporosis from osteomalacia.</summary>

**Answer:** Osteoporosis has reduced bone quantity with normal mineralization; osteomalacia has defective mineralization with low vitamin D, low phosphate and high ALP.

**Why:** Slide 31.
</details>

## 5. Pharmacological treatment

| Drug / class | Mechanism | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Alendronate, risedronate, ibandronate, zoledronic acid** | **Bisphosphonates: bind bone mineral, suppress osteoclasts** | **First-line**; **alendronate: empty stomach, plain water, stay upright**; **zoledronate IV annually**; **oral: dyspepsia, esophagitis; IV: acute-phase flu-like reaction**; **limited in renal impairment**; **jaw osteonecrosis risk low** (↑ with high dose, cancer therapy, dental procedures) |
| **Denosumab** | **Anti-RANKL** | **Do not stop abruptly** |
| **Teriparatide, abaloparatide** | **PTH analogues, anabolic** | **Very high risk; then an antiresorptive** |
| **Romosozumab** | **Anti-sclerostin** | **Anabolic + antiresorptive** |
| **Raloxifene** | **SERM** (estrogen agonist on bone) | **Vertebral benefit; VTE risk** |
| **HRT** | **Estrogen ± progestogen** | **Selected younger postmenopausal women; assess breast cancer, VTE, CV risk** |
| **Calcitonin** | — | **Limited role** |

- **Selection depends on:** fracture risk, age, sex, menopausal status, renal function, previous therapy, CV risk, cancer history, patient preference.
- **Anabolic for:** **very high risk, multiple vertebral fractures, severe osteoporosis, fractures on antiresorptives, selected glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis.**
- **Calcium:** prefer **dietary** calcium; excess supplements → **GI effects, kidney stones.**
- **Strategy:** **lower/moderate:** lifestyle, calcium/vitamin D ± antiresorptive; **high:** **bisphosphonate or denosumab**; **very high:** **anabolic (teriparatide, romosozumab) then antiresorptive.**
- **Dental procedures:** do **not** stop therapy automatically; assess risk and maintain oral hygiene.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Bisphosphonates first-line; denosumab must not be stopped abruptly.
- Very high risk: anabolic then antiresorptive.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is oral alendronate taken and why?</summary>

**Answer:** On an empty stomach with plain water, staying upright and avoiding food and other drugs for the required interval, to improve absorption and reduce esophageal irritation.

**Why:** Slide 34.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the adverse effects of bisphosphonates.</summary>

**Answer:** Oral: dyspepsia, esophagitis, upper GI irritation; IV: acute-phase flu-like reaction; caution in renal impairment; rare jaw osteonecrosis.

</details>

**Answer:** Lower risk: lifestyle and calcium/vitamin D; high risk: bisphosphonate or denosumab; very high risk: anabolic agent (teriparatide, romosozumab) followed by an antiresorptive.

**Why:** Slide 47.
</details>

## 6. Special situations, algorithm and prevention

- **Glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis prevention:** assess fracture risk; **lowest effective dose, shortest duration, calcium + vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise, fall prevention, drugs when indicated.**
- **Men:** consider **hypogonadism, glucocorticoids, alcohol, smoking, hyperparathyroidism, hyperthyroidism, myeloma, malabsorption**; a fragility fracture in a man → look for secondary causes.
- **CKD:** **CKD-mineral and bone disorder** (Ca, phosphate, PTH, vitamin D, turnover); low BMD ≠ simple postmenopausal osteoporosis; **specialist care in advanced CKD.**
- **Myeloma (CRAB):** **hypercalcemia, renal impairment, anemia, bone lesions**; think of it with back pain, fragility fractures, anemia, weight loss.
- **Turnover markers:** **P1NP (formation), CTX (resorption)**; for response and adherence only.
- **Algorithm:** **(1) identify fragility fracture or high risk → (2) FRAX ± DXA → (3) secondary causes → (4) correct vitamin D, calcium, falls, modifiable factors → (5) choose drug → (6) monitor adherence, fractures, adverse effects, BMD.**
- **Prevention:** **dietary calcium, vitamin D, weight-bearing (walking, stairs, dancing) and resistance exercise, stop smoking, limit alcohol, fall prevention, adequate protein.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- CRAB for myeloma.
- Six-step algorithm.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How is glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis prevented?</summary>

**Answer:** Assess risk, use the lowest effective dose for the shortest time, ensure calcium and vitamin D, exercise, prevent falls and give drugs when indicated.

**Why:** Slide 41.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. When should myeloma be considered?</summary>

**Answer:** In an older patient with back pain, fragility fractures, anemia, renal impairment, hypercalcemia or weight loss (CRAB).

**Why:** Slide 44.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Give the steps of the clinical algorithm.</summary>

**Answer:** Identify risk, calculate FRAX ± DXA, search for secondary causes, correct modifiable factors, choose medication and monitor.

**Why:** Slides 48-49.
</details>
