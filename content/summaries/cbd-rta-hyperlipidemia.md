# Case-Based Discussion — RTA and Hyperlipidemia — Exam Night Review

Second-stage case-based discussion (team-based learning, week 5): **a patient with hyperlipidemia and a traumatic brain injury after a road traffic accident**, linking **anatomy of the nervous system, lipid metabolism and cell physiology (ionic composition, Na⁺/K⁺ pump, active transport).** Items marked "standard teaching" go beyond the slides. After each topic: **Key points** and 3 short-essay questions (tap a question to reveal the answer).

## 1. The case

- **39-year-old obese woman** after a **road traffic accident**: **severe headache, repeated vomiting, progressive confusion; brief loss of consciousness** at the scene.
- **History:** **hyperlipidemia for one year**, **poor diet adherence**, **stopped lipid-lowering drug**; **no hypertension or diabetes.**
- **Examination:** **drowsy, GCS 13/15; BP 145/90; pulse 58; RR 18; temp 37 °C; BMI 33**; **pupils equal and reactive**; **mild left upper and lower limb weakness (4/5)**; **no external bleeding or long bone fractures.**
- **Interpretation (standard teaching):** raised BP with a slow pulse plus headache, vomiting and drowsiness suggests **raised intracranial pressure**; left-sided weakness suggests a **right hemisphere** problem.

## 3. The reflection question: the Na⁺/K⁺ pump

- **Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase** = **primary active transport**, **electrogenic**: **3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in** (standard teaching).
- **If inhibited:** **intracellular Na⁺ rises and K⁺ falls**, **water enters and the cell swells**, and the **membrane depolarizes** (standard teaching).
- **Link to the case:** failed membrane transport after injury lets water enter neurons → **cerebral edema.**
- **Ionic composition (standard teaching):** **ECF rich in Na⁺ and Cl⁻; ICF rich in K⁺** with protein and phosphate anions.
- **Primary active transport of H⁺** occurs in the **stomach and kidneys** (learning outcome).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pump blocked → Na⁺ in, K⁺ out, swelling, depolarization.
- 3 Na⁺ out for 2 K⁺ in.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Predict the effects of a Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase inhibitor.</summary>

**Answer:** Intracellular sodium increases and potassium decreases, the cell swells because water follows sodium, and the membrane potential becomes less negative.

**Why:** Reflection slide.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the Na⁺/K⁺ pump.</summary>

**Answer:** A primary active, electrogenic ATPase moving 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in, maintaining membrane potential and cell volume.

**Why:** Learning outcome 5 and standard teaching.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How does the pump relate to cerebral edema?</summary>

**Answer:** Disrupted membrane transport lets water enter neurons, causing swelling of the cells and cerebral edema.

**Why:** Slide 6.
</details>

## 4. Learning outcomes of week 5

1. **Clinical application:** evaluate a case of **hyperlipidemia with traumatic brain injury.**
2. **Anatomy:** divisions of the **central and peripheral nervous systems.**
3. **Biochemistry:** **lipid metabolism** and cardiovascular risk of abnormal **LDL, HDL, VLDL and IDL.**
4. **Cell physiology:** compare **ionic composition of extracellular and intracellular fluids**, **cell membrane structure** and **passive transport (diffusion).**
5. **Active transport:** **protein channels, the electrogenic Na⁺/K⁺ pump, primary active transport of H⁺ in the stomach and kidneys.**
