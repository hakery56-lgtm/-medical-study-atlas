## 1. Objectives, species and introduction

### The three human species
| | ***T. vaginalis*** | ***T. hominis*** | ***T. tenax*** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disease | **vaginal trichomoniasis (STD)** | **intestinal trichomoniasis** | **gingivitis** |
| Transmission | **sexual intercourse** | **orally** | **contaminated mouth cavity** (kissing, salivary droplets) |
| Cyst stage | **NO** | **YES** | **NO** |
| Habitat | **urogenital system** (vagina, urethra) | **human intestine** (ileum, cecum) | **oral cavity** (gums, between teeth, calcified matter) |

- **Infective stage of vaginal trichomoniasis = trophozoite**; **diagnostic stage = trophozoite**; **host = only humans**.

### Introduction
- **First observed by Alfred Donné in 1836 in vaginal secretion**; an **STD — sexual transmission is the usual mode**.
- **Only humans are hosts; no cyst; does not survive well outside the body**; **> 100 Trichomonas species, only 3 infect humans**.
- A **flagellate protozoan WITHOUT a kinetoplast** (like *Giardia*); **person-to-person by sexual intercourse**; **babies may be infected during birth → conjunctivitis**.
- **Often coexists with other STDs:** **vaginal candidiasis, gonorrhea, syphilis, HIV**.
- **Incubation 4 days–4 weeks**; **obligate parasite of the urogenital system**.

**Key points**
- T. vaginalis: STD, sexual, urogenital, no cyst; T. hominis: oral, intestine, has cyst; T. tenax: mouth, gingivitis, no cyst.
- Infective and diagnostic stage = trophozoite; humans only; Donné 1836.
- Coexists with candidiasis, gonorrhea, syphilis, HIV; newborn conjunctivitis; incubation 4 days–4 weeks.

</div>

## 2. Morphology and life cycle

### Morphology (all species share the same morphology)
- **Only the trophozoite stage**; **spindle, oval or pear-shaped**, **10–30 µm long × 5–10 µm wide**, **colorless**.
- **Four free anterior flagella**, a **costa**, a **posterior axostyle** and **one short undulating membrane reaching the middle of the body** → **spiral movement**.
- **Granular cytoplasm** with **one oval or spherical nucleus near the anterior end**, with **parabasal bodies** around it.
- **Lives at acidic pH ≈ 4.5**; **multiplies by longitudinal binary fission**; **rapid spiral motility, "like a falling leaf"**.
- **Species are differentiated by site, nucleus shape, size and length of the undulating membrane.**
- ***T. vaginalis* is the largest (≈ 27 × 18 µm), oval nucleus, rounded anterior end, pointed posterior end, short undulating membrane (to half of the body).**

### Life cycle (one human host, male or female)
- The **trophozoite cannot survive outside**; infection is **directly by sexual intercourse** (or **contaminated internal clothes, contaminated water**).
- It **multiplies by binary fission on the mucosa of the vagina or urethra**.
- **Females:** **vagina, cervix, Bartholin's glands, urethra, urinary bladder**. **Males:** **mainly the anterior urethra**, also **prostate and urinary tract**.
- Found on the **surface of squamous (not columnar) epithelium**; **non-invasive; disease only in the urogenital system, none extra-genital**.
- **High incidence in prostitutes and sexually promiscuous groups; found in all climates; one of the most common STDs worldwide.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Trophozoite only: pear/oval, 10–30 × 5–10 µm, 4 free flagella, costa, axostyle, short undulating membrane (to mid-body), 1 nucleus.
- pH 4.5, longitudinal binary fission, falling-leaf spiral motility; T. vaginalis largest ≈ 27 × 18 µm.
- Female: vagina, cervix, Bartholin's glands, urethra, bladder; male: anterior urethra, prostate. Squamous epithelium, non-invasive.

</div>

## 3. Pathogenesis, clinical features and complications

- Trophozoites reach the vaginal mucosa, **grow in acidic conditions, feed on cells and bacteria**, and multiply from the **upper vagina to the cervix**, **without invading the vaginal wall**.
- They **secrete cysteine proteases, lactic acid and acetic acid** that **disrupt glycogen levels and lower the pH** of the vaginal fluid; **incubation 1–4 weeks** → **acute or chronic trichomoniasis**.

### Female trichomoniasis
- **Heavy infection / severe inflammation of the vagina and vulva**: **severe pruritus**, **yellowish-green, often frothy, foul-smelling discharge, acidic (pH 4.5)**, irritation, pain, congestion, **urethritis, cystitis, dysuria**, **painful coitus (dyspareunia)**.
- **Cervical erosion** is common (may be complicated by bacteria, fungi or spirochetes).
- **Important complications: endometritis and pyosalpingitis.**
- **Petechial hemorrhage ("strawberry mucosa")**, **metaplasia and desquamation** of vaginal epithelium, **intracellular edema = "chicken-like epithelium" — the most characteristic feature.**

### Male trichomoniasis
- Usually **mild**: **urethritis, inflammation of the seminal vesicles**, **burning on micturition**, **purulent white discharge**, **dysuria**, **epididymitis and prostatitis**.

### Complications (both sexes)
- **Pelvic inflammatory disease** (infection of the uterus and fallopian tubes) → **infertility**; also **cervical or prostate cancer, prostatitis, epididymitis, bleeding after intercourse**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Non-invasive; secretes cysteine proteases, lactic and acetic acid → ↓ glycogen, ↓ pH; incubation 1–4 weeks.
- Female: yellow-green frothy foul discharge, pH 4.5, pruritus, dyspareunia, dysuria; strawberry mucosa; chicken-like epithelium.
- Male: mild; urethritis, burning, purulent white discharge, epididymitis, prostatitis.
- Complications: endometritis, pyosalpingitis, PID, infertility.

## 4. *T. hominis* and *T. tenax*

| | ***T. hominis*** | ***T. tenax*** |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | **Davaine 1860, Leuckart 1879** | — |
| Size | **smaller than T. vaginalis** | **the smallest: 6–10 × 3–4 µm** |
| Features | **spherical nucleus, LONG undulating membrane**; **cyst has a thick wall**; **trophozoite and cyst in semi-formed stool** | similar to the others |
| Site | **ileum and cecum** | **mouth: gums, between teeth, calcified matter** |
| Pathogenicity | **non-pathogenic (normal flora)**; in large numbers → **abdominal pain and diarrhea** | **non-pathogenic**; in large numbers (esp. after tooth extraction) → **gum congestion and redness**; **necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis** |
| Transmission | **orally** | **kissing, salivary droplets (trophozoite, no cyst)** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- T. hominis: smaller, long undulating membrane, cyst present, ileum/cecum, non-pathogenic (diarrhea if many).
- T. tenax: smallest, mouth, no cyst, kissing/saliva, gingivitis.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe T. hominis.</summary>

**Answer:** First identified by Davaine (1860) and Leuckart (1879); similar to T. vaginalis but smaller with a spherical nucleus and a long undulating membrane; it has trophozoite and thick-walled cyst stages found in semi-formed stool; it lives in the ileum and cecum, is generally non-pathogenic, and in large numbers causes abdominal pain and diarrhea.

**Why:** Slide "Trichomonas hominis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe T. tenax.</summary>

**Answer:** The smallest species (6–10 × 3–4 µm), normally present in the mouth on the gums, between teeth and in calcified matter; generally non-pathogenic but in large numbers (e.g. after tooth extraction) it causes gum congestion and redness and plays a role in necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis; it is transmitted by kissing and salivary droplets as the trophozoite (no cyst).

**Why:** Slide "Trichomonas tenax".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which species is the largest and which is the smallest?</summary>

**Answer:** T. vaginalis is the largest (about 27 × 18 µm) and T. tenax the smallest (6–10 × 3–4 µm).

**Why:** Size comparison.
</details>

## 5. Laboratory diagnosis, treatment and prophylaxis

### Direct examination
- **Wet-mount saline preparation** of **vaginal swab, urethral discharge, seminal fluid, prostatic massage fluid, urine deposit**; **fixed smears may be stained with Giemsa**; **motile trophozoites with spiral movement ("falling leaf")**. In males: **urine or prostatic secretions**.
- **Direct fluorescent antibody (DFA): more sensitive than the wet preparation.**

### Indirect examination
- ⭐ **Culture** — recommended when **direct microscopy is negative**; **the most sensitive and best method**; **CPLM medium (cysteine peptone liver maltose)** with antifungal and antibacterial agents; **35–37 °C, anaerobic, optimal pH 5.5–6.0, 3–5 days (< 1 week)**.
- **Molecular:** **DNA hybridization and PCR** — highly sensitive and specific.
- **Serological:** **ELISA** to demonstrate **Trichomonas antigen in vaginal smears**.

### Treatment
- ⭐ **Metronidazole (Flagyl) 2 g orally every 8 hours for one week, or 500 mg orally every 12 hours for 7–10 days, and refrain from sexual contact; safe in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters of pregnancy.**

### Prophylaxis
- **Treat the patient; test (and treat) the sexual partner when necessary; prevention is as for other sexually transmitted infections.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Saline wet mount (motile trophozoites), Giemsa-stained smear, DFA (more sensitive).
- Culture = most sensitive/best (CPLM, 35–37 °C, anaerobic, pH 5.5–6, 3–5 days); PCR; ELISA antigen.
- Metronidazole 2 g q8h × 1 week or 500 mg q12h × 7–10 days; avoid sex; safe in 2nd/3rd trimester.
- Treat the partner.

</div>
