## 1. Facts, functions and the respiratory tract

- ⭐ **Functions:** **gas exchange, acid-base balance, phonation, pulmonary defense, metabolic and endocrine function, handling of bioactive materials.**
- **Defense:** **mucociliary system; antimicrobial agents (IgA, proteases, ROS); alveolar macrophages (PAMs); fibrinolytic system.**
- **Facts:** right lung slightly larger; about **10,000 L of air per day**; surface area like a **tennis court**; capillaries **1,600 km**; **brain anoxia is fatal after more than 2 minutes.**
- **Upper tract:** nose, pharynx, sinuses. **Lower tract:** trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → terminal bronchioles → respiratory bronchioles → alveolar ducts → sacs → alveoli.

| Airway | Features |
|---|---|
| **Trachea** | 25 mm |
| **Bronchi** | 1-10 mm; cartilage + cilia + smooth muscle + mucous and goblet cells |
| **Bronchioles** | < 1 mm; **no cartilage**, well-developed smooth muscle |
| **Terminal bronchioles** | < 0.5 mm; **no mucous glands or goblet cells**, few ciliated cells |
| **Respiratory bronchioles** | < 0.5 mm; non-ciliated, scanty smooth muscle |
| **Alveolar duct, sac, alveoli** | type I and type II alveolar cells |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Six main functions; defense includes cilia, IgA, macrophages and fibrinolysis.
- Bronchioles have no cartilage; terminal bronchioles have no glands or goblet cells.

</div>

## 2. Nose, mucociliary system and airway innervation

- ⭐ **Nose:** **warms, humidifies and filters** the air; **cold dry air damages the epithelium** (e.g. endotracheal tube or tracheostomy).
- **Mucociliary system:** mucus traps particles; **~200 cilia per cell beating 10-20/s** move mucus to the **pharynx** (swallowed or coughed); **smoking destroys cilia.**
- **Sympathetic:** may control tracheobronchial blood vessels; **no demonstrated innervation of human airway smooth muscle**, but **beta receptors** on it cause **bronchodilation and less secretion.**
- **Parasympathetic:** **constricts airways, dilates pulmonary vessels, increases secretion.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Nose = warm, humidify, filter.
- Cilia 10-20 beats/s; smoking destroys them.
- Beta = bronchodilation; parasympathetic = bronchoconstriction.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** It warms it, almost completely humidifies it and filters it.

**Why:** Slide "Respiratory function of the nose".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the mucociliary system.</summary>

**Answer:** A mucus layer traps particles and ciliated cells (about 200 cilia, 10-20 beats/s) move it toward the pharynx to be swallowed or coughed out; smoking destroys cilia.

**Why:** Slide "Mucociliary system".
</details>

## 3. Conducting and respiratory zones; alveoli and surfactant

- ⭐ **Conducting zone** = first **16 generations** (trachea 0, bronchi 1-10, bronchioles 11-15, terminal bronchioles 16); **no gas exchange; ≈ 150 mL = anatomical dead space.**
- **Respiratory zone** = remaining **7 generations:** respiratory bronchioles **17-19**, alveolar ducts **20-22**, alveolar sacs **23**; gas exchange occurs here.
- **Alveolar wall:** **type I** (gas exchange), **type II** (surfactant), **macrophages.**
- **Surfactant:** lipoprotein from **type II cells** (300 million alveoli); **reduces surface tension, stabilizes alveolar size, prevents fluid accumulation.** Lack of it → **RDS in premature babies.**
- **Law of Laplace (P = 2T/r):** without surfactant air flows from small to large alveoli; surfactant equalizes the pressures (e.g. r 2/T 3 gives P 3 and r 1/T 3 gives P 6; with surfactant both P = 2).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Conducting zone = 16 generations, dead space 150 mL.
- Type I = exchange; type II = surfactant.
- Surfactant lowers surface tension and stabilizes alveoli.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the conducting and respiratory zones.</summary>

**Answer:** The conducting zone (trachea to terminal bronchioles, generations 0-16, about 150 mL) does not exchange gas and is the anatomical dead space; the respiratory zone (generations 17-23) contains respiratory bronchioles, alveolar ducts and sacs where gas exchange occurs.

**Why:** Slide "Respiratory tract is divided physiologically".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the functions of surfactant?</summary>

**Answer:** It reduces surface tension (prevents collapse), stabilizes alveolar size and prevents fluid accumulation in the alveoli.

**Why:** Slide "Surfactant".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why does surfactant matter according to the Law of Laplace?</summary>

**Answer:** Without it the smaller alveolus has the higher pressure and empties into the larger one; surfactant lowers surface tension and equalizes pressure in large and small alveoli.

**Why:** Law of Laplace diagram.
</details>

## 4. Pleura and thoracic cage

- ⭐ **Pleura:** **visceral (pain insensitive)** and **parietal (pain sensitive).**
- **Functions:** **lubrication; holding lung and rib cage together; preventing collapse (negative pleural pressure); compartmentalization.**
- **Pleural pressure:** **end of expiration -4 mm Hg; end of inspiration -6 mm Hg.**
- If pleural pressure ≥ atmospheric (**pneumothorax**) the lung **collapses.**
- **Thoracic cage:** respiratory pump, protects lungs, prevents lung collapse.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Parietal = pain sensitive; visceral = pain insensitive.
- Pleural pressure -4 to -6 mm Hg.
- Pneumothorax = lung collapse.

</div>

## 5. Mechanics of breathing

- ⭐ **Respiration includes:** **pulmonary ventilation, gas exchange (external and internal), transport of O2 and CO2, regulation.**
- **Inspiration (active):** **diaphragm 75%** (vertical diameter), **external intercostals 25%** (rib cage raised); accessory: **sternomastoid, anterior serrati, scaleni.**
- **Diaphragm** = **phrenic nerve C3-C5**; cord injury **above C3 is fatal** without ventilation.
- **Expiration (passive at rest):** relaxation of inspiratory muscles + **elastic recoil.** **Forced expiration:** **internal intercostals** + **anterior abdominal muscles.**
- **Elastic recoil:** **lung elasticity 1/3, surface tension 2/3.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Diaphragm 75%, external intercostals 25%.
- Phrenic C3-C5; injury above C3 is fatal.
- Recoil: elasticity 1/3, surface tension 2/3.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe quiet inspiration.</summary>

**Answer:** An active process: the diaphragm descends (75%) and the external intercostals raise the rib cage (25%); accessory muscles are used in exercise.

**Why:** Slide "Pulmonary ventilation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What happens in expiration?</summary>

**Answer:** At rest it is passive (relaxation plus elastic recoil); forced expiration uses the internal intercostals and abdominal muscles.

**Why:** Slide "Expiration".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is injury above C3 fatal?</summary>

**Answer:** The phrenic nerve to the diaphragm arises from C3-C5, so the diaphragm is paralyzed.

**Why:** Slide "Pulmonary ventilation".
</details>

## 6. Compliance, work of breathing and Valsalva

- ⭐ **Compliance = ΔV/ΔP ≈ 120-130 mL/cm H2O** (130 for lung + chest wall) = **1/elasticity.**
- **Increased** in **old age and emphysema.**
- **Decreased (restrictive):** **lack of surfactant; fibrotic/edematous lung; pleural fibrosis; pneumonectomy; muscle disease; chest deformities (kyphosis, severe scoliosis).**
- **Work of breathing:** **compliance work, tissue resistance work, airway resistance work**; normally **2-3%** of energy, up to **30%** in exercise or asthma.
- **Valsalva:** forced expiration against a **closed glottis** (lifting, defecation, coughing) makes intrapleural pressure positive.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Compliance 120-130 mL/cm H2O; the reciprocal of elasticity.
- Decreased in fibrosis, surfactant lack, pleural and chest wall disease.
- Work of breathing 2-3% normally.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define compliance and list causes of decreased compliance.</summary>

**Answer:** The volume increase per unit pressure increase (about 120-130 mL/cm H2O); decreased by lack of surfactant, lung fibrosis or edema, pleural fibrosis, pneumonectomy, thoracic muscle disease and chest deformities.

**Why:** Slides "Lung compliance".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the components of the work of breathing?</summary>

**Answer:** Compliance work, tissue resistance work and airway resistance work; it uses 2-3% of energy at rest and up to 30% in asthma or exercise.

**Why:** Slide "Work of breathing".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the Valsalva maneuver?</summary>

**Answer:** Forced expiration against a closed glottis with inflated lungs, making the intrapleural pressure positive (lifting, defecation, coughing).

**Why:** Slide "Valsalva Maneuver".
</details>

## 8. Lung volumes, capacities and ventilation

| Volume or capacity | Definition | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Tidal volume (TV)** | Each normal breath | **500 mL** |
| **Inspiratory reserve (IRV)** | Extra air after a normal inspiration | **3000 mL** |
| **Expiratory reserve (ERV)** | Extra air expired after normal expiration | **1100 mL** |
| **Residual volume (RV)** | Left after maximal expiration | **1200 mL** |
| **Inspiratory capacity (IC)** | TV + IRV | **3500 mL** |
| **Functional residual capacity (FRC)** | ERV + RV | **2300 mL** |
| **Vital capacity (VC)** | IRV + TV + ERV | **4600 mL** |
| **Total lung capacity (TLC)** | VC + RV | **5800 mL** |

- ⭐ **Minute ventilation** = TV × rate = 500 × 12 = **6000 mL/min.** **Alveolar ventilation** = (TV - dead space) × rate = (500 - 150) × 12 = **4200 mL/min.**
- **Slow deep breathing** gives better ventilation than rapid shallow (effective ventilation **85% vs 40%**; normal 70%).
- **RV** prevents sudden gas changes; measured by **helium dilution, nitrogen washout or plethysmograph** (not a spirometer). Helium: C1 × V1 = C2 × (V1 + FRC).
- **Dead space:** **anatomical 150 mL** (↑ with age and deep breathing); **physiological** = unperfused or poorly perfused alveoli (normally 0).
- **Factors:** age, **sex (25% higher in males)**, build, athletics, **position** (standing > sitting > lying: abdominal contents and ~500 mL blood shifted to the lungs).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- TV 500, IRV 3000, ERV 1100, RV 1200; VC 4600; TLC 5800.
- Alveolar ventilation subtracts dead space.
- Slow deep breathing is more effective than rapid shallow.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define VC, FRC and TLC and give their values.</summary>

**Answer:** VC = IRV + TV + ERV = 4600 mL; FRC = ERV + RV = 2300 mL; TLC = VC + RV = 5800 mL.

**Why:** Slides "Lung volumes and capacities".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Calculate the minute and alveolar ventilation.</summary>

**Answer:** Minute ventilation = 500 x 12 = 6000 mL/min; alveolar ventilation = (500 - 150) x 12 = 4200 mL/min.

**Why:** Slide "Minute respiratory volume".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is the residual volume measured and why is it important?</summary>

**Answer:** Indirectly (helium dilution, nitrogen washout, plethysmograph) because a spirometer cannot; it prevents sudden harmful changes in blood O2 and CO2.

**Why:** Slides "Residual Volume".
</details>

## 9. Pulmonary function tests

- ⭐ **PEFR:** **400-600 L/min**; reduced in obstructive and restrictive disease; Wright peak flow meter.
- **FVC:** maximum air expired after maximum inspiration (**3-4 s**); **reduced in restrictive, may be normal in obstructive.**
- **FEV1:** air expired in the first second; **decreased in both.** **FEV1% = FEV1/VC × 100, normally > 80%**; **reduced in obstructive, normal in restrictive.**

| Condition | Max flow | VC | FEV1 | FEV1/VC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Restrictive** | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | **Normal** |
| **Obstructive** | ↓ | N or ↓ | **Substantially ↓** | **↓** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- FEV1/VC > 80% normally; low in obstructive, normal in restrictive.
- FVC takes 3-4 s.
- PEFR 400-600 L/min.

</div>

## 10. Airway resistance, its control and reflexes

- ⭐ **Chief site of resistance: medium-sized bronchi; least: small and terminal bronchioles** (large total cross-sectional area).
- **Factors:** **airway diameter (main; inversely proportional)**, **lung volume**, **turbulence**, **dynamic compression** (forced expiration, a problem in emphysema).
- **Fixed** resistance: nose, pharynx, larynx, trachea. **Variable:** bronchi and bronchioles.
- **Control:** **vagus (ACh) constricts; adrenaline/noradrenaline dilate; high CO2 dilates, low CO2 constricts; histamine, leukotrienes, SRS-A constrict.**
- **Cough** (lower mucosa, vagal afferent) and **sneeze** (upper mucosa): inspiration of **2.5 L** → glottis closed → forceful expiratory effort (**≥ 100 mm Hg**) → explosive release (**75-100 mph**).
- **Heimlich maneuver:** from behind, fist between navel and rib cage, quick upward thrust.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Highest resistance in medium bronchi; lowest in small bronchioles.
- Vagus constricts; adrenaline and high CO2 dilate.
- Cough: 2.5 L in, pressure ≥ 100 mm Hg, 75-100 mph.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What affects airway resistance?</summary>

**Answer:** Airway diameter (main factor), lung volume, turbulence and dynamic compression during forced expiration.

**Why:** Slides "Airways Resistance".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How are airway muscles controlled?</summary>

**Answer:** Vagal ACh, histamine, leukotrienes, SRS-A and low CO2 constrict; adrenaline, noradrenaline and high CO2 dilate.

**Why:** Slide "Control of respiratory airway muscles".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the cough reflex sequence.</summary>

**Answer:** About 2.5 L is inspired, the epiglottis and cords close, expiratory muscles contract raising pressure to 100 mm Hg or more, then the cords open and air explodes out at 75-100 mph.

**Why:** Slide "In both cough or sneeze reflex".
</details>

## 11. Respiratory membrane and diffusion

- **Respiratory membrane (6 layers):** **surfactant fluid layer → alveolar epithelium → epithelial basement membrane → interstitial space → capillary basement membrane → capillary endothelium.**
- ⭐ **Diffusion depends on:** **membrane thickness (edema, fibrosis), surface area (emphysema), diffusion coefficient (solubility/molecular weight), pressure difference.**
- **CO2 diffuses 20× faster than O2; O2 2× faster than N2.** At **14,000 m** consciousness is lost.
- **Diffusing capacity:** **21-25 mL/min/mm Hg for O2** (400-450 for CO2); **↑ exercise, ↓ pulmonary fibrosis.**

| Gas (mm Hg) | Atmospheric | Humidified | Alveolar | Expired |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **N2** | 597 | 563.7 | 569 | 566 |
| **O2** | 159 | 149 | **104** | 120 |
| **CO2** | 0.3 | 0.3 | **40** | 27 |
| **H2O** | 37 | 47 | 47 | 47 |

- **Note:** the slide prints atmospheric water vapor as 37 (the total is 760 only if it is 3.7); no question is built on it.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Six layers; thickness, area, coefficient and pressure gradient determine diffusion.
- CO2 diffuses 20× faster than O2.
- Alveolar PO2 104 and PCO2 40 mm Hg.

</div>

## 12. Ventilation/perfusion (V/Q)

- ⭐ **Normal V/Q = 4-5 L/min ÷ 4-5 L/min = 0.8-1.0.**
- **Upright lung:** apex **V/Q 3.40** (ventilation 0.24, flow 0.07); base **V/Q 0.63** (ventilation 0.82, flow 1.29).
- **Dead space:** ventilated but not perfused (**V/Q = ∞**): **pulmonary embolism, hypotension, breathing against high pressure, emphysema.**
- **Shunt:** perfused but not ventilated (**V/Q = 0**): **pulmonary edema, pneumonia, emphysema, pulmonary contusion.**
- **Autoregulation:** ↑ ventilation → ↓ local PCO2 → **bronchoconstriction**; ↓ ventilation → ↓ local O2 → **vasoconstriction.**
- **Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction:** shunts blood away from the lungs in **fetal life** and from **poorly ventilated areas** after birth.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Normal V/Q 0.8-1.0; dead space = ∞; shunt = 0.
- Hypoxic vasoconstriction improves matching.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the normal V/Q and its extremes.</summary>

**Answer:** Normal 0.8-1.0; dead space (ventilated, not perfused) = infinity; shunt (perfused, not ventilated) = zero.

**Why:** Slide "Ventilation/Perfusion Ratio".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give causes of increased and decreased V/Q.</summary>

**Answer:** Increased: pulmonary embolism, hypotension, breathing against high pressure, emphysema. Decreased: pulmonary edema, pneumonia, emphysema, pulmonary contusion.

**Why:** Slide with the two lists.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the roles of hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction?</summary>

**Answer:** In fetal life it diverts blood away from the lungs; after birth it diverts blood from poorly ventilated regions to improve V/Q matching.

**Why:** Slide "Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction".
</details>

## 13. Transport of O2 and CO2

- ⭐ **O2:** **97% on Hb** (oxy-Hb), **3% dissolved.**
- **CO2:** **7% dissolved, 70% as carbonic acid** (inside RBCs), **23% as carbaminohemoglobin.**
- **Venous admixture:** bronchial vein blood (PO2 **40**) mixes with pulmonary vein blood (**104**) → systemic arterial PO2 **≈ 95 mm Hg.**
- **O2-Hb curve:** **S-shaped**, steep **10-60** and flat **70-100 mm Hg.**
- **Right shift (↓ affinity, ↑ P50):** **↑ H+ (pH 7.4 → 7.2), ↑ CO2, ↑ 2,3-DPG, ↑ temperature.** Left in the lungs, right in the tissues.
- **2,3-DPG:** from **RBC glycolysis**, binds the **beta chain**, **↑ in chronic hypoxia, ↓ in stored blood.**
- **Haldane:** O2 loading helps CO2 unloading. **Bohr:** CO2 loading helps O2 unloading.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- O2 97% Hb; CO2 70% as carbonic acid.
- Right shift: H+, CO2, 2,3-DPG, temperature.
- Bohr and Haldane are reciprocal effects.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How are O2 and CO2 transported?</summary>

**Answer:** O2: 97% with Hb, 3% dissolved. CO2: 7% dissolved, 70% as carbonic acid in the RBCs, 23% as carbaminohemoglobin.

**Why:** Slides "Transport of oxygen" and "Carbon dioxide".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which factors shift the Hb-O2 curve to the right?</summary>

**Answer:** Increased H+, CO2, 2,3-DPG and temperature; Hb then has less affinity for O2 (higher P50).

**Why:** Slide "The factors that displace the curve to the right".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the significance of 2,3-DPG in blood banking?</summary>

**Answer:** Stored RBCs lose the ability to make 2,3-DPG, so modern storage adds energy substrates and phosphate sources.

**Why:** Slide "2,3 DPG".
</details>

## 14. Regulation of respiration

- ⭐ **DRG:** basic rhythm (diaphragm via phrenic, external intercostals, VRG). **VRG:** medulla, inspiration and expiration, **active only when drive is increased.** **Pneumotaxic center (upper pons):** controls **duration of inspiration.**
- **Central chemoreceptors:** brain; **80% of the drive**; sensitive to **CSF H+**; **adapt within days**; **CO2 crosses the BBB quickly, H+ poorly.**
- **Peripheral chemoreceptors:** **aortic and carotid bodies**; sense **low arterial PO2** (dissolved O2); also low blood flow; **non-adaptable**; small normal role. **PO2 20-40 mm Hg is fatal.**
- **High altitude:** hypoxia stimulates little at first (CO2 washout), strongly after adaptation.
- **Reflexes:** **Hering-Breuer** (protective above 1.5 L), **J receptors** (distended vessels → tachypnea), chest wall receptors (dyspnea), irritant receptors (cough), proprioceptors, touch/thermal/pain.
- **Brain:** RAS (sleep apnea, SIDS), limbic system, motor cortex, vasomotor center, body temperature.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- DRG sets rhythm; pneumotaxic ends inspiration.
- Central chemoreceptors = 80% of drive (H+ in CSF).
- Peripheral = low PO2, non-adaptable.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare central and peripheral chemoreceptors.</summary>

**Answer:** Central: brain, 80% of drive, sensitive to CSF H+, adapt within days. Peripheral: aortic and carotid bodies, sensitive to low arterial PO2 and low flow, non-adaptable and play a small role normally.

**Why:** Slides "Chemical Control".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the respiratory groups of neurons.</summary>

**Answer:** The DRG sets the rhythm; the VRG (medulla) is active when drive is increased; the pneumotaxic center (upper pons) controls the duration of inspiration.

**Why:** Slide "The dorsal respiratory group".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why does hypoxia stimulate respiration little on first ascent to altitude?</summary>

**Answer:** Hyperventilation washes out CO2, which opposes the stimulation; after several days the center adapts to low CO2 and hypoxia stimulates strongly.

**Why:** Slide "Low arterial PO2".
</details>

## 15. Lung circulation, exercise and aging

- **Blood supply:** **pulmonary artery (major)**; **bronchial artery (minor) = physiological shunt**; **low pressure (23/8), highly compliant**; the **largest, thinnest, most anastomosing capillary system.**
- **Lymphatics:** superficial and deep; the lung is the **richest organ in lymphatics** → **rapid metastasis.**
- **Exercise:** ↑ diffusing capacity, ↑ alveolar ventilation (movement, temperature, epinephrine, cortex), better V/Q in the upper lung, **curve right in muscle and left in the lungs.**
- **Aging:** weak muscles (↓ VC, IRV), ↑ lung compliance offset by ↓ chest compliance (↑ RV, dead space), thickened membrane, fewer cilia and thick mucus.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pulmonary artery = gas exchange; bronchial artery = shunt.
- Rich lymphatics → metastasis.
- Aging: weaker muscles, less cilia, thicker membrane.

</div>

## 16. Clinical terms: hypoxia, cyanosis, hypercapnia, O2 therapy and toxicity

- ⭐ **Hypoxia** (cellular O2 lack; hypoxemia = in blood): **hypoxic, anemic, ischemic, histotoxic.**
- **Cyanosis:** bluish skin when **deoxy-Hb > 5 g/dL.** **Central:** arterial undersaturation or abnormal Hb, **mucous membranes involved** (bluish tongue). **Peripheral:** e.g. **cold weather**, mucous membranes spared (bluish hands).
- **Hypercapnia:** ↑ CO2; with hypoxia in **hypoventilation** and **decreased blood flow.** Chronic hypoxia + hypercapnia shifts the drive **from CO2 to O2**.
- **O2 therapy:** useless in **histotoxic hypoxia**; **dangerous in chronic hypoxia with hypercapnia** (inference: it can remove the O2 drive).
- **O2 toxicity** (80-100% hyperbaric O2 for hours): **superoxide anion, inhibited macrophages, ↓ surfactant, severe arteriolar constriction.** Hyperbaric O2 helps in **gas gangrene and CO poisoning** (< 5 h at 3 atm).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Four types of hypoxia; cyanosis when deoxy-Hb > 5 g/dL.
- Central cyanosis involves mucous membranes; peripheral does not.
- High-dose O2 harms surfactant and macrophages.
