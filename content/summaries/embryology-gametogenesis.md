## 1. Primordial germ cells and teratomas

- ⭐ **Gametes come from PGCs** that appear in the **yolk sac wall at week 4** and migrate by **ameboid movement** to the gonads, arriving by the **end of week 5.**
- **Fertilization** = union of **sperm and oocyte → zygote.**
- **Teratomas:** tumors containing **bone, hair, muscle, gut epithelia**; thought to arise from **pluripotent PGCs that strayed from their migration path**, or from **epiblast cells** (which form all three germ layers).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- PGCs: yolk sac, week 4 → gonads, week 5.
- Teratomas come from stray pluripotent cells.

</div>

**Answer:** In the yolk sac wall at week 4; they migrate by ameboid movement and reach the gonads by the end of week 5.

**Why:** Slide "Gametogenesis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is a teratoma?</summary>

**Why:** Slide "PGCs and Teratomas".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Define fertilization.</summary>

## 2. Oogenesis: before birth and at birth

- ⭐ **Oogenesis** = **oogonia → mature oocytes**, in the **ovarian cortex**; **begins before birth, stops at menopause.**
- **Before birth:** PGCs (**46,XX**) → **oogonia** (mitosis); by **month 3** some become **primary oocytes**; by **month 5** germ cells peak at **~7 million**; then **cell death (atresia)** removes most oogonia and many primary oocytes.
- Surviving primary oocytes enter **prophase of meiosis I**, surrounded by flat follicular cells = **primordial follicle.**
- **At birth:** all primary oocytes **arrested in prophase I**; **600,000-800,000**; by puberty only **~40,000** remain.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- 7 million at month 5; 600,000-800,000 at birth; 40,000 at puberty.
- Primary oocytes stay arrested in prophase I until puberty.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe oogenesis before birth.</summary>

**Answer:** PGCs become oogonia that divide mitotically; by month 3 some form primary oocytes; germ cells peak at about 7 million at month 5; atresia then removes most; survivors enter prophase of meiosis I inside primordial follicles.

**Why:** Slide "Steps of oogenesis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the state and number of oocytes at birth?</summary>

**Answer:** 600,000-800,000 primary oocytes arrested in prophase of meiosis I.

**Why:** Slide "At birth".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is a primordial follicle?</summary>

**Answer:** A primary oocyte surrounded by a layer of flat follicular epithelial cells from the ovarian surface epithelium.

**Why:** Slide "Steps of oogenesis".
</details>

## 3. Follicle development and ovulation

- ⭐ **At puberty:** flat follicular cells become **cuboidal granulosa cells** (**primary follicle**); the **zona pellucida** (glycoproteins) is secreted by granulosa cells and the oocyte.
- **Theca folliculi** (ovarian stroma): **theca interna** (inner, secretory) and **theca externa** (outer, fibrous).
- **Antrum** forms by coalescence of fluid spaces → **vesicular (antral) follicle**; granulosa cells around the oocyte = **cumulus oophorus**; **Graafian follicle ≥ 25 mm.**
- **Ovulation:** one follicle matures; **LH surge** → **meiosis I completes** → **secondary oocyte + first polar body** (unequal size, 23 double-structured chromosomes each); **meiosis II arrests in metaphase ~3 h before ovulation**; **completed only if fertilized**, otherwise degenerates **~24 h** after ovulation.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Granulosa cells + oocyte make the zona pellucida.
- LH surge triggers completion of meiosis I.
- Meiosis II finishes only after fertilization.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the primary follicle and its coverings.</summary>

**Answer:** Cuboidal granulosa cells in a stratified epithelium surround the oocyte, which with them secretes the zona pellucida; stromal cells form the theca interna (secretory) and theca externa (fibrous).

**Why:** Slide "At Puberty".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What changes occur at ovulation?</summary>

**Answer:** An LH surge completes meiosis I forming the secondary oocyte and first polar body; the oocyte then arrests in metaphase of meiosis II about 3 hours before ovulation.

**Why:** Slide "At ovulation".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. When is meiosis II completed?</summary>

**Answer:** Only if the oocyte is fertilized; otherwise it degenerates about 24 hours after ovulation.

**Why:** Slide "At ovulation".
</details>

## 4. Spermatogenesis

- ⭐ **Spermatogonia → spermatozoa** in the **seminiferous tubules**; **begins at puberty, lasts to old age**; **74 days**; stimulated by **FSH.**
- **Before birth:** PGCs (**46,XY**) invade the **sex cords**; no spermatogonia before puberty. **At birth:** large pale germ cells with **supporting cells → Sertoli cells.**
- **At puberty:** sex cords → **seminiferous tubules**; PGCs → **spermatogonial stem cells** → **type A spermatogonia** (start of spermatogenesis; mitotic clones) → **type B** → **primary spermatocytes** (**prolonged prophase 22 days**) → **meiosis I → secondary spermatocytes** → **meiosis II → haploid spermatids.**
- **Sertoli cells** embed spermatogonia and spermatids: **support, protect, nourish, release sperm**; progeny of one type A cell form a **clone** in contact.
- **Hormones:** **LH → Leydig cells → testosterone → Sertoli cells → spermatogenesis**; **FSH → Sertoli cells → testicular fluid and androgen receptor synthesis.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Spermatogonia type A → type B → primary → secondary spermatocyte → spermatid.
- Sertoli cells support germ cells; Leydig cells make testosterone.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the steps of spermatogenesis.</summary>

**Answer:** Spermatogonial stem cells give type A spermatogonia, then type B, then primary spermatocytes (22-day prophase); meiosis I forms secondary spermatocytes and meiosis II forms haploid spermatids.

**Why:** Slides "Steps of Spermatogenesis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What do Sertoli cells do?</summary>

**Answer:** They enclose spermatogonia and spermatids, supporting and protecting them, participating in their nutrition and helping release the mature sperm.

**Why:** Slide 13.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How is spermatogenesis regulated?</summary>

**Answer:** LH stimulates Leydig cells to make testosterone, which acts on Sertoli cells; FSH acts on Sertoli cells to stimulate testicular fluid and androgen receptor synthesis.

**Why:** Slide 13.
</details>

## 5. Spermiogenesis

- ⭐ **Spermiogenesis** = **spermatids → spermatozoa.**
- **Steps:** **1) acrosome formation** (covers half the nucleus; enzymes for penetrating the egg and its layers); **2) nuclear condensation**; **3) neck, middle piece and tail**; **4) shedding of cytoplasm as residual bodies.**
- **~300 million sperm per day.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Acrosome, condensed nucleus, tail, residual bodies.
- 300 million sperm daily.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define spermiogenesis and list its steps.</summary>

**Answer:** Transformation of spermatids into spermatozoa: acrosome formation, nuclear condensation, formation of neck, middle piece and tail, and shedding of cytoplasm as residual bodies.

**Why:** Slide "Spermiogenesis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What is the acrosome?</summary>

**Answer:** A cap covering half of the nuclear surface containing enzymes that help penetrate the egg and its layers at fertilization.

**Why:** Slide "Spermiogenesis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How many sperm are produced daily?</summary>

**Answer:** About 300 million.

**Why:** Slide "Spermiogenesis".
</details>

## 6. Spermatogenesis versus oogenesis and abnormal gametes

| Feature | Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
|---|---|---|
| **Site** | **Testis** | **Ovary** |
| **Start** | **After puberty** | **Fetal life** |
| **End** | **Old age** | **Menopause** |
| **Products** | **4 equal spermatids** per spermatogonium | **1 ovum** per oogonium |
| **Transformation** | **Every spermatid becomes a sperm** | **No transformation; ovum non-motile** |

- **Abnormal gametes:** a follicle may contain **2-3 primary oocytes** (or an oocyte with 2-3 nuclei; usually degenerate); **up to 10% of sperm** have defects (head, tail, giants, dwarfs, fused).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sperm: 4 per cell; ovum: 1 per cell.
- Up to 10% abnormal sperm.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare spermatogenesis and oogenesis.</summary>

**Answer:** Spermatogenesis occurs in the testis after puberty until old age and gives 4 equal spermatids per spermatogonium, each becoming a sperm; oogenesis occurs in the ovary from fetal life to menopause and gives one non-motile ovum per oogonium.

**Why:** Slide "Differences between spermatogenesis and oogenesis".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What abnormal gametes occur?</summary>

**Answer:** Follicles with two or three primary oocytes or oocytes with several nuclei (usually degenerate), and spermatozoa with abnormal heads or tails, giants, dwarfs or fused forms, up to 10% of the total.

**Why:** Slide "Clinical Correlates".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which process starts before birth?</summary>

**Answer:** Oogenesis (spermatogenesis starts at puberty).

**Why:** Slides 4 and 10.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Primary oocytes are arrested in prophase I from before birth until puberty; the secondary oocyte arrests in metaphase II.**
- **Maximum germ cells: 7 million at month 5; 600,000-800,000 at birth; 40,000 at puberty.**
- **PGCs do not become spermatogonia before puberty.**
- **LH works on Leydig cells; FSH works on Sertoli cells.**
- **Spermatogenesis lasts 74 days; the primary spermatocyte prophase lasts 22 days.**
