## 2. Finding a good mentor

- **Active:** **active publication record** in **peer-reviewed journals**.
- **Generous authorship:** a **sponsor, not just a supervisor** — connects you with his **network, peer reviewers, co-investigators**.
- **Dedicated time and reliable availability:** many publications ≠ good mentor if he **cannot control time management**.
- **Constructive and methodological critique:** **insightful feedback** develops **independence**.
- **Uncompromising ethical and scientific integrity:** sets your **ethical compass**.
- **Alignment with your personal goals**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Active, generous, available, constructive, ethical, aligned with your goals.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the criteria of a good mentor.</summary>

**Answer:** Active publication record in peer-reviewed journals; generous authorship (a sponsor who connects you with his network); dedicated time and reliable availability; constructive and methodological critique; uncompromising ethical and scientific integrity; and alignment with your personal goals.

**Why:** Slide "Criteria of a good mentor".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is a mentor with many publications not always good?</summary>

**Answer:** If he cannot control time management and is not reliably available, his publication record does not help the student.

**Why:** Slide "Dedicated time and reliable availability".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What is the value of constructive critique?</summary>

**Answer:** Insightful feedback is essential for developing independence.

**Why:** Slide "Constructive and methodological critique".
</details>

## 3. Courses, team and writing

- **Courses:** too many; **start with Introduction to Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis**.
- **Good team:** find through **your mentor, LinkedIn, social media groups, ResearchGate**.
- **Writing:** **good writer = good reader**; pick a **top-ranked journal**, **read one paper daily**, then **increase gradually**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- First course: systematic review and meta-analysis.
- Team via mentor, LinkedIn, social media, ResearchGate; read one paper daily.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Which course is recommended first?</summary>

**Answer:** Introduction to Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

**Why:** Slide "3rd challenge".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How can you find a good team?</summary>

**Answer:** Through your mentor, LinkedIn, social media groups and ResearchGate.

**Why:** Slide "4th challenge".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do you become a good writer?</summary>

- **Data (3 sources):** **(1) a good organization** (provides good data); **(2) a proper study design** (**systematic review, case report**); **(3) the mentor** (can provide data); the **mentor is the cornerstone** of the journey.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**

## 5. Publication and funding

- **Publication:** **don't start weak**; **find a good journal and read its guidelines carefully**; **learn how to submit**; **learn how to write a good email**.
- **Funding:** **many free ways** — **free journals**; **closed-access** publication (journals offer **open or closed**); **proper study design** (systematic review, case report); **the mentor** — if committed to specific journals, he usually **affords the cost**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Publication: good journal, guidelines, submission, email.
- Funding: free journals, closed access, suitable design, mentor.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What advice is given for publication?</summary>

**Answer:** Do not start with a weak beginning: learn to find a good journal and read its guidelines carefully, learn how to submit a paper and how to write a good email.

**Why:** Slide "8th challenge".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How can research funding be handled?</summary>

**Answer:** Use free journals, publish as closed-access instead of paying for open access, choose a suitable study design such as a systematic review or case report, and rely on a mentor committed to specific journals who usually affords the cost.

**Why:** Slide "9th challenge".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What publication options do many journals give?</summary>

**Answer:** Open or closed access; choosing closed access is a free way to publish.

**Why:** Slide "9th challenge".
</details>
