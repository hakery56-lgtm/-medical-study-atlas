## 1. Why AST, definitions and classification of methods

- ⭐ **AST (Antibiotic Susceptibility Testing)** describes the **susceptibility of bacteria to antibiotics**; it determines **which antibiotic will be most successful in treating the infection in vivo**.
- **Objectives:** (1) study the susceptibility of bacteria to a **specific antibiotic**; (2) provide information for **selection of an appropriate antimicrobial therapy**.
- **Why test continuously?** Bacteria **develop resistance** after **repeated or insufficient doses**, so **more advanced antibiotics** are continually required.
- **Bactericidal** = directly **kills** bacteria. **Bacteriostatic** = **inhibits multiplication**.

### Classification of AST methods
| Group | Methods | Type of result |
|---|---|---|
| **Diffusion** | **Kirby-Bauer**, **Stokes** | **Qualitative / semi-quantitative:** **Susceptible, Intermediate, Resistant** |
| **Dilution** | **Tube dilution**, **agar dilution** | **Quantitative:** **MIC** (minimum inhibitory concentration) |
| **Diffusion + dilution** | **E-test** | **Quantitative:** reads the **MIC** directly |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- AST chooses the right antibiotic and fights resistance caused by repeated or insufficient dosing.
- Bactericidal kills; bacteriostatic inhibits multiplication.
- Diffusion → S / I / R; dilution → MIC; E-test = diffusion + dilution.

</div>

## 2. Kirby-Bauer disk diffusion and modified agar techniques

- ⭐ **Principle:** small **antibiotic-impregnated filter paper disks** are placed on **agar seeded with bacteria**; the antibiotic **diffuses** into the medium. A **clear circular zone of inhibition** around a disk = **no bacterial growth**; **zone size corresponds to sensitivity**.
- The isolate is classified **R, I or S** using **standard tables (breakpoints)**.

### Procedure (agar disk diffusion SOP)
1. **Dip a swab** in the bacterial suspension and **seed the agar**; **rotate the plate 45°** and streak again, then **rotate 90°** and streak once more.
2. Dip **forceps tips in 70% alcohol**, **flame** rapidly, let them **cool**.
3. **Pick up a disc**, place it on the agar and **press gently**.
4. Repeat with **8 different discs**, with **equal spacing**.
5. **Invert** the plates and **incubate at 37°C overnight**.

### Modified agar techniques
| Technique | How the antibiotic is applied |
|---|---|
| **Agar well diffusion** | Introduced into **wells punched in the agar with a sterile cork borer** |
| **Agar incorporation** | **Incorporated into melted agar before pouring** → plates with **uniform drug concentration** |

### Zone diameter interpretive chart (mm)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ampicillin** | 10 µg | ≤ 11 | 12–13 | ≥ 14 |
| **Cephalothin** | 30 µg | ≤ 14 | 15–17 | ≥ 18 |
| **Chloramphenicol** | 30 µg | ≤ 12 | 13–17 | ≥ 18 |
| **Gentamicin** | 10 µg | ≤ 12 | 13–14 | ≥ 15 |
| **Penicillin** | 10 U | ≤ 20 | 21–28 | ≥ 29 |

## 3. The E-test (Epsilometer test)

- ⭐ **E-test** = **gradient diffusion** method: a **plastic strip** impregnated with a **predefined continuous exponential drug gradient**.
- The strip has a **printed concentration scale** matching the drug diffusion profile.
- After incubation an **elliptical zone** of inhibition forms; the **MIC is read where the edge of the ellipse intersects the strip**.
- It is **quantitative** and combines **diffusion with dilution**; the slide's example strip is **AC = amoxicillin**, and strips of **multiple antibiotics can be tested in one go**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- E-test = strip + exponential gradient + printed scale.
- Ellipse intersects the strip → read the MIC directly.
- Quantitative, unlike Kirby-Bauer.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the E-test and what does it measure?</summary>

**Answer:** A quantitative gradient diffusion method using a plastic strip with a continuous exponential antibiotic gradient and a printed concentration scale; it measures the MIC.

**Why:** Slides "Epsilometer Test (Etest)" and "E-test (strip test)".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. How is the MIC read from an E-test strip?</summary>

**Answer:** After incubation an elliptical inhibition zone forms around the strip; the MIC is the value on the scale where the edge of the ellipse intersects the strip.

## 4. Dilution methods: MIC, MBC and the tube dilution procedure

- ⭐ **MIC (minimal inhibitory concentration):** the **lowest concentration (highest dilution)** of antibiotic that **prevents visible growth (turbidity)**; reflects **bacteriostatic** action.
- ⭐ **MBC (minimal bactericidal concentration) = MLC (minimum lethal concentration):** the **lowest concentration that kills** the test bacterium. Determined by **subculturing the last clear MIC tube onto antibiotic-free medium**; the MBC is the **smallest concentration that prevents any growth**. **MBC cannot be done without testing for MIC.**
- The **tube dilution method** is the **standard and most commonly employed** method: **serial dilutions** of the agent in a **liquid growth medium**, inoculated with a **standardized number of organisms** and incubated for a prescribed time.

### MIC procedure (tetracycline example)
| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | **9 sterile capped tubes**, **aseptic technique** |
| 2 | **2.0 ml tetracycline (100 µg/ml)** in tube 1; **1.0 ml sterile broth** in all other tubes |
| 3–5 | **Transfer 1.0 ml** tube 1 → 2 → 3 … to **tube 8**, **mixing and changing pipettes** to prevent carryover |
| 6 | **Remove and discard 1.0 ml from tube 8**; **tube 9 = control, no tetracycline** |
| 7 | Suspend **several colonies** in **5.0 ml Mueller-Hinton broth** → slightly turbid |
| 8 | Dilute **0.2 ml** of it into **40 ml Mueller-Hinton broth** |
| 9 | Add **1.0 ml diluted culture to each tube** → **final tetracycline = one-half** the original |
| 10 | **Incubate at 35°C overnight** |
| 11 | **Highest dilution without visible growth = MIC** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- MIC = lowest concentration with no visible growth (bacteriostatic level); MBC = lowest concentration that kills.
- MBC is found by subculturing clear MIC tubes onto drug-free medium (needs the MIC first).
- 9 tubes; tube 9 = growth control; Mueller-Hinton broth; 35°C overnight.

</div>

## 5. CLSI categories and the drug-resistance spectrum

- ⭐ **CLSI** = **Clinical & Laboratory Standards Institute**; its interpretive categories:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **S — Susceptible** | Infection **may be treated with the recommended dosage regimen** |
| **I — Intermediate** | Response rates **may be lower** than for S; **buffer zone against technical factors** |
| **SDD — Susceptible-Dose Dependent** | Susceptibility depends on **higher drug exposure** (higher doses or more frequent administration) |
| **R — Resistant** | **Not inhibited by achievable concentrations**; clinical efficacy **not reliably shown** |

### Drug-resistance spectrum
| Term | Definition | Options left |
|---|---|---|
| **MDR** — Multi-Drug Resistant | Non-susceptible to **≥ 1 agent in ≥ 3** antimicrobial categories | **Multiple options** left |
| **XDR** — Extensively Drug-Resistant | Non-susceptible to ≥ 1 agent in **all categories except ≤ 2** | **Only 1–2 classes** left |
| **PDR** — Pan-Drug Resistant | Non-susceptible to **all agents in all categories** tested | **ZERO options** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Four CLSI categories: S, I, SDD, R.
- I = buffer zone; SDD = needs higher exposure.
- MDR ≥ 3 categories → XDR only 1–2 categories still active → PDR none.

</div>

**Answer:** Susceptible (treatable with the recommended regimen), Intermediate (lower response rates, buffer zone against technical factors), Susceptible-Dose Dependent (needs higher drug exposure by higher or more frequent dosing) and Resistant (not inhibited by achievable concentrations; efficacy not reliably shown).
