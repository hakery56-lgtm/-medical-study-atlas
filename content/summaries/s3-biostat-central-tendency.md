## 1. Goals of data analysis and the distribution

| Goal | Tool | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| **Describe** the sample | **Descriptive statistics** | Continuous variables: **mean, SD, range, percentiles (incl. median)**; categorical variables: **raw and relative frequencies** |
| **Infer** about the population | **Inferential statistics** | **Confidence intervals, hypothesis tests and p-values, correlation and association, regression** |

- ⭐ Statistics deals with the world as **distributions**, each summarized by a **central tendency** and the **variation around the center**.
- The most important distribution is the **normal (Gaussian)** distribution: **symmetric, bell-shaped**, one side the **mirror image** of the other.
- **Measures of central tendency** characterize the **center** of a sample: **mean, median, mode.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Descriptive = summarize the sample; inferential = draw conclusions about the population.
- Every distribution = center (central tendency) + spread (variation).
- Normal distribution = symmetric bell-shaped curve.

</div>

## 2. The mean (x̄)

- **Mean = sum of all scores ÷ number of scores** (arithmetic mean / average); the **most commonly used** and often the **most appropriate** measure.
- **Symbols:** sample mean = **x̄ (x bar)**; population mean = **µ**. **Formula: x̄ = ∑x / n.**
- **Characteristics:** **always present**, **simple**, **unique**, and ⭐ **highly affected by extreme values (sensitive to outliers).**
- **Example:** five patients aged 50-60 have **mean 56**; a sixth aged **92** raises it to **62**, although only one patient is over 60 → the **median** is the better mid-point.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Mean = ∑x / n; x̄ for a sample, µ for a population.
- Always present, simple, unique.
- Distorted by outliers: use the median when extreme values exist.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the mean and write its formula and symbols.</summary>

**Answer:** The mean is the sum of all scores divided by the number of scores: x̄ = ∑x / n, where x = the values, ∑x = their sum and n = the number of observations. The sample mean is x̄ (x bar); the population mean is µ.

**Why:** Slide "Mean (x̄)" and Figure 2.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. List the characteristics of the mean.</summary>

**Answer:** It is always present (even with two observations), simple, unique (one mean per data set) and highly affected by extreme values.

**Why:** Slide "Mean" (characteristics 1-4).
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why can the mean be misleading? Give the lecture's example.</summary>

**Answer:** Extreme values skew it. Five patients aged 50-60 have a mean age of 56, but adding a sixth patient aged 92 raises the mean to 62, so the median is more suitable.

**Why:** Slide "Mean" (age example).
</details>

## 3. The median

- **Median = the value that divides the ranked distribution into two equal parts** (half above, half below).
- **Arrange the data in increasing order first.** Position = **(n + 1) / 2.**
- **Odd n:** the **middle** value. **Even n:** the **average of the two middle values.**
- **Examples (Figure 3):** n = 10 → 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 → **(3 + 4)/2 = 3.5**; n = 11 → 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10 → **4** (the 6th value).
- **Characteristics:** simple, unique, ⭐ **not affected by extreme values.**
- **Uses:** the average for **skewed** (non-symmetrical) data; useful when **a few values are much larger** than the rest, e.g. **personal income** is reported as the **median**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Order first, then take the (n + 1)/2 th value.
- Even n: average of the two middle values.
- Not affected by outliers: best for skewed data (e.g. income).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the median and explain how to find it for odd and even n.</summary>

**Answer:** The median is the middle value of the ordered data (half the values above, half below), at position (n + 1)/2. With odd n it is the middle observation; with even n it is the average of the two middle values.

**Why:** Slide "Median" and Figure 3.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is income reported as the median rather than the mean?</summary>

**Answer:** The median is not unduly affected by the very high incomes of a very few members of the sample, whereas the mean would be pulled upward.

**Why:** Slide "Uses of the median".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the characteristics and uses of the median.</summary>

**Answer:** It is simple, unique and not affected by extreme values. It represents the average for skewed data and is useful when a few values are much larger than the others.

**Why:** Slide "Median" and "Uses of the median".
</details>

## 4. The mode

- **Mode = the most frequently occurring value.**
- **None, one, or more than one** mode: **unimodal, bimodal, trimodal.** Example with **no mode:** hemoglobin 11.3, 12.5, 14.2, 10.6.
- The **least useful** measure of central location: the most common value may not be near the center, and modes **cannot be used in more elaborate calculations.**
- ⭐ **Unlike the others, the mode can be used for qualitative data** (most preferred food, most frequent disease in outpatients in a month).
- To find it: **order the numbers, count each, the most frequent is the mode.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Mode = most frequent value; can be absent or multiple.
- Only measure usable for qualitative (categorical) data.
- Least useful measure of central location.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Define the mode and the terms unimodal, bimodal and trimodal.</summary>

**Answer:** The mode is the most frequently occurring value. A data set may have no mode, one mode (unimodal), two modes (bimodal), three modes (trimodal) or more.

**Why:** Slide "Mode".
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is the mode the least useful measure, and when is it still helpful?</summary>

**Answer:** The most common value may not be near the center and modes cannot be used in more elaborate statistical calculations. It is still helpful when interested in the most common value or most popular choice, and it can describe qualitative data.

**Why:** Slide "Mode".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How do you find the mode, and give an example with no mode.</summary>

**Answer:** Put the numbers in order, count how many of each, and the number that appears most often is the mode. Hemoglobin values 11.3, 12.5, 14.2 and 10.6 have no mode because each occurs once.

**Why:** Slide "Mode" (characteristic 2 and method).
</details>

## 5. Mean, median and mode in normal and skewed distributions, with the exercise

- ⭐ **Normal (symmetric) distribution:** **mean = median = mode.**
- **Skewed distribution:** the three have **different values** (Figure 4); extreme values affect the **mean more** than the median.
- The mean **"chases the tail"**; the median **"stays home."**

| | Mean | Median | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affected by extreme values | **Yes (highly)** | **No** | Not a calculation |
| Always present | **Yes** | Yes | **No (may be none)** |
| Unique | Yes | Yes | **No (may be several)** |
| Qualitative data | No | No | **Yes** |

### Exercise: plasma volumes (liters) of eight healthy adult males
2.75, 2.86, 3.37, 2.76, 2.62, 3.49, 3.05, 3.12
- **Mean** = 24.02 / 8 = **3.00 liters**.
- **Ordered:** 2.62, 2.75, 2.76, 2.86, 3.05, 3.12, 3.37, 3.49 → **median** = 4.5th value = (2.86 + 3.05)/2 = **2.96 liters**.
- **Mode:** none (all values differ).
- **Notation:** ∑ = sum; xi = individual value; **n** = sample size; **N** = population size.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Normal: mean = median = mode; skewed: they differ.
- Mean follows the tail; median stays near the bulk of the data.
- Exercise answers: mean 3.00 L, median 2.96 L, no mode.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. How do the mean, median and mode relate in normal and skewed distributions?</summary>

**Answer:** In a normal distribution they have the same value; in a skewed distribution they differ, with the mean affected more by extreme values ("chasing the tail") while the median stays home.

**Why:** Slide "Mode" (last paragraph) and Figure 4.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Calculate the mean and median of the eight plasma volumes.</summary>

**Answer:** The sum is 24.02 liters, so the mean is 24.02 / 8 = 3.00 liters. The ordered values give a median of the 4.5th value = (2.86 + 3.05) / 2 = 2.96 liters. There is no mode.

**Why:** Slide "Exercise".
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Which measure would you choose for skewed data, and which for qualitative data?</summary>

**Answer:** The median for skewed data, because it is not affected by extreme values; the mode for qualitative data, because it is the only one of the three that can describe it.

**Why:** Slides "Uses of the median" and "Mode" (characteristic 4).
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **The mean is NOT resistant to outliers; the median is.**
- **Median with even n = average of the two middle values**, not one of them.
- **Order the data BEFORE finding the median.**
- **The mode can be absent or multiple; it is the only measure usable for qualitative data.**
- **x̄ and n = sample; µ and N = population.**
- **Normal distribution: all three equal; skewed: all different.**
