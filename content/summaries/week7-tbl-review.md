## 1. Gluteal region and lower-limb nerves (Q1, Q3, Q7, Q12)

- ⭐ **Nerve emerging inferior to piriformis (Q1):** the **sciatic nerve**; supplies skin of the **lateral leg and foot** (via its branches). It is **not** the adductor nerve (obturator), does **not** supply all gluteal muscles, and does **not** supply the medial leg skin.
- **Burning/tingling over the lateral thigh, worse with tight belts (Q3):** **lateral femoral cutaneous nerve** (meralgia paresthetica, standard teaching).
- **Distal femur fracture, distal fragment pulled posteriorly (Q7):** **gastrocnemius**.
- **Lateral patellar dislocation (Q12):** likely intended answer **forward projection of the lateral femoral condyle**. Note: trochlear groove depth also contributes, so the options overlap; confirm the official key.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Nerve below piriformis | **Sciatic**: skin of lateral leg and foot |
| Lateral thigh burning, tight belts | **Lateral femoral cutaneous** |
| Distal fragment pulled posteriorly | **Gastrocnemius** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sciatic nerve exits inferior to piriformis; hip adduction = obturator nerve.
- Lateral thigh paresthesia = lateral femoral cutaneous nerve.
- Gastrocnemius displaces the distal femoral fragment posteriorly.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

## 2. Neoplasia (Q4, Q5, Q6, Q8, Q9, Q11)

- ⭐ **Malignant neoplasms:** may show **loss of differentiation**; **anaplasia** = large hyperchromatic nuclei, abnormal mitosis, severe pleomorphism (**low normal mitosis is NOT a feature**). Slow growth, a fibrous capsule and diploid DNA belong to benign tumors (standard teaching).
- **Benign neoplasms:** composed of **well-differentiated cells**; no rapid growth, invasion or metastasis.
- **Classification:** **squamous cell carcinoma of skin = malignant epithelial neoplasm**; **lymphoma = lymphoid neoplasm** (not hamartoma or choristoma).
- **Dysplasia (Q6):** likely intended answer is carcinoma in situ, only for full-thickness epithelial dysplasia with an intact basement membrane. Note: dysplasia does not always progress to cancer; the question wording is imperfect, so confirm the official key.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Malignant: loss of differentiation, anaplasia (pleomorphism, hyperchromasia, abnormal mitoses).
- Benign: well-differentiated cells.
- SCC = malignant epithelial; lymphoma = lymphoid neoplasm.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. List the morphologic features of anaplasia.</summary>

**Answer:** Large hyperchromatic nuclei, abnormal mitosis and severe cellular pleomorphism; low normal mitosis is not a feature.

**Why:** TBL Q5.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Contrast benign and malignant neoplasms using the review.</summary>

**Answer:** Benign neoplasms are composed of well-differentiated cells; malignant neoplasms may show loss of differentiation, grow rapidly, invade locally and metastasize.

**Why:** TBL Q4 and Q9.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Classify squamous cell carcinoma of the skin and lymphoma.</summary>

**Answer:** Squamous cell carcinoma is a malignant epithelial neoplasm; lymphoma is a lymphoid neoplasm.

**Why:** TBL Q8 and Q11.
</details>

## 3. Pharmacology of cancer drugs (Q2, Q10)

- ⭐ **Tamoxifen** = **antiestrogen** for postmenopausal women with metastatic breast cancer. Topotecan, tacrolimus, paclitaxel and lomustine are not antiestrogens.
- **Alkylating agents:** **busulfan, lomustine, carmustine, chlorambucil**. **Methotrexate is NOT** an alkylating agent (it is an antimetabolite, standard teaching).

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Tamoxifen = antiestrogen.
- Alkylating: busulfan, lomustine, carmustine, chlorambucil.
- Methotrexate = antimetabolite.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Name the antiestrogen used in metastatic breast cancer in postmenopausal women.</summary>

**Answer:** Tamoxifen.

**Why:** TBL Q2.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Which drug is NOT an alkylating agent?</summary>

**Answer:** Methotrexate; busulfan, lomustine, carmustine and chlorambucil are alkylating agents.

**Why:** TBL Q10.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why are topotecan, tacrolimus, paclitaxel and lomustine wrong answers for an antiestrogen?</summary>

**Answer:** They belong to other classes (topoisomerase I inhibitor, calcineurin inhibitor immunosuppressant, taxane, alkylating nitrosourea), not antiestrogens (standard teaching).

**Why:** TBL Q2 distractors.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Hip adduction = obturator nerve**, not the nerve below piriformis.
- **Low normal mitosis is NOT anaplasia**; anaplasia has abnormal mitosis.
- **Methotrexate is NOT alkylating.**
- **Q6 (dysplasia) and Q12 (patella) are ambiguous**: confirm the official key.
