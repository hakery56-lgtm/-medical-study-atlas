## 1. Michaelis-Menten model

- **Kinetics** = study of **reaction rates**; enzyme kinetics reveals the **catalytic mechanism**, **efficiency** and **substrate affinity.**
- ⭐ The **enzyme-substrate complex (ES)** is the central intermediate: **E + S ⇌ ES → E + P.**
- **Vmax:** maximum velocity when the enzyme is **fully saturated** with substrate.
- **Km (Michaelis constant):** substrate concentration at which the rate is **half of Vmax**; an **inverse** measure of the enzyme's affinity for the substrate.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Central intermediate: ES complex.
- Vmax = velocity at full saturation; Km = [S] at Vmax/2.
- Km is an inverse measure of affinity.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

**Answer:** Enzyme and substrate form an ES complex reversibly (E + S ⇌ ES), which gives enzyme and product (ES → E + P); ES is the central intermediate.

**Why:** Slide 4 (The Michaelis-Menten Model).
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Define Vmax and Km.</summary>

**Answer:** Vmax is the maximum velocity at full substrate saturation; Km is the substrate concentration at which the rate is half of Vmax.

**Why:** Slide 4.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why do we study enzyme kinetics?</summary>

**Answer:** To understand the catalytic mechanism and to determine efficiency and substrate affinity.

## 2. Equation and curve

- **Equation:** ⭐ **v₀ = Vmax [S] / (Km + [S])** (v₀ = initial velocity, [S] = substrate concentration).
- **Low [S]:** v₀ rises almost linearly with [S] → **first-order kinetics.**
- **High [S]:** rate approaches **Vmax**, independent of [S] → **zero-order kinetics.**
- **Km = [S] where v₀ = Vmax/2.** On the graph (Fig. 1) **Vmax** is the velocity extrapolated to infinite [S] (solid red line) and **Km** the [S] at Vmax/2 (dotted red line).

| Region | [S] | Order | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left of the curve | **Low** | **First-order** | v₀ ∝ [S] |
| Plateau | **High** | **Zero-order** | v₀ ≈ Vmax, independent of [S] |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- v₀ = Vmax[S] / (Km + [S]).
- Low [S] first-order; high [S] zero-order.
- Km is read at Vmax/2.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Write the Michaelis-Menten equation and define each term.</summary>

**Answer:** v₀ = Vmax[S] / (Km + [S]); v₀ is the initial velocity, [S] the substrate concentration, Vmax the maximum velocity, Km the Michaelis constant.

**Why:** Slide 5.

## 3. Lineweaver-Burk plot

- The Michaelis-Menten equation can be linearised: ⭐ **1/v₀ = (Km/Vmax)(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax.**
- Plotting **1/v₀ vs 1/[S]** gives a **straight line.**

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| **Y-intercept** | **1/Vmax** |
| **Slope** | **Km/Vmax** |
| **X-intercept** | **-1/Km** |

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Double-reciprocal plot = straight line.
- Y-intercept 1/Vmax; slope Km/Vmax; X-intercept -1/Km.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What is the Lineweaver-Burk plot?</summary>

**Answer:** A double-reciprocal plot of 1/v₀ against 1/[S], a linear form of the Michaelis-Menten equation: 1/v₀ = (Km/Vmax)(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax.

**Why:** Slide 8.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Give the intercepts and slope of the plot.</summary>

**Answer:** Y-intercept = 1/Vmax; slope = Km/Vmax; X-intercept = -1/Km.

**Why:** Slides 8-9 (Fig. 2).
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is the plot useful?</summary>

**Answer:** It gives a straight line from which Vmax and Km can be determined from experimental data.

**Why:** Slides 2 and 8.
</details>

## 4. Meaning of Km and hexokinase vs glucokinase

- **Low Km** = **high affinity**: half-maximal speed at a **low** [S]. **High Km** = **low affinity**: a **high** [S] is needed for half-maximal velocity.
- Both enzymes catalyse **glucose + ATP → glucose-6-phosphate + ADP** (first committed step of glucose metabolism).

| Enzyme | Site | Km for glucose | Affinity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hexokinase I** | Erythrocytes | **~0.05 mM** (very low) | **High** |
| **Glucokinase** | Liver (and pancreas) | **~5-6 mM** (high) | **Low** |

- ⭐ **Hexokinase I:** even when blood glucose falls far below the fasting level (**~5 mM**), RBCs phosphorylate glucose at **near-maximal rates.**
- **Glucokinase:** the liver **processes and stores excess glucose**; its high Km makes activity **proportional to blood glucose.** **Low glucose:** low activity, the liver does not compete with other tissues. **After a meal:** activity rises, promoting **glycogen storage or conversion to fat.**

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Low Km = high affinity; high Km = low affinity.
- Hexokinase I (RBC): Km ~0.05 mM, works near Vmax even in hypoglycaemia.
- Glucokinase (liver): Km ~5-6 mM, tracks blood glucose, stores glucose after meals.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. What does the Km value tell you?</summary>

**Answer:** It measures substrate affinity inversely: low Km means high affinity (half Vmax at low [S]); high Km means low affinity (a high [S] is needed).

**Why:** Slide 11.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is the low Km of hexokinase I important in red blood cells?</summary>

**Answer:** Even when blood glucose falls far below the fasting level of about 5 mM, RBCs can still phosphorylate glucose at near-maximal rates.

**Why:** Slides 12-13.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Explain how glucokinase suits the liver's role.</summary>

**Answer:** Its high Km (~5-6 mM) makes its activity proportional to blood glucose: low when glucose is low (no competition with other tissues) and high after a meal, promoting storage as glycogen or conversion to fat.

**Why:** Slide 13.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps

- **Km is an INVERSE measure of affinity** (low Km = high affinity); answer options calling it a "direct measure" are wrong.
- **X-intercept = -1/Km** (not Km, not 1/Vmax); Y-intercept = 1/Vmax; slope = Km/Vmax.
- **Hexokinase I** has the **low** Km (RBC); **glucokinase** has the **high** Km (liver).
- **Low [S] = first-order; high [S] = zero-order.**
