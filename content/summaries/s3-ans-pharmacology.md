## 1. Scope, anatomy and pathways of the ANS

- The **ANS adjusts visceral function to maintain homeostasis**: automatic CNS control of **heart, vessels and bronchi**; also **gut, glands, pupil, temperature and bladder**.

### Somatic vs autonomic motor output
| Somatic | Autonomic |
|---|---|
| **One motor neuron** from CNS to **skeletal muscle** | **Preganglionic neuron → ganglion → postganglionic neuron → effector** |
| **Nm receptors** at the neuromuscular junction; usually **voluntary** | Effector = **cardiac muscle, smooth muscle or gland**; mostly **involuntary** |
| No ganglia | **Ganglia permit amplification, divergence and local modulation** |

### Sympathetic vs parasympathetic anatomy
| | **Sympathetic (thoracolumbar)** | **Parasympathetic (craniosacral)** |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | **T1–L2** | **CN III, VII, IX, X** and **S2–S4** |
| Ganglia | **paravertebral chain or prevertebral** ganglia | **near or within the target organ** |
| Fibers | **short preganglionic, long postganglionic** | **long preganglionic, short postganglionic** |
| Function | **mobilizes energy, fight-or-flight** | **digestion, secretion, micturition, reproductive functions** |

### Enteric system and adrenal medulla
- **Enteric nervous system:** **myenteric and submucosal plexuses** in the gut wall; local reflexes govern **motility, secretion, intestinal blood flow**; modulated by autonomic input.
- **Adrenal medulla:** **preganglionic sympathetic ACh acts on chromaffin cells** = a **modified ganglion without a postganglionic axon**; releases mainly **epinephrine** (and some norepinephrine) into the **blood**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Somatic: 1 neuron, Nm, no ganglion. Autonomic: pre → ganglion → post → effector.
- Sympathetic: T1–L2, short pre/long post. Parasympathetic: CN III, VII, IX, X + S2–S4, long pre/short post, ganglia near the organ.
- Adrenal medulla = modified ganglion releasing epinephrine (mostly) + NE into blood.
- Enteric NS: myenteric + submucosal plexuses.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Compare the somatic and autonomic motor pathways.</summary>

**Answer:** Somatic: a single motor neuron from the CNS to skeletal muscle acting through Nm receptors, mostly voluntary. Autonomic: preganglionic neuron → ganglion → postganglionic neuron → effector (cardiac muscle, smooth muscle, gland), mostly involuntary; ganglia allow amplification, divergence and local modulation.

**Why:** The ganglion is the key difference.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Compare sympathetic and parasympathetic anatomy.</summary>

**Answer:** Sympathetic is thoracolumbar (T1–L2) with ganglia in the paravertebral chain or prevertebral and short preganglionic/long postganglionic fibers (fight-or-flight). Parasympathetic is craniosacral (CN III, VII, IX, X and S2–S4) with ganglia near or in the target organ and long preganglionic/short postganglionic fibers (digestion, secretion, micturition, reproduction).

**Why:** Ganglion position decides fiber length.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is the adrenal medulla called a modified ganglion?</summary>

**Answer:** Preganglionic sympathetic ACh acts directly on chromaffin cells, which behave like postganglionic neurons without an axon and release epinephrine (mainly) and some norepinephrine into the blood.

**Why:** No postganglionic axon.
</details>

## 2. Transmitters and receptors

### Transmitter maps
| Pathway | Ganglion | Postganglionic transmitter → receptor |
|---|---|---|
| **Sympathetic** | **ACh → Nn** | usually **NE → α/β** |
| **Parasympathetic** | **ACh → Nn** | **ACh → muscarinic (M)** |
| **Adrenal medulla** | **ACh → Nn** | **epinephrine/NE into blood** |
| **Sweat glands** (sympathetic) | **ACh → Nn** | **ACh → muscarinic** |

- Other modulators: **dopamine, VIP, nitric oxide, neuropeptide Y**; **dopamine receptors** take part in **renal vascular** pharmacology.
- ⭐ **Both divisions use ACh at ganglionic Nn receptors.**

### Receptor families
- **Nicotinic (Nn, Nm):** **ligand-gated ion channels**, **fast depolarization**.
  - **Nn (neuronal):** autonomic ganglia (both divisions) and **adrenal medulla**; fast ganglionic transmission.
  - **Nm (muscle):** **somatic neuromuscular junction**; **skeletal muscle contraction**.
- **Muscarinic (M1–M5):** **GPCRs**.
  - **M1:** neuronal and gastric sites; **gastric acid secretion**.
  - **M2:** **heart** — **slows HR and AV conduction**; **atropine blocks it**.
  - **M3:** **gland secretion and smooth-muscle contraction** (bronchi, detrusor).
- **α receptors:**
  - **α1:** **vascular smooth muscle → vasoconstriction**; **radial iris → mydriasis**; **bladder neck contraction**. **Phenylephrine** activates; **prazosin, tamsulosin** block.
  - **α2:** **presynaptic ↓ NE release**; central sites ↓ sympathetic outflow.
- **β receptors:**
  - **β1:** **heart and juxtaglomerular cells** → ↑ HR, contractility, **renin**; **dobutamine** activates, **metoprolol** blocks.
  - **β2:** **bronchi, uterus, skeletal-muscle vessels** → relaxation (bronchodilation, vasodilation); **salbutamol** activates.
  - **β3:** **adipose tissue and bladder detrusor** → **lipolysis**, **detrusor relaxation**; **mirabegron** activates.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Ganglionic ACh → Nn in BOTH divisions; sympathetic post = NE (α/β); parasympathetic post = ACh (M); sweat glands = sympathetic with ACh/M; adrenal medulla → epinephrine into blood.
- Nn = ganglia + adrenal medulla; Nm = skeletal muscle. M1 stomach, M2 heart (↓HR), M3 glands/smooth muscle.
- α1 vasoconstriction; α2 ↓ NE release; β1 heart + renin; β2 bronchodilation; β3 lipolysis + detrusor relaxation.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the transmitters and receptors of the sympathetic, parasympathetic and adrenal medullary pathways.</summary>

**Answer:** In both divisions ACh acts on Nn receptors in the ganglia. Sympathetic postganglionic neurons usually release NE on α/β receptors; parasympathetic postganglionic neurons release ACh on muscarinic receptors; the adrenal medulla releases epinephrine/NE into the blood. Sweat glands are sympathetic but use ACh on muscarinic receptors.

**Why:** Four output maps in the take-home slide.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Differentiate Nn from Nm and mention the muscarinic subtypes.</summary>

**Answer:** Both are ligand-gated ion channels. Nn (neuronal) are in autonomic ganglia and adrenal medulla; Nm (muscle) are at the neuromuscular junction. Muscarinic receptors are GPCRs: M1 (neuronal/gastric acid), M2 (heart, slows HR and AV conduction, blocked by atropine), M3 (glands and smooth muscle incl. bronchi and detrusor).

**Why:** Location defines function.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. List the adrenergic receptor subtypes with their main sites, effects and drug examples.</summary>

**Answer:** α1: vascular smooth muscle (vasoconstriction), radial iris, bladder neck; phenylephrine (agonist), prazosin/tamsulosin (blockers). α2: presynaptic, ↓ NE release. β1: heart/juxtaglomerular cells (↑ HR, contractility, renin); dobutamine, metoprolol. β2: bronchi, uterus, skeletal-muscle vessels (relaxation); salbutamol. β3: adipose tissue and detrusor (lipolysis, relaxation); mirabegron.

**Why:** Receptor number predicts the dominant target.
</details>

## 3. Organ responses

### Principle
- **Transmitter + receptor + tissue = effect**: **NE + α1 + arteriole → vasoconstriction**; **epinephrine + β2 + bronchus → relaxation**; **ACh + M2 + heart → slowing**.

### Cardiovascular
- **β1** ↑ HR, contractility, conduction; **α1** ↑ peripheral resistance; **M2** slows sinus rate and AV conduction; **limited direct parasympathetic control of most peripheral vessels**.

### Airway
- **β2** relaxes bronchi (**salbutamol/albuterol, salmeterol**); **M3** constricts bronchi and ↑ secretions (**ipratropium, tiotropium** block vagal effects).
- **β2 agonists** treat reversible bronchospasm in asthma/COPD; **antimuscarinics** are important in COPD.
- ⚠️ **Nonselective β-blockers can worsen bronchospasm** (block β2).

### Gastrointestinal
- **Parasympathetic** ↑ peristalsis, secretion, digestion. **Sympathetic** ↓ motility, secretion, intestinal blood flow and **contracts many GI sphincters**.

### Eye
- **α1** → radial iris contraction → **mydriasis** (phenylephrine).
- **M3** → sphincter pupillae → **miosis** (pilocarpine); **ciliary muscle** contraction → **accommodation for near vision**.
- Muscarinic agonists can improve **aqueous outflow** (glaucoma).

### Skin, sweat, temperature
- **Sweating:** sympathetic anatomy but **cholinergic at the gland (M)**; **atropine reduces sweating → impaired heat loss**.
- Cutaneous **vasoconstriction conserves heat**; **α-mediated piloerection**.

### Bladder
- **M3** → **detrusor contraction (micturition)**; **bethanechol** may help urinary retention.
- **β3** → **detrusor relaxation (storage)**; **mirabegron** for overactive bladder.
- **α1(A)** → **bladder neck/prostate contraction**; **tamsulosin** relaxes outlet in **BPH**; **α1 agonists** can treat **stress incontinence**.
- **Antimuscarinic (oxybutynin)** reduces detrusor overactivity.

### Metabolic effects of sympathetic activation
- ↑ **glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis**, **lipolysis** (free fatty acids), ↑ **blood glucose**; **β2** also drives **K⁺ into cells**.

### Baroreceptor reflex
- **↑ BP → stretch of carotid sinus and aortic arch baroreceptors → CN IX and X afferents → medullary NTS → ↓ sympathetic and ↑ vagal outflow → ↓ HR, contractility and vascular tone.**
- Impairment → labile BP or **orthostatic hypotension**.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Effect = transmitter + receptor + tissue (NE/α1/arteriole = constriction; epinephrine/β2/bronchus = relaxation; ACh/M2/heart = slowing).
- Airway: β2 dilates, M3 constricts; non-selective β-blockers worsen asthma.
- Eye: α1 mydriasis; M3 miosis and accommodation. Sweat glands cholinergic; atropine ↓ sweating.
- Bladder: M3 emptying, β3 + α1 storage; tamsulosin in BPH; mirabegron in OAB; oxybutynin ↓ detrusor overactivity.
- Baroreflex: ↑ BP → ↓ sympathetic, ↑ vagal.

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Explain the autonomic control of the airway and the drugs used.</summary>

**Answer:** β2 activation relaxes bronchial smooth muscle (salbutamol/albuterol, salmeterol, used in asthma/COPD); M3 activation constricts bronchi and increases secretions (blocked by inhaled ipratropium or tiotropium, important in COPD). Non-selective β-blockers oppose β2 bronchodilation and can worsen bronchospasm.

**Why:** β2 and M3 oppose each other.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Describe the autonomic control of the eye with drug examples.</summary>

**Answer:** α1 contracts the radial iris muscle → mydriasis (phenylephrine); M3 contracts the sphincter pupillae → miosis (pilocarpine) and the ciliary muscle → accommodation for near vision; muscarinic agonists improve aqueous outflow in glaucoma.

**Why:** Radial and circular iris muscles oppose each other.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Describe the baroreceptor reflex.</summary>

**Answer:** A rise in BP stretches carotid sinus and aortic arch baroreceptors; afferents travel in CN IX and X to the NTS in the medulla, which reduces sympathetic outflow and raises vagal outflow, lowering heart rate, contractility and vascular tone.

**Why:** Negative-feedback control of BP.
</details>

## 4. ANS drug classes and clinical application

| Class | Mechanism | Examples / uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Sympathomimetics** | activate adrenergic receptors or ↑ adrenergic transmission | **epinephrine** (α/β; **anaphylaxis, cardiac arrest**); **norepinephrine** (strong α1 + β1; **vasopressor in shock**); **isoproterenol** (non-selective β: ↑ HR, bronchodilation); **albuterol/salbutamol, salmeterol** (β2: asthma/COPD) |
| **Sympatholytics** | block adrenergic receptors or ↓ sympathetic tone | **α1 blockers** (**prazosin**, tamsulosin: vasodilation, relax bladder neck/prostate → BPH); **β-blockers** (↓ HR, contractility, renin; **metoprolol β1-selective**, **propranolol β1/β2**) |
| **Parasympathomimetics** | direct muscarinic agonists or **AChE inhibitors** ↑ ACh | **bethanechol** (bladder, GI), **pilocarpine** (xerostomia, glaucoma) |
| **Parasympatholytics (antimuscarinics)** | block M receptors | **atropine** (↑ HR by M2 blockade, ↓ secretions), **ipratropium/tiotropium** (inhaled bronchodilators) |

- **Antimuscarinic adverse effects:** **dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention, tachycardia**; toxicity → **reduced sweating, impaired heat loss**.
- **Drugs affecting transmitter handling:** **reserpine** depletes NE stores (rarely used); **MAO inhibitors** alter catecholamine metabolism; **tricyclic antidepressants** block catecholamine reuptake → autonomic side effects.

### Clinical application: anaphylaxis
- Pattern: **hypotension, bronchospasm, urticaria, possible airway edema**.
- **IM epinephrine promptly** for airway, breathing or circulation compromise: **α1** ↑ vascular tone and ↓ mucosal edema; **β1** supports cardiac output; **β2** bronchodilates and ↓ mediator release.

<div class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Sympathomimetics: epinephrine (anaphylaxis), norepinephrine (shock), isoproterenol, salbutamol/salmeterol.
- Sympatholytics: prazosin/tamsulosin (α1), metoprolol (β1), propranolol (β1/β2).
- Parasympathomimetics: bethanechol, pilocarpine, AChE inhibitors. Antimuscarinics: atropine, ipratropium, tiotropium.
- Antimuscarinic side effects: dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention, tachycardia, ↓ sweating.
- Anaphylaxis: IM epinephrine (α1 + β1 + β2).

</div>

**Short-essay questions**

<details><summary>Q1. Classify ANS drugs with an example for each class.</summary>

**Answer:** Sympathomimetics (epinephrine, norepinephrine, isoproterenol, salbutamol); sympatholytics (prazosin/tamsulosin α1 blockers; metoprolol, propranolol β-blockers); parasympathomimetics (bethanechol, pilocarpine, AChE inhibitors); parasympatholytics/antimuscarinics (atropine, ipratropium, tiotropium); and agents altering transmitter handling (reserpine, MAO inhibitors, tricyclics).

**Why:** Drugs act at receptors or alter transmitter handling.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Why is epinephrine the first-line drug in anaphylaxis?</summary>

**Answer:** It acts on several receptors: α1 raises vascular tone and reduces mucosal edema, β1 supports cardiac output, and β2 bronchodilates and reduces mediator release, so it treats the hypotension, bronchospasm and airway edema together; it is given IM promptly.

**Why:** One drug covers airway and circulation.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the adverse effects of antimuscarinic drugs?</summary>

**Answer:** Dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention and tachycardia (from receptor blockade); excessive blockade causes antimuscarinic toxicity, and reduced sweating may impair heat loss.

**Why:** Predicted from M blockade at each organ.
</details>

## ⚠️ Exam traps
- **Both divisions release ACh at the ganglion (Nn).**
- **Sweat glands are sympathetic but ACh/muscarinic.**
- **Adrenal medulla** = modified ganglion; releases **epinephrine into blood**.
- **M2 = heart (↓ HR); M3 = glands + smooth muscle (bronchi, detrusor, sphincter pupillae, ciliary muscle); M1 = gastric.**
- **β2 = bronchodilation; non-selective β-blockers worsen asthma.**
- **Metoprolol = β1-selective; propranolol = β1 + β2.**
- **Detrusor: M3 contracts (empties), β3 relaxes (stores);** bladder neck: **α1 contracts**.
- **Atropine blocks M2 → tachycardia.**
- **Parasympathetic ganglia are near/in the organ** (long pre, short post); sympathetic = short pre, long post.
