## 1. Overview & Parts of the clavicle

- **S-shaped long bone** يربط الـ **upper limb** بالـ **axial skeleton**.
- **Medially** يتمفصل مع الـ **manubrium** (sternum)؛ **laterally** مع الـ **acromion** (scapula).
- يشتغل **strut** يبعد الطرف العلوي عن الـ thorax، وينقل القوى من اليد للـ axial skeleton.
- **Soft-tissue coverage قليلة** → معرض للـ trauma.

### Parts (أثلاث)
| Part | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Medial / sternal third** | **sternoclavicular articulation** |
| **Middle third** | **junction of medial and lateral curves** — ⭐ **most common fracture site** |
| **Lateral / acromial third** | closely related to **coracoclavicular ligaments** |

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- S-shaped long bone linking upper limb to axial skeleton; medially manubrium, laterally acromion.
- Strut holding the limb away from the thorax; little soft-tissue cover → vulnerable.
- Middle third = junction of curves = most common fracture site; lateral third related to coracoclavicular ligaments.

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the clavicle and its articulations.</summary>

**Answer:** An S-shaped long bone connecting the upper limb to the axial skeleton; medially it articulates with the manubrium (sternoclavicular joint) and laterally with the acromion (acromioclavicular joint). It acts as a strut holding the upper limb away from the thorax and transmits forces to the axial skeleton; its little soft-tissue coverage makes it vulnerable to trauma.

**ليش؟** وصف شامل بسطرين.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the parts of the clavicle and their significance?</summary>

**Answer:** Medial (sternal) third — sternoclavicular articulation; middle third — junction of medial and lateral curves and the most common fracture site; lateral (acromial) third — closely related to the coracoclavicular ligaments.

**ليش؟** كل ثلث وياه نقطة مهمة.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why is the clavicle vulnerable to trauma?</summary>

**Answer:** Because it has relatively little soft-tissue coverage (almost subcutaneous) and acts as a strut transmitting forces from the upper limb.

**ليش؟** السبب التشريحي المباشر.
</details>

## 2. Attachments, Joints & Neurovascular relations

### Muscle attachments
| Muscle | Site |
|---|---|
| **Sternocleidomastoid** | **medial** clavicle |
| **Pectoralis major** | **anterior medial** clavicle |
| **Deltoid** | **lateral/anterior** clavicle |
| **Trapezius** | **posterior lateral** clavicle |

### Ligaments
- **Acromioclavicular ligament**: يثبت الـ **AC joint**.
- **Coracoclavicular ligament** (بالـ lateral third).
- **Costoclavicular ligament**.

### Joints
- **Sternoclavicular joint**: يربط الـ clavicle بالـ **axial skeleton**.
- **Acromioclavicular joint**: يربط الـ clavicle بالـ **scapula**.
- سوا يسمحون بـ **coordinated movement of the shoulder girdle**.

### ⭐ Neurovascular relations (تحت الـ clavicle)
- **Subclavian vessels**
- **Brachial plexus**
- **Apical pleura and lung** قريبين

### Functions
1. **Strut** بين الـ sternum والـ scapula.
2. يحافظ على **lateral position of the shoulder**.
3. **Transmits forces** من اليد للـ axial skeleton.
4. يسهّل **efficient shoulder movement**.

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- SCM: medial; pectoralis major: anterior medial; deltoid: lateral anterior; trapezius: posterior lateral.
- AC ligament stabilizes AC joint; coracoclavicular and costoclavicular ligaments.
- SC joint → axial skeleton; AC joint → scapula.
- Deep to clavicle: subclavian vessels, brachial plexus; apical pleura/lung nearby.
- Functions: strut, lateral shoulder position, force transmission, efficient movement.

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. List the muscle attachments of the clavicle.</summary>

**Answer:** Sternocleidomastoid — medial clavicle; pectoralis major — anterior medial clavicle; deltoid — lateral/anterior clavicle; trapezius — posterior lateral clavicle.

**ليش؟** مهمة لفهم إزاحة الكسور بعدين.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What important structures lie deep to the clavicle and why do they matter?</summary>

**Answer:** The subclavian vessels and brachial plexus lie deep to the clavicle, and the apical pleura and lung are nearby. They can be injured by fractures (neurovascular injury, pneumothorax), so skin and neurovascular status must always be assessed.

**ليش؟** يربط الـ anatomy بالمضاعفات.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What are the functions of the clavicle?</summary>

**Answer:** It acts as a strut between the sternum and scapula, maintains the lateral position of the shoulder, transmits forces from the upper limb to the axial skeleton and facilitates efficient shoulder movement.

**ليش؟** أربع وظائف مباشرة.
</details>

## 3. Epidemiology & Classification

- كسور الـ clavicle من **أشيع كسور الـ shoulder girdle**.
- الأسباب: **falls onto the shoulder**، **direct trauma**، **sports injuries**، **road-traffic accidents**.

### Classification by location
| Group | Site | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Group I** | **middle third** | ⭐ **most common** |
| **Group II** | **lateral / distal third** | stability depends partly on **coracoclavicular ligaments** |
| **Group III** | **medial third** | less common؛ لازم تقييم دقيق للـ **mediastinal structures** القريبة |

💡 **من ملزمة الـ Shoulder joint (anatomy)**: medial third ≈ **5%**، middle (junction of medial 2/3 and lateral 1/3) ≈ **80%**، والـ lateral third عنده **high nonunion risk** بسبب انقطاع الـ coracoclavicular ligament.

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Common causes: fall onto the shoulder, direct trauma, sports, road-traffic accidents.
- Group I middle third (most common); Group II lateral (coracoclavicular ligaments determine stability); Group III medial (check mediastinal structures).

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. Classify clavicle fractures by location.</summary>

**Answer:** Group I: middle-third fractures (most common). Group II: lateral/distal-third fractures, whose stability depends partly on the coracoclavicular ligaments. Group III: medial-third fractures, less common and requiring careful assessment of nearby mediastinal structures.

**ليش؟** التصنيف الوحيد بالملزمة.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. What are the common mechanisms of clavicle fracture?</summary>

**Answer:** Falls onto the shoulder, direct trauma, sports injuries and road-traffic accidents.

**ليش؟** أربع أسباب.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. Why do medial-third fractures need special assessment?</summary>

**Answer:** Because of their proximity to mediastinal structures, which may be injured.

**ليش؟** الـ mediastinum هو الكلمة المطلوبة.
</details>

## 4. Clinical presentation, Examination, Imaging & Displacement

### Clinical presentation
- **Pain around the clavicle**
- **Swelling and bruising**
- **Local tenderness** و **visible deformity**
- **Reduced shoulder movement**
- المريض **يسند اليد المصابة باليد الثانية**

### Neurovascular examination
- **Vascular**: **radial pulse**
- **Neurological**: motor and sensory، including **brachial plexus function**

### Imaging
- **Plain radiographs** = **initial investigation**
- **CT** ممكن يفيد بالحالات المعقدة

### Fracture displacement (midshaft)
- **Medial fragment** ينسحب **superiorly** بالعضلات المربوطة على الـ medial clavicle (⭐ **sternocleidomastoid**).
- **Lateral fragment** ينزل **inferiorly and medially** بسبب **وزن اليد** و **muscle forces**.

> ⚠️ **انتبه:** السؤال الكلاسيكي "which muscle elevates the medial fragment?" → **sternocleidomastoid**. والـ lateral fragment ينزل بسبب **gravity (weight of the limb)**.

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Pain, swelling, bruising, tenderness, deformity, reduced movement; patient supports the arm with the other hand.
- Always check radial pulse and brachial plexus (motor/sensory).
- Plain radiographs first; CT may help.
- Medial fragment ↑ (SCM); lateral fragment ↓ and medially (weight of limb + muscles).

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. Describe the clinical presentation of a clavicle fracture.</summary>

**Answer:** Pain around the clavicle, swelling and bruising, local tenderness and visible deformity, reduced shoulder movement, and the patient often supports the injured arm with the opposite hand.

**ليش؟** خمس علامات من الملزمة.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Explain the displacement of fragments in a midshaft clavicle fracture.</summary>

**Answer:** The medial fragment is pulled superiorly by muscles attached to the medial clavicle (sternocleidomastoid), while the lateral fragment moves inferiorly and medially because of the weight of the upper limb and muscle forces.

**ليش؟** العضلة + الجاذبية = الجواب.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. What must be examined in every clavicle fracture?</summary>

**Answer:** Skin and neurovascular status: vascular (radial pulse) and neurological (motor and sensory, including brachial plexus function).

**ليش؟** "Always assess skin and neurovascular status" من الـ key points.
</details>

## 5. Management & Complications

### Nonoperative (أغلب الكسور)
- **Sling** للراحة + **analgesia**.
- **Follow-up** سريري وشعاعي.

### Operative — Indications
1. **Open fracture**
2. **Threatened skin** (skin tenting)
3. **Associated neurovascular injury**
4. **Significant displacement/shortening** (selected patients)
5. **Unstable distal fracture**
6. **Symptomatic nonunion or malunion**

### Complications
| Early | Late |
|---|---|
| **skin compromise** | **delayed union** |
| **neurovascular injury** | **nonunion** |
| **pneumothorax** (rare) | **malunion** |
| | persistent pain، shoulder dysfunction |
| | **hardware irritation** (بعد الـ fixation) |

⭐ **Treatment should be individualized**.

<div dir="ltr" class="key-points">

**Key points**
- Most treated nonoperatively: sling + analgesia + clinical/radiographic follow-up.
- Surgery: open fracture, threatened skin, neurovascular injury, significant displacement/shortening, unstable distal fracture, symptomatic nonunion/malunion.
- Early: skin compromise, neurovascular injury, rare pneumothorax. Late: delayed union, nonunion, malunion, pain, dysfunction, hardware irritation.

</div>

**أسئلة Short essay**

<details><summary>Q1. List the indications for operative treatment of clavicle fractures.</summary>

**Answer:** Open fracture, threatened skin, associated neurovascular injury, significant displacement/shortening in selected patients, unstable distal fracture, and symptomatic nonunion or malunion.

**ليش؟** ست نقاط، سؤال مضمون.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2. Classify the complications of clavicle fractures.</summary>

**Answer:** Early: skin compromise, neurovascular injury and, rarely, pneumothorax. Late: delayed union, nonunion, malunion, persistent pain, shoulder dysfunction and hardware irritation after fixation.

**ليش؟** قسمها early و late.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3. How are most clavicle fractures managed?</summary>

**Answer:** Nonoperatively with a sling for comfort, analgesia, and clinical and radiographic follow-up; selected unstable or displaced fractures may benefit from fixation, and treatment should be individualized.

**ليش؟** الجواب يوازن بين الـ conservative والجراحة.
</details>
